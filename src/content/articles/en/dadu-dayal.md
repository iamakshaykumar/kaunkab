## The Dhunia of Ahmedabad

Dadu Dayal was born in **1544**. According to the biography *Dadu Janmalila*, written by his disciple **Jangopal**, he was born in **Ahmedabad**, although some scholars say that other sources do not confirm this. Most sources say he came from a Muslim **dhunia** (pinjara) family whose occupation was **cotton carding**. Some later traditions tried to link him to a Brahmin family and to make his birth miraculous, but historians regard this as a later addition.

Dadu's formal education was very little. According to tradition, at the ages of **11 and 18** he had a vision of a mysterious guru, **Baba Buddha** (Buddhan), whom he regarded as the source of his spiritual awakening. Some sources also speak of a deep influence on him of the **Nath yogis**.

## From Sambhar to Naraina

In his youth Dadu wandered and reached Rajasthan. At about the age of 25 he settled in **Sambhar** (the town of the salt lake), where his son **Garibdas** was born in **1573**. By **1579** he had come to **Amer**, the capital of the Kachwaha Rajputs.

The Dadu Panth tradition says that at the invitation of **Bhagwant Das** of Amer he also went to the court of **Akbar** and discussed religion with the emperor. This story is found in the Panth's texts; historians view it with caution. In the last years of his life he lived at **Naraina** near Jaipur and died there in **1603**. According to tradition his body was neither burned nor buried but left in the forest; nearby **Bhairana** is regarded as his place of samadhi.

## Dadu's Teaching

Dadu was a saint of **nirguna bhakti** — he regarded God as formless. His main points were:

- **Remembrance of the Name** (sumiran) and inner experience are true devotion;
- **Caste distinctions**, idol worship, pilgrimage and showy ritual are futile;
- Hindus and Muslims are both children of the same God;
- A true seeker does not take the side of any sect — Dadu called this being **"nipakh"** (impartial).

The echo of **Kabir** can be clearly heard in these ideas, so Dadu is often compared with Kabir.

## The Dadu Vani

Dadu composed sakhis and padas in a mixed language of **Braj** and Rajasthani, which survive as about **five thousand** verses. His disciple **Rajjab** compiled them as the *Dadu Anubhav Vani*. Today the **Dadu Vani** is the main scripture of the Dadu Panth, and in the Panth's temples it is recited and worshipped in place of an idol. Many disciples of the Dadu Panth were poets themselves — such as Rajjab and **Sundardas** — which produced a vast Dadupanthi literature. The early manuscripts of the songs of many saints of north India are also preserved in the collections of the Dadu Panth.

## The Dadu Panth

Dadu's followers at first called themselves the **Brahma Sampradaya**, which later came to be called the **Dadu Panth**. According to tradition Dadu had 152 disciples, of whom **52** established centres (**thambhas** or dwaras) in different places. Five places are regarded as the most sacred — **Naraina, Bhairana, Sambhar, Amer and Karadala**. In the course of his travels (**ramat**) Dadu stayed for long periods at these places. Naraina is the main seat of the Panth, where the head of the Panth lives, and a big fair is held there on Dadu's birth anniversary in February-March.

In the 18th century armed **Naga** sadhu akharas also formed within the Panth, which gave military service to the Jaipur state. Their organisation began in the time of **Jait Sahib** (1693–1734), and under an agreement of 1793 the Panth supplied the Jaipur state with about 5,000 armed sadhus. This was a historical turn away from Dadu's peaceful, nonviolent teaching. Today the Dadu Panth is active mainly in Rajasthan and is a living legacy of the nirguna stream of the Bhakti movement.
