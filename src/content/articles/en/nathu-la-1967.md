## Background — The Border after 1962

After the defeat in the **1962** India-China war, India greatly expanded its army and deployed new units along the Himalayan border. At that time **Sikkim** was a separate princely state and an Indian protectorate; India was responsible for its defence. Between Sikkim and Tibet lie passes such as **Nathu La** (over 14,000 feet high) and **Jelep La**, which open towards Tibet's **Chumbi Valley**. Nathu La was a place where Indian and Chinese soldiers were posted only a few metres from each other.

During the **1965** India-Pakistan war, China, putting pressure on India, demanded that posts on the Sikkim border be vacated. **Major General Sagat Singh**, commander of the **17 Mountain Division** deployed there, refused to vacate Nathu La, as the height was strategically important. After this, patrolling, scuffles and propaganda battles increased on both sides. At the same time China was going through the upheaval of the **Cultural Revolution**.

## The Wire Fence and the Firing of 11 September

To avoid daily confrontations on the border, the Indian Army decided to lay a **wire fence** from Nathu La to **Sebu La**, along the line India regarded as the border. Chinese soldiers objected. The defence of this area was under the **112 Mountain Brigade** (Brigadier **M. M. S. Bakshi**), and at Nathu La the **2 Grenadiers** battalion was deployed, commanded by **Lieutenant Colonel Rai Singh**.

On the morning of **11 September 1967**, while Indian engineers and soldiers were laying the wire, an argument and scuffle was followed by sudden **machine-gun** fire from Chinese positions. Indian soldiers working in the open suffered heavy losses. Lieutenant Colonel Rai Singh was wounded. **Captain Dagar** of **2 Grenadiers** and **Major Harbhajan Singh** of **18 Rajput** led their men in an assault on a Chinese machine-gun post; they reached the enemy but were killed in heavy fire. Major Harbhajan Singh was posthumously awarded the **Maha Vir Chakra** and Captain Dagar the **Vir Chakra**.

## The Artillery Battle

Soon **artillery** fire began from both sides. Here India had a big advantage — the Indian Army held high ground such as **Sebu La** and **Camel's Back**, from where the Chinese positions were clearly visible. Indian guns inflicted heavy damage on Chinese bunkers and positions. The shelling lasted about three days, and around **14 September** an uneasy ceasefire took hold. On **15 September** the two sides handed over the bodies of their dead to each other.

## The Cho La Clash

Only a few weeks later, on **1 October 1967**, there was another confrontation at the **Cho La** pass, a few kilometres north of Nathu La. Here an argument over a post turned into a scuffle and then firing. The fighting lasted about a day. The Indian units — **7/11 Gorkha Rifles** and **10 Jammu and Kashmir Rifles** — held firm. According to Indian military accounts, the Chinese soldiers fell back about three kilometres.

## Casualties — Differing Claims

The two countries' figures for casualties in these clashes differ. According to the **Indian Ministry of Defence**, in both clashes India lost **88** soldiers killed and **163** wounded, while China lost about **340** killed and **450** wounded. According to **Chinese** claims, 65 Indian and 32 Chinese soldiers were killed at Nathu La. The general view of independent analysts is that India gained a **decisive tactical advantage** in these clashes and the Chinese soldiers had to fall back from their positions.

## Significance and Legacy

The 1962 defeat had deeply affected the Indian Army's morale. The Nathu La and Cho La clashes showed that with better preparation, the right leadership and the advantage of height, the Indian Army could put up a tough fight against the Chinese army. Many historians regard this as a **turning point** in India-China military relations. After these events India's position on the Sikkim border grew stronger; in **1975** Sikkim became a state of India.

For a long time these clashes almost vanished from popular memory, but in recent years books, articles and films about them have appeared. Today Nathu La is also a tourist spot, and since **2006** India-China border trade has passed through it. Memorials have been built in the area in memory of the soldiers who fell in the fighting.
