## Why Kannauj?

In the seventh century **Harshavardhana** had made Kannauj his capital. After him this city became the symbol of power and prestige in northern India, as Pataliputra had been earlier. Lying in the middle of the fertile Ganges plain, it was also important for agriculture, trade and control of river routes. So any dynasty that wanted to be called "emperor of northern India" had its eye on Kannauj.

## The Three Claimants

- **Gurjara-Pratiharas:** the power of western India (Rajasthan, Malwa, Gujarat). Nagabhata I of this dynasty had checked the Arab invasions in the eighth century.
- **Palas:** the Buddhist-patron rulers of eastern India (Bengal and Bihar). Dharmapala and Devapala were their mightiest kings.
- **Rashtrakutas:** the power of the Deccan (today's Maharashtra-Karnataka), whose kings Dhruva, Govinda III and later Indra III kept campaigning in the north.

At that time Kannauj was ruled by weak kings of the **Ayudha dynasty** (Indrayudha and Chakrayudha), who became puppets in the hands of the big powers.

## The First Phase of the Struggle (about 785–816 CE)

- The Pratihara king **Vatsaraja** attacked Kannauj and also defeated the Pala king **Dharmapala**.
- Then the Rashtrakuta king **Dhruva** came north from the Deccan and defeated both Vatsaraja and Dharmapala, but went back south.
- Taking advantage of the chance, Dharmapala installed **Chakrayudha** at Kannauj as a subordinate king.
- Around 800 CE the Rashtrakuta king **Govinda III** campaigned in the north again, defeated the Pratihara king **Nagabhata II**, and Dharmapala and Chakrayudha also accepted his overlordship.
- After Govinda III returned, and after his later death, in about **816 CE** Nagabhata II conquered Kannauj and made it the capital of the Pratiharas. He also defeated the Palas near Munger.

## The Second Phase of the Struggle

The rivalry continued after this. In the time of the Pala king **Devapala** the Palas again showed their strength, but in the ninth century, under the Pratihara king **Mihira Bhoja** (about 836–885 CE) and his son **Mahendrapala I**, Pratihara power reached its peak, and their kingdom spread far and wide from west to east.

In the early tenth century, in about **916 CE**, the Rashtrakuta king **Indra III** attacked Kannauj and conquered it for a short time. The Pratiharas took the city back, but after this their power kept declining.

## Consequences

- In this struggle of about a century and a half, the resources and military strength of all three dynasties were continually drained.
- When the Pratiharas weakened, their feudatories such as the **Paramaras**, **Chandelas**, **Kalachuris** and **Chahamanas (Chauhans)** gradually became independent.
- By the end of the tenth century the Palas, Pratiharas and Rashtrakutas had all lost their old glory, and northern India was divided into many small and large kingdoms.

Many historians believe that because of this political fragmentation it became difficult for northern India in the eleventh century to face invaders such as Mahmud of Ghazni.
