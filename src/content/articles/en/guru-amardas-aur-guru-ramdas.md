## Guru Amar Das: A Journey That Began Late

Guru Amar Das was born on **5 May 1479** in the village of Basarke in today's Amritsar district. For a long time he was a follower of the Vaishnava tradition and engaged in farming and trade. At about the age of sixty, after hearing the hymns of Guru Nanak, he went to the second Guru, **Angad Dev**, at Khadur and served selflessly for years. In 1552 Guru Angad chose him as his successor — he was then about 73 years old.

## Goindwal, the Baoli and Langar

Guru Amar Das made **Goindwal**, on the bank of the Beas River, his centre. Here he built a **baoli (stepwell) with 84 steps**, which became an early Sikh pilgrimage place. He strengthened the tradition of **langar** — every person coming to the congregation was expected to forget caste distinctions and first sit in the pangat and eat.

According to Sikh tradition, Emperor **Akbar** also came to meet the Guru around 1567 and first sat in the pangat and ate langar. This episode is famous in Sikh sources, but many of its details rest on tradition and are hard to confirm from contemporary Persian sources.

## The Manji System and Social Reform

To manage the growing congregations Guru Amar Das created the **manji system** — he set up **22 manjis** (preaching centres) in different regions and entrusted trusted Sikhs there with the work of teaching and organisation. According to tradition, women were also among them. He began the tradition of mass gatherings at Goindwal on Baisakhi and Diwali.

He opposed **sati** and **purdah** and supported widow remarriage. His composition **Anandu Sahib** is still an integral part of Sikh weddings and rituals. The **Goindwal Pothis**, collecting the Gurus' hymns, were also prepared in his time, and became the basis for the compilation of the Adi Granth. He died at Goindwal on **1 September 1574**.

## Guru Ram Das

The fourth Guru, Ram Das, was born on **24 September 1534** in the Chuna Mandi of Lahore in a Sodhi Khatri family. His childhood name was **Jetha**. After being orphaned he came to Goindwal and became absorbed in the service of Guru Amar Das. The Guru married his daughter **Bibi Bhani** to him and in 1574 handed him the Guru's seat in preference to his own sons.

## The Founding of Ramdaspur

Guru Ram Das laid the foundation of a new town at a place called 'Guru da Chak', which was called **Ramdaspur**. Here he had work started on digging a sarovar (pool); the town later came to be called **Amritsar** after this 'Amrit Sarovar'. There is disagreement among the sources on how the land was obtained — according to one account it was bought from the landlords of Tung village with the congregation's offerings, while some traditions link it to a grant from Akbar. The Guru invited people of various occupations to settle here, so the town also became a centre of trade.

He added to the manji system representatives called **masands**, who led the distant congregations and collected offerings. The Guru Granth Sahib contains 638 of his shabads, including the four **Lavan** of the Sikh wedding.

## Succession and Legacy

Guru Ram Das chose his youngest son **Arjan Dev** as his successor. The elder son **Prithi Chand** opposed this and later led a separate faction (the 'Minas'). Guru Ram Das died at Goindwal on **1 September 1581**. From his time onward the Guru's seat remained in the Sodhi family. In Ramdaspur, founded by him, Guru Arjan Dev built the Harmandir Sahib — thus the organised congregation that began at Goindwal got a permanent centre.
