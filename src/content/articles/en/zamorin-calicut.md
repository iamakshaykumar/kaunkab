## Samuthiri: Name and Origin

The rulers of Calicut were called **Samuthiri** in Malayalam, which European travellers turned into **"Zamorin"**. They belonged to a dynasty called the **Nediyirippu Swaroopam**, who had earlier been chiefs of **Eranad**. Succession was **matrilineal** — the eldest male of the family became the Samuthiri.

About their origin there is a story in the 17th-century work *Keralolpatti*: the last **Cheraman Perumal** gave two brothers, **Manichchan** and **Vikraman**, the marshy land of Kozhikode, his sword, and permission to expand the kingdom further. This is a legend. According to historians the rise of Calicut as a city and port took place in the **13th-14th century**. The title **"Samuthiri"** is mentioned in the writing of the traveller **Ibn Battuta** (1340s). So there is no single certain date for the beginning of the kingdom.

## The Port of Spices

After the old port of Muziris silted up around 1340, Calicut rose rapidly. **Pepper** and **ginger** from Malabar, cloves from the east, cinnamon from Sri Lanka, and silk and porcelain from China — all were sold here. A large part of the Zamorin's income came from the tax on this trade. The port was managed by an officer called the **Shahbandar**.

The **Mappila** (Muslim) community, formed by a mixing of Arab traders and local people, was a close ally of the Zamorins. Ibn Battuta mentioned large **Chinese ships** here, and from **1406** the Ming fleets of the Chinese admiral **Zheng He** came to Calicut several times. It is believed that Zheng He died around Calicut in 1433, though there is no complete agreement on this.

## Mamankam and the Expansion of the Kingdom

The Zamorins expanded their kingdom by defeating neighbouring chiefs. The most important step was the taking of **Tirunavaya** on the banks of the **Bharathapuzha** (Nila) river, where a great festival and trade fair called **Mamankam** was held every **12 years**. Earlier it was presided over by the rulers of **Valluvanad**; after the Zamorin seized it, the **Chaver** warriors of Valluvanad made suicidal attacks at every Mamankam to kill the Zamorin. The date of the last Mamankam is disputed — 1743, 1755 or 1766.

## The Portuguese and the Conflict

In **May 1498** **Vasco da Gama** arrived at **Kappad** near Calicut. The Zamorin allowed him to trade, but the Portuguese goods were not suited to the market here, and his demand for a monopoly on pepper was not accepted. When **Cabral** came in **1500**, there was friendship at first, but after a riot at the Portuguese warehouse the Portuguese **bombarded** Calicut with cannon.

After this a conflict of about a hundred years followed. The Portuguese allied with the king of **Cochin**, the Zamorin's rival. In the battle of Diu in 1509 the Egyptian-Gujarati fleets, allies of the Zamorin, were defeated. In **1510** **Albuquerque** attacked Calicut, but he failed and was himself wounded; after this the Portuguese made **Goa** their main base.

## The Kunjali Marakkars: Defenders of the Sea

The command of the Zamorin's navy was in the hands of a Muslim seafaring family called the **Marakkars**, four of whose chiefs received the title **"Kunjali"**. They are credited with the first organised naval defence of the Indian coast:

- **Kunjali I** — made sudden attacks on Portuguese ships with small, fast boats; killed near Sri Lanka in 1539.
- **Kunjali II** — continued the fight against the Portuguese; but in 1540 the Zamorin had to make a settlement with them. The Portuguese fort at **Chaliyam** near Calicut remained a constant threat to the Zamorin.
- **Kunjali III** — in **1571** brought down the fort of Chaliyam and built a strong fort at **Kottakkal** (Iringal) near Vadakara.
- **Kunjali IV** — his growing power led to a clash with the Zamorin. The Zamorin, together with the Portuguese, besieged Kottakkal; the attack of 1598 failed, but a second one in **1600** succeeded. On a promise of pardon Kunjali surrendered to the Zamorin, but the Zamorin handed him over to the Portuguese and he was put to death at **Goa**.

Historians are not agreed on the reasons for this clash — some sources speak of Kunjali IV's pride and the insult to the Zamorin, while others hold the discord spread by the Portuguese responsible.

## Decline

In the 17th century the Dutch and the English also came to Malabar. In 1755 Travancore defeated the Zamorin at Purakkad. In **1766** **Hyder Ali** of Mysore invaded Calicut; seeing defeat certain, the Zamorin set fire to his palace and gave up his life in it. In **1792** Tipu Sultan ceded Malabar to the British, and the Zamorin family was left as a nominal house drawing a pension from the Company.
