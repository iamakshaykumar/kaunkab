## Heir of the Chaulukya Dynasty

From the tenth to the thirteenth century, Gujarat was ruled by the **Chaulukya** dynasty, also popularly called **Solanki**. Its capital was **Anahilavada Patan** (Anahilapataka). **Jayasimha**, son of King **Karna** and Queen **Mayanalla Devi**, came to the throne in about **1092 CE**. Mayanalla Devi was a princess of the Kadamba dynasty. According to the historian A. K. Majumdar, Jayasimha became king at a young age, so in the early years his mother probably handled the affairs of the state as regent.

Jayasimha ruled for about fifty years, and the title **'Siddharaja'** became so attached to his name that he is known by it today.

## Conquest of Malwa: 'Avantinatha'

For generations there had been conflict between the Chaulukyas of Gujarat and the **Paramaras** of Malwa. In the 1130s Siddharaja defeated the Paramaras decisively and probably captured their capital **Dhara** in **1135–1136 CE**. Sources **disagree** on whether the Paramara king was **Naravarman** or his son **Yashovarman**.

After this victory Siddharaja took the title **'Avantinatha'** (lord of Avanti, that is, Malwa), which first appears in an inscription of 1137 CE.

## Saurashtra and Khengar

Siddharaja also defeated **Khengar**, the **Chudasama** king of Saurashtra. A famous **folk tale** is attached to this war — it is said that Siddharaja wished to marry **Ranakadevi**, but she was married to Khengar; after Khengar's defeat and death, Ranakadevi chose to become a sati. This story is found in Gujarati folk songs and later accounts; its historicity is **not proven**.

A later inscription calls him **'Barbarakajishnu'**, that is, 'the conqueror of Barbaraka'. In Hemachandra's account Barbaraka is a demon; historians link him to some local tribal chief, but his identity is **uncertain**.

## Hemachandra and the Patronage of Learning

The greatest figure at Siddharaja's court was the Jain scholar **Hemachandra**. In the king's name he composed a vast grammar of Sanskrit and Prakrit, the **'Siddha-Hema-Shabdanushasana'** — joining 'Siddha' (Siddharaja) and 'Hema' (Hemachandra). Jain prabandha texts tell a story that after conquering Dhara the king saw the works written by King Bhoja there and wished that Gujarat too should have its own grammar; this story rests on **tradition**. Hemachandra also wrote the **'Dvyashraya Kavya'** on the history of the Chaulukya kings.

Siddharaja himself was a Shaiva, but he also respected Jain scholars — a sign of the religious tolerance of Gujarat at that time.

## Sahasralinga Lake and Rudra Mahalaya

Siddharaja was also a great builder:

- **Sahasralinga Lake (Patan)** — he renovated a lake built by one of his ancestors and had **1008 Shiva temples** built around it, naming it Sahasralinga.
- **Rudra Mahalaya (Siddhapur)** — this vast Shiva temple complex was completed in his time, around **1140–1142 CE**. It is believed that earlier Chaulukya kings had begun its construction and Siddharaja completed it on a far larger scale. In the 1290s the army of Alauddin Khalji, and in the fifteenth century Ahmad Shah I, the Sultan of Gujarat, did heavy damage to it; part of the complex was converted into a mosque. Today only its ruins survive.

## Succession and Significance

Siddharaja had no son. After his death in about **1142 CE**, his distant relative **Kumarapala** became king, who later, under Hemachandra's influence, became a great patron of Jainism.

In Siddharaja Jayasimha's time the Chaulukya kingdom of Gujarat became one of the greatest powers of western India. The conquest of Malwa, control over Saurashtra, the patronage of a scholar like Hemachandra and grand construction — all of these made him the most famous king in the history and folklore of Gujarat.
