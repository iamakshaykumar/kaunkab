## Childhood and Education

**Subhas Chandra Bose** was born on **23 January 1897** at **Cuttack** (then in the Bengal Presidency, today in Odisha). His father **Janakinath Bose** was a well-known lawyer and his mother's name was **Prabhavati Devi**. In his teens he was deeply influenced by the ideas of Swami Vivekananda. At Presidency College, Calcutta, he was expelled in 1916 after an incident involving the English professor E. F. Oaten; later he took a B.A. in philosophy in 1918 from the Scottish Church College. In 1919 he went to England, studied at Cambridge and in the **ICS** examination of **1920** he secured **fourth place**.

## Leaving the ICS for the Movement

The Non-Cooperation Movement was going on in the country. On **22 April 1921** Subhas wrote to the Secretary of State for India, asking to have his name removed from the list of ICS probationers, and he returned to India in July 1921. Here he began working with **Chittaranjan Das**, whom he regarded as his political guru. In 1924, while Das was Mayor, he became the Chief Executive Officer of the Calcutta Corporation. In 1925 he was arrested and sent to **Mandalay Jail** in Burma, where his health deteriorated. Released in 1927, he became General Secretary of the Congress and, along with Jawaharlal Nehru, emerged as a leader of that stream of youth who wanted **complete independence** instead of dominion status. In 1930 he was elected Mayor of Calcutta.

## Congress President and Clash with Gandhi

In the mid-1930s, because of illness and exile, he lived in Europe for a long time. There he met **Emilie Schenkl** of Austria, whom he married; their daughter **Anita** was born in 1942.

In 1938, at the **Haripura** session in Gujarat, Subhas was elected **Congress President**. He took the initiative in forming the National Planning Committee. In 1939 he contested again and defeated **Pattabhi Sitaramayya**, who was backed by Gandhi. Gandhi called this defeat his own defeat, and after the **Tripuri** session a deadlock continued over the formation of the Working Committee. In the end, in **April 1939**, Subhas resigned as President.

## The Forward Bloc and Leaving the Country

In 1939 Subhas formed the **All India Forward Bloc** to bring together those within the Congress with leftist and radical ideas. When the Second World War began, he believed that Britain's difficulty was India's opportunity and that a mass movement should be launched at once. In 1940 he was arrested and then kept under house arrest in his Calcutta home.

On the night of **16–17 January 1941** he slipped out of the house in disguise and, travelling through Afghanistan and the Soviet Union, reached **Berlin** in April 1941. There he formed the 'Free India Centre' and an 'Indian Legion' from Indian prisoners of war. In February 1943 he made a long journey by submarine to South-East Asia, where from July 1943 he took over the leadership of the **Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj)** and in October 1943 announced the **Provisional Government of Free India** in Singapore. A detailed account of this period is in the article on 'Quit India and the Azad Hind Fauj'.

## 1945: Death and Controversy

After Japan's defeat in the war, it was announced that on **18 August 1945** he died at **Taihoku** (today's Taipei, Taiwan) from severe burns in a plane crash. His ashes are kept at the **Renkoji Temple** in Tokyo. The Government of India's **Shah Nawaz Committee (1956)** and **Khosla Commission (1970–74)** accepted that he died in the crash. By contrast, the **Mukherjee Commission (2005)** called the plane crash story unbelievable, but the government did not accept its report in 2006. Most historians accept the account of the crash, while public debate on it continues even today.

## Legacy

There have been differences among historians about Subhas Bose's methods — especially his wartime cooperation with Germany and Japan — but there is wide agreement about his patriotism, sacrifice and organising ability. Remembered by the name 'Netaji', Bose's birth anniversary, 23 January, has been celebrated by the Government of India since 2021 as **'Parakram Diwas'**.
