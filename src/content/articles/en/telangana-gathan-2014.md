## The 1956 Merger and Old Discontent

At the time of independence, **Telangana** was the Telugu-speaking part of the Nizam's **Hyderabad State**. Hyderabad joined India in 1948. Meanwhile, in 1953 the Telugu-speaking **Andhra State** was carved out of Madras State. When states were being reorganised on the basis of language, the question arose whether Telangana should be merged with Andhra.

The **States Reorganisation Commission** (Fazal Ali Commission) advised caution on this in 1955. The Commission felt that the people of Telangana feared that the better-educated and more prosperous people of coastal Andhra would get ahead in jobs and resources, so the decision on a merger should be taken some years later after ascertaining Telangana's wishes. Nevertheless, the leaders of the two regions concluded the **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956)**, which contained promises to protect Telangana's interests, and on **1 November 1956** **Andhra Pradesh** was formed.

## The 1969 Agitation and 'Jai Andhra'

Within a few years complaints grew in Telangana that the promises of the agreement on jobs, irrigation and the budget were not being kept. In **1969** an agitation that began among students spread across the whole region. The **Telangana Praja Samithi** was formed, and **Marri Chenna Reddy** later became its main leader. A large number of people, especially students, were killed in police firing and violence; estimates of the number of dead differ between sources.

In the 1971 Lok Sabha election the Telangana Praja Samithi won 10 of the region's 14 seats, but in the same year its leaders returned to the Congress. In 1972 the **'Jai Andhra' movement** arose from the opposite direction, after the Supreme Court upheld the **Mulki rules**, which gave preference in jobs to local people. In 1973 the **Six-Point Formula** was introduced, which kept the demand quiet for some decades, but did not end it.

## The TRS and a New Phase of the Movement

In **April 2001** **K. Chandrashekar Rao** (KCR) left the Telugu Desam Party and founded the **Telangana Rashtra Samithi (TRS)**, whose sole aim was to secure a separate Telangana state with Hyderabad as its capital. The thinker **Prof. K. Jayashankar** came to be regarded as the movement's intellectual guide. The TRS allied with the Congress and other parties at different times, but the demand was not met.

On **29 November 2009** KCR began a fast unto death. Protests by students, particularly those of **Osmania University**, raised tension in Hyderabad. On **9 December 2009** Union Home Minister **P. Chidambaram** announced that the process of forming Telangana state would be initiated. In protest an agitation flared up in coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema, and on **23 December** the Centre said it would move forward only after a consensus among all parties.

## The Srikrishna Committee and the Mass Movement

On **3 February 2010** the Centre set up a committee chaired by **Justice B. N. Srikrishna**. Its report, made public on **6 January 2011**, contained six options; the committee described keeping the state united while giving constitutional safeguards to Telangana as the best option, and bifurcation as the second.

After this the movement intensified. The **Telangana Joint Action Committee (JAC)**, led by Prof. **M. Kodandaram**, held the **'Million March'** in Hyderabad on **10 March 2011**. From **13 September 2011** the **Sakala Janula Samme** (general strike) began, lasting 42 days. In this period the suicides of several young people also drew the attention of the whole country.

## The Bill in Parliament and the New State

On **30 July 2013** the Congress Working Committee passed a resolution to create a separate Telangana state. On **3 October 2013** the Union Cabinet approved it, providing for **Hyderabad** to remain the **common capital** of both states for ten years. The Andhra Pradesh Legislative Assembly rejected the draft bill, but under Article 3 of the Constitution the final decision rests with Parliament.

On **18 February 2014** the Lok Sabha, amid heavy uproar, passed the **Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014** by voice vote, and the Rajya Sabha passed it on **20 February**. On **1 March 2014** the President signed it. In the April–May 2014 elections the TRS won a majority in the Telangana Legislative Assembly, and on **2 June 2014** **Telangana** became India's **29th state**. **K. Chandrashekar Rao** became the first Chief Minister, and **E. S. L. Narasimhan** the Governor of both states. At the start the state had 10 districts.

## Legacy

The formation of Telangana is an example of how in India, even between regions that speak the same language, questions of development, water, jobs and identity can become a demand for a new state. For supporters it was the victory of a struggle lasting decades, while many people in Andhra Pradesh saw it as the loss of a developed capital like Hyderabad. After the division Andhra Pradesh planned the construction of its new capital, **Amaravati**. Telangana celebrates **State Formation Day** every year on **2 June**, and in 2022 the TRS changed its name to **Bharat Rashtra Samithi (BRS)**.
