## The Cobbler Saint of Kashi

**Ravidas** (also Raidas or Rohidas) was born near Kashi (Varanasi) in a **cobbler** (chamar) family whose work was tanning leather and making shoes. At that time this community was considered "untouchable". His date of birth is uncertain: some traditions give a very early date such as 1377, but most scholars consider him active in the **latter part of the fifteenth century and the early sixteenth century**.

His followers regard **Seer Govardhanpur** in Varanasi as his birthplace, while some scholars name the nearby village of Mandurgarh (Mandur). His wife is said to have been named **Lona Devi**. Ravidas did not give up his hereditary work; he went on mending shoes while composing devotional verses.

## Guru, Contemporaries and Disciples

Medieval texts describe him as a disciple of the Vaishnava teacher **Ramananda**, and **Kabir** is regarded as his fellow disciple or contemporary. Some manuscripts also contain a dialogue between Kabir and Ravidas on the nature of God.

According to tradition, the princess of Chittor, **Mirabai**, accepted Ravidas as her guru; the name "Raidas" appears in some of Mira's verses. There is also a story of the queen of Chittor, Jhali, becoming his disciple. Later **Anantdas** wrote his biography, the 'Parchai', which contains many miracle stories. Historians believe that these stories show the social feelings of that time more than historical fact.

## The Voice of Nirguna Bhakti

Ravidas was a saint of **nirguna** bhakti: he stressed worshipping God without binding Him to any idol or form, through remembrance of the Name and a sincere heart. His utterances are in simple spoken language that bears the mark of Braj, Awadhi and Punjabi.

In his verses the idea comes up again and again that no person is high or low by birth; before God all are equal. He does not hide his caste but proudly says that devotion has made even a cobbler worthy of reverence. The well-known saying "If the heart is pure, the Ganga is in the washing bowl" is also traditionally attributed to him.

## Begumpura: The City Without Sorrow

Ravidas's most discussed hymn is about **Begumpura**, "be-gham" meaning where there is no sorrow. In this hymn he imagines a city where there is no tax, no fear, no worry; no second or third class, and where everyone can roam freely at will without hindrance. This hymn is recorded in the Guru Granth Sahib in **Raga Gauri**.

Historians regard it as one of the oldest visions in Indian literature of a society based on equality.

## The Guru Granth Sahib and Legacy

The fifth Sikh Guru, **Guru Arjan Dev**, included about **40 hymns** of Ravidas in the Adi Granth (40 or 41 according to the count). Sikh tradition calls him **Bhagat Ravidas**.

Today Ravidas has crores of followers, especially in Punjab, Uttar Pradesh and among Indians settled abroad. His birth anniversary is celebrated on **Magh Purnima**. The **Ravidassia** community regards him as its chief guru. The Government of India issued postage stamps on him in 1971 and 2001. For movements struggling for a caste-free society, Ravidas and his "Begumpura" remain an inspiration even today.
