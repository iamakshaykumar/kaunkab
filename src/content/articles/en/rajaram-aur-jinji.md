## A Throne Received in Crisis

Rajaram was born on **24 February 1670** at **Rajgad** fort. He was the son of Chhatrapati Shivaji and Soyrabai and the younger half-brother of Sambhaji. After Shivaji's death in 1680 a faction at court tried to seat him on the throne, but Sambhaji took power and Rajaram lived for a long time in a situation like house arrest.

In February 1689 Sambhaji was captured by the Mughals and in March 1689 he was killed. In this crisis Rajaram was proclaimed Chhatrapati at **Raigad** (the sources mention both February and March 1689 for the date). From **25 March 1689** the Mughal army besieged Raigad. It was decided that it was dangerous for the king to remain trapped in the fort — Sambhaji's wife Yesubai and the child Shahu stayed in Raigad, and later went into Mughal captivity.

## Flight Towards Jinji

Rajaram set out south with a few companions by way of Pratapgad and Vishalgad. In disguise, crossing rivers, he reached the far side of the Tungabhadra, where **Chennamma**, queen of Keladi, helped him. On **1 November 1689** he reached the fort of **Jinji** in the Tamil country — this fort, spread over three hills, had been won by Shivaji in his southern campaign of 1677.

The responsibility of government in Maharashtra was given to **Ramchandra Pant Amatya**, while at Jinji advisers such as **Prahlad Niraji** stayed with the king. In this way one centre of the Maratha state moved to the south, and the battlefield of the struggle spread over the whole Deccan.

## The Eight-Year Siege

Aurangzeb sent the commander **Zulfiqar Khan** to Jinji, whose army reached there in 1690. But the fort's circuit was so large that the Mughals could not close it fully, and supplies kept coming from outside. Around 1693 Prince **Kam Bakhsh** was suspected of secret talks with Rajaram and was taken into custody by his own commanders. Because of such internal wrangling in the Mughal camp, the strength of the fort and Maratha attacks, the siege dragged on for about eight years. Finally Rajaram slipped out of the fort and on **8 January 1698** Jinji fell to the Mughals. According to some contemporary rumours, Mughal officers themselves had given Rajaram advance information — this is not confirmed.

## Santaji and Dhanaji

In this period the real strength of the Marathas was their cavalry commanders. **Santaji Ghorpade** and **Dhanaji Jadhav** wore out the Mughal armies with swift guerrilla attacks. In December 1692 they captured the Mughal faujdar Ali Mardan Khan, and in **November 1695** Santaji surrounded and defeated the Mughal commander Qasim Khan at **Dodderi**. In Maratha tradition there is also the famous story that Santaji's soldiers raided Aurangzeb's camp and cut off the golden finials of his tent — this should be seen only as a folk tale.

But there were also deep differences between the two commanders, and between Santaji and Rajaram. Around 1696–97 Santaji was murdered, after which Dhanaji became the chief commander.

## Satara and the Last Years

Returning from Jinji, Rajaram made **Satara** his centre. Aurangzeb was now himself besieging the Maratha forts one by one. Struggling with a lung illness, Rajaram died at **Sinhagad** on **3 March 1700**; he was then about thirty years old.

## Significance

Rajaram is not regarded as a military hero like Shivaji or Sambhaji, but his period was decisive in Maratha history. With the king going south it became impossible for the Mughals to end the Maratha state at one stroke, and the struggle, no longer dependent on a single capital, turned into a scattered but flexible network of chiefs. This arrangement also had a cost — granting jagirs and collection rights to chiefs increased their independent power. After Rajaram his wife **Tarabai** carried the same struggle forward.
