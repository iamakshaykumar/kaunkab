## Background: Damin-i-Koh and the 'Dikus'

In the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, many Santhal families came from Manbhum, Bankura and nearby areas and settled around the **Rajmahal Hills**. In the 1830s the East India Company demarcated this region as **Damin-i-Koh** and encouraged the Santhals to clear the forest and cultivate, so that revenue would increase.

The Santhals worked hard to turn the forest into fields, but soon outsiders, whom they called **'dikus'**, tightened their grip on them. Moneylenders lent at high interest, cheated in the accounts, and when borrowers could not repay, seized their land or made the debtor a bonded labourer. Zamindars and the police often sided with the moneylenders, and the courts were distant and expensive. The Santhals' complaints went unheard again and again.

## 30 June 1855: The Meeting at Bhognadih

Four brothers of the village of **Bhognadih** (today in Sahibganj district, Jharkhand), **Sido, Kanhu, Chand and Bhairav Murmu**, organised the Santhals. Sido and Kanhu announced that they had received a divine command to drive the exploiters out of the region and establish the Santhals' own rule.

On **30 June 1855**, about ten thousand Santhals gathered at Bhognadih (some accounts give a far larger number). It was here that the 'Hul' was declared. Along with the Santhals, people of some other poor and oppressed communities also joined the movement.

## Spread of the Rebellion

In July 1855 the rebellion spread rapidly. The rebels attacked the houses of moneylenders and zamindars, police posts and Company outposts. There were clashes in many places in the direction of Rajmahal, Pakur and Birbhum. Fighting with bows and arrows and traditional weapons, the Santhals pushed back some Company detachments in the early days.

Later, when the Santhals suffered heavy losses in open battle against an army equipped with guns and cannon, they began guerrilla attacks from the forests.

## Suppression: Martial Law and Heavy Loss of Life

In November 1855 the government imposed **martial law** in the rebel area, which was lifted in early January 1856. The army burned village after village. Estimates of the number of Santhals killed differ; generally deaths of ten to fifteen thousand or more are mentioned.

The main leaders, including Sido and Kanhu, were captured and killed during 1855–56. Sources differ on the exact dates and circumstances of their deaths; many details connected with Bhognadih and Barhait are current in folk memory. In Santhal tradition the bravery of their sisters **Phulo and Jhano** is also remembered with special respect.

## Results and Legacy

The rebellion was crushed, but the government had to accept the Santhals' grievances in part. A special law of 1855 made the **Santhal Parganas** a separate district, where a different administrative system was applied instead of the general laws. In the rules and laws made later, especially the Santhal Parganas Tenancy Act, the transfer of tribal land to non-tribals was prohibited.

Occurring two years before the revolt of 1857, the Santhal Hul is counted among the biggest tribal rebellions of colonial India. In Jharkhand **30 June** is celebrated every year as **'Hul Diwas'**, and a memorial to Sido and Kanhu stands at Bhognadih.
