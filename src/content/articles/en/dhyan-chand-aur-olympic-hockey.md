## From Soldier to Sportsman

**Dhyan Chand** was born on **29 August 1905** in **Allahabad** (today's Prayagraj). His family later settled in **Jhansi**. In **1922** he joined the British Indian Army as a soldier in the **First Brahmans** regiment; this unit later became part of the Punjab Regiment. His hockey flourished while playing for army teams. His control of the ball was so remarkable that people began to call him the **"Wizard of Hockey"**.

His younger brother **Roop Singh** was also an excellent player, and the two played the Olympics together.

## Amsterdam 1928: The First Gold

At the **1928 Amsterdam Olympics** India took part in Olympic hockey for the first time. The team's captain was **Jaipal Singh**. India beat Austria, Belgium, Denmark and Switzerland in the group and on **26 May 1928** won the gold by defeating host **Netherlands 3–0** in the final. Over the whole tournament India scored 29 goals and did not concede a single one.

Dhyan Chand was the top goal-scorer of the tournament, with **14 goals** according to most sources (15 in some counts). This was a big thing for an India that was then under British rule; for the first time an Indian team made such a mark on the world stage.

## Los Angeles 1932: The Second Gold

The **1932** Olympics were held in **Los Angeles** in the United States. Because of the economic depression and the long distance, only **three teams** reached the hockey event, India, Japan and the United States. Under captain **Lal Shah Bokhari**, India beat Japan **11–1** on **4 August** and the United States **24–1** on **11 August 1932** to win the second gold. Dhyan Chand and Roop Singh both scored many goals, and they came to be called the "hockey twins".

## Berlin 1936: Captain Dhyan Chand

At the **1936 Berlin Olympics** Dhyan Chand was the team's **captain**. India beat Hungary, the United States and Japan, and crushed France **10–0** in the semi-final. On **15 August 1936** the final was against host **Germany**. India won **8–1** and completed a third consecutive gold, a "hat-trick" in Olympic hockey. This was the only goal scored against India in the whole tournament, and in five matches India scored a total of **38 goals**. In the final, besides Dhyan Chand, Roop Singh, Carlyle Tapsell, Syed Mohammad Jaffar and Iqtidar Ali Dara also scored for India.

In the final Dhyan Chand himself scored **three goals**, as his autobiography and the records of the International Hockey Federation say, although some newspapers reported four goals. He also lost a tooth in a collision with the German goalkeeper.

## Legends and Facts

Many stories are famous about Dhyan Chand. It is said that **Hitler** offered him German citizenship and a high rank in the army, which he refused. This story is very popular, but no solid contemporary evidence for it is found, so historians regard it as a legend. Similarly, the tale is told that officials somewhere broke his stick to check whether there was a magnet in it; this too is an unverified episode linked to the magical image of his play.

## Honours and Legacy

Dhyan Chand retired from the army in **1956** and is commonly called **Major** Dhyan Chand. The same year he received the **Padma Bhushan**. His autobiography *Goal* (1952) counts 570 goals in international matches; these figures cannot be fully verified today. He died in Delhi on **3 December 1979**.

His birthday, **29 August**, is celebrated every year as **National Sports Day**, and since 2021 the country's highest sporting honour has carried his name. These three golds of 1928–1936 recall the era when the whole world acknowledged India's dominance on the hockey field.
