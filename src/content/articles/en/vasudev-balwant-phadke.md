## Early Life

**Vasudev Balwant Phadke** was born in 1845 in the village of **Shirdhon** in today's Raigad district, in an ordinary Chitpavan Brahmin family. He left his studies midway and came to Pune, where he worked as a clerk in the army accounts department (Military Finance) for about fifteen years. In this period he came in contact with the wrestler and physical-training teacher **Lahuji Vastad Salve**, who trained him in the use of arms and stressed bringing the backward castes into the struggle for freedom. In 1860 he was also among the founders of an educational institution in Pune.

Many biographers say that when his mother was seriously ill he was refused leave and could not see her at the end, which made Phadke bitter toward British rule. This episode is very popular in tradition, but its details come mainly from later biographies.

## Famine and Discontent

In 1876–78 the Deccan was in the grip of a terrible famine. Phadke saw that even amid starvation, peasants were distressed by the collection of government land revenue and the lack of relief. He concluded that petitions and speeches alone would change nothing. He travelled around the Deccan trying to awaken people to the cause of swaraj (self-rule), but did not get much support from the educated middle class.

## The Rebellion (1879)

At the beginning of 1879 Phadke left his job and formed a band of about 300 young men from the **Ramoshi** community around Pune, together with Kolis, Dhangars and others. His plan was to raise money by raiding government treasuries and the houses of rich moneylenders, buy weapons with it, and raise a large rebellion. In the first months of 1879 such raids took place in the Pune district and many villages around it.

The British government declared him a 'dacoit' and put a reward on his arrest. In reply Phadke issued a pamphlet announcing a reward on the Governor himself — this story is widely told.

## The End of the Struggle

Around **10 May 1879** his chief Ramoshi companion **Daulatrao Naik** was killed in an encounter with Major Daniell's detachment, which dealt a heavy blow to the movement. Phadke went south and, stopping toward Srisailam, tried to recruit Rohilla and Arab mercenaries. But after the betrayal of a companion, on **20 July 1879** (21 July in some sources) at **Devar Navadgi** in the **Gulbarga** district, where he was in the disguise of an ascetic, he was captured by **Major Henry Daniell** and officers of the Hyderabad police.

## The End in Aden

He was tried in Pune and sentenced to **transportation for life**, and sent to the jail at **Aden** on the Arabian peninsula. In February 1883 he escaped from jail but was soon caught again. After this he went on a fast and also suffered from tuberculosis. On **17 February 1883**, at about the age of 37, he died in Aden.

## Significance

Phadke's rebellion was small and was soon crushed. There was disagreement, then as now, about his methods — especially the plundering raids; the British and many contemporary Indians regarded him as a dacoit. Even so, he was among the first educated Indians after 1857 to challenge British rule with arms, and he tried to bring peasants and deprived communities along with him. This is why in Maharashtra he is called the **'Adya Krantikari'** (the first revolutionary).
