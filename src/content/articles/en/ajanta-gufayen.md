## A World of Art Hidden in a Valley

In Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (formerly Aurangabad) district of Maharashtra, the **Waghora river** makes a horseshoe-shaped bend. In the steep rock of this valley, about **30 caves** were cut for Buddhist monks (some of them unfinished). Some of these are **chaityagrihas** (places of worship that contain a stupa) and the rest are **viharas** (places where monks lived and studied).

## First Phase: The Satavahana Period

The oldest caves date from about the **2nd–1st century BCE**, when the Satavahanas held influence in the Deccan. Caves 9 and 10 (chaityagrihas) and 12, 13 and 15A (viharas) are considered to belong to this phase. The art of this time is plain, and the Buddha is shown not as an image but through symbols such as the stupa. Fragments of paintings of this period also survive in Cave 10.

## Second Phase: The Vakataka Period

After a gap of several centuries, large-scale work began here again in the fifth century CE. This phase is linked to the rule of the **Vakataka** emperor **Harishena** (c. 460–480 CE). According to the scholar Walter Spink, most of the later caves were made in just a few decades, though not all scholars agree on this short chronology.

- According to an inscription in **Cave 16**, it was built by Harishena's minister **Varahadeva**.
- **Cave 17** is also linked to a donation by a local ruler subordinate to Harishena.
- In this phase large statues of the Buddha were made, and the walls, ceilings and pillars of the caves were filled with coloured paintings.

After Harishena's death Vakataka power weakened, and many caves were left unfinished.

## Features of the Paintings

The Ajanta paintings are among the most important examples of ancient Indian painting.
- They mainly show **Jataka tales** (stories of the Buddha's previous births) and episodes from the Buddha's life.
- The **Padmapani Bodhisattva** (holding a lotus in his hand) painting in **Cave 1** is world famous.
- Kings and queens, merchants, dancers, animals, birds and palace life are shown in such fine detail that they give a glimpse of the clothing, jewellery and way of life of that time.
- Before the paintings were made, a layer of clay, chaff and lime was applied to the rock, and then the pictures were painted on it with colours made from minerals and plants.

## Rediscovery and Conservation

With time the place was hidden in the jungle. In 1819 the British officer **John Smith** reached here while hunting, after which Ajanta became known to the world. Today it is looked after by the **Archaeological Survey of India**. In 1983 Ajanta was included, along with Ellora, the Agra Fort and the Taj Mahal, among India's first **UNESCO World Heritage** sites.

## Significance

The art of Ajanta influenced not only India but also the Buddhist art of Sri Lanka, Central Asia and East Asia. These caves show how, with the cooperation of kings, ministers, merchants and monks, a tradition of art stayed alive in one place for centuries.
