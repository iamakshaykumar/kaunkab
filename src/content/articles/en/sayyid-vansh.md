## The vacuum after Timur

In 1398 **Timur**'s invasion looted and devastated Delhi, and the Tughluq Sultanate almost collapsed. Provinces such as Gujarat, Malwa and Jaunpur became separate independent kingdoms. In Delhi the weak Tughluq sultans and their chiefs kept fighting one another for power.

In this period **Khizr Khan** rose. Timur had made him governor of the Multan–Lahore region. He built up his strength in Punjab and on **28 May 1414** took Delhi and made Daulat Khan Lodi a prisoner. This is where the **Sayyid dynasty** began, the fourth dynasty of the Delhi Sultanate.

But this was not the sultanate that had stretched to the Deccan in the time of Alauddin Khilji or Muhammad bin Tughluq. Now the Delhi sultan's direct authority was limited to Delhi, parts of the Doab and Punjab. The surrounding chiefs and zamindars paid tribute only when an army reached their door, so the Sayyid rulers had to launch military campaigns almost every year.

## Why "Sayyid"?

The name came from the claim that Khizr Khan was a descendant (sayyid) of the Prophet Muhammad. This claim is found in the **Tarikh-i-Mubarakshahi** of the contemporary historian **Yahya bin Ahmad Sirhindi**. But modern historians consider it to rest on weak evidence; some link them to an Arab origin in Multan, others to a Punjabi Khokhar origin.

## Khizr Khan (1414–1421)

Khizr Khan did not take the title "Sultan", perhaps out of fear of a Timurid invasion. He was called **Rayat-i-Ala** ("the one with the high banner") and accepted the nominal suzerainty of Timur's successor **Shah Rukh**. In 1417, with Shah Rukh's permission, he began to join his own name with Shah Rukh's.

Most of his time was spent suppressing revolts and collecting tribute. His general Taj-ul-Mulk forced Har Singh, the raja of Katehar (Rohilkhand), to pay tribute and in 1416 campaigned towards Bayana and Gwalior. Khizr Khan died on **20 May 1421**.

## Mubarak Shah and after

His son **Mubarak Shah** (1421–1434) dropped the name of Timurid suzerainty, took the title "Shah" and recognised the Abbasid caliph. He also collected tribute from Hoshang Shah of Malwa. But in court conspiracies he was murdered in **1434**.

The reign of his nephew **Muhammad Shah** (from 1434 to about 1443–45) was spent amid revolts and intrigues; in this period Multan became independent under the Langah chiefs. The Afghan chief of Sirhind, **Bahlul Lodi**, kept growing in strength.

## Alam Shah and the end

The last Sayyid ruler, **Alam Shah**, had no interest in governing and left Delhi to live in **Badaun**. In April **1451** he gave up the throne of Delhi in favour of Bahlul Lodi, and with this the Sayyid dynasty ended and the **Lodi dynasty** began.

The Sayyid dynasty had no large empire; its rule remained confined to a few hundred kilometres around Delhi. Even so, it kept the throne of Delhi alive in the anarchy after Timur. Today the finest memorial of this period is the **octagonal tomb of Muhammad Shah** in Lodi Gardens, built in about **1444**, whose style is also seen later in Lodi tombs.
