## French India at the Time of Independence

In the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries France had founded several trading settlements on the coasts of India. After its defeat by the British, only five small territories remained with it: **Chandannagar (Chandernagore)** on the bank of the Hooghly river in Bengal, **Pondicherry** and **Karaikal** on the Tamil coast, **Mahe** on the Malabar coast, and **Yanam** on the Andhra coast. They were governed by the French governor who sat in Pondicherry.

When India became independent on 15 August 1947, the demand to merge with India grew stronger in these settlements too. In October 1947 the remaining French 'loges' (small trading establishments) at places such as Surat, Machilipatnam and Calicut were handed over to India. In 1948 India and France agreed that the future of the remaining settlements would be decided by the opinion of their people.

## Chandannagar: From Referendum to Bengal

A referendum was held in Chandannagar on **19 June 1949**, in which about **98 percent** of voters voted in favour of merging with India. On **2 May 1950** France handed over its administration to India de facto. On **2 February 1951** the treaty of transfer was signed, and on **9 June 1952** Chandannagar became legally part of India. On **2 October 1954** it was merged into the state of West Bengal.

## Pondicherry: The Movement and the Kizhoor Vote

In the four southern settlements the path was not so straight. France kept postponing a referendum here, while a movement for merger was going on at the local level. The local leader **Edouard Goubert**, once considered pro-French, also came out in favour of merger in 1954, which gave the movement great strength. In 1954 local pro-merger activists also took action against the French administration in Mahe and Yanam.

Finally the two governments decided that instead of a large referendum the elected representatives of these settlements would decide. On **18 October 1954**, in the village of **Kizhoor** near Pondicherry, members of the representative assembly and municipal councils voted by secret ballot: **170 out of 178** voted in favour of merger with India. On **21 October 1954** an India-France agreement was reached, and on **1 November 1954** the four settlements were handed over to India de facto. India appointed **Kewal Singh** as the first High Commissioner here.

## The Treaty and the Legal Merger (1956–1962)

On **28 May 1956**, in New Delhi, **Jawaharlal Nehru** for India and the ambassador **Stanislas Ostroróg** for France signed the treaty of transfer. It had provisions such as the option for residents to choose their citizenship, protection of French institutions, and continuation of the official status of the French language. Ratification of the treaty in France took several years; the de jure transfer was completed on **16 August 1962** with the exchange of instruments of ratification. After this, through a constitutional amendment, they were included as union territories of India, and from **1 July 1963** Pondicherry formally became a Union Territory. In 2006 its name became **Puducherry**.

## Significance

The merger of the French settlements took place on the basis of negotiation and the opinion of the people, without war; this was unlike Portuguese Goa, where military action had to be taken in 1961. Because of this peaceful path, Puducherry has preserved its French heritage, buildings, language, institutions and some legal traditions, to a considerable extent. Today Puducherry remembers these two dates, 1 November as 'Merger Day' and 16 August as 'De Jure Day'.
