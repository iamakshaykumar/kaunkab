## From Mulshankar to Dayananda

Swami Dayananda Saraswati was born on **12 February 1824** at **Tankara** in Kathiawar (Gujarat). His childhood name was **Mulshankar**. According to a well-known episode, while keeping the Shivaratri fast as a teenager, he saw a mouse eating the offering placed on a Shivalinga. The question arose in his mind: how could an image that cannot protect itself protect the world? His doubts about idol worship began there. This episode comes from his biographical tradition.

In his youth he left home, took sannyasa and received the name 'Dayananda Saraswati', and for years wandered in search of truth. Eventually he became a disciple of the blind scholar **Swami Virjanand** at Mathura, who inspired him to return to the Vedas and ancient Sanskrit grammar.

## Debates and Preaching

After completing his education with his teacher, Dayananda travelled across the country and held debates (shastrarth) with pandits. The most famous debate took place at Varanasi on **22 October 1869**, on the question: do the Vedas support idol worship? Both sides claimed victory, but this debate made Dayananda famous across northern India.

## Founding of the Arya Samaj

On **10 April 1875** Swami Dayananda founded the **Arya Samaj** in **Bombay**. In the same year the first edition of his major work **'Satyarth Prakash'** was also published; its revised edition, which he prepared at Udaipur in 1882–83, was printed in 1884. The Samaj's **ten principles** were finalised at Lahore in 1877.

The main ideas of the Arya Samaj were:

- The **Vedas** alone are the supreme authority for true knowledge — hence the call 'Back to the Vedas';
- Worship of one formless God, and opposition to idol worship, the doctrine of incarnations and ritualism;
- Caste is determined by merit and deeds, not by birth — opposition to untouchability;
- Opposition to child marriage, and support for women's education and widow remarriage;
- Education through Hindi and Sanskrit.

## Expansion in Punjab

The Arya Samaj found its greatest success in **Punjab**. On **30 October 1883**, the day of Diwali, Swami Dayananda died at Ajmer. According to popular accounts he had been poisoned; the details of this story differ from source to source.

In his memory the **Dayananda Anglo-Vedic (D.A.V.)** schools and colleges were started in Lahore, with which people such as Lala Hans Raj and Lala Lajpat Rai were associated. Later a split arose in the Arya Samaj — one faction continued with the D.A.V. system alongside modern English education, while the faction led by **Munshi Ram (Swami Shraddhanand)** stressed the ancient gurukul method and founded the **Gurukul Kangri** near Haridwar.

## Significance and Controversy

The Arya Samaj promoted education, women's education and caste reform in northern India, and awakened self-confidence among Hindus. Many people connected with it, such as Lala Lajpat Rai, became major leaders of the national movement. On the other hand, its sharp criticism of other religions and the later **shuddhi movement** (bringing back to Hinduism those who had converted) also increased tension with other communities in its time — historians discuss both these sides of the Arya Samaj.
