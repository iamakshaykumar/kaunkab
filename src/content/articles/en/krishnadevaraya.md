## Rise to the Throne

**Krishnadevaraya** was the third ruler of the **Tuluva dynasty**, the third dynasty of Vijayanagara. His father **Tuluva Narasa Nayaka** was a powerful commander. After the death of his half-brother Vira Narasimha Raya, he became king in **1509 CE**. His most trusted adviser was the prime minister **Timmarusu** (Appaji), and many stories of his wisdom are found in folk tales.

At that time, the Deccan sultanates to the north of Vijayanagara (Bijapur, Golconda and others, which were forming out of the breakup of the Bahmani kingdom) and the **Gajapatis** of Odisha to the east were major challenges.

## Military Campaigns

- **Conflict with the Deccan (1509–1512):** In the early years the Vijayanagara army defeated the Deccan forces and strengthened its hold on the **Raichur Doab**, the land between the Krishna and the Tungabhadra.
- **Gajapati campaign (from 1512):** The fort of **Udayagiri** was captured after a long siege, and then **Kondavidu** also fell. Finally the Gajapati king **Prataparudra** made a treaty and married his daughter to Krishnadevaraya; the Krishna river was accepted as the boundary between the two kingdoms.
- **Raichur (1520):** On **19 May 1520**, the fort of Raichur was won from **Ismail Adil Shah** of Bijapur. In this battle, Portuguese gunmen also helped Vijayanagara. After this his armies reached Gulbarga and Bijapur.

Not all campaigns were successful — a defeat by the Golconda army around 1513 is also mentioned.

## The Portuguese and Foreign Accounts

Krishnadevaraya kept friendly relations with the **Portuguese**, who had taken Goa in 1510. A major reason was the trade in **Arabian horses**, which came through Portuguese ports and on which the Vijayanagara cavalry depended.

The Portuguese traveller **Domingo Paes**, who visited Hampi during his rule, gave a detailed description of the capital's markets, festivals and the king himself. The account of another Portuguese, **Fernao Nuniz**, also gives information about this period. Today these accounts are major sources for the history of Vijayanagara.

## Amuktamalyada and the Ashtadiggajas

Krishnadevaraya was himself a poet. His Telugu work **'Amuktamalyada'** ('she who gave away the garland she had worn') tells the story of the marriage of the Alvar saint **Andal** and Lord Ranganatha (Vishnu). It also contains thoughts on politics and administration, so it is also regarded as a text on the duties of a king. Works in Sanskrit such as 'Jambavati Kalyanam' are also linked to his name.

His court had eight famous Telugu poets, called the **'Ashtadiggajas'** — among them **Allasani Peddana** (the 'Grandfather of Andhra Poetry', author of 'Manucharitramu') and **Nandi Timmana** were the most prominent. Tradition also counts the popular **Tenali Ramakrishna** in this group, although many scholars consider him to belong to a later period. Krishnadevaraya received titles such as **'Andhra Bhoja'** and **'Kannada Rajya Rama Ramana'**; he also patronised Kannada, Tamil and Sanskrit poets.

## Temples and the Capital

At Hampi he had the Virupaksha temple expanded, and work on the Vitthala temple complex moved forward. The **Krishna temple**, built after the Gajapati campaign, is linked to his victory. He made valuable gifts several times to the Venkateswara temple at **Tirumala**; there in 1518 bronze statues of him and his two queens — **Chinnadevi** and **Tirumaladevi** — were installed.

## Final Years and Assessment

In 1524 he made his young son Tirumala Raya the crown prince, but the boy died soon — it is said that he was poisoned. After this Timmarusu came under suspicion, which some accounts regard as a sad episode of the king's last years. Krishnadevaraya died on **17 October 1529**, and his half-brother **Achyuta Deva Raya** succeeded him.

Krishnadevaraya is regarded as the most successful ruler of Vijayanagara — military power, a prosperous capital and literary patronage all reached their peak in his time. But after him, succession disputes and Deccan politics weakened the empire, which ended in the Battle of Talikota in 1565.
