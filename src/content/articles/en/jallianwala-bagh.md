## Background

In 1919 the British government enforced the **Rowlatt Act**, under which anyone could be kept in jail without trial. It was opposed across the country. In Amritsar the popular leaders **Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew** and **Dr. Satyapal** were arrested, after which tension spread in the city.

## 13 April 1919

That day was the festival of **Baisakhi**. Thousands of people had gathered at Jallianwala Bagh — a field enclosed on all sides by high walls, with very narrow exits. Some had come for a protest meeting, while many had come from nearby villages to celebrate the festival.

**Brigadier General Reginald Dyer** arrived there with his soldiers, blocked the main exit and, **without any warning**, ordered firing on the unarmed crowd. The firing went on for about ten minutes, until the ammunition was nearly exhausted. Many people jumped into the well in the Bagh to save themselves.

According to official figures 379 people were killed, but by the estimates of Indian leaders the number of dead was about a thousand or more. Thousands were wounded.

## The Country's Reaction

- **Rabindranath Tagore** returned the **knighthood** given by the British in protest.
- The British government set up the **Hunter Committee** to investigate. Dyer was removed from his post, but many people in Britain also supported him.
- After this event Mahatma Gandhi launched the **Non-Cooperation Movement** (1920) against the British.

## Significance

Jallianwala Bagh brought the cruel face of British rule before everyone. Even those who had until then trusted British justice lost their faith. Today the place is a **national memorial**. In the next article of this series, read about 15 August 1947.
