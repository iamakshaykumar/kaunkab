## Background: Purna Swaraj and the Choice of Salt

At the historic Lahore session in December 1929, the Congress passed the resolution of 'Purna Swaraj' (complete independence), and 26 January 1930 was celebrated as Independence Day. Now it was time to begin a great mass movement.

Mahatma Gandhi chose **salt** as the medium for the movement. Salt was a free gift of nature, but under the British salt law (the Salt Monopoly) no Indian had the right to make or sell salt, and it carried a heavy tax. The then Viceroy, Lord Irwin, did not at first take Gandhi's warning about the salt movement seriously.

## A Historic 24-Day March

On the morning of **12 March 1930**, the 61-year-old Mahatma Gandhi set out from the Sabarmati Ashram in Ahmedabad with 78 chosen ashram residents:

- He declared that he would not return to the ashram until the country was free or the salt law was abolished.
- They covered about 10 to 12 miles every day. In the villages along the way, thousands of people thronged to see Gandhi and hear his speeches. Hundreds of government employees resigned.
- On the morning of **6 April 1930**, after bathing on the shore of the Arabian Sea at Dandi, Gandhi picked up a handful of natural salt and symbolically broke the British salt law. He said:
  > *"With this, I am shaking the foundations of the British Empire."*

## The Nationwide Civil Disobedience Movement

This spark from Dandi set the whole country alight with the movement:
- In Tamil Nadu, **C. Rajagopalachari** led a salt march from Tiruchirappalli to Vedaranyam.
- In the North-West Frontier Province, the 'Khudai Khidmatgar' (Red Shirt Movement) under **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (the Frontier Gandhi)** offered unprecedented peaceful resistance.
- In Peshawar, when soldiers of the Garhwal Rifles were ordered to fire on unarmed protesters, the soldiers led by Chandra Singh Garhwali flatly refused to open fire.
- Women held widespread pickets at liquor shops and foreign-cloth shops.

## Historical Significance

The Dandi March, for the first time, placed Indian women in large numbers on the front line of the freedom struggle. It led to the Gandhi-Irwin Pact, and Indians gained the right to make salt for their own domestic use on the seashore. It proved that the moral force of non-violence could compel even the largest empire in the world to bend.
