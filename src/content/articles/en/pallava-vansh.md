## Background: The Rise of the Pallavas

After the fall of the Satavahana empire, the **Pallava dynasty** rose in South India. The Pallavas made northern Tamil Nadu and southern Andhra Pradesh (Tondaimandalam) their field of action and made the historic city of **Kanchipuram** their capital, which later became a famous centre of education, philosophy and Kanchi silk.

At the end of the sixth century, Emperor **Simhavishnu** defeated the Cholas and Pandyas and established the real political standing of the Pallava empire.

## The Golden Age of Mahendravarman I and Narasimhavarman I

The two most brilliant rulers of Pallava history were a father and son:

1. **Mahendravarman I (600 – 630 CE):** He was a great builder, musician and poet. Instead of wood, brick and mortar, he began for the first time a new style of cutting cave temples out of solid rock. He took titles such as 'Vichitrachitta' and 'Mattavilasa'.
2. **Narasimhavarman I 'Mahamalla' (630 – 668 CE):** He was a unique malla (wrestler/warrior). When the Chalukya king of Badami, Pulakeshin II, attacked the Pallava kingdom, Narasimhavarman defeated him, seized his capital Vatapi (Badami) and took the title **'Vatapikonda'**.

## The Unique Art of Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram)

Narasimhavarman 'Mahamalla' founded on the shore of the Bay of Bengal a port city named after himself, **Mamallapuram (Mahabalipuram)**, and made it an open laboratory of sculpture:

- **Monolithic Raths:** Here, standing granite rocks were carved from top to bottom to make five raths — the Dharmaraja Ratha, Bhima Ratha, Arjuna Ratha, Nakula-Sahadeva Ratha and Draupadi Ratha. Each ratha is a marvel of Indian architecture in itself.
- **Gangavataran (Descent of the Ganges):** On two huge rocks, 96 feet wide and 43 feet high, is carved the scene of Bhagiratha's penance and the Ganga descending from heaven to earth. Gods, gandharvas, yakshas, sages and a lifelike herd of elephants are depicted in it.
- **Shore Temple:** Built in the eighth century by Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha), this temple stands right at the edge of the sea waves and is an early peak of structural stone temples.

## The Cultural Legacy of the Pallavas

The Pallava kings gave wide patronage to both Sanskrit and Tamil. The great poet **Dandin** (author of the Dashakumaracharita) was connected with the Pallava court. The Bhakti movement of the Shaiva Nayanars and the Vaishnava Alvar saints gathered momentum in the Pallava period itself.

Pallava architecture itself later laid the foundation of the huge Thanjavur temple of the Cholas and of the temples of Cambodia (Angkor Wat) and Indonesia.
