## The Morning of 13 December

On **13 December 2001** the winter session of Parliament was under way. That day the proceedings of both Houses had been adjourned a little earlier because of uproar by the Opposition, but many MPs, ministers and staff were present in the complex. Several senior leaders, including Home Minister **Lal Krishna Advani**, are said to have been inside the building.

Before noon a white Ambassador car drove into the Parliament complex. It carried **fake Home Ministry and Parliament stickers**. Inside were five armed terrorists with AK-47 rifles, grenades and explosives. As they drove in, their car collided with a vehicle in the convoy of **Vice-President Krishan Kant**. The attackers then got out and began firing.

## The Courage of the Security Personnel

**CRPF** constable **Kamlesh Kumari** was the first to see the attackers and shouted a warning. She was shot on the spot and fell, but her warning allowed the doors of Parliament to be shut in time. She was posthumously honoured with the **Ashoka Chakra**.

In an encounter lasting about half an hour, personnel of the **Delhi Police**, the Parliament Security Service and the CRPF killed all five terrorists inside the complex. One attacker was wearing an explosive-laden jacket, which blew up before he could reach the building. **Nine people**, including Delhi Police personnel, two Parliament Security Service staff and a gardener, were killed in the attack and several were injured. All the MPs and ministers were safe.

## Investigation and Trial

The Government of India held the Pakistan-based groups **Jaish-e-Mohammed** and **Lashkar-e-Taiba** responsible for the attack. Lashkar denied any role, and Pakistan also denied any official involvement.

The Delhi Police arrested four people in the case: **Mohammad Afzal Guru**, his relative **Shaukat Hussain Guru**, Shaukat's wife **Afsan Guru** (Navjot Sandhu), and Delhi University Arabic lecturer **S. A. R. Geelani**. In December 2002 a special court sentenced Afzal, Shaukat and Geelani to death. On appeal the **Delhi High Court** acquitted Geelani and Afsan.

On **4 August 2005** the **Supreme Court** upheld Afzal Guru's death sentence, reduced Shaukat's sentence to ten years, and upheld the acquittal of Geelani and Afsan. The President rejected Afzal Guru's mercy petition, and on **9 February 2013** he was hanged in Delhi's Tihar Jail. The trial was also debated: some human rights organisations and critics questioned the fairness of the trial, while the government and the prosecution maintained that the judicial process had been carried out fully in accordance with the law.

## Operation Parakram

India regarded the attack on Parliament as a direct attack on its democracy. Prime Minister **Atal Bihari Vajpayee's** government demanded that Pakistan act against these groups, recalled its High Commissioner, and halted rail, bus and air links between the two countries.

From **20 December 2001** India began a large-scale deployment of troops along the western border and the **Line of Control** under **Operation Parakram**. By January 2002 about **5 lakh** Indian soldiers and three armoured divisions were on the border; Pakistan also deployed about 3 lakh soldiers. The whole world grew anxious at the prospect of war between two nuclear-armed countries.

On **12 January 2002** Pakistan's President **Pervez Musharraf** said in a speech that Pakistan would not allow its soil to be used for terrorism, and banned some groups. This eased tension somewhat. But on **14 May 2002** a terrorist attack on the **Kaluchak** army camp near Jammu killed more than 30 people, including soldiers' family members, and tension peaked again. After diplomatic efforts by several countries, including the United States and Britain, the situation gradually calmed in June 2002. In **October 2002** India announced the withdrawal of troops from the border, and Pakistan did the same.

## Outcome and Significance

Operation Parakram ended without a war, but its cost was heavy. According to official figures, about **798** Indian soldiers died during this period from landmines, accidents and other causes, and several billion dollars were spent on it. Military experts also acknowledged that moving such a large army to the border took several weeks, which meant the chance for swift action was lost. After this experience, discussion began in the Indian Army on a new approach of rapid, limited action, commonly called the **"Cold Start"** doctrine.

The attack on Parliament is counted among the most serious acts of terrorism in India's history. Every year on **13 December**, tribute is paid in Parliament to the security personnel who fell. After this incident the security arrangements of Parliament were made much tighter.
