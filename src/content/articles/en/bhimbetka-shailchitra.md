## Rock Shelters in the Lap of the Vindhyas

Bhimbetka lies in the **Raisen district** of Madhya Pradesh, about 45 kilometres south-east of Bhopal. It is at the southern edge of the **Vindhya range**, inside the densely forested **Ratapani Wildlife Sanctuary**. Over an area of about 10 kilometres on seven hills are spread **more than 750 rock shelters** (natural shelters or cave-like overhangs in the rock). Paintings have been found at about 400–500 of them.

These huge sandstone rocks were eroded by wind and water in such a way that they give protection from sun and rain. That is why early humans lived here for a very long time.

## The Story of the Discovery

The tribal people of the surrounding area already knew these rocks and paintings. **W. Kincaid**, an official of the British administration, mentioned the place in an article in 1888, but its real importance went unnoticed.

In 1957 the archaeologist **Vishnu Shridhar Wakankar** came here and recognised that these rock shelters were prehistoric. He compared them with the famous cave-painting sites of Spain and France. After this, surveys went on for many years, and in the 1970s the full extent of the site came to light. Wakankar is counted among the pioneering scholars of rock-art study in India.

## How Old Is It?

The special thing about Bhimbetka is that the sequence of human life here is very long:
- According to archaeology, in some rock shelters humans lived **more than 100,000 years ago** (in the Acheulian phase of the Palaeolithic).
- The oldest paintings here are generally considered to date to around **10,000 BCE**, that is, to the **Mesolithic period**.
- Wakankar considered some green line drawings to belong to the Upper Palaeolithic, but scholars do not agree on such early dates.
- The practice of painting continued through the historical period and into the medieval period.

## The World of the Paintings

Wakankar divided the paintings here into several periods:
- **Mesolithic** — small, lively human figures; scenes of hunting, group dances and tribal fights.
- **Chalcolithic** — signs of contact with the farming communities of the Malwa plains.
- **Early historical period** — horse riders, elephant riders, archers and religious symbols; red, white and yellow colours.
- **Medieval period** — geometric and simple figures, relatively less fine in detail.

The paintings show animals such as bison, elephants, deer, bears, rhinoceroses, fish and dogs. One rock shelter has so many animals painted that it is called the "Zoo Rock". The colours were made from minerals — red from haematite (ochre), black from manganese oxide and charcoal. Because many paintings were made in the inner parts of the rocks, they survived thousands of years later.

The largest rock shelter here is called the **"Auditorium Cave"**, surrounded by high quartzite rocks. A large upright rock at its entrance is called the "Chief's Rock", but its connection with any ritual has not been proven.

## World Heritage

Since 1990 the site has been under the protection of the **Archaeological Survey of India** (ASI). In **2003** UNESCO included the "Rock Shelters of Bhimbetka" in the World Heritage List. UNESCO considered it unique evidence that even in the present-day traditions of the surrounding villages, glimpses of these paintings can be seen.

## Significance

Bhimbetka gives us a chance to peep into the lives of people thousands of years before written history — what they ate, how they hunted, how they danced and sang. It is one of the oldest and largest repositories of human art in India, and shows that the urge to create art existed in humans even before farming and cities.
