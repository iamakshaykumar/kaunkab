## Anger Against Corruption

Around 2010–11 several big cases of corruption were in the headlines in India — the **2G spectrum allocation**, irregularities in the preparations for the **2010 Commonwealth Games**, and Mumbai's **Adarsh Housing Society** case. Among ordinary people the feeling was growing that there was no strong institution for a fair investigation of corruption by those in high positions.

The idea of such an institution was old. In **1963** MP **Laxmi Mall Singhvi** coined the word 'Lokpal' — 'lok' meaning the people and 'pal' meaning protector. The Administrative Reforms Commission headed by **Morarji Desai** recommended in 1966 the creation of a Lokpal and Lokayuktas. In **1968** a Lokpal Bill came to Parliament for the first time, but over the next four decades, although introduced many times, it could not be passed.

## The Fast at Jantar Mantar

Regarding the government's draft Lokpal Bill as weak, some members of civil society — **Arvind Kejriwal**, **Prashant Bhushan**, **Justice N. Santosh Hegde** and **Kiran Bedi**, among others — prepared an alternative draft, called the **'Jan Lokpal Bill'**. **Anna Hazare**, a Gandhian social activist from Maharashtra known for the development of his village **Ralegan Siddhi** and for the right to information movement, was invited to lead this campaign.

On **5 April 2011** Anna Hazare began a fast unto death at **Jantar Mantar** in Delhi. Within a few days people in many cities of the country came out onto the streets with candles. Social media, TV channels and caps reading 'I am Anna' carried the movement into every home. On **9 April** the government accepted the demand to form a **joint committee** to draft the bill, with five ministers and five representatives of civil society. **Pranab Mukherjee** became its chairman and **Shanti Bhushan** its co-chairman. After this Anna broke his fast.

## Arrest and Ramlila Maidan

There was no agreement in the joint committee on several issues — such as bringing the Prime Minister, the higher judiciary and the lower bureaucracy under the Lokpal. Anna announced that if only the government bill was brought in Parliament, he would fast again from **16 August 2011**.

On the morning of 16 August, even before the fast began, Delhi Police detained Anna Hazare and hundreds of his supporters. When he refused to sign a personal bond, Anna was sent to **Tihar Jail**. This action was sharply criticised across the country and protests began everywhere. As the pressure grew, the government had to release him. After this Anna continued his fast at Delhi's **Ramlila Maidan**, where thousands of people kept arriving every day.

On **27 August 2011** a special discussion on the subject was held in both Houses of Parliament. Parliament expressed agreement in principle on Anna's three main demands — a **Citizen's Charter**, bringing the lower bureaucracy under its scope, and setting up **Lokayuktas** in the states. The next day, on **28 August**, Anna ended his fast after **12 days** (about 288 hours).

## A Long Debate and the Lokpal Act

In December 2011 the government introduced a new **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Bill** in the Lok Sabha, which was passed there on **27 December 2011**. But in the Rajya Sabha, even after a debate that went on late into the night, no vote could take place, and the bill was sent to a Select Committee.

Meanwhile, differences also emerged within the movement. Anna Hazare wanted to keep the movement away from politics, while **Arvind Kejriwal** and his colleagues believed that entering electoral politics was necessary to change the system. On **26 November 2012** Kejriwal formed the **Aam Aadmi Party**.

In December 2013 Anna began another fast in his village of Ralegan Siddhi. With amendments, the bill was passed by the Rajya Sabha on **17 December 2013** and by the Lok Sabha on **18 December**. That same day Anna broke his nine-day fast. On **1 January 2014**, with the assent of President **Pranab Mukherjee**, it became the **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013**.

## Key Features of the Act and Legacy

Under this Act there is to be a **Lokpal** at the Centre, with a chairperson and up to eight members, half of whom are to have a judicial background. The Prime Minister, Union ministers, MPs and officers of the central government come under its scope; in the case of the Prime Minister, certain special conditions and safeguards were laid down. Every state was directed to set up a **Lokayukta** within one year.

Even after the Act was passed, it took more than five years to appoint the first Lokpal. In **March 2019** retired Supreme Court judge **Pinaki Chandra Ghose** became India's first Lokpal. Critics have said that the Lokpal has not been as effective as expected, while supporters see it as an institutional step towards accountability.

The Anna movement is counted among the biggest citizens' movements of independent India. It showed how non-violent sit-ins, fasting and new communication technology together can unite crores of people on one issue. It also had a deep effect on the country's politics — corruption became a major issue in the 2014 general election, and the Aam Aadmi Party, which emerged from this movement, later formed the government in Delhi.
