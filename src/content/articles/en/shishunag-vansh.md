## Magadha after the Haryankas

In the 6th–5th centuries BCE, Bimbisara and Ajatashatru of the **Haryanka** dynasty had made Magadha the most powerful of the sixteen mahajanapadas. But the later Haryanka kings proved weak. According to the **Mahavamsa**, the Buddhist chronicle of Sri Lanka, the people removed the last Haryanka king, **Nagadasaka**, from the throne and made his **amatya** (minister) **Shishunaga** king. This is believed to have happened in about **413 BCE**.

In this way the second great dynasty of Magadha began, called the **Shishunaga** or **Shaishunaga** dynasty. It ruled until about **345 BCE**.

## The tangle of sources

The history of this dynasty has been put together from different traditions, and these do not agree with one another. The **Puranas** place Shishunaga *before* Bimbisara and Ajatashatru and regard those two also as part of the Shishunaga dynasty. The **Buddhist** texts (Mahavamsa, Dipavamsa) place Shishunaga *after* the Haryankas. Most modern historians consider the Buddhist order more reliable. The **Jain** tradition does not even name this dynasty. Some historians suggest that this tangle of succession may point to several claimants to the throne at the same time.

## Shishunaga and the conquest of Avanti

Shishunaga's greatest achievement was the conquest of **Avanti** (Ujjain). The **Pradyota** dynasty of Avanti had long been a rival of Magadha. Shishunaga ended this dynasty and merged Avanti into the kingdom of Magadha. With this, Magadha's border reached Malwa and no equal rival was left in the Ganga valley.

Shishunaga's capital remained **Rajagriha** (Rajgir), but **Vaishali** also gained the importance of a second capital.

## Kalashoka and the Second Buddhist Council

After Shishunaga his son came to the throne, called **Kalashoka** in Buddhist texts and **Kakavarna** in the Puranas; the two are generally regarded as the same person. According to tradition, in his time the capital was permanently moved to **Pataliputra**, which later became the capital of the Nanda and Maurya empires.

Under Kalashoka's patronage, about **100 years after** the Buddha's nirvana, the **Second Buddhist Council** was held at the **Valukarama** monastery in Vaishali. Its date depends on the date of the Buddha's nirvana; by the usual reckoning it is placed in about **383 BCE**. The dispute concerned the "ten points" of the **Vajjiputtaka** monks of Vaishali, the most serious of which was **accepting gold and silver (money)** from lay followers. The monk **Yasa Kakandakaputta** raised an objection to this. According to tradition, an assembly of **700** monks, in which the senior monk **Revata** played a leading part, rejected these ten points.

According to Buddhist tradition, the first split in the Sangha arose from this dispute: the **Sthaviravada** and **Mahasanghika** branches separated. Some modern scholars think this division actually happened later.

## The end of the dynasty

About the end of Kalashoka, Banabhatta's *Harshacharita* (7th century CE) says that King Kakavarna was murdered by a dagger thrust into his throat near his city. Buddhist tradition says that after him his **ten sons** ruled together, while the Puranas name **Nandivardhana** and the last king **Mahanandin** among the later kings. According to the Puranas, Mahanandin was murdered by his own son **Mahapadma** (born of a Shudra wife), who founded the **Nanda dynasty** in about **345 BCE**.

## Importance

The Shishunaga dynasty's rule was short and its sources are tangled, yet its contribution is large. With the conquest of Avanti, Magadha left behind almost all the major rival powers of North India, and Pataliputra became established as the capital. On this same foundation the Nandas and then the Mauryas built India's first vast empire. In the history of Buddhism, too, the council at Vaishali is regarded as an important turning point.
