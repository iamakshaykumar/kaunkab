## Outline of His Life

According to tradition **Ramanuja** (Ramanujacharya) was born in **1017 CE** at **Sriperumbudur** in Tamil Nadu, which was then part of the Chola kingdom. Tradition gives his death as **1137 CE**, that is, at the age of 120. Many modern scholars regard these dates as exaggeration and place his time at about **1077–1157 CE**. Most information about his life comes from biographies (guru-parampara texts) written several centuries later, so history and devotion are mixed in it.

In youth he became a disciple of a Vedanta scholar named **Yadavaprakasha** at **Kanchipuram**, but teacher and pupil came to differ over the interpretation of the Upanishads. Later **Periya Nambi** (Mahapurna) initiated him into the Sri Vaishnava tradition.

## Yamunacharya and Srirangam

**Yamunacharya** (Alavandar) of Srirangam was a great scholar of the Sri Vaishnava tradition. The biographies tell a story that at the time of Yamunacharya's death three fingers of his hand were curled, a sign of his three unfulfilled wishes; when Ramanuja resolved to fulfil them, the fingers opened. This is a story of devotion, not historical evidence.

After taking sannyasa Ramanuja became associated with the Ranganatha temple at **Srirangam** and organised its religious affairs and system of worship. Srirangam became the main centre of his activities.

## Vishishtadvaita: The Main Ideas

Ramanuja's philosophy is called **Vishishtadvaita** ("non-dualism with attributes"). Its main ideas are:
- **Brahman** is one, and is not without qualities but is endowed with infinite auspicious qualities. Ramanuja takes Brahman to be **Vishnu (Narayana)**.
- Both the **soul** (jivas) and the **world** (the material universe) are real, not maya or illusion. They are like the "body" of God — wholly dependent on God, yet having an identity distinct from him.
- Even in liberation the soul does not merge into God, but finds bliss in his company.
- The path to liberation is **bhakti** and complete **surrender (prapatti)** to God.

This view differs from Shankaracharya's **Advaita**, in which the world is regarded as maya and Brahman as without qualities. Ramanuja gave a reasoned critique of Advaita, and this debate enriched Indian philosophy.

## Major Works

Nine Sanskrit works are attributed to Ramanuja. The most important are:
- **Sribhashya** — a detailed commentary on the Brahma Sutras.
- **Vedarthasangraha** — an independent work on the meaning of the Upanishads.
- **Gitabhashya** — a commentary on the Bhagavad Gita.
- **Gadyatraya** — three prose works connected with surrender.

Some scholars doubt that the shorter works were written by him.

## Stay in the Hoysala Kingdom

According to the biographies, because of opposition from a **Chola king** Ramanuja had to leave Srirangam and went to the **Hoysala** kingdom in Karnataka. Historians do not agree on the identity of this king, and some believe that the opposition was personal, not wide religious persecution. According to tradition he spent about 14 years in the Hoysala region and played a role in temple-building at **Melukote**. The story is that the Hoysala king **Bittideva** became a Vaishnava under his influence and was called **Vishnuvardhana**; the miraculous parts of this story come from the biographical tradition. Later he returned to Srirangam, and died there.

## Legacy

By joining temple worship, the Tamil hymns of the Alvar saints and Sanskrit Vedanta, Ramanuja gave the **Sri Vaishnava** tradition a firm foundation. Tradition also links him with social inclusion — as in the story that he proclaimed from the top of a temple, for all to hear, the sacred mantra given by his guru, so that everyone could benefit from it. His ideas are believed to have influenced many later Vaishnava teachers and the bhakti movement of north India. In his memory the **"Statue of Equality"** was installed near Hyderabad in 2022.
