## From Mecca to Calcutta

**Abul Kalam Ghulam Muhiyuddin** was born on **11 November 1888** at **Mecca**, where his father Maulana Khairuddin, a scholar, was settled. In childhood the family returned to **Calcutta**. Azad studied Arabic, Persian, Urdu, Islamic theology, philosophy and mathematics not in any modern school or college but at home, and later taught himself English. In his teens he adopted the pen name **"Azad"** and began writing for newspapers and magazines.

## The Echo of "Al-Hilal"

In 1912 Azad brought out the Urdu weekly **"Al-Hilal"** from Calcutta. At that time the politics of the Muslim League and of a large section at Aligarh rested on loyalty to the British; "Al-Hilal", in contrast, called on Muslims to oppose British rule and to join Hindus in the national struggle. With its forceful language and its pictures it became very popular. In 1914 it was shut down under the Press Act. Then he started **"Al-Balagh"**, which closed in 1916, and Azad was interned at **Ranchi**, from where he was released on 1 January 1920.

## At the Head of the Congress

After his release Azad joined Gandhiji in the Khilafat and **Non-Cooperation Movement**. In 1923, at the special session in Delhi, at about 35 years of age, he became the **youngest president of the Congress**. He went to jail in the Salt Satyagraha.

At the **Ramgarh session** of 1940 he was elected president again and remained in that post until 1946 — that is, he led the Congress side of the negotiations in decisive phases such as the Cripps Mission, the Quit India Movement, the Simla Conference and the Cabinet Mission. After the Quit India Movement, from 1942 to 1945, he was imprisoned in **Ahmednagar Fort** along with Nehru, Patel and other leaders; during this imprisonment his wife Zulekha Begum died.

## A Champion of Unity

Azad was an open opponent of the two-nation theory and the demand for Pakistan. He believed that the future of Indian Muslims was secure only in a common, united India. Supporters of the Muslim League made him a target of harsh criticism. In June 1947, when the Congress accepted the plan of Partition, Azad abstained from voting. After Partition he appealed to the Muslims of Delhi not to leave the country.

## The First Education Minister

From **15 August 1947** until his death (**22 February 1958**), Azad was the first **education minister** of India — the longest tenure in this post to date. He stressed free primary education for all, adult education and the education of women. During his tenure the first **IIT** (Kharagpur) was set up in 1951, and the **University Grants Commission** (UGC) was inaugurated in 1953. In the field of culture he played a major part in founding the **Sangeet Natak Akademi** (1953), the **Sahitya Akademi** and the **Lalit Kala Akademi** (1954), and the **Indian Council for Cultural Relations** (ICCR).

## Writing and Legacy

Azad's commentary on the Quran, **"Tarjuman al-Quran"**, and the collection of letters written in captivity, **"Ghubar-i-Khatir"**, are important works of Urdu literature. His memoir **"India Wins Freedom"** (1959), dictated in English, gives his opinion on many decisions of the Partition period; some of his assessments of the role of Nehru and Patel have also been challenged by other historians and contemporaries. In 1992 he was posthumously awarded the **Bharat Ratna**, and since 2008 his birthday, **11 November**, has been observed as **National Education Day**.
