## Early life

Savitribai was born on **3 January 1831** in **Naigaon** in Satara district, in a Mali family. Like most girls of that time, she was married very young, at about the age of nine, in 1840, to **Jyotirao Phule**, who was then about thirteen.

At the time of her marriage Savitribai could not read or write. Jyotirao began to teach her at home. Later she also took formal training in teaching. In this way she became one of the few trained Indian women teachers of her time.

## 1848: the school at Bhide Wada

In **1848** the Phule couple started a school for girls in Pune in the house of **Tatyasaheb Bhide**, the **Bhide Wada**. Popular tradition gives its date as 1 January 1848, while some researchers mention only the year or August 1848. It is counted among the first girls' schools run by Indians; missionary institutions had been teaching girls in some places even before this.

Savitribai was the teacher at this school. Over the next few years the Phule couple opened several more schools in Pune and nearby, including schools for children of communities considered untouchable. In this work **Fatima Sheikh** was their associate, and she is often counted among the early Muslim women teachers of India. (Reliable information available about Fatima Sheikh's life is very limited.)

## Opposition and courage

This work of women's education and caste equality offended the orthodox society of the time. According to popular accounts, while Savitribai was on her way to school, people threw mud and cow dung at her and abused her; she carried an extra sari with her so that she could change after reaching school. Because of social pressure the Phule couple also had to leave the house of Jyotirao's father.

## Work for widows and the deprived

The struggle of the Phule couple was not limited to teaching:

- They opened the **Balhatya Pratibandhak Griha** (Home for the Prevention of Infanticide), where pregnant widows who had been victims of exploitation could safely give birth.
- A barbers' strike against the practice of shaving widows' heads was supported.
- They opened the water tank of their house to people considered untouchable.
- They adopted **Yashwant**, the son of a widow, who later became a doctor.

## The Satyashodhak Samaj

On **24 September 1873**, Jyotirao Phule founded the **Satyashodhak Samaj** (Society of Truth Seekers). Its aims were to oppose caste-based hierarchy, to conduct religious rites without the mediation of priests, and to secure education and equal rights for Shudras, Atishudras and women. Savitribai was a leading figure in the women's work of this society. After Jyotirao's death in 1890 she carried the society's work forward.

## Last years and legacy

In 1897 **bubonic plague** broke out in Pune. Savitribai and Yashwant ran a medical camp for patients. While carrying a sick child to the camp she herself became infected, and she died on **10 March 1897**.

Today Savitribai Phule is regarded in India as a pioneer of women's education and social justice. Her life shows that for her education was not merely reading and writing but a path to equality and self-respect.
