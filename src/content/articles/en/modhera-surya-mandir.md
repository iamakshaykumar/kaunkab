## The Chaulukya (Solanki) Dynasty and Bhima I

From the 10th to the 13th century Gujarat was ruled by the **Chaulukya** dynasty, popularly also called **Solanki**. Its capital was **Anahilavada Patan**. In the time of **Bhima I** (reigned about 1022-1064 CE) of this dynasty, Gujarat faced a great crisis — in 1025-26 **Mahmud of Ghazni** attacked Somnath. It is said that Bhima had to leave the capital at that time.

According to some accounts, about twenty thousand warriors also tried to stop Mahmud near Modhera. The historian A. K. Majumdar guessed that this temple may be linked with the memory of that struggle, but this is only a possibility.

## The Question of the Date

On the back wall of the sanctum is a small inscription of **Vikram Samvat 1083**, equal to **1026-27 CE**. This is the main basis for the temple's date. But the inscription is fixed upside down, so it is not certain whether it gives the date of the original construction or of a rebuilding after some damage. The main temple is believed to be of Bhima I's time, while the assembly hall and parts of the kund were perhaps added later, up to the time of his successor **Karna I**.

## A Structure in Three Parts

The Modhera complex consists of three parts built in a line along the east-west direction:
- **Gudhamandapa and sanctum (garbhagriha)** — the main temple, where once stood the image of the Sun god. Its spire no longer survives.
- **Sabhamandapa** — a separately standing, open hall whose carved pillars and archways are extremely beautiful. Religious gatherings and dances were held here.
- **Surya Kund (Rama Kund)** — the rectangular stepped reservoir in front, with many small shrines built among its steps.

The walls are carved with various forms of the Sun, other gods and goddesses, dancers and scenes of life. This style is called **Maru-Gurjara**, which is later seen also in the temples of Gujarat and Rajasthan, such as the Jain temples of Mount Abu.

## The Sun and Astronomy

The temple is built in such a way that on the days of the **equinox** (when day and night are equal, in March and September) the first rays of the rising Sun reached the place of the Sun image in the sanctum. Modhera is near the Tropic of Cancer (about 23.6° north), so around the summer solstice the Sun is almost directly overhead at noon. This is an example of the combination of sun worship and knowledge of astronomy.

## Modhera Today

Today no worship is held in the temple. It is a **monument of national importance** protected by the Archaeological Survey of India. In December 2022 it was added to UNESCO's **Tentative List** of World Heritage. Since 1992 the Gujarat Tourism Corporation has organised a three-day dance festival (the Uttarardh Mahotsav) here every January. In 2022 Modhera village was also developed as a solar-powered village — a lovely coincidence for the village of a Sun temple.

## The Tradition of Solanki Architecture

The period of Bhima I is considered golden for the architecture of Gujarat. At this time his minister Vimala had the Vimala Vasahi Jain temple built on Mount Abu, and at Patan the **Rani ki Vav** was built. This magnificent stepwell is linked with **Udayamati**, queen of Bhima I; scholars differ on its exact date of construction. The Sun Temple of Modhera is the finest example of this rich tradition.
