## Siachen: A Long River of Ice

The **Siachen Glacier** lies in the eastern Karakoram range, north of Ladakh. About **76 kilometres** long, it is the longest glacier in the Karakoram and one of the longest outside the polar regions. Its upper end, **Indira Col**, is at a height of about 5,750 metres. West of the glacier rises a high range called the **Saltoro Ridge**, whose passes — **Sia La, Bilafond La** and **Gyong La** — are regarded as the gateways to the whole area.

More than ten metres of snow falls here in winter, and the temperature can drop to 50 degrees below zero. At this height the air holds little oxygen, so simply staying alive here is a battle.

## The Root of the Dispute: An Unfinished Line

After the 1947–48 war, the ceasefire line between India and Pakistan was drawn in the **Karachi Agreement of July 1949**. The line was fixed in the north up to a point named **NJ9842**, and beyond it the agreement only said that it would run "north to the glaciers". At that time nobody imagined that there would ever be a fight over these empty icy lands. The Line of Control drawn after the **1972** Simla Agreement also stopped at NJ9842.

In the 1970s some American and other maps began to draw the line from NJ9842 straight north-east to the **Karakoram Pass**, which showed the whole of Siachen on Pakistan's side. Pakistan was also allowing foreign mountaineering expeditions into the area. India saw this as an attempt to stake a claim to the territory. India's position was that the line should run north from NJ9842 along the Saltoro Ridge. In 1978 an Indian Army mountaineering team led by Colonel **Narendra Kumar** also went into the area.

## Operation Meghdoot

In 1983–84 India received information that Pakistan was preparing to send its troops into the Siachen area. It later emerged that this Pakistani plan was named **Operation Ababeel** and was to begin around **1 May 1984**. India decided to move first.

**Operation Meghdoot** began on **13 April 1984**. Its name was taken from 'Meghaduta', the work of the Sanskrit poet **Kalidasa**. **Lt Gen P. N. Hoon**, commander of 15 Corps, and **Brigadier Vijay Channa**, commander of 26 Sector, were the main officers associated with the operation. Soldiers of the **Kumaon Regiment** and the **Ladakh Scouts** were taken to the high positions with the help of helicopters and transport aircraft of the **Indian Air Force**.

It was the first major joint operation of the Indian Army and Air Force carried out at such a height. Within a few days Indian soldiers had set up positions on **Sia La** and **Bilafond La**, and later took control of the main peaks of the Saltoro Ridge, including **Gyong La**. By the time Pakistani units arrived, the high ground was in India's hands. Through this operation India gained control of about **2,500 square kilometres** of territory.

## The World's Highest Battlefield

After this Pakistan tried several times to take these positions. The best-known incident came in **1987**, when Pakistan had set up the 'Quaid Post' on a peak about 6,700 metres high. Under **Operation Rajiv**, **Naib Subedar Bana Singh** and his comrades climbed a sheer wall of ice and captured the post. Bana Singh received the **Param Vir Chakra**, and the post is today called **'Bana Post'**.

At Siachen the real enemy has often been the weather. According to government figures, many times more soldiers have died here from avalanches, falls into crevasses, altitude sickness and cold than from enemy fire. In **2012**, 140 people were killed in an avalanche at Gayari on the Pakistani side, and in **2016** the whole country followed the story of **Lance Naik Hanamanthappa**, who stayed buried in snow for six days after an avalanche struck an Indian post. A ceasefire has been in force between the two countries since **November 2003**, yet both armies remain at their positions.

## Legacy and Significance

Strategically, Siachen is considered very important. It lies between Pakistan-administered Gilgit-Baltistan to the west and the China-controlled **Shaksgam Valley** to the north. Indian experts believe that a presence on the Saltoro Ridge helps prevent direct contact between the two and strengthens the security of Ladakh. Critics have kept raising the huge cost of this deployment and the risk to soldiers' lives, and the two countries have held several rounds of talks on 'demilitarising' the area, but no agreement could be reached.

Every year on **13 April** the Indian Army marks **Siachen Day** to remember Operation Meghdoot and the soldiers posted here. Operation Meghdoot is a campaign in India's military history that was not a single day's battle but the beginning of an icy vigil that has continued for more than four decades.
