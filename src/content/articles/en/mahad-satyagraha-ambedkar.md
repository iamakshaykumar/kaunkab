## Background: Even Water Was Forbidden

In the early twentieth century, in most parts of India the communities regarded as untouchable were kept away from public wells, tanks, temples and schools. Even for a basic need like drinking water they had to depend on the mercy of others.

In **1923** the Bombay Legislative Council passed a resolution of the social reformer **S. K. Bole**, according to which water sources built with public money or otherwise public should be open to all communities. The municipality of the Konkan town of **Mahad** also decided to open its **Chavdar Tank** to everyone. But because of social opposition this decision remained only on paper.

## 20 March 1927: At the Chavdar Tank

**Dr. Bhimrao Ambedkar** had founded the 'Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha' in 1924, whose aim was to spread awareness of education and rights among Dalit communities. In March 1927 a conference of the Dalit communities of Kolaba district was called at Mahad. The local reformer **Surendranath Tipnis** (president of the Mahad municipality) and Ambedkar's associates such as **A. V. Chitre** and **G. N. Sahasrabuddhe** also cooperated in it.

After the conference, on **20 March 1927**, Dr. Ambedkar walked peacefully to the Chavdar Tank with thousands of people. He drank water from the tank and, after him, the people present also drank. This was not merely quenching thirst but a declaration that all human beings have an equal right to public resources.

## Reaction and Struggle

After this incident tension spread in the town. Some orthodox people attacked those who had come to the conference. Later the tank was also 'purified', as if its water had become impure. This humiliating reaction made the movement even more determined.

Ambedkar announced a second satyagraha at Mahad in December 1927. Meanwhile some people claimed in court that the Chavdar Tank was private property, and the court imposed a temporary restraint. Since the matter was in court, Ambedkar, respecting the law, postponed the satyagraha at the tank in December, but continued the conference.

## 25 December 1927: The Burning of the Manusmriti

During the Mahad conference of December 1927, on **25 December 1927**, a copy of the **Manusmriti** was publicly burned. Ambedkar and his associates believed that this text gives religious sanction to high and low status and discrimination on the basis of caste. Under Ambedkar's guidance his associate **Sahasrabuddhe** carried out the burning.

This was not violence against any religion but a symbolic rejection of ideas that justify inequality. At the same conference Ambedkar presented his views in detail on social equality and human dignity.

## The Court's Verdict

The Chavdar Tank case went on in the courts for almost ten years. Finally, in **1937**, the **Bombay High Court** ruled that the communities regarded as untouchable also had the right to take water from the Chavdar Tank. This was the legal victory of the Mahad movement.

## Historical Significance

The Mahad Satyagraha is regarded as a decisive beginning of the Dalit rights movement. It showed that along with political freedom, social freedom is equally necessary. After this, movements such as the **Kalaram Temple Entry Satyagraha** at Nashik took place in 1930. The spirit of the Mahad struggle was later seen in the Indian Constitution, whose Article 17 abolished untouchability and Article 15 prohibited discrimination in the use of public wells, tanks and places.
