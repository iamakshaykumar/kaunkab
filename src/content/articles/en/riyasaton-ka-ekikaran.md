## Background: The Grave Danger of 'Balkanisation'

When the British Parliament passed the 'Indian Independence Act' in 1947, it contained a dangerous condition: as soon as the Paramountcy of the British Crown ended, India's **565 princely states** would become fully independent. They had three options — to join India, to join Pakistan, or to remain independent countries.

If these states had stayed apart, India would have broken into several dozen small countries (as Winston Churchill had feared). To meet this huge challenge, the **Ministry of States** was set up on 5 July 1947, and its command was given to India's Deputy Prime Minister and Home Minister, **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel**. His secretary was the highly skilled bureaucrat **V. P. Menon**.

## Sardar Patel's Strategy: The Instrument of Accession

Showing far-sightedness, Sardar Patel appealed to the rulers' patriotism and historical responsibility:
- He asked the rulers to sign the **'Instrument of Accession'**, under which the states had to hand over only three subjects — **Defence, External Affairs and Communications** — to the central government, while their autonomy in internal matters was to remain.
- Because of Lord Mountbatten's influence and Sardar Patel's personal persuasion, by 15 August 1947 most of the states had accepted accession to India.

## Three Difficult Challenges and Their Solution

The main crisis arose in the states whose rulers were looking towards Pakistan or were insisting on staying independent:

### 1. Junagadh (Gujarat)
More than 80% of the people of this Kathiawar state were Hindu, but the Nawab, Mahabat Khan, announced accession to Pakistan. The people formed the 'Aarzi Hukumat' (provisional government) and revolted. The Nawab fled to Karachi. After this, in a plebiscite held in February 1948, more than 99% of the people voted in favour of India, and Junagadh became an integral part of India.

### 2. Hyderabad: Operation Polo (September 1948)
Hyderabad was India's largest state, surrounded on all sides by Indian territory. Its Nizam, Osman Ali Khan, declared independence, and the 'Razakars', the cruel paramilitary organisation of Qasim Razvi, began terrible atrocities against the common Hindu and Muslim people.
- When the situation became unbearable, on 13 September 1948 Sardar Patel sent the Indian Army under **'Operation Polo'** (Hyderabad had the largest number of polo grounds in the world, hence the name).
- Within only 108 hours the Nizam's army and the Razakars surrendered, and Hyderabad was fully merged into India.

### 3. Kashmir (October 1947)
Maharaja Hari Singh at first tried to remain neutral, but on 22 October 1947, when Pakistan launched a barbaric attack on Kashmir through tribal fighters backed by its army, the Maharaja sought help from Sardar Patel and Nehru.
- On **26 October 1947** the Maharaja signed the Instrument of Accession, and the very next day the Indian Army landed in Srinagar and drove back the Pakistani infiltrators.

## Historical Significance

The peaceful integration of 565 states without any major civil war is regarded as the greatest miraculous political feat in world history. Bismarck had unified Germany by a policy of iron and blood, but Sardar Patel built an undivided India through firm resolve, diplomacy and respect for popular sentiment. The **'Statue of Unity'**, the world's tallest statue, built on the banks of the Narmada in Gujarat, is dedicated to this historic unity.
