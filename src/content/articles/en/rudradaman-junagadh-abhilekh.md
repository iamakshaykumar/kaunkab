## Who Were the Western Kshatrapas?

In the 1st and 2nd centuries CE, western India — Gujarat, Malwa and the surrounding areas — was ruled by rulers of **Shaka** origin, called the **Western Kshatrapas**. The word "kshatrapa" comes from the Iranian "satrap" (provincial governor). One branch of them was the **Kardamaka dynasty**, whose foundation was laid by **Chashtana**. Chashtana's grandson and Jayadaman's son **Rudradaman I** (reign about 130–150 CE) became the most famous ruler of this dynasty. His capital is believed to have been **Ujjain**.

## One Rock, Three Ages

At **Junagadh** in Gujarat, on the route up Mount **Girnar**, there is a huge granite rock on which inscriptions of three different ages are carved:

- The oldest are the fourteen rock edicts of Emperor **Ashoka** (3rd century BCE);
- Higher up on the same rock is the inscription of **Rudradaman**;
- And the third is an inscription of the time of the Gupta emperor **Skandagupta** (5th century CE).

Rudradaman's inscription is dated **Shaka year 72**, which comes to about **150 CE**. It is written in the **Brahmi script**, in prose, and in nearly standard **Sanskrit**. According to the scholar Richard Salomon, it is the first long inscription written entirely in Sanskrit — before this most inscriptions were in Prakrit.

## The History of Sudarshana Lake

The opening lines of the inscription tell the history of **Sudarshana Lake**. According to it:

- **Pushyagupta**, the Vaishya governor of **Chandragupta Maurya**, built this lake (dam);
- in the time of **Ashoka**, the **Yavana king Tushaspha** had canals drawn out of it;
- in Rudradaman's time a terrible storm and flood breached the dam and the lake was almost emptied.

This account is rare evidence that even in the Mauryan period attention was paid to irrigation and water management, and that people remembered its history about four hundred years later.

## The Repair and the Praise of the King

The inscription says that although the ministers opposed the work as useless, Rudradaman, through **Suvishakha**, an officer of **Pahlava** (Parthian) origin who was the governor of Anarta and Surashtra, had the dam rebuilt three times stronger than before — and that too **from his own treasury**, without putting taxes or forced labour on his subjects.

A large part of the inscription is a **prashasti** (eulogy) of Rudradaman. It lists regions under him such as Malwa, Anupa, Anarta, Surashtra, Kachchha, Sindhu-Sauvira and Aparanta. It claims that he defeated the brave **Yaudheyas** and, having twice defeated **Satakarni, lord of Dakshinapatha**, did not destroy him because of their close relationship. An inscription at Kanheri shows that Rudradaman's daughter was married to the Satavahana king **Vashishthiputra Satakarni**. All these are claims made on behalf of the king, so historians read them with caution.

## The Later Story and Significance

The story of Sudarshana Lake does not end here. According to Skandagupta's inscription, in Gupta year 136 (about 455–456 CE) the dam broke again, and its repair was carried out by the city governor **Chakrapalita**. That is, the record of the care of a single reservoir spans three empires — Maurya, Shaka and Gupta.

Rudradaman's inscription is important for several reasons: it shows the beginning of Sanskrit becoming a language of the court, it is the forerunner of the later Gupta-period eulogies (such as the Prayag Prashasti), and it is a rare document of public works in ancient India.
