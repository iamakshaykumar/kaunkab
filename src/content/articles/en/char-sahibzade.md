## The Four Sons of Guru Gobind Singh

The four sons of the tenth Sikh Guru, **Guru Gobind Singh**, are known in Sikh history as the **"Chaar Sahibzade"**:

- **Ajit Singh** (born 1687, Paonta Sahib) — the eldest;
- **Jujhar Singh** (born about 1691, Anandpur);
- **Zorawar Singh** (born about 1696);
- **Fateh Singh** (born about 1699) — the youngest.

The elder two are called the **"Vadde Sahibzade"** and the younger two the **"Chhote Sahibzade"**. All four gave their lives within the same month of 1704, so this time of December is regarded by Sikhs as a month of mourning and remembrance. (Birth years differ by a year or two between sources.)

## The Siege of Anandpur and the Crossing of the Sarsa

After the founding of the Khalsa in **1699**, pressure on Anandpur from the hill rajas and Mughal faujdars kept growing. In **1704** Anandpur was under a long siege, and even food began to run out in the fort. According to Sikh sources, the besiegers swore oaths promising safe passage. On this assurance, one night in **December 1704**, the Guru's family and the Sikhs left Anandpur.

But the promise was broken and they were pursued and attacked. While crossing the flooded **Sarsa river** there was great confusion; many Sikhs were killed and the family was separated. The Guru, with his two elder sons and a few Sikhs, moved towards **Chamkaur**, while the grandmother **Mata Gujri** was separated with the two younger grandsons.

## The Battle of Chamkaur

At Chamkaur the Guru took position with about **40 Sikhs** in a mud haveli (garhi). All around was a much larger Mughal force. The Sikhs went out in small groups, fighting until they fell. **Ajit Singh**, about **eighteen**, took his father's permission to lead a sortie and died fighting. After him **Jujhar Singh**, about **fourteen**, also went into battle and was killed.

At night the **Panj Pyare** ordered the Guru to save himself for the sake of the Panth. **Bhai Sangat Singh**, who resembled the Guru, put on his clothes and held the position, while the Guru slipped away in the darkness with a few companions. Further on, two Pathans, **Nabi Khan** and **Ghani Khan**, helped him. Later, in the Persian letter **Zafarnama**, the Guru held Aurangzeb responsible for the broken oaths.

The date of this battle is disputed — depending on how the Vikrami date is converted, historians give **6–7 December** or **21–22 December 1704**, and some sources write the year as 1705.

## Sirhind — the Sacrifice of the Younger Sahibzade

Mata Gujri and the younger Sahibzade were taken to his village by **Gangu**, a former cook. According to Sikh tradition, tempted by a reward, he informed the officials of **Morinda**. The three were arrested, brought to **Sirhind** and, in the bitter cold, held in an open tower called the **Thanda Burj** (cold tower).

In the court of **Wazir Khan**, the faujdar of Sirhind, Zorawar Singh, about **nine**, and Fateh Singh, about **six**, were offered life and honour if they changed their religion. The children refused. In the court the Nawab of **Malerkotla**, **Sher Muhammad Khan**, said it was unjust to punish children for their father's deeds, but he was not heeded. Wazir Khan ordered the children to be bricked up alive in a wall. According to the sources, when the wall collapsed they were put to death. Their martyrdom is dated **26–27 December 1704**; on hearing the news, Mata Gujri also died in the Thanda Burj.

Wazir Khan made it difficult even to obtain land for the cremation. According to tradition, **Diwan Todar Mal**, a wealthy merchant of Sirhind, paid for the land by laying gold coins over it and cremated the three.

## Remembrance — from Fatehgarh Sahib to Veer Bal Diwas

At the place where the younger Sahibzade were martyred now stands **Gurdwara Fatehgarh Sahib**, and the town itself is named **Fatehgarh Sahib** after Sahibzada Fateh Singh. Every year at the end of December the **Shaheedi Jor Mela** is held here, drawing hundreds of thousands of people. At Chamkaur Sahib, **Gurdwara Qatalgarh Sahib** and **Garhi Sahib** commemorate the elder Sahibzade. A few years later, in **1710**, the forces of **Banda Singh Bahadur** defeated Wazir Khan at the Battle of Chappar Chiri and captured Sirhind.

On **9 January 2022**, on the Prakash Purab of Guru Gobind Singh, Prime Minister **Narendra Modi** announced that **26 December** would be observed every year as **"Veer Bal Diwas"**. The first national event was held in Delhi on **26 December 2022**. Some Sikh bodies, such as the Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee, objected to the name and suggested names such as 'Sahibzade Shahadat Diwas'.

The story of the four Sahibzade is regarded in Sikh history as the most moving example of **sacrifice for faith and self-respect** — especially because the younger Sahibzade were still children. It also recalls the courage of people like Sher Muhammad Khan and Todar Mal, who took risks to oppose injustice.
