## Who Was Abdali

**Ahmad Shah Abdali** (later called **Ahmad Shah Durrani**) belonged to the **Abdali** tribe of the Afghans. He was born about **1720–1722**; there are claims for both Herat and Multan as his birthplace. He was an officer in the army of the Iranian ruler **Nader Shah**, the same Nader Shah who plundered Delhi in 1739. After Nader Shah's assassination in **1747**, the Afghan chiefs chose Ahmad Shah as their ruler, and with Kandahar as its centre the foundation of the **Durrani Empire** was laid. Some stories about his coronation, such as that of placing a sheaf of wheat on his turban, are considered later legends.

## Repeated Attacks on Punjab (1748–1752)

The richest frontier province of the weakening Mughal Empire was **Punjab**, so it became Abdali's first target.

- **First invasion (1748):** In January 1748 the Afghans took **Lahore**, but after an explosion in their gunpowder store in the battle of **Manupur** (near Sirhind) against the Mughal army, they had to turn back. In this period the Mughal wazir Qamar-ud-din Khan was killed.
- **Second invasion (1748–49):** The Mughal governor of Lahore, **Mu'in-ul-Mulk** (Mir Mannu), agreed to pay Abdali the revenue of some districts.
- **Third invasion (1751–52):** In March 1752 Lahore was again besieged, and through a treaty **Punjab and Multan** formally passed into Afghan hands.

## Delhi, Mathura and Vrindavan (1756–57)

Abdali's most destructive invasion was that of **1756–57**. On **28 January 1757** he reached Delhi. The Mughal emperor **Alamgir II** was reduced to a nominal ruler, heavy sums were extracted from the wazir Imad-ul-Mulk, and the city was plundered. Estimates of the loot differ widely, but it is certain that the wealth was carried to Afghanistan on thousands of animals.

After Delhi, a detachment of his army under the general **Jahan Khan** massacred pilgrims and residents at **Mathura** and then at **Vrindavan**. The story of the resistance of Naga sadhus at Gokul is also famous, though some details of it rest on popular tradition. Because of heat and disease Abdali had to return. On leaving, he secured an influential position in Delhi for the Rohilla chief **Najib ud-Daula** and made his son **Timur Shah** governor of Punjab.

## The Third Battle of Panipat (1759–61)

As soon as Abdali went back, the **Marathas** expanded rapidly in north India and in 1758 drove Timur Shah out of Punjab. In response Abdali came again in 1759. Najib ud-Daula and the Nawab of Awadh, **Shuja-ud-Daula**, joined him. On **14 January 1761**, a decisive battle was fought at **Panipat** against the Maratha army led by **Sadashivrao Bhau**. The Marathas were badly defeated, the Peshwa's son **Vishwasrao** was killed and tens of thousands of lives were lost (historians differ on the figures). Even so, Abdali did not stay on in Delhi — he recognised **Shah Alam II** as emperor and returned to Afghanistan.

## Conflict with the Sikhs (1762–1767)

After Panipat, Abdali's greatest opposition in Punjab came from the **Sikh misls**. On **5–6 February 1762**, at **Kup** near Malerkotla, he attacked the Sikhs by surprise, and thousands of people — among them a large number of women, children and elderly — were killed. In Sikh history this is called the **"Vadda Ghallughara"** (the Great Massacre); estimates of the dead range from about 5,000 to 50,000. In **April 1762** his army blew up the Harmandir Sahib at **Amritsar** with gunpowder.

Even so, within a few months the Sikhs reorganised. Abdali came again in 1764–65 and in **1766–67**, but each time as soon as he left, the Sikhs reasserted control over their areas. Gradually the Afghan hold over Punjab weakened.

## Results and Assessment

Abdali's invasions hollowed out the already weak **Mughal Empire** further, halted the **Marathas'** expansion in north India for nearly ten years, and opened the way for the rise of **Sikh power** in Punjab. His aim was mainly wealth, revenue and control of frontier regions such as Punjab, Sindh and Kashmir, not ruling from Delhi. In Afghanistan he is honoured as the founder of the modern Afghan state and as **"Ahmad Shah Baba"**, while in India his memory is associated with plunder and destruction. He died in **October 1772** and was buried at **Kandahar**.
