## The Rise of the Rashtrakutas

In the middle of the eighth century a big change came over the politics of the Deccan. Around 753 CE **Dantidurga** challenged the power of the Chalukyas of Badami and laid the foundation of the **Rashtrakuta** kingdom. After him his uncle **Krishna I** (reigned c. 756–773 CE) took the throne and strengthened the kingdom by almost ending Chalukya power.

Later, in the time of rulers such as Govinda III and Amoghavarsha I, the Rashtrakutas began to intervene in the politics of North India as well. In Amoghavarsha's time the capital became Manyakheta (today's Malkhed in Karnataka).

## A Temple Made from a Single Rock

**Cave 16** at Ellora is called the Kailasa or Kailasanatha Temple. It is dedicated to Lord Shiva and is named after Mount Kailasa, Shiva's abode.

The greatest feature of this temple is its method of construction:
- The craftsmen first cut deep trenches all around in the basalt hill, then carved the huge rock left in the middle from top to bottom into the form of a temple.
- The main temple, the Nandi pavilion, the gateway, the flagstaff and the figures of elephants — all are parts of that same single rock.
- It is estimated that hundreds of thousands of tons of rock must have been removed for it, though scholars' estimates of the exact quantity differ.

## Krishna I and the Evidence of Inscriptions

No inscription has been found on the temple that directly gives its date. It is attributed to Krishna I mainly on the basis of later Rashtrakuta inscriptions. The Baroda copper plates of **Karka II**, a Rashtrakuta ruler of the Gujarat branch, praise a wonderful Shiva temple built by "Krishnaraja" at **Elapura** (Ellora), which scholars take to be the Kailasa Temple.

Some historians, such as M. K. Dhavalikar, hold that the plan may have been made in Dantidurga's time and the main work done in the time of Krishna I, while some parts of the complex were added in the time of later kings. It is therefore not right to link the construction to any single year.

## Sculpture and Stories

The walls of the Kailasa Temple are like a book of mythology in stone. Episodes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata are carved here in rows of panels. Among the most famous scenes is **Ravana's attempt to lift Mount Kailasa**, in which Shiva and Parvati are shown seated on the mountain. The sculptures of the river goddesses Ganga, Yamuna and Saraswati and of Gajalakshmi are also notable. The temple once had a coating of lime plaster and paint as well, some traces of which are still seen today.

## Ellora: A Meeting of Three Traditions

Ellora is counted as having 34 main caves in all — Buddhist (about 1–12), Brahmanical/Hindu (about 13–29) and Jain (about 30–34). They were made in different centuries and show that different religious traditions flourished side by side in one place. UNESCO declared the Ellora Caves a World Heritage Site in 1983.

## Significance

The Kailasa Temple is regarded as the peak of Indian rock-cut architecture (construction by cutting rock). It shows how deep was the knowledge of planning, calculation and stone craft among the craftsmen of the eighth century. It is also proof of the prosperity and art patronage of the Rashtrakuta rulers.
