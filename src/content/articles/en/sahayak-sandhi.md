## Background

By the end of the eighteenth century the East India Company had established itself in Bengal, Bihar and the coastal areas of Madras and Bombay. But in the Deccan the **Maratha Confederacy**, the **Nizam of Hyderabad** and **Tipu Sultan** of Mysore were major powers. In Europe a war was going on between Britain and revolutionary France, and in several Indian courts French officers were training armies. The British feared that France might join Indian rulers and become a threat to them.

In **1798** **Richard Wellesley** (later Marquess Wellesley) came as Governor-General. He openly held that the Company should become the supreme power in India.

## Terms of the Alliance

A ruler who made a Subsidiary Alliance generally had to accept these terms:

- A permanent army of the Company would stay in the state, with its cost paid by the ruler — in cash or by handing over some territory of his state.
- A British **Resident** would stay at the court.
- The ruler would not make war or a treaty with any other power without the Company's permission.
- He would not keep any European (especially French) in his service.
- In return the Company would protect him from external attacks and internal revolt.

## The Beginning with Hyderabad

The Nizam, **Nizam Ali Khan**, pressed by the Marathas, accepted this treaty on **1 September 1798**. His French-trained army was disbanded and the Company's subsidiary force was put in its place. In 1800, under a new treaty, the Nizam had to hand over some of his districts to the Company in return for the cost of the army.

## Mysore, Awadh and the Marathas

Tipu Sultan rejected this arrangement. In the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War of **1799** Tipu was killed; the larger part of Mysore was divided between the Company and the Nizam, and on the remaining kingdom a child of the Wodeyar dynasty was seated and the Subsidiary Alliance was imposed. In the same year **Thanjavur** also came into it. In **1801** the Nawab of **Awadh**, Saadat Ali Khan, had to give a large region of Rohilkhand and the Doab in return for the cost of the army.

The biggest step was taken with the Marathas. The Peshwa **Baji Rao II**, who had fled after being defeated by Holkar, made the **Treaty of Bassein** on **31 December 1802**. Scindia and Bhonsle regarded it as a blow to Maratha independence, and the **Second Anglo-Maratha War** (1803–1805) followed. After defeat, in 1803–04 Bhonsle and Scindia also had to accept such treaties.

## Results and Assessment

This arrangement was very profitable for the Company: a large army was raised at the expense of Indian rulers, French influence was ended and control over large regions was gained without direct rule. For the dependent states the results were the opposite — their own armies were disbanded, thousands of soldiers became unemployed, the burden of cost fell on the people and Residents began to interfere in the internal politics of the court. Later it also became easy to annex such states by charging them with "misrule", as happened with Awadh in 1856.

The Company's directors in London were worried about the heavy cost of Wellesley's wars, and in 1805 he was recalled. Even so, the system of the Subsidiary Alliance remained, and by 1818 most of the major states, including Rajputana and Holkar, had come under the Company in this way.
