## A Scientific Family from Ahmedabad

**Vikram Ambalal Sarabhai** was born on **12 August 1919** into a wealthy industrialist family of **Ahmedabad**. His father **Ambalal Sarabhai** owned textile mills, and the family was also connected with the freedom movement. People such as Rabindranath Tagore and Jawaharlal Nehru were visitors to the house, which awakened in young Vikram an interest in both science and society.

After studying at Gujarat College in Ahmedabad he went to the **University of Cambridge** in England, where he passed the Natural Sciences Tripos in **1940**. Because of the Second World War he returned to India and began researching **cosmic rays** (energetic particles arriving from space) at the **Indian Institute of Science** in **Bangalore**, under the guidance of Nobel laureate **C. V. Raman**. It was here that he met **Homi Bhabha**. After the war he went back to Cambridge and in **1947** received his PhD for research on cosmic rays in tropical latitudes.

In 1942 he married the famous dancer **Mrinalini Swaminathan**, who later became known as Mrinalini Sarabhai.

## A Builder of Institutions

On returning to India, Sarabhai founded the **Physical Research Laboratory (PRL)** in Ahmedabad in **November 1947**. At first it ran from a few rooms of the M. G. Science Institute in Ahmedabad, but it soon became a leading centre for space science and cosmic ray research. Even today PRL is called the "cradle" of Indian space science.

Sarabhai was not only a scientist but also a skilled builder of institutions. He played a role in setting up the **Ahmedabad Textile Industry's Research Association (ATIRA)** for the textile industry. He was also a pioneer in creating the **Indian Institute of Management (IIM) Ahmedabad**, founded in **1961**, and led it in its early years. He also built institutions such as the Community Science Centre and the Nehru Foundation for Development, and with his wife laid the foundation of the **Darpana Academy**.

## The Space Age Begins at Thumba

After the Soviet satellite Sputnik in 1957, Sarabhai persuaded the government that for a developing country like India space technology was not a luxury but a necessity. His famous view was that if India was to play its role in the world, it must be second to none in applying advanced technology to the real problems of ordinary people and society — communication, weather and education.

On his suggestion the **Indian National Committee for Space Research (INCOSPAR)** was formed in **1962**, with him as its chairman. For launching rockets he chose the Kerala fishing village of **Thumba**, because it lies near the Earth's **magnetic equator**. A church there was turned into an office, and on **21 November 1963** the first sounding rocket was launched from Thumba. The young **A. P. J. Abdul Kalam** was part of this team.

On **15 August 1969** the **Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO)** replaced INCOSPAR, and Sarabhai became its first chairman. He signed an agreement with the American space agency NASA for an experiment to take educational television to villages by satellite (SITE), and began the plan for India to build its own satellite, which bore fruit in **1975** as **Aryabhata**.

## Responsibility for the Atomic Energy Commission

In January **1966** **Homi Bhabha** died in a plane crash. After this, Sarabhai was made chairman of the **Atomic Energy Commission**. So for some years he led both the country's space and nuclear programmes. He stressed the peaceful use of atomic energy and advanced long-term planning for electricity generation. He received the Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Prize in **1962** and the **Padma Bhushan** in **1966**.

## Untimely Death and Legacy

In the midst of all this work, on **30 December 1971**, he died of a heart attack at Kovalam near **Thiruvananthapuram** in Kerala. He was only **52 years** old. In **1972** he was posthumously awarded the **Padma Vibhushan**.

In his memory the space centre at Thumba is called the **Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre**. A crater on the Moon is named 'Sarabhai', and the landers of **Chandrayaan-2** and **Chandrayaan-3** were named **'Vikram'** in his honour. The institutions Sarabhai built — PRL, ISRO, IIM Ahmedabad — remain a strong foundation of India's science, technology and management education today. That is why he is called the **father of the Indian space programme**.
