## From Karnata to Bengal

At the end of the 11th century, when the **Pala dynasty** of Bengal began to weaken, a new dynasty arose: the **Sena dynasty**. Sena inscriptions themselves say that their ancestors came from the **Karnata** region of South India, and they called themselves "**Brahmakshatriya**". How they reached Bengal is not clear; one view is that they came in the service of the Pala kings, another that they settled here along with campaigns in the Deccan.

The first known person of this dynasty was **Samantasena**, who in his old age came to settle on the banks of the Ganga in **Radha** (today's West Bengal). His son **Hemantasena** was a feudatory under the Palas and, when Pala power slackened, set up a small kingdom of his own in Radha.

## Vijayasena — the real founder of the kingdom

The credit for making the Sena kingdom independent and large goes to **Vijayasena**, who ruled from about **1096/1098 to 1159/1160**, that is, for more than sixty years. At first he supported the Pala king **Ramapala** against the Kaivarta revolt and kept friendship with the Ganga king of Odisha, Anantavarman Chodaganga.

When Pala power weakened, Vijayasena removed the Palas from North Bengal and the **Varman** dynasty, whose capital was **Vikrampur**, from south-eastern Bengal. He was a Shaiva and bore titles such as "Parama-maheshvara Maharajadhiraja". In his praise the poet **Umapatidhara** composed the **Deopara Prashasti**, which describes a tall temple of Pradyumneshvara (Shiva) and a pond dug nearby. This inscription is today kept in the Indian Museum, Kolkata.

## Ballalasena — the learned king

Vijayasena's son **Ballalasena** ruled from about **1160 to 1178** (some sources say 1159–1179). In his time the Pala dynasty finally came to an end: he defeated the king of Gauda, **Govindapala**. According to the *Adbhutasagara*, he had conquered **Mithila** during his father's lifetime. He married the Western Chalukya princess **Ramadevi**, which indicates the Senas' close ties with South India.

Ballalasena is remembered more for his learning than for war. He wrote the **Danasagara** (**1168**) on gifts and began the **Adbhutasagara** (begun in **1169**) on omens and astronomical events, which was later completed by his son. Tradition credits him with starting the **Kulin system** among the Brahmins and Kayasthas of Bengal, but historians view this with doubt, because it is found only in works written centuries later. Tradition also links the **Dhakeshwari temple** of Dhaka with him.

## The court of Lakshmanasena

**Lakshmanasena** (about **1178/1179 to 1206**) is regarded as the most famous king of the Sena dynasty. He was the first Sena king to adopt the title "**Gaudeshvara**" and, unlike his father and grandfather, was a **Vaishnava**. As crown prince he had defeated the kings of Gauda and Varanasi.

His court was a major centre of Sanskrit literature. Poets here included **Jayadeva** (author of the *Gita Govinda*), **Dhoyi**, the poet of the *Pavanaduta*, **Govardhana**, **Sharana** and **Umapatidhara**. The chief minister and judge **Halayudha** wrote the *Brahmanasarvasva*, and Shridharadasa compiled the poetry anthology *Saduktikarnamrita*. The Persian historian **Minhaj-us-Siraj** also praised Lakshmanasena's generosity.

## The attack on Nadia and the end

In Lakshmanasena's last years many local chiefs began to become independent and the kingdom weakened. At this time, around **1203–1204**, the Ghurid general **Bakhtiyar Khalji** made a sudden raid on **Nadia (Nabadwip)**. According to Minhaj's account, Bakhtiyar advanced so fast that only **18 horsemen** could keep up with him, and this small party entered the town and took everyone by surprise. This story rests on a single source, so historians read its details with caution.

Lakshmanasena moved towards East Bengal, and Bakhtiyar made **Lakhnauti** (Gauda) his capital. After this, **Vishvarupasena** and **Keshavasena** went on ruling southern and eastern Bengal (Vikrampur and Vanga) for about 25 years. By the middle of the 13th century Sena power had ended completely.

## Legacy

The Sena dynasty for the first time brought **almost all of Bengal under one rule**. In their time Brahmanical religion and Sanskrit learning received royal patronage, while the influence of Buddhism, which had flourished under the Palas, kept declining. Works like the *Gita Govinda* later influenced devotion, music and art across all of India.
