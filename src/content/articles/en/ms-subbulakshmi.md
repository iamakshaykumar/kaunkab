## A Musical Home in Madurai

**Madurai Shanmukhavadivu Subbulakshmi**, known to the world as **'M. S.'**, was born on **16 September 1916** in **Madurai**, Tamil Nadu. Her mother, **Shanmukhavadivu Ammal**, was an accomplished **veena player**, so music filled the house from her childhood. She was affectionately called 'Kunjamma'.

Sitting on stage beside her mother, Subbulakshmi began to train her voice. At about **11 years of age**, in **1927**, she gave her first public performance. Later she received deep training in Carnatic music from the renowned singer **Semmangudi Srinivasa Iyer** and also learnt Hindustani music from **Pandit Narayanrao Vyas**. In **1929**, at just 13, she sang at the **Madras Music Academy** — a big achievement for a teenage girl in those days.

## Madras, Marriage and Cinema

While still a teenager she moved from Madurai to **Madras**. There she met the freedom fighter and journalist **T. Sadasivam**, whom she married in **1940**. Sadasivam gave direction to her career and remained her companion and manager all her life.

Subbulakshmi also acted in a few Tamil films — **'Sevasadanam'** (1938), **'Sakuntalai'** (1940), **'Savitri'** (1941) and **'Meera'** (1945). In 'Savitri' she played the role of Narada, and its earnings gave financial support to Sadasivam's magazine 'Kalki'. The greatest success came with **'Meera'**, which was also made in **Hindi** in **1947**. Mirabai's bhajans made her popular across the whole country beyond Tamil Nadu. After this she gave up acting and devoted her whole life to music.

It is said that on hearing her sing, Prime Minister **Jawaharlal Nehru** remarked — "Who am I, a mere Prime Minister, before a Queen of Song." **Mahatma Gandhi** too was an admirer of her bhajans. A well-known story is that in 1947 Gandhi wished to hear Mira's bhajan **"Hari Tum Haro"** in her voice. Subbulakshmi was not familiar with its tune, yet she learnt it overnight and recorded it, and the recording was sent to Delhi for Gandhi's birthday on 2 October 1947. After Gandhi's assassination in January 1948, many people were moved to tears on hearing this same bhajan played on All India Radio.

## On the Stages of India and the World

Subbulakshmi gave thousands of concerts in India and abroad. In **1963** she sang at the **Edinburgh International Festival** in Britain. Her most historic performance took place on **23 October 1966** in the hall of the **UN General Assembly** in **New York**, where she had been invited for United Nations Day by Secretary-General **U Thant**. She became the **first Indian** to sing there.

For this event, **Chandrasekharendra Saraswati**, the Shankaracharya of the **Kanchi Kamakoti Peetham**, wrote the Sanskrit composition **'Maithreem Bhajata'**, carrying a message of world peace and friendship, which Subbulakshmi sang with her daughter **Radha Viswanathan**. With its spirit of "cultivate friendship with all, regard others as yourself", the song remains bound up with her identity. Later she also performed on many international stages, including **Carnegie Hall** in New York.

## Voice of Devotion

Subbulakshmi's voice became synonymous with devotional music. Her **'Sri Venkatesa Suprabhatam'**, sung for Lord Venkateswara of Tirupati, still plays at dawn in countless homes and temples of south India. Adi Shankaracharya's **'Bhaja Govindam'**, the **'Vishnu Sahasranamam'**, **C. Rajagopalachari's** composition **'Kurai Onrum Illai'** and the bhajans of Mira, Kabir and Tulsidas are counted among her immortal recordings. She sang in many languages, including Tamil, Sanskrit, Telugu, Kannada, Hindi and Bengali.

She was also known for her generosity. She gave hundreds of **charity concerts** for hospitals, schools and charitable institutions and donated the royalties of many recordings.

## Honours and Legacy

Subbulakshmi received the **Padma Bhushan** in **1954**, the **Sangeet Natak Akademi Award** in **1956**, the title **'Sangita Kalanidhi'** in **1968**, the **Ramon Magsaysay Award** in **1974** and the **Padma Vibhushan** in **1975**. In **1998** she was given the country's highest civilian honour, the **Bharat Ratna**; she was the **first musician** to receive it.

After the death of her husband Sadasivam in 1997, she almost stopped giving public performances. She died in **Chennai** on **11 December 2004**, at the age of 88. M. S. Subbulakshmi, who carried classical Carnatic music to ordinary people and India's voice to the world's biggest stage, is counted among the most respected figures of Indian music.
