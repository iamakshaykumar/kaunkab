## From Slave to Sultan

**Shamsuddin Iltutmish** was born in about 1192 in the **Ilbari Turk** tribe of Central Asia. In childhood his brothers, out of jealousy, sold him — this story is given by the contemporary historian **Minhaj-us-Siraj**. Sold through many hands, he reached Delhi, where **Qutb-ud-din Aibak** bought him. Aibak was himself a slave of Muhammad Ghori, so Iltutmish was called 'the slave of a slave'.

He rose through his ability. Aibak gave him the iqta of **Badaun** and married his daughter to him. After Aibak's sudden death in 1210, the nobles of Delhi considered his successor **Aram Shah** weak and called Iltutmish. In **1211**, after defeating Aram Shah, Iltutmish became Sultan and made **Delhi** his capital.

## Struggle with Rivals

At first Iltutmish's position was weak — Ghori's other old slaves also claimed territories in India:
- **Taj-ud-din Yildiz** (Ghazni) he defeated in **1216** at **Tarain**.
- A campaign was launched against **Nasir-ud-din Qabacha** (Multan-Uch) in 1228; after Uch slipped from his hands, Qabacha drowned in the Indus river and died.
- In **Bengal** the independence of the Khalji chiefs was ended in the campaigns of 1225–1230.
- In the Rajput regions he took **Ranthambore** (1226), **Mandor** (1227) and, after a long siege, **Gwalior** (December 1232).

## The Mongol Crisis

In 1221, pursued by the army of **Genghis Khan**, the prince of Khwarazm **Jalal-ud-din Mangburni** reached the Indus and asked Iltutmish for shelter. Iltutmish politely refused, so that the anger of the Mongols would not turn towards Delhi. The Mongol army did not come deep into India. This careful diplomacy is considered the decision that saved the new Sultanate.

## Administration: Iqta, Tanka and Jital

- **The iqta system:** Military officers were given the revenue of a region (iqta) in place of salary; in return they had to maintain troops and serve the Sultan. Iltutmish tried, by transferring the iqta holders, to stop them from becoming local centres of power.
- **Currency:** He introduced a **silver tanka** of about 11 grams (about 175 grains) and a **copper jital**, which later became the basis of the Sultanate's currency.
- **Recognition by the Caliph:** In **1229** a letter of recognition from the Abbasid caliph of Baghdad reached Delhi, which gave his rule legitimacy in the Islamic world.
- **The Chihalgani:** The later historian Ziauddin Barani calls the group of Iltutmish's chosen Turkish slave-nobles the 'Chihalgani' (the Forty). Whether this group was exactly forty, and whether Iltutmish formally created it, is a matter of disagreement among historians.

## Architecture

Iltutmish had the upper storeys of the **Qutb Minar** completed and extended the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque. The **Hauz-i-Shamsi** reservoir at Mehrauli and the **Sultan Ghari** tomb (1231) also belong to his time. In the Qutb complex is his own tomb (about 1235), whose inner walls have fine carving.

## Succession and Assessment

After the death of his eldest son Nasiruddin Mahmud in 1229, Iltutmish declared his daughter **Razia** his successor — during the Gwalior campaign (1231) Razia had handled the affairs of Delhi well. After his death on **30 April 1236**, the nobles first seated his son **Ruknuddin Firoz** on the throne, but within a few months Razia became Sultan.

Aibak laid the foundation of the Sultanate, but it was Iltutmish who gave it the form of a stable state — which is why many historians consider him the **real founder of the Delhi Sultanate**.
