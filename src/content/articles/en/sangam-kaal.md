## Background: The Meaning of 'Sangam' and the Tradition

The peninsular region south of the Krishna and Tungabhadra rivers was called **'Tamilakam'** in ancient times. The word 'Sangam' actually refers to a literary assembly or academy of Tamil poets, teachers and scholars, held at Madurai under the patronage of Pandya kings.

According to tradition, three Sangams took place:
1. **First Sangam:** at Madurai (none of whose books is now available).
2. **Second Sangam:** at Kapatapuram (from which only the Tamil grammar *Tolkappiyam* survived).
3. **Third Sangam:** at northern Madurai, to which most of the Sangam literature available today belongs.

## Muvendar: The Three Major Dynasties

Sangam literature gives detailed accounts of three major Tamil dynasties (called the **'Muvendar'**, or three crowned kings):
1. **Cholas:** in the Kaveri valley and northern Tamil Nadu. Their famous port was **Puhar (Kaveripattinam)**. The mighty Chola king **Karikala** built an embankment on the Kaveri river (the Kallanai / Grand Anicut), which is one of the oldest functioning water structures in the world.
2. **Pandyas:** in southern Tamil Nadu, with their capital at **Madurai**. This kingdom was famous for pearl fisheries and literary gatherings.
3. **Cheras:** in present-day Kerala and western Tamil Nadu, with their capital at **Vanji** and main port at **Muziris**.

## The Nature of Sangam Literature: Agam and Puram

Sangam poems present deep human experiences of life in two main categories:
- **Agam:** poems about inner feelings, love, nature and personal emotions.
- **Puram:** works about outer life, war, heroism, the valour of kings, generosity and justice.

The major works of this period include the *Ettuthokai* (Eight Anthologies), the *Pattuppattu* (Ten Idylls) and the epics:
- **Silappadikaram (The Tale of the Anklet):** this epic, composed by Ilango Adigal, tells the story of Kovalan, Madhavi and the chaste, virtuous **Kannagi**. When the Pandya king put Kovalan to death on a false charge, Kannagi proved the truth with her anklet and burned the whole city of Madurai to ashes.
- **Manimekalai:** a work composed by the Buddhist poet Sittalai Sattanar, which presents philosophical ideas.
- **Tirukkural:** a work of 1,330 couplets composed by the saint **Thiruvalluvar**, regarded as the 'Tamil Veda'.

## Sea Trade and Foreign Contacts

The Sangam age was economically very prosperous:
- Excavations at **Arikamedu**, near Puducherry, have yielded Roman pottery (amphorae), seals and coins of the Roman emperor Augustus.
- Greek and Roman traders are called **'Yavanas'** in Sangam literature. The Yavanas brought gold in their ships and took away in exchange Indian black pepper, cinnamon, muslin, ivory and precious gems.

## Historical Significance

The Sangam age strengthened the distinctive language, culture, architecture and secular moral values of south India. It is one of the most vibrant and authentic heritages of Indian literature.
