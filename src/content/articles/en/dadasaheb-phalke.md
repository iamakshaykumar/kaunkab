## From Trimbak to Bombay

**Dhundiraj Govind Phalke** was born on **30 April 1870** into a scholarly Brahmin family in the pilgrim town of **Trimbak** near Nashik. He later became famous as **Dadasaheb Phalke**. Because of his interest in art he learned painting, sculpture and photography at the **Sir J. J. School of Art** in Bombay and then at the **Kala Bhavan** in Baroda.

Before films, Phalke did many kinds of work — photographer, stage designer, magic shows and printing. He ran a printing press that did photo-litho work for Raja Ravi Varma's press. This experience of printing colourful pictures of gods and goddesses later showed in the visual world of his films.

## A Film That Changed His Life

Around **1911**, in a Bombay cinema hall, Phalke watched the foreign silent film **'The Life of Christ'**. (Some sources date this event to 1910.) Watching the life of Jesus move on the screen, an idea came to him — if the story of Christ could come to the screen, why not that of **Rama and Krishna**? He decided to make films on Indian stories, made by Indians.

This was not easy. India had no film-making industry and no training. Phalke put almost all his property into this dream, so his friends began jokingly calling him "Harishchandra". To win the trust of investors he made a short film, after seeing which some people lent him money. On **1 February 1912** he sailed to **London**, learned the finer points of film-making there and bought a **Williamson camera** and other equipment. On returning, he founded the **Phalke Films Company** on **1 April 1912**.

## Making 'Raja Harishchandra'

For his first feature film Phalke chose the famous story of the truthful **King Harishchandra**, who gives up his kingdom, family and everything for the sake of truth. Sets were built at his bungalow in **Dadar**, and outdoor scenes were filmed around Vangani and Trimbakeshwar. The shooting took **six months and twenty-seven days**.

The biggest difficulty was the **female roles**. At that time women from respectable families were not willing to appear on screen. In the end the role of Queen **Taramati** was played by a male actor, **Anna Salunke**. **Dattatraya Dabke** played King Harishchandra, and the role of Prince Rohitashva was played by Phalke's son **Bhalchandra**. Phalke himself was director, screenwriter, cameraman, editor and producer — everything — and his family too helped with the film work.

It was a **silent film**, about **3,700 feet** long (roughly four reels). Its intertitles were in **English, Marathi and Hindi**. On **21 April 1913** it premiered for invited viewers at Bombay's **Olympia Theatre**, and from **3 May 1913** it was shown to the general public at the **Coronation Cinema** in Girgaon. It ran continuously for **23 days**, a great success at the time.

Some historians regard Dadasaheb Torne's **'Shree Pundalik'** (1912) as the first Indian film, but according to critics it was mainly a recording of a staged play. The **Government of India** regards 'Raja Harishchandra' as the first Indian feature film.

## The Journey Ahead

After 'Raja Harishchandra', Phalke made **'Mohini Bhasmasur'**, in which **Durgabai Kamat** and **Kamlabai Gokhale** acted — they are regarded as the first women to act in Indian cinema. **'Lanka Dahan'** of **1917** was a great success; in it Anna Salunke played both **Rama and Sita**. On **1 January 1918** the **Hindustan Cinema Films Company** was formed, under which films such as 'Shri Krishna Janma' (1918) and **'Kaliya Mardan'** (1919) came out.

Later, differences with partners, changing technology and financial difficulties troubled Phalke. Between 1913 and 1937 he made about **94 feature films** and **27 short films**. **'Gangavataran'** of **1937** was his only talkie. In the era of sound cinema he was left behind, and he died at **Nashik** on **16 February 1944**.

## Legacy

Dadasaheb Phalke is called the **"Father of Indian Cinema"**. He not only made the first feature film but also showed that Indian stories, Indian artists and Indian technicians together could take up a new art like cinema. He used trick photography and special effects to bring mythological miracles to the screen, which is still one of the hallmarks of Indian cinema.

In his honour the Government of India instituted the **Dadasaheb Phalke Award** in **1969**, the highest honour in Indian cinema; the first person to receive it was the actress **Devika Rani**. In **1971** a postage stamp was issued on him. The cinema whose foundation Phalke laid with one camera and the help of his family is today counted among the largest film industries in the world.
