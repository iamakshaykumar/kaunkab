## Early Life: Hyder Ali's Successor

Tipu Sultan was born on 1 December 1751 at **Devanahalli** near Bengaluru. His father **Hyder Ali** had risen from an ordinary officer in the army of the Mysore kingdom to become its real ruler. Hyder had his son taught horse riding, marksmanship and swordsmanship, as well as several languages and religious and philosophical subjects.

From his teens Tipu began joining his father's military campaigns. During the Second Anglo-Mysore War, in the battle of **Pollilur** in 1780, he inflicted a crushing defeat on the British detachment of Colonel William Baillie — counted among the worst defeats of the Company in India.

## Taking Power and the Treaty of Mangalore

In December 1782 Hyder Ali died in the middle of the war and Tipu became ruler of Mysore. He continued the war, and finally the Second Anglo-Mysore War ended with the **Treaty of Mangalore of 1784**. In this treaty both sides returned each other's conquered territories — that is, the Company could not bring Tipu to his knees.

As ruler Tipu made many new experiments:
- He introduced a new currency, a new calendar and a new system of weights and measures.
- He trained the army in the European manner and stressed the manufacture of weapons.
- He sent envoys to France and other countries to seek support against the British.
- He promoted silk production (sericulture) and trade.

## The Third Anglo-Mysore War (1790-1792)

In 1789 Tipu attacked Travancore, an ally of the British. In response, Governor-General **Lord Cornwallis**, together with the Marathas and the Nizam of Hyderabad, marched against Mysore. In 1791 the British captured Bengaluru and in 1792 the capital Srirangapatna was besieged.

The **Treaty of Srirangapatna of 1792** was very harsh on Tipu — he had to hand over about half his kingdom (including Malabar) to the victors, pay a heavy war indemnity and keep two of his sons as hostages with the British as security.

## The Fourth War and 4 May 1799

The new Governor-General **Lord Wellesley** regarded Tipu as a possible ally of France and wanted to finish him completely. In 1799 British armies, with the help of the Nizam, attacked Mysore from several directions and besieged Srirangapatna.

On 4 May 1799 the British army breached the fort wall and stormed in. Tipu chose to fight rather than surrender and was killed fighting near one of the gates of the fort. The next day he was buried in the **Gumbaz** at Srirangapatna, near the grave of his father Hyder Ali.

## Consequences and Legacy

After Tipu's death the British gave the throne of Mysore to a child of the old **Wodeyar dynasty** and imposed a subsidiary alliance on the state. Large parts of the kingdom were divided between the British and the Nizam. The greatest power challenging the Company in South India was gone.

The assessment of Tipu Sultan is still debated among historians today — some regard him as a warrior who stood firm against a colonial power and an experimenter with modernity, while others criticise the harshness of some of his military campaigns. But there is no doubt that in his time he was one of the British's most difficult opponents.
