## From Porbandar to London

**Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi** was born on **2 October 1869** at **Porbandar** in Kathiawar. His father Karamchand Gandhi was the diwan of the princely state, and his mother Putlibai was a woman of deep religious faith, whose vows and fasts influenced Gandhi all his life. At the age of thirteen he was married to **Kasturba**.

In September 1888 he went to **London** to study law, and in June 1891 became a barrister from the Inner Temple. His legal practice in India did not do well, so in 1893 he went to South Africa in connection with a merchant's lawsuit.

## South Africa: The Laboratory of Satyagraha

About 21 years spent in South Africa shaped Gandhi. There, the humiliation of racial discrimination, the fight for the rights of Indians, and the **satyagraha** that began in 1906 made him a mass leader. In this period he wrote **"Hind Swaraj"** (1909), which contains a critique of modern machine civilisation and a vision of true swaraj. (For more, see "Gandhi in South Africa".)

## The Idea of Truth and Non-violence

For Gandhi, **satyagraha** meant standing firm on truth while resisting injustice non-violently, and trying to change the opponent's heart by suffering, not by hatred. He stressed the purity of both means and ends. Simple living, fasting, celibacy, prayer and self-discipline were part of his public life. His autobiography **"The Story of My Experiments with Truth"** is an account of these experiments.

## Major Movements in India

On **9 January 1915** Gandhi returned to India and, on Gokhale's advice, travelled the country for a year to understand it. The main milestones after that, in brief:

- **1917–18:** Local satyagrahas at Champaran (indigo farmers), Kheda (land revenue) and Ahmedabad (mill workers).
- **1920–22:** The **Non-Cooperation Movement** — after the violence at Chauri Chaura in February 1922, Gandhi called it off; in March 1922 he was arrested and sentenced.
- **1930:** The **Dandi March** from 12 March to 6 April, and the Civil Disobedience Movement by breaking the salt law.
- **1931:** The sole Congress representative at the Second Round Table Conference in London.
- **1942:** The **Quit India Movement** and the call of "Do or Die", followed by long detention; during this time Kasturba died in 1944.

## The Constructive Programme

Gandhi believed that swaraj would come not merely from the British leaving, but from change within society. In 1941, in a booklet called **"Constructive Programme"**, he set out its outline — **khadi and the charkha**, village industries, **the end of untouchability**, communal unity, prohibition of liquor, basic (Nai Talim) education, equality for women, sanitation and organising farmers and workers. In 1932 he founded the **Harijan Sevak Sangh** and brought out the paper "Harijan". The Sabarmati ashram and later the **Sevagram** ashram near Wardha became centres of these experiments.

## Debates, Partition and the Last Days

There were also several serious debates about Gandhi. **Dr. Ambedkar** held that Gandhi did not strike at the root of the caste system, and that in the Poona Pact of 1932 the Dalits had to give up separate electorates. The decision to call off the movement after Chauri Chaura was criticised even at the time. Historians have considered some of his remarks about Africans in his early years in South Africa to reflect racial prejudice. He also had differences with the revolutionary current and later with Subhas Bose.

Gandhi was deeply grieved by the Partition and riots that came with independence in 1947. He walked through Noakhali on foot and fasted for peace in Calcutta and Delhi. On the evening of **30 January 1948**, as he was walking to a prayer meeting at Birla House in Delhi, **Nathuram Godse** shot and killed him. The title "Mahatma" is usually linked to Rabindranath Tagore, and today movements of non-violent resistance around the world regard him as a source of inspiration.
