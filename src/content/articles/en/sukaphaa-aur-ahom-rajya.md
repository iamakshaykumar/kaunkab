## A Prince from Mong Mao

**Sukaphaa** (Chaolung Sukaphaa) was a **Tai** prince connected with the Tai kingdom called **Mong Mao** — this region lies near the Yunnan province of China and the border of Myanmar today. According to the Ahom chronicles, in **1215 CE** he set out westward with thousands of people, a few hundred horses and elephants. Tradition gives a succession dispute as the reason for his departure.

## Crossing the Patkai to the Brahmaputra Valley

The journey lasted several years. Passing through the upper Irrawaddy valley, Sukaphaa's party reached the **Patkai Hills** and crossed them through a mountain pass. In **1228 CE** they reached the **Namrup** region of the Brahmaputra valley. This year is considered the year of the founding of the Ahom kingdom.

After this, for several years they settled and moved on at different places in the valley, and finally, around **1253 CE**, made **Charaideo** (near present-day Sivasagar) the capital. Later the capitals changed many times, but Charaideo always remained the symbolic and sacred centre of the Ahom dynasty. Sukaphaa died in **1268 CE** and his son **Sutephaa** became king.

## A Kingdom Built Through Blending

Sukaphaa's biggest success was not only military victory. He formed marriage ties with local communities such as the **Moran** and **Borahi**, encouraged his companions to mix with the local people, and took into Ahom society those who adopted the Ahom system of farming and administration. The Ahoms had brought with them advanced techniques of wet-rice cultivation, which strengthened the economy of the valley.

In time the Ahom rulers also adopted the Assamese language and local religions. This very blending later shaped a shared **Assamese** identity, which is why Sukaphaa is remembered in Assam as the builder of 'Bor Asom' (Greater Assam).

## Buranjis: The Written History of the Ahoms

A special contribution of the Ahoms is the **Buranjis** — chronicles written at the court, first in the Tai-Ahom language and later in Assamese. They give dated accounts of kings, wars, envoys and events. These Buranjis are the main basis of our information about Sukaphaa's journey. Such a systematic tradition of historical writing is found in very few kingdoms of medieval India.

## A Dynasty of Six Hundred Years

The kingdom established by Sukaphaa lasted from **1228 to 1826**, that is, about six hundred years. During this time the Ahoms faced external invasions many times, of which the Mughal campaigns of the seventeenth century were the main ones (see: the Battle of Saraighat). In the early nineteenth century the kingdom was weakened by Burmese invasions, and after the Treaty of Yandabo in 1826, Assam passed under British control.

## The Moidams of Charaideo

At Charaideo the Ahom kings and members of the royal family were buried in **moidams** — high earthen mounds with vaulted chambers of brick or stone inside. Reverence for ancestors in Tai tradition is the basis for them. On **26 July 2024**, at the 46th session of the World Heritage Committee held in New Delhi, "Moidams — the Mound-Burial System of the Ahom Dynasty" was added to the **UNESCO World Heritage** List. This is Assam's third World Heritage Site — and it brings the memory of the capital founded by Sukaphaa before the world.
