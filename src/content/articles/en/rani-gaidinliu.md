## Childhood and the Heraka Movement

**Gaidinliu** was born in 1915 in a Rongmei Naga family in the village of Longkao (Nungkao) in the Tamenglong region of Manipur. Her date of birth is generally taken to be **26 January 1915**, although some sources give a different date. She received no formal schooling.

At about the age of 13 (1927) she joined the movement of her cousin **Haipou Jadonang**. Jadonang was reforming and giving a new form to the traditional faith of the Zeliangrong (Zeme, Liangmai and Rongmei) Nagas, which later came to be called **Heraka**. The movement was an attempt to save the traditional religion and identity amid the growing influence of Christian missionaries, and it also held out the dream of a 'Naga Raj' against British rule.

## Leadership After Jadonang

The British arrested Jadonang and hanged him at Imphal on **29 August 1931**. After this, Gaidinliu, only 16 years old, became the spiritual and political successor of the movement. She told the people not to pay taxes to the British and not to cooperate with them. Her followers believed her to have divine power.

The British administration deployed Assam Rifles detachments to catch her and announced a reward. Gaidinliu kept moving through the villages of Manipur, the Naga Hills and North Cachar, and there were clashes with supporters too.

## Arrest and Long Imprisonment

On **17 October 1932** the Assam Rifles made a sudden raid and arrested her in the village of **Pulomi**. She was tried at Imphal and sentenced to **life imprisonment**. She spent the next nearly 14 years in the jails of Guwahati, Shillong, Aizawl and Tura.

## Nehru and the Title 'Rani'

In 1937 **Jawaharlal Nehru** met her in Shillong jail. Moved by her story, Nehru called her 'Daughter of the Hills' and gave her the title **'Rani'**; from then on she became known as 'Rani Gaidinliu'. Nehru made efforts for her release, but the British government did not agree. She was released only after India's independence in 1947.

## In Independent India and Legacy

After independence Gaidinliu continued to work for the welfare of her people and for the Heraka faith. She opposed the Naga nationalist groups that demanded separation from India, and demanded a separate administrative unit within India for the Zeliangrong people. In 1960 she went underground, and in 1966 she came out and returned to the peaceful path.

She received the Freedom Fighter's Tamra Patra in 1972 and the **Padma Bhushan** in 1982. She died at Longkao on 17 February 1993. Views on her legacy differ: in India she is honoured as a leading freedom fighter of the Northeast, while in the Christian-majority Naga society and among Naga nationalist groups there has been a mixed feeling about her because of her stance towards Christianity and her closeness to the Government of India.
