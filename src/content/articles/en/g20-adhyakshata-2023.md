## What Is the G20

The **G20 (Group of Twenty)** is a forum of the world's major economies, which began in **1999** as a meeting of finance ministers after the Asian financial crisis. After the global recession of **2008**, its summits began to be held at the level of heads of state and government. Together its members represent about 85 per cent of the world economy and two-thirds of its population. It has no permanent secretariat; each year one member holds the presidency and sets that year's agenda.

## India's Presidency

After the **Bali** summit in Indonesia (November 2022), India took over the G20 presidency on **1 December 2022**, which lasted until **30 November 2023**. Under Prime Minister **Narendra Modi**, India made its theme **"Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam — One Earth, One Family, One Future"**, based on a saying from the Maha Upanishad. The presidency's logo showed the Earth on a lotus. India's **sherpa** (chief negotiator) was **Amitabh Kant**.

According to the government, India did not confine the presidency to Delhi alone — **more than 200 meetings** were held in about **60 cities** across the country, the widest geographical spread of any G20 presidency so far. In January 2023 India also held a virtual summit called **"Voice of the Global South"**, so that the concerns of developing countries could reach the G20 agenda. India's priorities included green development and climate finance, inclusive growth, progress on the Sustainable Development Goals, **digital public infrastructure**, reform of multilateral institutions and women-led development.

## The New Delhi Summit

On **9–10 September 2023** the 18th G20 summit was held at the newly built **Bharat Mandapam** convention centre at Pragati Maidan — the first G20 summit to be held in India. China's President **Xi Jinping** and Russia's President **Vladimir Putin** did not attend; in their place China's Premier **Li Qiang** and Russian Foreign Minister **Sergey Lavrov** took part.

In the very first session Prime Minister Modi announced that the **African Union** was now a **permanent member** of the G20. The then chairperson of the African Union, Comoros President **Azali Assoumani**, took his place among the members. India had long advocated this.

## The New Delhi Declaration and Other Outcomes

Before the summit the biggest question was whether any joint declaration could be agreed amid the differences between Western countries and Russia-China over the war in Ukraine. On the very first day the **New Delhi Leaders' Declaration** was adopted by consensus. Referring to the war in Ukraine, it called on all countries to respect territorial integrity and sovereignty and described the use or threat of use of nuclear weapons as inadmissible, but it did not condemn Russia by name. Some Western analysts and Ukraine called this language softer than the previous year's Bali declaration, while India called it a successful consensus reached in difficult circumstances.

Some other major steps were also taken on this occasion:

- The **Global Biofuels Alliance** was launched.
- In the presence of several leaders, including US President **Joe Biden**, the **India–Middle East–Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC)** was announced — a plan to link India to Europe through the Gulf countries by rail and sea routes.
- Agreement was reached on reform of multilateral development banks, climate finance and digital public infrastructure.

On **10 September** the leaders paid tribute to Mahatma Gandhi at **Rajghat**. At the close, the symbolic gavel of the next presidency was handed to Brazil's President **Lula da Silva**. At the end of its presidency India also held a virtual leaders' summit on **22 November 2023**.

## Criticism and Significance

The event also drew some criticism. The opposition and some citizens' groups questioned the heavy expenditure, the roughly three-day shutdown in Delhi for security, and the removal of slums in the name of beautification; critics said the event was also used for domestic political publicity. The government said it strengthened India's global image and diplomatic role.

Overall, India's G20 presidency is remembered as a platform that raised the voice of the developing countries — the "Global South". The African Union's permanent membership changed the shape of the G20, and securing a consensus declaration in a difficult geopolitical climate was regarded as a notable achievement of Indian diplomacy.
