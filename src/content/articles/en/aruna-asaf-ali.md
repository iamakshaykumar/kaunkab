## Aruna Ganguly of Kalka

**Aruna Asaf Ali** was born on **16 July 1909** in **Kalka** (then Punjab, today Haryana) into a Bengali Brahmin family. Her original name was **Aruna Ganguly**. She studied at the Sacred Heart Convent in Lahore and All Saints' College in Nainital, and then worked as a teacher at the Gokhale Memorial School in Calcutta.

In **1928** she married the Congress leader and lawyer **Asaf Ali**. Asaf Ali was a Muslim and more than 20 years older than her, so her family opposed the marriage. But Aruna stood firm in her decision. After the marriage she became an active member of the Congress.

## Prison and Fighting Spirit

During the **Salt Satyagraha** she took part in processions and was arrested at about the age of 21. After the Gandhi–Irwin Pact of 1931, when most political prisoners were freed, she was still not released; she was let out only after a public agitation and pressure. In **1932**, in **Tihar Jail**, she went on a **hunger strike** to protest against the ill-treatment of prisoners. Conditions improved somewhat after this, but she was taken to Ambala and kept in solitary confinement. These experiences made her even more resolute.

## 9 August 1942: Gowalia Tank Maidan

On **8 August 1942**, at Bombay's **Gowalia Tank Maidan**, the All India Congress Committee passed the **"Quit India"** resolution and Gandhiji gave the slogan **"Do or Die"**. The next morning, **9 August**, the British government arrested almost all the senior Congress leaders, including Gandhiji, Nehru, Patel and Azad. The government believed that without its leadership the movement would fall apart.

That same morning, 33-year-old **Aruna Asaf Ali** reached the ground, where a huge crowd had gathered. In the presence of the police she **hoisted the Congress tricolour flag**, presided over the meeting and announced the start of the movement. The police fired on the gathering and dispersed the crowd, but the scene became a signal to people across the country that the movement would not stop despite the arrest of its leaders.

Today this same ground is called **August Kranti Maidan**, and it has a memorial to the movement of 1942.

## Life Underground

After hoisting the flag, Aruna Asaf Ali went **underground**. Together with socialist leaders such as **Jayaprakash Narayan**, **Ram Manohar Lohia** and **Achyut Patwardhan**, she ran the movement in secret. With Lohia she edited the Congress monthly magazine **"Inquilab"**, through which messages were passed on to the activists.

The government announced a **reward of 5,000 rupees** for her arrest and **seized her property** and sold it. Still she was not caught. During this time she also fell ill, and Gandhiji sent her a handwritten letter advising her to come out of hiding, but she did not surrender. Only in **1946**, when the warrant against her was withdrawn, did she appear in public. Because of this courage she came to be called the **"Queen of the 1942 movement"**.

Even after coming out, her spirit did not soften. When the **naval mutiny** broke out in Bombay in **February 1946**, Aruna Asaf Ali openly showed sympathy for the rebel sailors, while the senior leaders of the Congress and the Muslim League were advising the sailors to lay down their arms. She believed that Hindus and Muslims could become united only by standing together in the struggle against British rule. This stance shows that, even while within the Congress, she leaned towards more revolutionary and socialist ideas.

## After Independence and Legacy

After independence she was for some time in the Socialist Party, and in the early 1950s she joined the **Communist Party of India**, which she left in **1956**. In **1954** she played a role in forming the **National Federation of Indian Women (NFIW)**. In **1958** she was elected **the first Mayor of Delhi**. She also took part in founding the **Link** publishing house and the daily newspaper **Patriot**.

She received the **International Lenin Peace Prize** in **1964**, the **Jawaharlal Nehru Award** in **1991** and the **Padma Vibhushan** in **1992**. She died in New Delhi on **29 July 1996** at the age of 87. In **1997** she was posthumously given the country's highest civilian honour, the **Bharat Ratna**.

Aruna Asaf Ali's story shows that the Quit India Movement was not a movement of senior leaders alone. When the leaders were locked up in jails, brave activists like her kept the movement alive — and that flag fluttering at Gowalia Tank Maidan became the symbol of that spirit.
