## Background

In 1921 the archaeologist **Daya Ram Sahni** began excavations at Harappa in Punjab (now in Pakistan). The very next year **Rakhal Das Banerji** discovered the remains of Mohenjo-daro in Sindh. These discoveries showed that the history of India is far older than had been believed. Because the first discovery was at Harappa, it is also called the **Harappan Civilisation**.

This civilisation had a very wide spread — across present-day Pakistan, Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Gujarat and western Uttar Pradesh. Its most developed phase is considered to be between about 2600 and 1900 BCE.

## Planned Cities

The cities of the Indus Valley are known for their planning:

- Streets often crossed one another at right angles.
- Houses were built of baked bricks, and the size of the bricks was almost the same in many cities.
- Water from houses passed through covered drains into larger drains.
- The **Great Bath** of Mohenjo-daro is a large brick-built tank, which was probably used for bathing on special occasions.

## Trade and Life

The people here practised farming, animal herding and crafts. Beads, pottery, and copper and bronze objects were made with great skill. Small stone **seals** have been found, engraved with pictures of animals and a script. These seals were probably used for identification in trade. Evidence of trade with Mesopotamia (present-day Iraq) has also been found.

The **Indus script** has still not been deciphered. For this reason much of what we know about the rulers, language and beliefs of this civilisation is only guesswork.

## Decline

After about 1900 BCE the big cities slowly began to be abandoned. Scholars disagree about the reasons — many causes are suggested, such as rivers changing course, climate change, floods and a decline in trade. Probably it was not one single cause, but several causes together that were responsible.

## Significance

The Indus Valley Civilisation shows that even four and a half thousand years ago, people on this land lived in well-planned cities. **Dholavira** in Gujarat was added to the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2021.
