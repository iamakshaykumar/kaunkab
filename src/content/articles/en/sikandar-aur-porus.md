## Background: Alexander's Campaign to the East

**Alexander**, the king of Macedon (north of Greece), began his campaign of conquest in Asia in 334 BCE. Within a few years he conquered the vast Persian (Achaemenid) empire. The eastern edge of the Persian empire stretched to around the Indus River, so Alexander's eyes fell next on India.

At that time north-western India was divided into many small kingdoms and republics (ganas), which often fought among themselves. In about 327 BCE Alexander crossed the Hindu Kush and reached this region.

## The Surrender of Taxila

**Ambhi**, the king of **Taxila**, had an old rivalry with his neighbour, King Porus. Instead of resisting Alexander, he welcomed him and helped him. After this Alexander faced **Porus**, the powerful king of the region between the Jhelum and Chenab rivers. Historians believe that "Porus" is probably the Greek form of the name "Puru" or "Paurava".

## The Battle of the Jhelum (326 BCE)

Porus took position on the far bank of the Jhelum (Greek name Hydaspes) with his army, chariots and war elephants. Because of the rains the river was in flood, so crossing it directly was difficult.

- For several days Alexander kept Porus confused by moving his troops back and forth along the riverbank.
- Then, on a stormy night, he moved some distance upstream with his chosen troops and quietly crossed the river.
- Porus's son was sent to stop Alexander but was killed in this skirmish.
- In the main battle, Porus's elephants inflicted heavy losses on the Macedonian soldiers, but when they were surrounded and wounded, the elephants went out of control and began trampling their own army.

Greek sources give differing figures for the size of the two armies, so the exact numbers are not certain. In the end Porus was wounded and defeated.

## The Meeting of Porus and Alexander

According to the Greek historian **Arrian**, when the captive Porus was brought before Alexander and asked how he should be treated, Porus said that he should be treated like a king. Alexander was impressed by his bravery. He returned Porus's kingdom to him and also handed over some neighbouring territories to him. In this way Porus became an allied ruler under Alexander.

## The Army's Refusal at the Beas and the Return

Alexander advanced to the **Beas** (Hyphasis) River. But his army, tired from years of continuous war, distance from home, the monsoon rains and news of the powerful kingdoms of the Ganga valley (the Nanda empire) ahead, flatly refused to go further. Alexander was forced to turn back. He went south along the Indus, fought several ganas on the way (such as the Malavas), and died in Babylon in 323 BCE.

## Significance

Alexander stayed in India for only about two years and no lasting rule of his was established here. Even so, this invasion had some far-reaching results:
- Land and sea routes opened between India and the Greek world, which later increased trade and cultural contact.
- With the small kingdoms of the north-west weakened, the path to the unification of this region became easier. Only a few years later **Chandragupta Maurya** removed the Greek satraps from here and laid the foundation of the great Mauryan empire.
- The accounts of Greek writers give valuable information about India of that time, and their dates help in fixing the chronology of Indian history.
