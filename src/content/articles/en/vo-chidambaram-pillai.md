## The Lawyer of Ottapidaram

**Valliappan Olaganathan Chidambaram Pillai** — usually called **V. O. C.** — was born on **5 September 1872** at **Ottapidaram** in Tirunelveli district of the Madras Presidency. Chidambaram studied in Tuticorin and Tirunelveli, then studied law at Tiruchirappalli, passed the pleadership examination in **1894** and began practising law at Ottapidaram.

At that time the **Swadeshi movement** was spreading across the country in protest against the **Partition of Bengal (1905)**. People were boycotting foreign goods and there was talk of building up Indian industries. Chidambaram Pillai was deeply influenced by Bal Gangadhar Tilak and the ideas of the Extremists. He realised that swadeshi could be given concrete shape not by speeches alone, but by directly challenging the economic monopoly of the British.

## The Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company

Sea trade and passenger traffic from the port of **Tuticorin** in south India to **Colombo** in Sri Lanka was almost a monopoly of the British **British India Steam Navigation Company**. To break it, Chidambaram Pillai registered the **Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company** on **16 October 1906**. Its capital was set at **ten lakh rupees**, divided into **40,000 shares** of **25 rupees** each. Traders, landholders and ordinary people bought shares, so that it would truly be a company "of the country".

The company's first ship, the **S.S. Gallia**, was bought from France and reached Tuticorin in **1907**. It could carry about 1,300 passengers and 40,000 bags of cargo, and it flew a flag inscribed "Vande Mataram". Later another French ship, the **S.S. Lavo**, joined the fleet. When the Indian ships began sailing on the Tuticorin–Colombo route, the British company cut its fare to one rupee. In reply the swadeshi company cut its fare to half a rupee. The rival company then began handing out free umbrellas to passengers. Even so, nationalist traders and passengers kept supporting the swadeshi ships. This rivalry became a symbol not only of trade but of political struggle, because an Indian company was standing up to a powerful company of the Empire.

## The Coral Mill Strike and the Arrest

Chidambaram Pillai also came forward for workers' rights. On **23 February 1908** he called on the workers of the British-owned **Coral Mill** in Tuticorin to raise their voice against low wages and harsh conditions. Within a few days a strike began, which he led together with his colleague **Subramania Siva**. After about nine days the management had to agree to raise wages, reduce working hours and give Sunday as a holiday. It is counted among the early organised labour movements of south India.

The government was alarmed by these activities. On **12 March 1908** Chidambaram Pillai and Subramania Siva were arrested. As soon as the news spread, riots broke out in **Tirunelveli** and there was a general strike in Tuticorin; four people were killed in police firing. Some historians count this Tuticorin strike among the first political strikes in India.

## The Sedition Trial and Suffering in Jail

Chidambaram Pillai was tried for **sedition**. The trial court sentenced him to **two life terms**, which in practice meant about **40 years** of imprisonment. Such a harsh sentence was criticised across the country. On appeal the **Madras High Court** reduced it to four years' imprisonment and six years' exile, and later, at the Privy Council stage, the sentence was reduced somewhat further.

From **9 July 1908** to **1 December 1910** he was held in **Coimbatore Central Prison**, and then sent to the **Kannanur** jail. In jail he was treated not as a political prisoner but as an ordinary criminal. He was made to do hard labour such as pulling an **oil press** in place of a bullock, which ruined his health. While he was in jail the company collapsed under economic pressure — it was liquidated in **1911**, and one ship was even sold to the very British company that had been its rival. Chidambaram Pillai was released on **12 December 1912**.

In the same period, on **17 June 1911**, the Collector of Tirunelveli, **Robert Ashe**, was shot dead at Maniyachi Junction by a young man named **Vanchinathan**. At the trial it emerged that Vanchinathan held Ashe responsible for crushing the swadeshi shipping company, and this event shows how much anger the repression of 1908 had created in the region.

## Later Life and Legacy

After his release Chidambaram Pillai was not allowed to return to Tirunelveli district, and his licence to practise law had also been taken away. He moved to **Madras** and ran a provisions store and a kerosene store to make a living. He worked in labour unions, left the Congress in 1920 over ideological differences, and later, having regained his licence, practised law at Kovilpatti.

He was also a fine writer in Tamil. He wrote books such as **'Meyyaram'** (1914) and **'Meyyarivu'** (1915), wrote a commentary on the **Tirukkural**, and also wrote his autobiography. The poet **Subramania Bharati** was a close associate of his. He spent the last years of his life in poverty in Tuticorin and died there on **18 November 1936**.

After his death he came to be remembered as **"Kappalottiya Thamizhan"** — the Tamil who sailed the ship. In **1961** a film of the same name was made, starring Sivaji Ganesan. On **5 September 1972**, his birth centenary, a postage stamp was issued, and the Tuticorin port today is named the **V. O. Chidambaranar Port Authority**. His story shows that the swadeshi movement was not only about bonfires of foreign cloth, but also a bold attempt by Indians to build their own companies and industries — a price that many paid with their freedom and their health.
