## Childhood and Education

**Rajendra Prasad** was born on **3 December 1884** in the village of **Ziradei** in what is today Siwan district of Bihar. His father **Mahadev Sahay** was a scholar of Sanskrit and Persian, and his mother **Kamleshwari Devi** used to tell him stories from the Ramayana and Mahabharata. According to the custom of the time he was married to **Rajvanshi Devi** at the age of only 12, in June 1896.

After early schooling at Chhapra and Patna he stood first in the entrance examination of **Calcutta University** and in 1902 entered **Presidency College, Calcutta**. He did an M.A. in economics and then studied law; in 1915 he won a gold medal in the master's examination in law, and later took a doctorate in law from Allahabad University. He also played a large role in founding the **Bihari Students' Conference** at Patna College in 1906.

## From the Bar to the Movement

Rajendra Prasad taught for a short time at Muzaffarpur and Calcutta, then in 1916 began practising law at the Patna High Court. At the Lucknow Congress session of 1916 he met **Mahatma Gandhi**. In **1917**, when Gandhiji came to **Champaran** to hear the complaints of indigo farmers, Rajendra Prasad became his associate along with his colleagues. Gandhiji's simplicity and sincerity impressed him deeply.

When the **Non-Cooperation Movement** began in 1920 he gave up his flourishing legal practice. He helped in founding the **Bihar Vidyapith** and sent his son to study there too. He wrote for and raised funds for newspapers such as 'Searchlight' and 'Desh'. He went to jail during the **Salt Satyagraha (1930)** and the **Quit India Movement (1942)**; in 1942 he was arrested from **Sadaqat Ashram** in Patna and sent to **Bankipore Jail**, from where he was released nearly three years later, on 15 June 1945. It was in jail that he wrote his **'Autobiography'**.

## Relief Work and Congress Leadership

A terrible earthquake struck Bihar on **15 January 1934**. He was in jail at the time; as soon as he was released he formed the **Bihar Central Relief Committee** and raised funds and aid for the victims. After the Quetta earthquake of 1935 too he formed a relief committee.

He was **Congress president three times** — at the Bombay session of October 1934, in 1939 after Subhas Chandra Bose's resignation, and in November 1947 when J. B. Kripalani stepped down. In the interim government of September 1946 he was given the **Food and Agriculture** portfolio.

## President of the Constituent Assembly

On **11 December 1946** Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent president of the **Constituent Assembly**. In the debates that lasted about three years he conducted the proceedings of the Assembly with patience and impartiality. The work of drafting the Constitution was done by Dr. Ambedkar's Drafting Committee, but the responsibility of holding the whole Assembly together rested with Rajendra Prasad as president. On 24 January 1950 the Assembly elected him the first President of India.

## The First President (1950–1962)

On **26 January 1950** the Republic of India came into being and Rajendra Prasad took office as the **first President**. A touching episode is that on the night of 25 January his elder sister **Bhagwati Devi** died, yet he completed the Republic Day ceremony the next day and only afterwards went to the funeral.

Winning the elections of **1952** and **1957**, he remained President for about 12 years — the longest term to date. He set the tradition of keeping the office of President above party politics. Even so, his differences with Prime Minister **Nehru** on some issues were public, especially over the **Hindu Code Bill**, to some of whose provisions and the manner of bringing it he objected. Historians also see this as an early debate on the constitutional relationship between the President and the Cabinet. Between 1958 and 1960 he made state visits to Japan, the Soviet Union, Sri Lanka (then Ceylon) and several countries of South-East Asia.

## The Last Years

After completing his term on **13 May 1962** he returned to Patna the next day. In this same year he was honoured with the country's highest civilian award, the **Bharat Ratna**. His wife died in September 1962. He spent his last days at **Sadaqat Ashram** in Patna, where he died on **28 February 1963**. The **Rajendra Smriti Sangrahalaya** (museum) in Patna is dedicated to his memory. 'India Divided', 'Satyagraha at Champaran' and 'At the Feet of Mahatma Gandhi' (Bapu ke Kadmon Mein) are among his major works. Because of his simplicity, humility and scholarship people affectionately called him **"Rajendra Babu"**.
