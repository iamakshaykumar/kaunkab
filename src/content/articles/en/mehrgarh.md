## A Village Near the Bolan Pass

Mehrgarh lies in the **Balochistan** province of present-day Pakistan, on the **Kachi Plain**. It is near the **Bolan Pass**, west of the Indus River, between Quetta, Kalat and Sibi. The Bolan Pass has long been the old route linking hilly Balochistan with the Indus plains.

The site spreads over about 495 acres and has six mounds. In 1974 a French archaeological team led by **Jean-Francois Jarrige** and **Catherine Jarrige** discovered it. Excavations were carried out here from 1974 to 1986 and again from 1997 to 2000.

## How Old — The Debate over the Date

Jarrige placed the oldest phase of Mehrgarh **before 7000 BCE**, and this is the date most often found in textbooks. But later some scholars (Meadow and Zazzo) re-analysed the radiocarbon dates and placed the first phase at about **5250-4650 BCE**. So "7000 BCE" should be understood as a traditional, debated date. Even so, by either reckoning Mehrgarh remains among the oldest farming villages in South Asia.

## Farming, Herding and Houses

- **Crops:** The people here mainly grew **six-row barley**; evidence of einkorn and emmer wheat, jujube and dates has also been found.
- **Animals:** Sheep, goats and cattle were kept. Many scholars believe that humped **zebu** cattle were domesticated locally here.
- **Houses:** People lived in houses of **mud (sun-dried) bricks** and built separate granaries to store grain.
- **Crafts:** The earliest phase had no pottery; pottery begins to appear from the second phase, and the use of the potter's wheel is seen from the third phase. In later phases, pots with designs like a peepal leaf were also found.

## Dental Treatment and Graves

Skeletons found in Mehrgarh's cemetery surprised scientists. A study published in *Nature* in 2006 reported that very fine holes had been drilled in eleven molars of nine adults, dated to about 7,500-9,000 years ago. According to the researchers, this is the oldest known evidence of drilling into the teeth of living people — that is, a kind of primitive dentistry.

## Gradual Change

Over thousands of years Mehrgarh grew from a small village into a settlement using copper (Chalcolithic). In its later phases, features appear in its art and pottery that are later found in the Harappan culture. Around 2600 BCE this settlement was largely abandoned and people moved to the nearby fortified settlement of **Nausharo**, which flourished later in the period of the Indus Civilisation.

## Significance

Before the discovery of Mehrgarh it was thought that the urban civilisation of the Indus Valley emerged relatively suddenly, perhaps through outside influence. Mehrgarh showed that this region had a long local tradition of farming, herding and village life, on whose foundation cities like Harappa and Mohenjo-daro were later built. For this reason it was also placed on UNESCO's Tentative World Heritage List in 2004.
