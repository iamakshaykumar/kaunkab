## Why the Capital Came to Delhi

Until the time of the Mughal emperor **Shah Jahan** (reigned 1628–1658), Agra was the main capital of the empire. But for the growing court, army and trade, the fort and city of Agra were becoming cramped. Shah Jahan decided to found a new royal city on the western bank of the Yamuna, north of the settlements of old Delhi. The city was named **Shahjahanabad** after him — today this area is called "Old Delhi".

## Construction: 1639 to 1648

Construction of the fort began in **1639** (some sources say it began in 1638) and was completed in **1648**. Credit for its design is usually given to **Ustad Ahmad Lahori**, whose name is also linked to the building of the Taj Mahal. Because of its high ramparts of red sandstone it came to be called the "Red Fort", while the Mughals called it **Qila-e-Mubarak**.

The fort has two main gates — the **Lahori Gate**, which faces the direction of Lahore, and the **Delhi Gate**. On entering through the Lahori Gate one comes to a covered market called Chhatta Chowk.

## The Palaces and Halls Inside

- **Diwan-i-Aam** — the hall for the general public, where the emperor listened to petitions from a balcony window (jharokha).
- **Diwan-i-Khas** — the marble chamber for meeting nobles and special guests. The famous **Peacock Throne** (Takht-e-Taus) was kept here.
- **Rang Mahal** and **Khas Mahal** — royal residences, through which the **Nahr-e-Bihisht** flowed.
- **Moti Masjid** — this was built later (about 1659) by Aurangzeb for his private prayers.

## Shahjahanabad: The City Outside the Fort

The city grew up to the west of the fort and was enclosed by a stone wall with several gates. **Chandni Chowk** was its main market, and on a nearby hill Shah Jahan built the **Jama Masjid**. The mansions of nobles, bazaars and caravanserais made this city one of the great cities of seventeenth-century Asia. The historian Stephen Blake has studied this city as a "sovereign city", in which the emperor's palace was the centre of the whole city.

## Ups and Downs: From Nader Shah to 1857

In the eighteenth century Mughal power weakened and the fort was plundered several times. In **1739** **Nader Shah** of Iran attacked Delhi and looted the fort's treasury and the Peacock Throne. Later the Marathas, Rohillas and the British also interfered here.

In the revolt of **1857**, the rebel soldiers declared the aged emperor **Bahadur Shah Zafar** their leader. In September 1857 the British recaptured Delhi; Bahadur Shah was tried in the fort itself and in 1858 he was exiled to Rangoon. After this the British demolished many buildings inside the fort and built military barracks there.

## A Symbol of Independent India

On **15 August 1947**, Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru hoisted the tricolour above the Lahori Gate. Since then, every Independence Day the Prime Minister hoists the national flag from here and addresses the nation. In **2007** the "Red Fort Complex" was included in the UNESCO World Heritage list. The fort tells the story of the peak of Mughal architecture, the decline of the empire and independence — all three together.
