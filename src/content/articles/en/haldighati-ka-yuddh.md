## Background: The Unbroken Pride of Mewar

In the latter half of the 16th century, the Mughal emperor Akbar had brought most of the kingdoms of North India under his rule. But in Rajasthan, the Sisodia dynasty of **Mewar** was not ready to compromise on its sovereignty and independence.

**Maharana Pratap**, who took the throne at Gogunda in 1572, made it clear that he could give up the comforts of palaces but would never bow his head in the Mughal court. Akbar sent Jalal Khan, Man Singh, Bhagwan Das and Todar Mal as peace envoys four times, but the Maharana rejected any settlement that cost him his freedom. As a result, war became inevitable.

## 18 June 1576: Rakt Talai and Haldighati

The two armies clashed in the Haldighati pass, set in the narrow hills of the Aravalli range between Khamnor and Gogunda:
- The Mughal army was led by **Kunwar Man Singh** of Amber and Asaf Khan.
- The formation of Maharana Pratap's army was remarkable:
  - The harawal (the front line) was held by the brave Afghan commander **Hakim Khan Sur**,
  - The chandrawal (the rear command) was held by **Rana Punja Bhil**, a tribal hero and archer from the Aravalli forests,
  - In the centre was the Maharana himself, with his famous war elephant 'Ramprasad' and his horse 'Chetak'.

## The Fierce Charge and the Valour of Chetak

In the very first assault of the morning, the Maharana's army tore apart the Mughal ranks. According to the contemporary historian Badayuni, the Mughal soldiers fled as far as the other side of the Banas river.

Then Maharana Pratap, riding his blue horse **Chetak**, advanced right up to Man Singh's elephant:
- Chetak placed both his front legs on the forehead of Man Singh's elephant.
- The Maharana struck with his spear with all his strength, but Man Singh hid in the howdah and his mahout was killed. Chetak's hind leg was cut by the poisoned sword fixed to the elephant's trunk.

## The Sacrifice of Jhala Bida and the Immortal Run of Chetak

Seeing the wounded Maharana surrounded by Mughal soldiers on all sides, the brave chieftain **Jhala Bida (Jhala Man)** at once took the royal umbrella and crown from the Maharana's head and put them on himself. The enemy army, mistaking Jhala Bida for the Maharana, fell upon him, and Pratap got the chance to escape safely.

The wounded Chetak ran on three legs carrying his master and, near Balicha, cleared a huge 26-foot monsoon stream in a single leap. As soon as he crossed the stream, this faithful horse gave up his life in the Maharana's lap.

## Aftermath and Guerrilla War

The Mughal army could neither take Maharana Pratap prisoner nor gain full control of Mewar.

After this, Maharana Pratap waged a long guerrilla war from the caves of the Aravalli hills. The generous **Bhamashah** offered his entire ancestral wealth (25 lakh rupees and 20,000 ashrafis) to the Maharana. With this help the Maharana raised a new army and, in **the Battle of Dewair in 1582**, defeated the Mughals and freed all of Mewar again, except Chittor and Mandalgarh.

## Historical Significance

Colonel James Tod wrote: *"There is not a petty state in Rajasthan that has not its Thermopylae, and scarcely a city that has not produced its Leonidas — Haldighati is the Thermopylae of Mewar."* Maharana Pratap is an eternal symbol of freedom and self-respect.
