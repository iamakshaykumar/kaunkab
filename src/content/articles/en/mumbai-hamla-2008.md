## Attackers Who Came by Sea

At about half past eight on the night of **26 November 2008**, ten young men landed in rubber boats on the **Colaba** shore of south Mumbai. They were trained members of the Pakistan-based terrorist organisation **Lashkar-e-Taiba**. According to the investigation, they had set out from **Karachi** on a ship and in the middle of the sea had seized the Indian fishing boat **'Kuber'**. They killed the boat's four crew members and made its captain, Amar Singh Solanki, steer the boat to Mumbai; later they murdered him too.

After landing on the shore, the terrorists split into groups of two. They carried automatic rifles, hand grenades and explosives. Within a short time firing began simultaneously in several parts of the city, which made it difficult for the police to understand how large the attack was.

## One Night, Many Targets

The largest number of people were killed at the **Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus (CST)** railway station, where two attackers fired indiscriminately into the crowd. **58** people were killed here and more than a hundred were injured. Railway announcer **Vishnu Dattaram Zende**, in the midst of danger, kept making announcements asking passengers to get out.

At the same time other groups targeted the **Leopold Cafe**, the **Taj Mahal Palace Hotel**, the **Oberoi Trident Hotel** and the Jewish community centre **Nariman House** (Chabad House). There was firing around **Cama Hospital** as well, and people were also killed when bombs placed in two taxis exploded at Vile Parle and Mazagaon. In both hotels and in Nariman House the terrorists took people hostage and kept firing from inside. The historic dome of the Taj Hotel was seen engulfed in flames, and that scene became the most recognisable image of this attack.

## Police Courage and Kasab's Capture

Two attackers who left CST — **Ajmal Kasab** and his accomplice — reached the area near Cama Hospital. There, in a lane, they ambushed a police vehicle and opened fire. Several policemen were killed in this, including the chief of the Mumbai Anti-Terrorism Squad **Hemant Karkare**, Additional Commissioner of Police **Ashok Kamte** and Senior Inspector **Vijay Salaskar**.

The terrorists fled in the seized police vehicle and then took over a car. After midnight the police set up a roadblock at **Girgaon Chowpatty** and stopped them. Kasab's accomplice was killed in the encounter. Assistant Sub-Inspector **Tukaram Omble**, though almost unarmed, grabbed Kasab's rifle and, despite taking several bullets, did not let go until the other policemen had overpowered Kasab. Omble was killed, but because of him Kasab was captured alive — and the whole later investigation rested on this arrest.

## Operation Black Tornado

On the morning of **27 November**, commandos of the **National Security Guard (NSG)** arrived in Mumbai from Delhi. The Army and the Navy's **Marine Commandos** also joined the operation. The NSG surrounded the terrorists at the Trident, Nariman House and the Taj through room-by-room searches. This operation was named **Operation Black Tornado**.

By **28 November** the Trident and Nariman House had been cleared. The fight at the Taj lasted the longest. It was here that NSG's **Major Sandeep Unnikrishnan** was killed while rescuing hostages; **Havildar Gajender Singh Bisht** gave his life in the Nariman House operation. On the morning of **29 November** the last terrorists hiding in the Taj were killed and the siege of about 60 hours ended.

In all, **166** civilians and security personnel were killed in this attack and more than 300 were injured; nine terrorists were also killed. Among the dead were several foreign nationals as well. Several policemen and commandos were posthumously awarded the **Ashoka Chakra** for their courage.

## Investigation, Trial and Political Impact

Kasab's interrogation and the evidence recovered showed that the attack had been planned and directed from Pakistan. Pakistan at first denied this, but later accepted that Kasab was a Pakistani citizen. **David Headley**, arrested in the United States, admitted that he had scouted the Mumbai targets before the attack. His associate **Tahawwur Rana** was extradited from the United States and brought to India in April 2025.

A special court in Mumbai sentenced Kasab to death on **6 May 2010**. The Bombay High Court and then, on **29 August 2012**, the Supreme Court upheld the sentence. After the President rejected his mercy petition, he was hanged in Pune's **Yerwada Jail** on **21 November 2012**.

After the attack there was outrage across the country over the security lapses. Union Home Minister **Shivraj Patil** and Maharashtra Chief Minister **Vilasrao Deshmukh** had to leave their posts. The peace talks under way between India and Pakistan were halted.

## Legacy: A Changed Security System

26/11 exposed many weaknesses in India's internal security — the lack of maritime surveillance, intelligence not being shared in time, and the late arrival of special forces. After this, in December 2008, the **National Investigation Agency (NIA)** was created to investigate terrorism cases, and the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act was made stricter. The responsibility for strengthening coastal security was given to the Navy and the Coast Guard, and NSG units were stationed in several cities including Mumbai.

Even today, every year on **26 November**, the country remembers the innocent people and the policemen, commandos, hotel staff and ordinary citizens who did not care for their own lives in saving the lives of others.
