## Background: A Bold Decision

The Constitution, which came into force on 26 January 1950, gave every adult citizen the right to vote — without distinction of caste, religion, sex, property or education. At that time a large part of the country's population could not read or write, the wounds of Partition were fresh, and crores of people had never taken part in any election. Many foreign observers doubted that universal suffrage would succeed in such a large and diverse country.

Under the Constitution the **Election Commission** was set up, and in March 1950 **Sukumar Sen** — a mathematician and experienced officer of the Indian Civil Service — was appointed the first **Chief Election Commissioner**. In 1951 Parliament passed the **Representation of the People Act**, which laid down the rules for holding elections.

## The Huge Preparation

Preparing the electoral rolls was in itself a big task. About **173 million** voters were registered. Thousands of clerks were temporarily hired to prepare the lists and lakhs of reams of paper were used. The **489 seats** of the Lok Sabha were divided into 401 constituencies — many of these were to elect two members, of which one seat was reserved for a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe. Elections to the assemblies of most states were also held at the same time.

## Election Symbols and Ballot Boxes

Two measures were taken so that even illiterate voters could vote. First, every party and candidate was given an **election symbol** — such as the Congress's 'pair of bullocks'. Second, instead of marking the ballot paper, a **separate ballot box for each candidate** was kept at every polling station, with his name and symbol on it. The voter simply had to drop the ballot paper into the box of his choice. For this, lakhs of steel ballot boxes were made across the country.

## Voting: Beginning in Himachal

For fear that snow would block the routes in winter, voting was held first, on **25 October 1951**, in **Chini tehsil** (today's Kinnaur) of Himachal Pradesh. The schoolteacher here, **Shyam Saran Negi**, is generally regarded as the first voter of independent India. In most of the other states voting took place in January-February 1952, and the whole election ended on **21 February 1952**. The election was completed in 68 phases.

**53 parties** and 533 independents were in the field. Turnout was about 45 per cent (the figure differs slightly among sources). Voting for the Lok Sabha was not held in Jammu and Kashmir then.

## The Results

The **Indian National Congress** won **364 seats** out of 489 with about 45 per cent of the votes, and **Jawaharlal Nehru** became the first elected Prime Minister of the country. The Communist Party of India became the largest opposition party with 16 seats; the Socialist Party got 12, the Kisan Mazdoor Praja Party 9 and the Bharatiya Jana Sangh 3 seats, while 37 independents won.

Some big names also lost: **Dr. Ambedkar**, chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constitution, lost the Bombay (North Central) seat to the Congress candidate Narayan Sadoba Kajrolkar, and **Acharya Kripalani** lost from Faizabad, while his wife **Sucheta Kripalani** won from New Delhi.

## Significance

The first general election proved that, despite poverty and illiteracy, the common people of India could embrace democracy. It established the tradition of election symbols, an independent Election Commission and the peaceful determination of power, which is still the foundation of Indian democracy today. In his book 'India After Gandhi' the historian Ramachandra Guha titled the very chapter on this election 'The Biggest Gamble in History'.
