## Born among the Pathans

Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan was born on **6 February 1890** in the village of **Utmanzai** near **Charsadda** in the **North-West Frontier Province** of what was then British India (today Khyber Pakhtunkhwa in Pakistan). His father, Bahram Khan, was a prosperous and respected landowner. His elder brother, **Dr. Khan Abdul Jabbar Khan**, known as **'Dr. Khan Sahib'**, later became the province's Chief Minister.

Pathan society at that time was known not only for its bravery but also for its feuds, its tradition of revenge and its illiteracy. The young Ghaffar Khan decided to work for **education and reform** among his people. In **1910**, at just twenty, he opened a madrasa (school) in Utmanzai. Later he started a whole chain of **'Azad' schools**, which were open to all and in which girls were also taught. The British government viewed them with suspicion and had many of the schools closed. He walked from village to village spreading awareness, and people began to call him respectfully **'Badshah Khan'** or **'Bacha Khan'**.

## Khudai Khidmatgar: The Non-Violent Pathans

In **1928** he started the Pashto magazine **'Pakhtun'**, and in **1929** he formed an organisation called the **Khudai Khidmatgar** ('Servants of God'). Its members took an oath of **non-violence**, service and discipline — they promised not to take revenge, not to use violence and to serve every human being. It is said that their uniform was at first white, which got dirty quickly, so it was dyed a brick-like reddish-brown. Because of this they came to be called the **'Red Shirts'**. Within a few years its membership grew from thousands to nearly one hundred thousand.

Bringing the Pathans, who saw the gun as a matter of pride, onto the path of non-violence was an extraordinary achievement in itself. Ghaffar Khan said that non-violence was not a new idea but part of the Prophet's teaching of patience and forbearance. He had a deep friendship with **Mahatma Gandhi**, and the two greatly respected each other. That is why he was called the **'Frontier Gandhi'**.

## The Freedom Struggle and Repression

During the **Civil Disobedience Movement** of **1930**, Ghaffar Khan and other leaders were arrested on **23 April 1930**. In protest, the same day, British troops fired on an unarmed crowd gathered in the **Qissa Khwani Bazaar** of Peshawar, killing a large number of people. Despite this repression, the Khudai Khidmatgar remained non-violent. Later the organisation worked together with the **Congress**, and after the elections of **1937** and **1946** a Congress government was formed in the province under Dr. Khan Sahib.

Ghaffar Khan spent most of his life in jail. He was imprisoned again and again, both under British rule and later in Pakistan. It is estimated that in all he spent around **thirty years** in prison or detention.

## Opposition to Partition

Ghaffar Khan was a strong opponent of the **partition of India** on the basis of religion. When the Congress accepted the partition plan in 1947, he was deeply hurt. His remark that the Congress had "thrown us to the wolves" is famous. A referendum was held in **July 1947** to decide the future of the Frontier Province, but it offered only the choice of joining India or Pakistan. The Khudai Khidmatgar demanded a separate option for the Pathans and, when it was not given, **boycotted** the referendum. The result went in favour of Pakistan.

After Pakistan was created he took an oath of allegiance to it, but continued to demand autonomy for the Pathans. The Pakistani government regarded him with suspicion, and from **1948** onwards for several decades he was repeatedly kept in jail or under house arrest. He also spent some years in exile in **Afghanistan**. In **1962** **Amnesty International** declared him a **'Prisoner of Conscience'**.

## Honours, Death and Legacy

India gave him the **Jawaharlal Nehru Award** in **1967** and the country's highest civilian honour, the **Bharat Ratna**, in **1987**. He is often remembered as the first non-Indian citizen to receive the Bharat Ratna. His autobiography, **'My Life and Struggle'**, was published in 1969.

He died in **Peshawar** on **20 January 1988** at the age of **97**. In keeping with his wishes, he was buried in **Jalalabad** in Afghanistan. Thousands of people crossed the border to join his funeral procession, and a ceasefire was declared for it in the Soviet-Afghan War then under way.

Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan's greatest legacy is the belief that **non-violence** is not a weapon of the weak but the path of the bravest. In a society associated with violence, he raised the largest army of peace. Roads and institutions in many cities, including Delhi, are named after him, and India has also issued a postage stamp in his honour.
