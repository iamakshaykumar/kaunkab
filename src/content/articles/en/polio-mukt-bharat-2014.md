## An Old and Frightening Disease

**Polio** (poliomyelitis) is an infectious disease caused by a virus, which mainly affects children under five. It spreads through contaminated water and food. About **one in 200 infections** leads to permanent paralysis, and among those paralysed some even die when their breathing muscles stop working. There is no cure; the only protection is vaccination.

In the 1980s and 1990s thousands of children in India were crippled by polio every year. From 1985 routine vaccines were given under the **Universal Immunisation Programme**, but because of the large population, poverty and weak health services, many children were missed. In 1988 the World Health Assembly set the goal of eradicating polio from the whole world.

## The Beginning of Pulse Polio

On **2 October 1994** a large-scale campaign to give polio drops was run in Delhi on the initiative of **Dr Harsh Vardhan**, then Health Minister in the Delhi government. After its success, the **Pulse Polio Immunisation Programme** began across the country from **1995**. 'Pulse' meant that on a fixed day every child under five in the entire country would be given a dose of **oral polio vaccine** at the same time, so that the chain of the virus's spread would be broken.

Partners such as the **World Health Organization**, **UNICEF**, **Rotary International** and the US **CDC** joined the campaign alongside the **Government of India** and the state governments. People came to know **National Immunisation Day** as 'Polio Sunday'. Besides the booths, vaccinators went from house to house over the following days to find children who had been missed, keeping records by marking doors with chalk. **Transit teams** were also posted at railway stations, bus stands, airports and river ghats.

## The Hardest Phase

Even in the 2000s, Uttar Pradesh and Bihar remained the biggest strongholds of polio. Because of dense population, floods, open defecation and diarrhoeal diseases, the vaccine could not work properly in children's bodies there. The Health Ministry recommended giving children **8 to 10 doses** in such areas. In some communities there were rumours about and distrust of the vaccine, and religious leaders, local leaders and community workers were enlisted to overcome this.

In **2009**, **741** cases were recorded in India, the highest in the world that year; most of them were from Uttar Pradesh and Bihar. After this special attention was given to **107 high-risk blocks**, a separate strategy was made to reach migrant families, and use began of the **bivalent vaccine (bOPV)**, which acts on two types of the virus at once. The actor **Amitabh Bachchan** became the main face of the campaign, and his message **"Do Boond Zindagi Ki"** (two drops of life) reached every home.

## The Last Case and Certification

The result was astonishing: in **2010** cases fell to **42**, a drop of about 94 per cent in a single year. On **13 January 2011** polio was detected in a little girl, **Rukhsar Khatoon**, in the **Howrah** district of **West Bengal**. This proved to be India's **last** recorded case of wild polio.

On **24 February 2012** the World Health Organization removed India from the list of polio-endemic countries. After no new case had been found for three consecutive years and all the surveillance evidence had been examined, on **27 March 2014** the World Health Organization's regional certification commission certified the entire **South-East Asia Region**, including India, as **polio-free**.

## Legacy

Even after becoming polio-free, India did not drop its guard. In view of the risk of the virus coming in from neighbouring countries, national and sub-national immunisation days are held every year, vaccination takes place at the borders, and from 2015 the injectable **IPV** vaccine was also added to routine immunisation. On a National Immunisation Day in 2021, about **1.2 million** vaccinators gave doses to children at **700,000** booths.

The Pulse Polio campaign is counted among the largest public-health campaigns in the world. The surveillance system, micro-planning and experience of reaching every house built up during it later proved useful in campaigns such as those against **measles**, **Mission Indradhanush** and **COVID-19 vaccination**. This example shows that even a very large health challenge can be won when the government, international institutions, volunteers and society work together.
