## Background: The Blessing of a Saint

By the 1560s Akbar's empire had expanded, but none of his children had survived. The Chishti Sufi saint **Sheikh Salim Chishti**, who lived in the village of Sikri about 36 kilometres from Agra, blessed him with the birth of a son.

In 1569 Akbar's son was born at Sikri itself. In honour of the saint he was named **Salim**, and he later became the emperor **Jahangir**. After this Akbar decided to build a religious complex near the saint's khanqah at Sikri, and then an entire new capital.

## The Building of the New Capital

From **1571** construction went on rapidly here and the court moved from Agra. The whole city was built of local **red sandstone**. After the success of the Gujarat campaign of 1573 it came to be named **Fatehpur** ("City of Victory").

The buildings here show a blend of Persian, Central Asian and Indian architectural traditions, especially those of Gujarat and Rajasthan. Balconies, chhatris, carved brackets and flat roofs are the gift of local craftsmanship.

## The Main Buildings

- **Jama Masjid**: this huge mosque, completed around 1571–72, was the religious centre of the city. In its courtyard is the **dargah of Sheikh Salim Chishti**, whose white marble lattice screens still attract devotees.
- **Buland Darwaza**: added in about 1575–77 as the southern gate of the mosque in memory of the conquest of Gujarat. Including the steps, its height is said to be about 54 metres.
- **Diwan-i-Aam**: where the emperor met the general public and officials.
- **Diwan-i-Khas**: a unique building with a central carved pillar.
- **Panch Mahal**: a five-storeyed open pavilion, each storey smaller than the one below.
- **The Palace of Mariam-uz-Zamani**: popularly called the "Jodha Bai Palace", but this name comes from later tradition.

## The Ibadat Khana and the Centre of Ideas

In 1575 Akbar had the **Ibadat Khana** built here, where discussions were held first with Islamic scholars and then with thinkers of various religions. These debates shaped Akbar's idea of "Sulh-i-Kul" (universal peace). The building of the Ibadat Khana has not been identified today and scholars differ about its exact location.

During the Fatehpur Sikri years, many large projects of history-writing such as the Akbarnama, translations and painting also moved forward.

## Why the Capital Was Abandoned

In **1585**, because of problems on the north-western frontier and the campaign in Punjab, Akbar moved to Lahore, and the court never again returned here permanently.

It is often said that the city was abandoned because of a shortage of water. Many historians believe that the water problem may have been one reason, but the real reasons were political and military needs, as the centre of the empire was shifting. Gradually the city became almost deserted.

## Significance

Because it was the capital for a relatively short period, the buildings of Fatehpur Sikri largely escaped later alterations. It is therefore the best example for understanding the architecture and town planning of Akbar's time. In **1986** UNESCO declared it a World Heritage Site.
