## The Companion at Bithoor

Tatya Tope's original name was **Ramchandra Pandurang** (Yeolkar). He was born at **Yeola** near Nashik in about **1814**. His father was connected with the court of the last Peshwa, **Baji Rao II**, and after the Peshwa's rule ended in 1818, the family came with the Peshwa to **Bithoor** near Kanpur. Here Tatya spent his childhood with the Peshwa's adopted son **Nana Sahib**.

## Kanpur (1857)

In **June 1857** the sepoys of Kanpur revolted and accepted Nana Sahib as their leader. Tatya became Nana Sahib's chief military adviser and commander. The events at Kanpur in June–July 1857 — the bloodshed at Satichaura Ghat and the Bibighar — became sad examples of violence by both sides; historians differ on Tatya's personal role in them.

In **November 1857** Tatya attacked Kanpur with the soldiers of the **Gwalior Contingent** and pushed back General Windham's force. But in early **December 1857** he was defeated by the large army of Sir **Colin Campbell**.

## Jhansi, Kalpi and Gwalior (1858)

In March 1858, when Sir **Hugh Rose** besieged Jhansi, Tatya came to help Rani Lakshmibai, but was defeated near the **Betwa** on **1 April 1858**. After Jhansi fell, the Rani, Tatya and Nana Sahib's nephew **Rao Sahib** gathered at **Kalpi**. In May 1858 Kalpi too slipped out of their hands.

These leaders then took a bold step and advanced towards **Gwalior**. A large part of Scindia's army joined them and in early **June 1858** the Gwalior fort came into their hands. But Hugh Rose soon counter-attacked; on **18 June 1858** Rani Lakshmibai died a hero's death and within a few days Gwalior was again under British control.

## Ten Months of Guerrilla War

After Gwalior, Tatya chose **guerrilla warfare** instead of open battle. From June 1858 to April 1859 he kept moving through Rajputana, Malwa and Bundelkhand — sometimes crossing rivers, sometimes pushing on with the treasuries and cannon of princely states. Many British columns were chasing him at once, yet he kept slipping away again and again. In this campaign he crossed the Narmada and went as far as the south, but returned north when he did not get the expected popular support. Gradually his companions grew fewer.

## Betrayal and Hanging (1859)

In the end he was in the **Paron forest** near Narwar, under the protection of **Man Singh**. It is believed that Man Singh made a deal with the British in return for the safety of himself and his family, and on **7 April 1859** (8 April in some sources) had Tatya captured while asleep. He was tried by a military court at **Shivpuri** (then "Sipri"). According to sources, he admitted taking part in the battles but said he was answerable only to his master the Peshwa (Nana Sahib). On **18 April 1859** he was hanged at Shivpuri.

## Memory

Tatya Tope is counted among the most skilful military leaders of 1857; British officers too noted his guerrilla agility. There is a memorial to him at Shivpuri, where he is remembered every year on 18 April, and a postage stamp was issued on him in 1984. Stories that he escaped and someone else was hanged are also current, but these are not regarded as solid evidence.
