## From Lucknow to the Sky

**Shubhanshu Shukla** was born on **10 October 1985** in **Lucknow**, Uttar Pradesh. He trained at the National Defence Academy (NDA) and in **2006** was commissioned into the fighter stream of the Indian Air Force. He became an experienced **test pilot** and gained about 2,000 hours of flying experience on many aircraft such as the Su-30MKI, MiG-29 and Jaguar.

In **2019** he was selected in the choice of astronauts for **Gaganyaan**, ISRO's first indigenous human space mission. He trained at the Yuri Gagarin Cosmonaut Training Centre in Russia and later in Bengaluru. On **27 February 2024**, Prime Minister **Narendra Modi** made public the names of the four astronaut-designates for Gaganyaan — **Prasanth Balakrishnan Nair**, **Ajit Krishnan**, **Angad Pratap** and **Shubhanshu Shukla**.

## The Axiom-4 Mission

**Axiom-4 (Ax-4)** was a mission of the American private company **Axiom Space**, flown to the International Space Station (ISS) in cooperation with **NASA** and **SpaceX**. ISRO made an agreement for India's seat on this mission so that an Indian astronaut could gain real spaceflight experience before Gaganyaan. In August 2024 Shubhanshu Shukla was chosen as the prime astronaut and Prasanth Nair as the backup.

The four-member crew consisted of:

- **Peggy Whitson** (USA) — commander, a former NASA astronaut who holds the record for the most time spent in space by an American.
- **Shubhanshu Shukla** (India) — **pilot**.
- **Sławosz Uznański-Wiśniewski** (Poland) — mission specialist, a project astronaut of the European Space Agency.
- **Tibor Kapu** (Hungary) — mission specialist.

For technical reasons — such as a liquid oxygen leak in the Falcon 9 rocket and a pressure problem in a Russian module of the space station — the launch was postponed several times.

## Launch and Life on the Space Station

Finally, on **25 June 2025**, at about **12 noon** Indian time, a **Falcon 9** rocket sent the **Crew Dragon** spacecraft into space from launch pad 39A at the **Kennedy Space Center** in Florida, USA. It was a new Dragon capsule, which the crew named **"Grace"**. After reaching orbit, Shubhanshu sent a message in Hindi to his fellow citizens that the tricolour on his shoulder told him they were all with him.

On **26 June 2025** the spacecraft docked with the space station, and Shubhanshu Shukla became the **first Indian** to set foot on the ISS. On **28 June** Prime Minister Modi spoke to him over a video call; Shubhanshu said that from space India looks very grand and no borders are visible.

During a stay of about **18 days** on the station, the crew carried out dozens of scientific experiments from several countries. Shubhanshu conducted **seven experiments** of ISRO and Indian institutions — such as the sprouting of moong and fenugreek (methi) seeds in weightlessness, the growth of microalgae and cyanobacteria, the survival of the microscopic animals called **tardigrades**, a study of muscle loss, and the mental effects of using computer screens in space. He also talked with Indian students by radio and video.

## The Return

On **14 July 2025** the Dragon spacecraft undocked from the space station, and after a journey of about 22 hours, on **15 July**, at about **3 p.m.** Indian time, it landed safely in the **Pacific Ocean** near **San Diego**, USA. The whole mission lasted about **20 days**. After a few days of rehabilitation to readjust his body to Earth's gravity, Shubhanshu returned to India in August 2025, where he received a grand welcome.

## Significance

In **1984**, **Rakesh Sharma** had gone into space aboard the Soviet Union's Soyuz spacecraft. About **41 years** later, Shubhanshu Shukla became the **second Indian** to go into space. Kalpana Chawla and Sunita Williams, of Indian origin, also went into space, but they flew on NASA missions on behalf of the United States.

Shubhanshu was born the year after Rakesh Sharma's flight; as a child he had read stories of space travel in books, and now he himself became the next chapter of that story.

According to ISRO, the experience gained from this mission — training, spacecraft operations, health in space and the management of experiments — is very useful for the **Gaganyaan** mission and the proposed future **Bharatiya Antariksh Station** (Indian space station). Shubhanshu Shukla's flight awakened a new curiosity about science and space among the country's youth, just as Rakesh Sharma's answer "Saare Jahan Se Achha" had done four decades earlier.
