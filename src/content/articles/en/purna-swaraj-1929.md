## Background: Beyond Dominion Status

In the 1920s the Congress's declared goal was 'swaraj', but its meaning was not clear. Many leaders were satisfied with **dominion status** (self-government like Canada or Australia) while remaining within the British Empire.

In 1927 the British government appointed the **Simon Commission** for constitutional reforms in India, which had not a single Indian member. It was opposed across the country with slogans of 'Simon Go Back'. In response, Indian leaders prepared the **Nehru Report** in 1928, which demanded dominion status. But young leaders such as Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose wanted nothing less than complete independence.

## The Calcutta Ultimatum

At the **Calcutta session** of December 1928 a compromise was reached: the Congress gave the British government one year. If India did not get dominion status by 31 December 1929, the Congress would declare complete independence its goal and start a civil disobedience movement for it.

In October 1929 the Viceroy **Lord Irwin** announced that a Round Table Conference would be called and that dominion status was the future goal, but gave no firm timeline. In December 1929 the Congress leaders' talks with Irwin ended without result. The one-year deadline had run out.

## The Lahore Session, December 1929

In December 1929 the historic session of the Congress was held at **Lahore**, on the banks of the Ravi. With Gandhiji's support, **Jawaharlal Nehru** was elected president — this was an acceptance of leadership by the younger generation.

The main decisions taken at the session were:

- The goal of the Congress would now be **Purna Swaraj** (complete independence).
- The proposed Round Table Conference would be boycotted.
- The Congress Working Committee was given the authority to start a **civil disobedience movement** at a suitable time, including non-payment of taxes.

At midnight on **31 December 1929**, Nehru hoisted the tricolour flag of independence on the banks of the Ravi. The crowd present welcomed it with slogans of 'Inquilab Zindabad'.

## 26 January 1930: The First Independence Day

The Congress decided that '**Independence Day**' would be celebrated across the whole country on **26 January 1930**. On this day meetings were held in villages and cities, the tricolour was hoisted, and people collectively read the pledge of independence. This declaration stated that British rule had harmed India in four ways — economically, politically, culturally and spiritually — and therefore Indians had the right to be completely free of this rule.

## The Road Ahead and Significance

Only a few weeks after the declaration of Purna Swaraj, on 12 March 1930, Gandhiji began the civil disobedience movement with the **Dandi March**. The Lahore session made the goal of the freedom struggle clear for ever — now the compromise was no longer on self-government within the empire but on complete freedom.

When the Constitution of independent India was made, the day of **26 January 1950** was deliberately chosen to bring it into force, in order to honour that pledge of 1930. For this reason 26 January is celebrated today as **Republic Day**.
