## The World's Largest Election

India's Chief Election Commissioner **Rajiv Kumar** announced the election to the 18th Lok Sabha on **16 March 2024**. Voting for the **543 seats** of the Lok Sabha took place in **seven phases** — on **19 April, 26 April, 7 May, 13 May, 20 May, 25 May and 1 June 2024**. The votes were counted on **4 June 2024**. Assembly elections in Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, Arunachal Pradesh and Sikkim were held at the same time.

According to the Election Commission, there were about **970 million** registered voters, making it the largest election in human history. About 1.05 million polling stations were set up, and voting was by **electronic voting machine (EVM)** with **VVPAT**. Overall turnout was about **66 per cent**, slightly lower than in 2019; according to the Commission, more than **640 million** people voted.

## The Main Contest and the Issues

The contest was mainly between two alliances. On one side was the **National Democratic Alliance (NDA)** led by the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), headed by Prime Minister **Narendra Modi**. The BJP gave the slogan "Abki baar 400 paar" (this time, beyond 400) and put development, welfare schemes, the Ram temple and strong leadership at the centre of its campaign.

On the other side was the **'INDIA' alliance** of the Congress, the Samajwadi Party, the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK) and other parties, formed in 2023. The opposition raised issues such as unemployment, inflation, farmers' problems, a caste census and the protection of the Constitution. The Trinamool Congress fought alone in West Bengal, separately from the alliance. During the campaign both sides made sharp allegations against each other, and many complaints of violations of the model code of conduct reached the Election Commission.

## The Results

The results that came on **4 June** overturned many predictions. Most post-poll surveys (exit polls) had given the NDA a big win, but the actual results were quite different.

- The **BJP** won **240** seats — it became the largest party but fell short of the majority mark of **272** on its own. In 2019 it had won 303 seats.
- The **Congress** won **99** seats, almost double its 52 seats of 2019.
- The **Samajwadi Party** became the third-largest party with **37** seats. In Uttar Pradesh it won more seats than the BJP (33).
- The **Trinamool Congress** won 29, the **DMK** 22, the **Telugu Desam Party (TDP)** 16 and the **Janata Dal (United)** 12 seats.

Overall the **NDA** won **293** seats and the **INDIA** alliance about **234**. The BJP performed strongly in states such as Odisha, Madhya Pradesh, Delhi and Gujarat and won a Lok Sabha seat in Kerala (Thrissur) for the first time, while its seats fell in Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan and West Bengal. In Andhra Pradesh the TDP won big in both the Lok Sabha and the assembly, and in Odisha the BJP formed a state government on its own for the first time.

## A Coalition Government

Because it had fewer seats than a majority, allies — especially **N. Chandrababu Naidu's** TDP and **Nitish Kumar's** JD(U) — became very important for the BJP. On **7 June** the NDA parliamentary party chose Narendra Modi as its leader, and he presented the support of 293 MPs to President **Droupadi Murmu**.

On **9 June 2024**, at Rashtrapati Bhavan, Narendra Modi took the oath as Prime Minister for a **third consecutive time**. He became the second leader after **Jawaharlal Nehru** to become Prime Minister by winning three general elections in a row. It was the first government since 2014 in which the BJP did not have a majority on its own.

On **26 June 2024**, **Om Birla** was re-elected Speaker of the Lok Sabha by voice vote; the opposition had fielded K. Suresh, and for the first time since 1976 there was a contest for the post of Speaker. The Congress's **Rahul Gandhi** became **Leader of the Opposition** in the Lok Sabha — a post that had been vacant for ten years.

## Significance

The 2024 election was a major example of the scale and diversity of Indian democracy. According to supporters, the NDA's third successive victory was the people's seal of approval on the government's policies, while the opposition described the results as a mandate against one-party dominance. Many analysts pointed out that after ten years the era of coalition politics had returned at the Centre and a strong opposition was visible in Parliament. The election also showed how different the leanings of voters in India's various states can be from one another.
