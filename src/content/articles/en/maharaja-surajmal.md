## From Badan Singh to Suraj Mal

At the end of the seventeenth century, the Jat peasants of the Agra–Mathura region rose in several revolts against Mughal rule. The revolt of 1669 led by Gokula, and later the struggles of Jat leaders such as Rajaram and Churaman, were part of this chain. From these struggles an organised Jat state later emerged, which was put on a stable footing by Churaman's nephew **Badan Singh**, who made Deeg his centre by working in step with the Kachhwaha rulers of Jaipur. His son **Suraj Mal** (childhood name Sujan Singh) was born on **13 February 1707**.

Even in his father's lifetime, Suraj Mal had begun to take charge of administration and the army. Around 1733 he began the construction of the **Lohagarh** fort at **Bharatpur**, with thick mud walls and a wide moat. His formal rule is counted from **1755**, when Badan Singh had grown old.

## War and Diplomacy

Suraj Mal played his part shrewdly in the tug-of-war of the Mughal court. In **1748**, at the Battle of **Bagru**, he sided with Ishwari Singh of Jaipur. In 1753, when civil war broke out in Delhi between the wazir **Safdarjung** and his opponents, Suraj Mal fought on Safdarjung's side.

In response, in 1754 the Mughal wazir Imad-ul-Mulk and the Marathas together besieged the fort of **Kumher**. In this siege **Khanderao Holkar**, son of Malharrao Holkar, died when a cannonball struck him. Even after months the fort did not fall, and in the end a settlement was reached.

## Panipat 1761 and After

In the campaign against Ahmad Shah Abdali, Suraj Mal first joined the Maratha commander **Sadashivrao Bhau**. But differences arose over questions such as war strategy, taking heavy artillery and families along, and the arrangements for Delhi, and he separated from the Maratha camp. After the Maratha defeat at Panipat on **14 January 1761**, the wounded and hungry Maratha soldiers and women returning home found shelter and food in Suraj Mal's kingdom — Maratha sources also praise this.

Suraj Mal made full use of the power vacuum that arose in North India after Panipat. On **12 June 1761** he captured the **fort of Agra**. His kingdom spread over Agra, Mathura, Aligarh and large parts of the surrounding area.

## The End on the Banks of the Hindon

Over influence in Delhi, Suraj Mal clashed with the Rohilla chief **Najib-ud-Daula**, an ally of Abdali who held control of Delhi. On the night of **25 December 1763**, Suraj Mal was killed in an ambush near the **Hindon River**, close to Delhi. His son **Jawahar Singh** succeeded him.

## Lohagarh and Legacy

Suraj Mal built Lohagarh at Bharatpur and buildings such as the Jal Mahal at **Deeg**. The fame of Lohagarh grew further in 1805, when the fort could not be taken despite a siege of several weeks by Lord Lake, a commander of the East India Company. His cenotaph at **Kusum Sarovar** in Govardhan was built by his son Jawahar Singh.

Historians count Suraj Mal among those eighteenth-century regional rulers who, in the age of Mughal decline, turned the strength of the peasant community into an organised state. He is known less for war than for practical politics, fortification and revenue administration.
