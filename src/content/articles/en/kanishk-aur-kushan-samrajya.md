## Background: The Meeting of Central Asia and India

After the fall of the Mauryan empire, Greek (Indo-Greek), Shaka and Parthian invaders came to north-western India. In the first century CE, the **Kushans**, a branch of the Central Asian Yuezhi tribe, expanded their empire over Afghanistan, Pakistan, Punjab, Sindh and as far as Mathura in Uttar Pradesh.

The most powerful and world-famous emperor of the Kushan dynasty was **Kanishka I** (c. 78 CE). He had two capitals — the main capital **Purushapura (present-day Peshawar)** and the second, the major commercial and cultural capital, **Mathura**.

## Control of the Silk Route and Economic Prosperity

Kanishka's empire extended from Pataliputra in the east to the Oxus (Amu Darya) river in the west and to Kashgar, Yarkand and Khotan in Central Asia:
- The historic **Silk Route**, running from China to Rome (Europe), passed through the Kushan empire.
- By taxing this trade and providing protection, the Kushans earned immense wealth.
- Kanishka issued large numbers of excellent gold and copper coins, bearing images of Indian, Greek, Persian and Central Asian gods and goddesses, which reflect the multicultural character of his empire.

## The Fourth Buddhist Council and the Rise of Mahayana

Like Emperor Ashoka, Kanishka gave royal patronage to Buddhism:
- In his time the **Fourth Buddhist Council** was held at **Kundalavana** in Kashmir, presided over by Vasumitra, with the great poet Ashvaghosha as vice-president.
- It was here that Buddhism was formally divided into two main sects — **Hinayana (Theravada)** and **Mahayana**.
- In the Mahayana sect, the Buddha was regarded as God, worship of his images began, and the concept of the bodhisattva became popular. Through Kanishka's envoys and traders, Mahayana Buddhism spread to Central Asia and China.

## The Gandhara and Mathura Schools of Art

In Kanishka's time two immortal styles of Indian sculpture developed:
1. **Gandhara art:** In this style, very beautiful images of the Buddha were carved in dark grey stone, with Greek (Greco-Roman) bodily forms, wavy hair and folds of drapery.
2. **Mathura art:** Here, in red sandstone, images of the Buddha and Jain Tirthankaras were made with Indian expressions, spirituality and the padmasana posture. A huge headless royal stone statue of Kanishka has also been found at Mathura.

## Navaratna and Patronage of Scholars

Kanishka's court was adorned with great scholars:
- **Ashvaghosha:** who wrote the famous Sanskrit epics *Buddhacharita* and *Saundarananda*.
- **Charaka:** the great Ayurvedic physician and Kanishka's royal physician, who composed the *Charaka Samhita*.
- **Nagarjuna:** the great Buddhist philosopher who propounded the doctrine of 'Shunyavada' and the theory of relativity (Madhyamaka Karika); he is also called the 'Einstein of India'.
- **Vasumitra:** who compiled the *Mahavibhasha Shastra*, an encyclopaedia of Buddhist philosophy.

## Historical Significance

Kanishka and the Kushan empire made India a great meeting point of the cultures and trade of East and West. The 'Shaka Samvat', which began with his accession in 78 CE, is still India's official national era today.
