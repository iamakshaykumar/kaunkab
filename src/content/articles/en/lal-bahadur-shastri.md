## From Mughalsarai to Kashi Vidyapith

**Lal Bahadur Shastri** was born on **2 October 1904** at **Mughalsarai** in Uttar Pradesh — the same date as Mahatma Gandhi's birthday. When he was about a year and a half old, his father died in the plague epidemic of **1905**. His mother raised him through hardship.

In his teens he gave up the caste-indicating surname **"Srivastava"**. Inspired by Gandhi's **Non-Cooperation Movement** in **1921**, he jumped into the freedom struggle. Later he graduated in **1925** from **Kashi Vidyapith** in Varanasi and received the title **"Shastri"** (scholar), which later became part of his name.

## The Freedom Struggle and Jail

Shastri joined the **Servants of the People Society**, founded by Lala Lajpat Rai, and later became its president. He was a devoted Congress worker and went to jail repeatedly in campaigns such as the Salt Satyagraha, the Individual Satyagraha and the Quit India Movement. In all he spent about **nine years** in British jails.

## As a Minister — Moral Responsibility

After independence he first served as a minister in the Uttar Pradesh government, and then in **1952** became **Railway Minister** at the Centre. In **1956**, after a terrible train accident at **Ariyalur** in Tamil Nadu, he **resigned**, taking moral responsibility. He had earlier offered to resign after another accident too, but Nehru had not accepted it. This step became an example in Indian politics. Later he was Minister of Commerce and Industry (1959) and **Home Minister** (1961–63).

## Prime Minister (1964–66)

After Nehru's death in May 1964, Shastri became Prime Minister on **9 June 1964**. His tenure was only about a year and a half, but it was full of challenges. There was a **severe shortage of food grains** in the country and India depended on wheat imported from America. Shastri appealed to people to **give up one meal a week** and promoted the policies for self-reliance in agriculture that later led to the **Green Revolution**. To increase milk production, he supported the formation of the **National Dairy Development Board** to spread the **Amul** model across the country — this later became the **White Revolution**.

In **1965**, agitations took place in South India, especially Madras, against the imposition of Hindi. Shastri assured that English would continue as an official language as long as the non-Hindi states wanted.

## The 1965 War and "Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan"

In **1965** the **India-Pakistan war** broke out. Shastri's calm but firm leadership kept the nation's morale high. During the war, on **19 October 1965**, at **Urwa** near Allahabad, he gave the slogan **"Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan"** — calling both the soldier fighting on the border and the farmer growing grain in the fields the backbone of the nation. A ceasefire came in September 1965 through United Nations intervention.

## Tashkent and the Mysterious Death

Talks were held at **Tashkent** (now in Uzbekistan) between Shastri and Pakistan's President **Ayub Khan** from 4 to 10 January 1966, mediated by the Soviet Premier **Alexei Kosygin**. On **10 January 1966** the **Tashkent Declaration** was signed, under which the armies of both countries were to return to their pre-war positions by 25 February 1966. Some people in India criticised it, because it had no no-war agreement and some of the territory won had to be returned.

The very next day, on the night of **11 January 1966**, Shastri died in Tashkent. The official cause was given as a **heart attack**, but his family and many supporters doubted this and expressed fears that he had been poisoned. This suspicion was never confirmed, but the debate continues even today.

He was posthumously awarded the **Bharat Ratna** in **1966**. His memorial in Delhi is called **Vijay Ghat**. Shastri is still remembered for his simplicity, honesty and firmness in hard times.
