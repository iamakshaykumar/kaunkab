## From Princess to Queen

Velu Nachiyar was born in 1730 in the house of King Chellamuthu Vijayaraguntha Sethupathi of Ramanathapuram. She was the only child of her parents, so she was brought up like a prince. According to popular accounts she learned horse riding, archery, swordsmanship and traditional martial arts such as 'valari' and 'silambam', and also gained knowledge of several languages along with Tamil.

In 1746 she was married to King **Muthu Vaduganatha Thevar** of the neighbouring kingdom of **Sivaganga**. She was not only the queen but also her husband's adviser in the work of the state.

## The Blow of 1772

In the eighteenth century the small states of south India were under pressure from the alliance of the **Nawab of Arcot** and the **East India Company**. The Nawab wanted to collect tribute from these palayams and states and to bring them under his control, and the Company's army supported him.

In 1772 the combined army of the Nawab and the Company attacked Sivaganga. In this attack near **Kalaiyarkoil**, King Muthu Vaduganatha Thevar was killed and the Nawab took over the kingdom. The queen escaped with her young daughter **Vellachi** and trusted companions.

## Exile and Preparation

The queen took shelter at **Virupakshi** near Dindigul, which was then under the influence of the ruler of Mysore, **Hyder Ali**. Her minister **Thandavarayan Pillai** sought military help from Hyder Ali. Because he was an opponent of both the British and the Nawab of Arcot, Hyder Ali gave her protection and military aid.

For about eight years the queen prepared in this way. During this time two brothers who were commanders of Sivaganga — **Periya Marudu** and **Chinna Marudu**, known as the **Marudu brothers** — became her greatest allies. They organised the local people and raised an army.

## The Recapture of Sivaganga (c. 1780)

Around **1780** the queen, with the Marudu brothers and the help sent by Hyder Ali, advanced towards Sivaganga and, winning back the territory one by one, regained the capital. The details of this victory are not the same in the sources — some accounts speak of the Nawab's army fleeing, others of a later settlement.

The story of **Kuyili** is linked to this very campaign. According to folk tradition, on the day of Vijayadashami women entered the fort on the pretext of visiting the temple, and Kuyili poured ghee or oil on her body, set herself on fire and leapt into the Company's armoury, which destroyed the ammunition. This story is very popular in Tamil Nadu and also appears in government publications, but no contemporary documents confirming it are found, so historians regard it only as tradition.

## Rule and Final Years

After the victory the queen ruled Sivaganga for about ten years, and the Marudu brothers continued to play a leading role in the administration. Around 1790 she handed over power to her daughter Vellachi. Rani Velu Nachiyar died at Sivaganga on **25 December 1796**. Later the Marudu brothers themselves became leaders of the revolt of 1801 against the British and were hanged.

## Memory and Significance

In Tamil Nadu, Velu Nachiyar is remembered as 'Veeramangai' (brave woman). She is often called the first Indian queen to fight the British — this claim is popular, but strictly speaking her main fight was against the alliance of the Nawab of Arcot and the Company. Even so, her story shows that long before 1857 there was armed resistance in south India to the growing dominance of the Company, and that women were in leading roles in it.
