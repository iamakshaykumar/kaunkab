## A Wandering Maulvi

**Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah** is believed to have been born around **1787**. Different accounts exist of his early life. According to one view his family was linked to Gopamau in Hardoi district of Awadh, and his father, **Ghulam Hussain Khan**, was an officer in the army of Hyder Ali of Mysore; other accounts connect him to a distinguished family of South India (the Arcot region). He received a traditional Islamic education, also knew English, and travelled far and wide, including for Hajj. Later he became famous as a Sufi-inclined faqir and maulvi.

He believed that armed struggle against the British could succeed only if the common people were on its side. So before 1857 he travelled through cities such as Agra, Delhi and Lucknow, preaching. A **danka** (kettledrum) was beaten ahead of him, so people began to call him **'Danka Shah'**. He also wrote and circulated a pamphlet calling for jihad against British rule.

## Arrest and Release

Worried by his speeches and growing influence, British officials arrested him in **early 1857**. According to most accounts he was held in **Faizabad** jail and tried for sedition. (Some sources give Patna as the place of arrest, but most historians mention Faizabad.)

The revolt that began at Meerut in **May 1857** reached Awadh by **June**. When the sepoys at Faizabad rose, the jail gates were thrown open and the Maulvi was freed. The rebel sepoys chose him as their leader, and from here, as the **'Maulvi of Faizabad'**, he came to the centre of the revolt.

## Chinhat and Lucknow

The Maulvi advanced towards Lucknow with the rebel army. On **30 June 1857**, at **Chinhat** (Ismailganj) near Lucknow, the rebels defeated the force of Chief Commissioner **Sir Henry Lawrence**. In this battle **Barkat Ahmad** was the chief commander and the Maulvi was among the leading commanders. After the defeat the British had to take refuge in the **Residency**, and its long siege began.

In Lucknow, **Begum Hazrat Mahal** declared her son Birjis Qadr Nawab of Awadh and took charge of the government. The Maulvi remained a major leader of this struggle, but he also had differences with some factions of the court; he took no post in the new government and kept fighting with his own army. His camp was around Badshah Bagh. In **March 1858** **Sir Colin Campbell** recaptured Lucknow. On **21 March 1858**, after a final fight, about **1,200** of the Maulvi's fighters were forced out of a fortified building in the middle of the city; this was the last major rebel stand in Lucknow.

## The Struggle in Shahjahanpur and Rohilkhand

Even after losing Lucknow the Maulvi did not give up. He went to **Mohammadi** and then towards **Shahjahanpur**, where allies of leaders such as Nana Saheb and **Khan Bahadur Khan** of Bareilly also joined him. In **May 1858** he besieged a British detachment at Shahjahanpur and for some time kept a large British army engaged. His guerrilla style and rapid movements troubled the British; a reward of **50,000 rupees** was announced for his capture dead or alive.

## The End at Powayan

In early **June 1858** the Maulvi reached **Powayan** in Shahjahanpur district. Things went wrong between him and the local **Raja Jagannath Singh**: by one account he wanted to bring the Raja into the revolt, by another he wanted to take custody of officials to whom the Raja had given refuge. On **5 June 1858**, as the Maulvi arrived at the palace gate riding an elephant, he was shot at from inside and killed. His head was cut off and handed over to the British, by the Raja according to some accounts and by his brother **Kunwar Baldeo Singh** according to others, and the reward was collected. The next day the head was hung at the Shahjahanpur kotwali.

## Legacy

The British officer **Thomas Seaton** called him "the best soldier" among the rebels, and the historian **G. B. Malleson** also praised his determination and integrity. Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah is also regarded as a symbol of Hindu-Muslim unity in 1857, since his army and allies included people of both communities. His mazar (tomb) is in Shahjahanpur. In **2021** the trust linked to the proposed mosque project at Dhannipur in Ayodhya announced that the project would be associated with his memory, bringing this forgotten hero back into public discussion.
