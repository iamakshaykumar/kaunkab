## Many Streams, Many Regions

**Bhakti** means devotion or love toward God. In Indian history this idea is found in different forms from very early times. In the medieval period many regional traditions of poets and saints emerged. It would not be right to treat them as a single organisation, a uniform teaching or an event of one time.

In South India the Tamil **Alvar** poets composed verses in devotion to Vishnu and the **Nayanar** poets in devotion to Shiva. Andal was a famous woman poet of the Alvar tradition. Later, thinkers such as Ramanuja influenced bhakti and philosophy. These compositions made local languages an important medium of religious expression.

## Saints and Poets of North India

In North India, **Kabir**, in his sakhis and padas, stressed the search for God, love and moral life. His utterances contain words familiar from both Hindu and Islamic traditions, and he questions outward show. Kabir's compositions were passed on orally for a long time and were recorded in different collections.

The teachings of **Guru Nanak** laid the foundation of the Sikh tradition. He emphasised remembering the Name, honest labour and sharing. In Punjab, memories of his verses and journeys were preserved in the later Sikh tradition.

**Mirabai** of Rajasthan composed verses of devotion to Krishna. In Bengal, Chaitanya's Vaishnava tradition gave special importance to group singing and kirtan. The language, music and social conditions of each region gave these traditions different forms.

## Language and Legacy

Bhakti poets composed in Tamil, Awadhi, Braj, Punjabi, Rajasthani and other languages alongside Sanskrit. Their poetry and music spread in popular life and influenced later literary traditions. These sources help us understand the religious ideas, languages and diverse voices of people of that time.
