## Background: The Challenge of a Vast Empire

In 1556, at the age of only 13, Akbar sat on the throne of Delhi after winning the Second Battle of Panipat. The biggest question before him was how to rule permanently over a vast subcontinent whose majority was Hindu and which had centuries-old cultural diversity.

Akbar understood that India could not be ruled permanently by the sword and military force alone. Social harmony, respect for all communities and shared participation were necessary.

## Rajput Policy and Social Harmony

Akbar followed a policy of cooperation and respect towards the Rajput kings:
- Raja Bharmal of Amer, his son Bhagwan Das and grandson **Raja Man Singh** were given the highest mansabs (military ranks) in the Mughal court.
- Akbar established marriage alliances and gave the Rajput queens complete religious freedom to worship in his palace.
- In 1563 he completely abolished the **pilgrim tax** and in 1564 the **jizya tax**, which created deep trust in the state among Hindu subjects.

## The Ibadat Khana and Religious Churning

In 1575 CE, in his new capital **Fatehpur Sikri**, Akbar had the **'Ibadat Khana'** (House of Worship) built.

At first only Muslim theologians were invited here, but seeing their narrow quarrels with one another, in 1578 Akbar opened the doors of the Ibadat Khana to thinkers of all religions:
- Hindu scholars (Devi and Purushottam),
- Jain monks (Hiravijaya Suri, who was given the title 'Jagadguru'),
- Parsi dasturs (Meherji Rana, through whose influence the sacred fire was kept burning at court),
- Christian priests from Goa (Fathers Aquaviva and Monserrate).

From these debates Akbar concluded that truth is not the monopoly of any one religion, but that love of God and morality lie at the root of all religions.

## The Principle of Sulh-i-Kul

The historian Abul Fazl gave the name **'Sulh-i-Kul'** (universal peace and tolerance) to Akbar's political and philosophical outlook.
- The duty of the state was to protect people of all religions and ideas without discrimination.
- No one could be persecuted because of his religion or faith.
- Under this very philosophy Akbar presented a moral code called **'Tawhid-i-Ilahi' (Din-i-Ilahi)**, which was the essence of the good in all religions.

## The Navratnas and Administrative Efficiency

Akbar gave merit the highest place in administration. The **'Navratnas'** (Nine Jewels) of his court were a meeting of the finest talents of the country:
- **Raja Todar Mal:** expert in land revenue and finance,
- **Birbal:** witty adviser and judge,
- **Tansen:** emperor of music,
- **Abul Fazl:** historian and philosopher,
- **Faizi:** poet and scholar,
- **Raja Man Singh:** chief commander,
- **Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan:** composer of couplets and warrior.

## Historical Significance

Akbar's reign is regarded as the golden example of a syncretic culture (the Ganga-Jamuni tehzeeb) in medieval India. His policy of 'Sulh-i-Kul' remains an inspiration for India's pluralist and secular character even today.
