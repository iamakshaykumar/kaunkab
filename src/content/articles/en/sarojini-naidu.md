## A Gifted Childhood

**Sarojini Chattopadhyay** was born on **13 February 1879** in a Bengali family in **Hyderabad**. Her father **Aghorenath Chattopadhyay** was a scientist and principal of Nizam College, and her mother Barada Sundari Devi wrote poems in Bengali. In 1891, at only twelve years of age, she passed the matriculation examination with the highest rank.

On a scholarship from the Nizam she went to England in 1895 and studied at **King's College** in London and **Girton College**, Cambridge. There, writers such as Edmund Gosse advised her to bring the scenes, colours and people of India itself into her English poetry.

## Sarojini the Poet

Returning to India in 1898, she married Dr. **Govindarajulu Naidu**. This marriage between different castes and regions was considered bold at that time. Her poetry collections **'The Golden Threshold'** (1905), **'The Bird of Time'** (1912) and **'The Broken Wing'** (1917) contain pictures of Indian bazaars, festivals, folk life and nature; 'In the Bazaars of Hyderabad' is her famous poem. Because of the rhythm and colours of her poetry she was called the **'Nightingale of India'**.

## Entry into Politics

After the Partition of Bengal in 1905 she joined the national movement. She drew inspiration from Gopal Krishna Gokhale and in 1914 met **Gandhiji** in London, whose close associate she remained for life. Travelling across the country she gave speeches on women's education, women's suffrage and Hindu-Muslim unity. In 1917 she was also part of the delegation that met the Secretary of State Montagu with the demand for women's suffrage.

## Congress President and Satyagrahi

In December 1925, presiding over the **Kanpur session**, she became the **first Indian woman to be Congress president** (before her, Annie Besant, of British origin, had been president in 1917). In the 1920s she travelled to South Africa and America to present the problems of Indians there and India's case.

In the **Salt Satyagraha** of 1930 she was with Gandhiji. After Gandhiji's arrest on 6 April 1930 she was prominent among those who took over the leadership of the movement; while leading the picketing of the satyagrahis at the **Dharasana** salt works she was arrested. In 1931 she went with Gandhiji to the Second Round Table Conference in London, and she also went to jail in the **Quit India Movement** of 1942.

## The First Woman Governor

On the day of independence, **15 August 1947**, Sarojini Naidu became Governor of the **United Provinces** (today's Uttar Pradesh); she was the country's **first woman Governor**. While still holding this post she died of a heart attack at Raj Bhavan, Lucknow, on **2 March 1949**. Her daughter **Padmaja Naidu** published her final collection of poems, 'The Feather of the Dawn', in 1961; Padmaja herself later became Governor of West Bengal.

## Assessment

Sarojini Naidu was also known for her quick wit and sense of humour; her relationship with Gandhiji had both respect and affectionate teasing. Literary critics have differed on her poetry: some found it emotional and musical, while others saw in it a deep influence of the English Romantic tradition and a somewhat 'decorative' image of India. Her family home in Hyderabad, named 'Golden Threshold' after her first collection, is today attached to the University of Hyderabad and preserves her memory. There is no doubt that she opened new paths for Indian women in public life and gave the movement an influential, popular voice.
