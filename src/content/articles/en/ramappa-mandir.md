## A Temple of the Kakatiyas

In the thirteenth century the **Kakatiya** dynasty was ruling a large part of the Telugu land from Warangal. During the long reign of King **Ganapatideva** (reigned about 1199–1262) the kingdom expanded and prospered. His general **Recherla Rudra** had a Shiva temple built about 65 kilometres from Warangal, near the village of **Palampet** in what is today **Mulugu district**.

According to the Sanskrit **foundation inscription** standing in the temple courtyard, it was consecrated in **1213 CE**. The name of the deity is **Rudreshwara** — joining Rudra, the builder, with 'Ishvara'. For the Kakatiya kingdom and Ganapatideva's daughter, read the separate article "Rudrama Devi and the Kakatiya Kingdom".

## Why the Name 'Ramappa'

According to local tradition the chief sculptor of the temple was named **Ramappa**, and people began to call the temple by his name. There is no contemporary inscriptional proof of this, but the tradition is very old. If it is correct, this is one of very few temples in India known by the name of the sculptor.

## Architecture and Engineering

The temple is built on a high platform (jagati), and its style blends the Chalukya tradition of the Deccan with Kakatiya features. The walls and pillars are mainly of **red sandstone**, while hard stone like black **dolerite** was used in the parts with fine carving, on which a metal-like shine can still be seen today.

Two engineering features are much discussed:
- **Lightweight bricks** — the vimana (tower) is built of porous, light bricks, called 'floating bricks'. This kept the weight of the upper structure low.
- **'Sandbox' foundation** — the foundation pit was filled with sand and the structure raised on it, so that the shocks of an earthquake are absorbed to some extent. The temple is believed to have remained largely safe even in an earthquake of the seventeenth century.

## Madanikas and Sculpture

The most famous feature of the temple is the **bracket figures** on the outer pillars — **madanikas** (dancers) in dance poses, musicians and imaginary animals like **gaja-vyalas**. On the pillars and ceiling of the inner hall are scenes from mythology, dance and daily life. Scholars also regard these sculptures as important for the study of the dance tradition of the Telugu region. In the hall in front of the temple is a large **Nandi**.

## The Lake, Damage and Conservation

Close to the temple is **Ramappa Lake** — the Kakatiya rulers are known for building large reservoirs for irrigation, and this lake is considered part of that system.

Around 1310 the temple is said to have suffered some damage during the Warangal campaign of Alauddin Khilji's general **Malik Kafur**. In the twentieth century **Ghulam Yazdani** of the archaeology department of the Hyderabad state surveyed and repaired it, and after 1948 it came under the care of the Archaeological Survey of India.

## World Heritage

On **25 July 2021** UNESCO added it to the World Heritage list under the name **'Kakatiya Rudreshwara (Ramappa) Temple, Telangana'**. It is the first World Heritage Site of Telangana. The temple shows the level that art, engineering and water management had reached in the medieval Deccan.
