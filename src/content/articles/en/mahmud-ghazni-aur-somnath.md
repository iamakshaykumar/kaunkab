## The Sultan of Ghazni

Mahmud (reigned 998–1030 CE) became ruler of Ghazni (in present-day Afghanistan) after his father Sebuktigin. His kingdom stretched over large parts of Central Asia and Iran. The main aim of his campaigns towards India was to plunder wealth from rich cities and temples, with which he could pay for his army and wars in Central Asia.

## Repeated Invasions of India

Traditionally Mahmud's **17 invasions** of India are counted (between about 1000 and 1027 CE), though different historians give different numbers. The main events:
- The defeat of the **Hindu Shahi** kings Jayapala and his son Anandapala, which opened the way to the Punjab.
- Raids on **Thanesar** (around 1014), **Mathura** and **Kannauj** (about 1018–19).
- He annexed the Punjab to his kingdom, but did not set up permanent rule in the Ganga valley.

## The Attack on Somnath (1025–26)

Somnath was a famous Shiva temple at Prabhas Patan on the coast of Gujarat, counted among the twelve Jyotirlingas. Rich offerings came to the temple, and it lay in a prosperous region linked to sea trade.

According to Persian sources, Mahmud set out from Ghazni at the end of 1025 CE, crossed the desert to reach Gujarat, and attacked Somnath around January 1026 CE. The local people fought to defend the temple, but the temple was plundered and broken. The Chaulukya (Solanki) king of Gujarat, **Bhima I**, had left his capital and withdrawn at this time. Some sources place the event in 1024–25 CE; this difference in date is due to the method of reckoning.

## Different Ways of Seeing the Event

The historian Romila Thapar has shown in her book that different sources remembered this event in different ways:
- Later Persian historians exaggerated Mahmud as a warrior of the faith, and the figures for the amount of plunder are also often inflated.
- The Sanskrit and Jain sources of Gujarat say relatively little about this attack and give more attention to the rebuilding of the temple.
- In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries the event became a major symbol in national memory and politics.

Understanding these different views is necessary to read history in a balanced way.

## Al-Biruni: A Scholar's View

The scholar **Al-Biruni**, who came to India with Mahmud, learned Sanskrit and wrote a detailed work, "Kitab-ul-Hind" (Tahqiq ma li'l-Hind), on Indian mathematics, astronomy, philosophy and society. He also mentions the harm done to India's prosperity by Mahmud's invasions. This work is still an important source for understanding eleventh-century India.

## Rebuilding and Legacy

The Somnath temple was rebuilt again and again. After the attack the Chaulukya rulers had it rebuilt, and in 1169 CE, in the time of King **Kumarapala**, it was given a grand form again. In later centuries it was damaged several times more. After independence a new temple was built on the initiative of Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, and was inaugurated in 1951. In this way Somnath tells the story of both destruction and rebuilding.
