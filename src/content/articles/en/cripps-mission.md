## Background: The Crisis of War

In September 1939 Britain declared India a participant in the Second World War without asking Indian leaders. In protest the Congress provincial ministries resigned. In 1940–41 Gandhiji conducted the Individual Satyagraha.

At the end of 1941 Japan entered the war and advanced rapidly. Singapore fell to Japan in February 1942 and Rangoon in March 1942; the danger was now on India's eastern border. Allied countries such as America and China also wanted Britain to win the cooperation of Indians. Under this pressure the government of Prime Minister **Winston Churchill** sent **Sir Stafford Cripps**, a member of the War Cabinet and a Labour leader, to India.

## Cripps's Proposals

Cripps reached Delhi on **22 March 1942** (some sources say 23 March). At the end of March the main points of his draft declaration were made public:

- After the war India would get **Dominion status**, and could even separate from the Commonwealth if it wished.
- After the war a **Constituent Assembly** would be formed; the representatives of the provinces would be elected by the lower houses of the provincial legislatures by proportional representation, while the representatives of the princely states would be nominated by the rulers.
- Any province could, if it wished, not accept the new constitution and keep a separate arrangement.
- During the war the control of **defence** would remain with the British government, although Indian leaders were invited to join the Viceroy's Executive Council.

## Negotiations and Rejection

On behalf of the Congress, **Maulana Azad** (then president) and **Jawaharlal Nehru** held the talks. The biggest dispute was how much real responsibility Indians would get in the defence department during the war, and whether the Viceroy's Executive Council could function like a true national government or the Viceroy's veto would remain. The Congress saw in the provision for provinces to separate a danger of the country's partition, and also objected to the nominated representatives of the princely states.

By the second week of April the talks broke down and the Congress rejected the proposals. The **Muslim League** also rejected them — according to **Jinnah**, they did not clearly recognise the demand for Pakistan. Other parties also expressed disagreement for their own reasons. Cripps returned to London empty-handed in April 1942.

## Reasons for Failure: The Debate

Historians differ on why the Cripps Mission failed. Many historians believe that Churchill, the Secretary of State for India Leo Amery and the Viceroy **Lord Linlithgow** were unwilling to hand over power and limited Cripps's room for negotiation. On the other hand it is also said that Cripps raised hopes beyond his authority, and that deep differences among the Indian parties were making any agreement difficult.

## Results and Significance

The failure of the mission strengthened the belief among Indians that Britain did not want to give real power even in the middle of the war. Amid this disappointment the Congress passed the **Quit India** resolution in Bombay on **8 August 1942**.

Even so, the Cripps proposals were a turning point: for the first time the British government officially accepted that Indians would frame their own constitution through an elected Constituent Assembly and could, if they wished, leave the Commonwealth. The provision for provinces to separate also became a foreshadowing of the later debate on partition.
