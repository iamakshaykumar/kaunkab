## The Beginning: A Scientist's Dream

After independence, science in India was seen as a tool of nation-building. **Vikram Sarabhai**, founder of the Physical Research Laboratory in Ahmedabad, believed that for a developing country space technology was not a luxury but a means of meeting needs in communication, weather and education. The launch of the Soviet Union's Sputnik in 1957 had begun the space age across the world.

In **1962**, at Sarabhai's suggestion, the government of Prime Minister **Jawaharlal Nehru** set up the **Indian National Committee for Space Research (INCOSPAR)** under the Department of Atomic Energy (headed by **Homi Bhabha**).

## Thumba: 21 November 1963

For rocket launches, the fishing village of **Thumba** near Thiruvananthapuram in Kerala was chosen because it lies very close to the Earth's magnetic equator, which was useful for studying the upper atmosphere. Land was obtained with the help of the local church and community, and rockets were assembled in an old school building. **H. G. S. Murthy** became the first director of this centre.

On **21 November 1963**, the American-made two-stage **Nike-Apache** sounding rocket was launched from Thumba — the first step in India's space journey. On 20 November 1967 the first Indian-designed rocket, **RH-75**, flew, beginning the 'Rohini' series of sounding rockets. On 2 February 1968 Prime Minister Indira Gandhi formally dedicated the Thumba centre to the United Nations.

## The Birth of ISRO (1969)

On **15 August 1969** the **Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO)** replaced INCOSPAR. In 1972 the Space Commission and the Department of Space were created and ISRO was brought under them. In September 1969 Sarabhai reached an agreement with the American space agency NASA, under which an experiment (SITE) later delivered educational television by satellite to thousands of villages.

Sarabhai died on 30 December 1971. In his memory Thumba and the nearby space institutions were named the **Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre**.

## Aryabhata and SLV-3

At Bengaluru, ISRO built India's first satellite under the leadership of **Udupi Ramachandra Rao**, named after the ancient mathematician-astronomer **Aryabhata**. Under a 1972 India–Soviet agreement, it was sent into orbit on **19 April 1975** from the Soviet Union's Kapustin Yar centre by a Cosmos-3M rocket. This 26-faced satellite was meant for experiments in X-ray astronomy and solar physics. A few days later a fault in the power system stopped the experiments, but India still gained its first experience of building and operating a satellite.

The goal now was to send its own satellite on its own rocket. Under **A. P. J. Abdul Kalam** the **Satellite Launch Vehicle (SLV-3)** was built — a four-stage, solid-fuel rocket. Its first flight from Sriharikota on 10 August 1979 failed, but on **18 July 1980** SLV-3 successfully placed the **Rohini** satellite in orbit. With this India joined the small group of countries that launch satellites on their own rockets.

## Rakesh Sharma: The First Indian in Space (1984)

Under India–Soviet space cooperation, Indian Air Force pilot **Rakesh Sharma** was selected. On **3 April 1984** he went to space on the Soviet **Soyuz T-11** and, after spending about eight days on the Salyut-7 space station, returned on 11 April. When Prime Minister Indira Gandhi asked him how India looks from space, he replied in the words of Iqbal's line — "Saare jahan se achha" (better than the whole world).

## Significance

In twenty years India went from a small borrowed rocket to its own satellite, its own launch vehicle, and even its own traveller in space. The experience of SLV-3 laid the foundation for rockets such as PSLV, and the same tradition later reached missions such as Chandrayaan and Mangalyaan.
