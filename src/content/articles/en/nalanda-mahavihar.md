## Founding

Nalanda is in Bihar near Rajgir, on the land of ancient Magadha. According to Buddhist tradition, the Buddha and his disciple Sariputta also had a connection with this area. But the mahavihara that made Nalanda world-famous was founded in the **Gupta period**. On the basis of Xuanzang's account and the seals found here, its founding is linked with the time of **Kumaragupta I** (about 415-455 CE).

After this, many rulers had new viharas and temples built here and donated the revenue of villages for its expenses. In the 7th century **Harshavardhana**, and in the 8th-12th centuries the **Pala** kings of Bengal, were its great patrons. According to a copper plate of the Pala king **Devapala**, the Shailendra king Balaputradeva of Suvarnadvipa (today's Indonesia region) also had a vihara built here.

## Study and Life

Nalanda was not just a single building but a large complex of viharas (residential-cum-teaching buildings), temples and stupas.
- Mainly **Mahayana Buddhist philosophy** was taught here, but the Vedas, grammar, logic (hetuvidya), medicine and other subjects were also studied.
- Admission was considered hard; according to Xuanzang, learned gatekeepers at the gate itself asked visitors difficult questions and very few succeeded.
- The Chinese traveller **Yijing**, who stayed here at the end of the 7th century, mentions more than three thousand monks. In Xuanzang's biography the figure of ten thousand is found, which many historians consider exaggerated.
- According to Tibetan tradition, there was a huge library here called "Dharmaganja".

## Xuanzang and International Fame

The Chinese Buddhist monk **Xuanzang** came to India in the 7th century in the time of Harshavardhana, stayed at Nalanda for about two years and studied Yogachara philosophy under its chief teacher **Shilabhadra**. On returning he took hundreds of Buddhist texts to China. It is from his account that we get much of our information about Nalanda's buildings and daily routine.

The influence of Nalanda's teachers spread across Asia. In the 8th century **Shantarakshita**, a teacher associated with Nalanda, went to Tibet and played an important role in establishing Buddhism there.

## Decline

By the 12th century state patronage of Buddhism had declined and new viharas such as Vikramashila and Odantapuri had also emerged. Around 1200 CE, attacks by Turkish armies led by **Bakhtiyar Khalji** badly damaged the Buddhist viharas of Bihar, and the decline of Nalanda is also linked with this period. Even so, the Tibetan monk Dharmasvamin, who came here around 1235 CE, still saw some monks teaching. Gradually the site became deserted and was buried in mounds of earth.

## Discovery and Nalanda Today

In the 19th century **Alexander Cunningham** identified this site as Nalanda. From 1915 the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) carried out large-scale excavations here, which revealed rows of viharas, huge brick temples, seals, sculptures and inscriptions. In 2016 the **"Archaeological Site of Nalanda Mahavihara"** was included in the UNESCO World Heritage List.

Nalanda reminds us that ancient India had institutions of knowledge that brought together students from home and abroad.
