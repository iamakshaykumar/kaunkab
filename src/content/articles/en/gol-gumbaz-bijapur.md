## Background: The Adil Shahi Sultanate

When the Bahmani Sultanate broke up, around **1489–90** the governor of Bijapur, **Yusuf Adil Khan**, laid the foundation of the independent **Adil Shahi** dynasty. It was one of the five sultanates of the Deccan and lasted nearly two hundred years.

In 1565, in the time of **Ali Adil Shah I**, Bijapur joined Ahmadnagar, Golconda and Bidar to defeat Vijayanagara in the Battle of **Talikota**. After this Bijapur kept expanding southwards.

## Ibrahim Adil Shah II: The Art-Loving Sultan

When Ali Adil Shah died in 1580, his nephew **Ibrahim Adil Shah II** was about nine years old. For about ten years regents ruled, among whom **Chand Bibi** was prominent.

After taking over the rule himself around 1590, Bijapur reached its peak under Ibrahim II (reigned 1580–1627):

- He composed the **"Kitab-e-Nauras"** on music in the Dakhni language, whose songs include invocations to Saraswati and Ganesha.
- He planned a new settlement, **Naurasapur**, dedicated to music.
- Though a Sunni Muslim, he was tolerant towards other religions and appointed Hindus to high posts. Folk tradition also mentions his being called "Jagadguru".
- The famous historian **Firishta** wrote the history of the Deccan under his patronage.

His tomb, the **Ibrahim Rauza**, is famous for its fine stone carving. It was completed by his wife **Taj Sultana**.

## The Building of the Gol Gumbaz

Ibrahim II's son **Muhammad Adil Shah** came to the throne in 1627. Unlike his father's beautiful and ornate tomb, he chose grandeur. This tomb began to be built in the early years of his reign and work continued until his death in **1656** — some of the plaster work was left unfinished.

The name of the chief architect is not found in any contemporary record. Some historians believe **Malik Yaqub** of Dabhol played a major role, but this is only a guess.

## Architectural Features

- The outer diameter of the dome is about **44 metres** and the inner diameter about 38 metres.
- The huge chamber inside has no pillars; the height from the floor to the top of the dome is about 54 metres.
- At the four corners are seven-storey octagonal towers, inside which staircases lead up to the dome.
- The walls are of dark brown basalt stone, while the dome is made of brick and lime mortar.

It is counted among the largest domes of its time and has been called the largest unsupported dome in India. Inside, on a raised platform, are the cenotaphs of the sultan, his wives and family members, while the real graves are in the vault below.

## The Whispering Gallery

In the lower part of the dome there is a circular gallery all around, called the **"Whispering Gallery"**. Because of the dome's curvature, a soft sound from one side reaches the other side clearly, and a single clap echoes many times. Today this is the most popular experience of the Gol Gumbaz among tourists.

## Decline and Legacy

After Muhammad Adil Shah, Bijapur had to contend with both the Mughals and the rising Maratha power. Finally in **1686** **Aurangzeb** conquered Bijapur and ended the Adil Shahi sultanate.

Today the Gol Gumbaz is the symbol of Vijayapura (Bijapur) in Karnataka. It is the peak of the architectural tradition of the Deccan sultanates in which Persian, Turkish and local Indian elements blended together.
