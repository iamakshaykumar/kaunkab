## Vallabhbhai the Lawyer

**Vallabhbhai Jhaverbhai Patel** was born on **31 October 1875** at **Nadiad** in Gujarat; his ancestral village was Karamsad. Brought up in a farming family, Vallabhbhai passed his matriculation late, at about the age of 22, and then, becoming a district pleader, made a name as a sharp criminal lawyer at Godhra and Borsad. In 1909 his wife Jhaverba died.

At about the age of 36 he went to the **Middle Temple** in London and, having become a barrister, returned to Ahmedabad in 1913. At first he used to mock Gandhiji's ways, but soon became one of his most trusted associates.

## From Kheda to Bardoli

In 1918, in the **Kheda Satyagraha** against the collection of revenue despite crop failure, Gandhiji gave Patel the responsibility. Giving up his legal practice and foreign cloth, he travelled from village to village organising the peasants. He was also president of the **Ahmedabad Municipality** (elected in 1922, 1924 and 1927), where he worked on sanitation, drainage and education. In 1923 he also led the **Flag Satyagraha** of Nagpur and the tax resistance of Borsad.

In the **Bardoli Satyagraha** of 1928, in protest against an increase of about 22 percent in the land revenue, his disciplined organisation forced the government to hold an inquiry and reduce the increase. It was here that the women began calling him **'Sardar'**. (For details see 'Bardoli Satyagraha'.)

## Organiser of the Congress

In 1931 Patel presided over the **Karachi session**, where the resolution on **Fundamental Rights and economic policy** was passed. In the 1930s, as head of the Congress Parliamentary Board, he kept the organisation's hold on the 1937 elections and the provincial governments. He went to jail in both the Salt Satyagraha and the Quit India Movement; in 1942–45 he was imprisoned in Ahmednagar Fort.

## Integration of the Princely States

After independence Patel became **Deputy Prime Minister** and **Home Minister** and also took charge of the States Department. Together with the secretary **V. P. Menon** he put the **Instrument of Accession** before about 565 princely states and, through persuasion, pressure and assurances such as the privy purse for the rulers, got almost all of them to join India before 15 August 1947. A plebiscite was held in Junagadh, and in September 1948, after the military action in **Hyderabad** (Operation Polo), the Nizam accepted accession. (For details see 'Integration of the Princely States'.) It is for this work that he is called India's **'Iron Man'**.

## Foundations of Administration

Patel strongly supported all-India services such as the **Indian Administrative Service (IAS)** and **Indian Police Service (IPS)** in place of the ICS, calling them the 'steel frame' of the country's unity. In the Constituent Assembly he also chaired the committees on Fundamental Rights and on minorities. After Gandhiji's assassination, on **4 February 1948** he, as Home Minister, banned the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh, a ban which was lifted with conditions in July 1949.

## Differences, Assessment and Legacy

There were many differences between Nehru and Patel on economic policy, Kashmir, Tibet-China and administrative matters, which are still debated politically today; even so, in keeping with Gandhiji's wish, the two ran the government together. Some critics criticised him for accepting Partition quickly and for harshness in some decisions. He died of a heart attack in Bombay on **15 December 1950**. In 1991 he was posthumously awarded the **Bharat Ratna**, and since 2014 his birthday has been celebrated as **National Unity Day**.
