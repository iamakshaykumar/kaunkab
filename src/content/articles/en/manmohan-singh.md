## From Gah to Oxford

**Manmohan Singh** was born on **26 September 1932** in the village of **Gah** in Punjab, which is today in Pakistan. The village had no electricity or school; as a child he studied by the light of an oil lamp. At the time of the **Partition of 1947** his family moved to India and settled in Amritsar.

He always excelled at his studies. He took his B.A. (1952) and M.A. (1954) in economics from **Panjab University**. He then went on a scholarship to the **University of Cambridge**, where he completed his economics degree in 1957. After this he earned a D.Phil. from the **University of Oxford**. His research was on India's exports, and it was later published as a book. In 1958 he married **Gursharan Kaur**; they have three daughters.

## Economist and Administrator

On returning to India he taught at Panjab University and the Delhi School of Economics. For some years he also worked at the United Nations body **UNCTAD**. In 1972 he became **Chief Economic Adviser** in the Ministry of Finance. After that he rose steadily to high offices:

- **Governor of the Reserve Bank of India** (1982–1985)
- **Deputy Chairman of the Planning Commission** (1985–1987)
- Economic Adviser to the Prime Minister and Chairman of the University Grants Commission (UGC)

In 1987 he was awarded the **Padma Vibhushan**. Up to this point he was known not as a politician but as a respected economist and civil servant.

## 1991: Finance Minister and Liberalisation

In **1991** India was in a serious economic crisis. Foreign exchange reserves had fallen so low that they could barely pay for a few weeks of imports. **P. V. Narasimha Rao**, who became Prime Minister in June 1991, handed the country's economy to a non-political expert: on **21 June 1991** Manmohan Singh became **Finance Minister**. That same year he was elected to the Rajya Sabha from **Assam**, and he remained a member from there until 2019.

The Rao–Manmohan Singh pair devalued the rupee, largely dismantled the "licence raj", opened the doors to foreign investment and cut import duties. In his budget speech of **24 July 1991** he quoted Victor Hugo's line that "no power on earth can stop an idea whose time has come." (These reforms are described in detail in the article "The Economic Crisis of 1991 and Liberalisation".) He remained Finance Minister until **1996**. From 1998 to 2004 he was **Leader of the Opposition** in the Rajya Sabha.

## Ten Years as Prime Minister (2004–2014)

In the **2004** Lok Sabha election, the Congress-led **United Progressive Alliance (UPA)** came to power. Congress president **Sonia Gandhi** declined the post of Prime Minister and put forward Manmohan Singh's name, and he took the oath on **22 May 2004**. He became India's first **Sikh Prime Minister**.

Major steps taken by his government:

- **MGNREGA (2005)** — a legal guarantee of 100 days of employment a year for rural households.
- **Right to Information (2005)** — the right of citizens to seek government information.
- **Right to Education (2009)** — free and compulsory education for children aged 6 to 14.
- **Aadhaar** — the Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI) was set up in 2009.
- **National Food Security Act (2013)** — a legal entitlement to subsidised grain.

The biggest step in foreign policy was the **India–US civil nuclear agreement**, whose foundation was laid in 2005 with President **George W. Bush** and which was signed on **10 October 2008**. In protest the Left parties withdrew their support, but on **22 July 2008** the government won a confidence vote in the Lok Sabha by **275 votes to 256**. In this period the economy recorded rapid growth for several years, and the 2008 global recession hit India relatively lightly.

In **2009** the UPA won again and Manmohan Singh became Prime Minister once more. The second term was difficult: there were allegations of scams linked to the 2G spectrum, coal block allocation and the Commonwealth Games, rising inflation, and criticism of "policy paralysis". Critics said he was not a decisive leader; supporters said he was honest and working within the compulsions of a coalition. At his last press conference on **3 January 2014** he said that "history will be kinder to me than the contemporary media." His term ended on **26 May 2014**.

## Final Years and Legacy

Even after leaving the office of Prime Minister he remained in the Rajya Sabha, from **Rajasthan** from 2019, and retired in **April 2024**. He died on **26 December 2024** at **AIIMS** in New Delhi at the age of 92. The government declared national mourning, and on **28 December** he was cremated with full state honours at Nigambodh Ghat.

Manmohan Singh is counted among the builders of India's modern economy. The 1991 reforms set India on the path from a closed economy to an open one, and as Prime Minister he enacted several rights-based laws that are still in force today. Even his opponents praised his simplicity, calm temperament and scholarship. His life shows that a village boy who left his home during Partition could, through education, reach the highest office in the country.
