## A Government Officer Who Became the Emperor of Literature

**Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay** was born on **26 June 1838** in **Kanthalpara (Naihati)** in Bengal, in a Brahmin family. He studied at Hooghly Mohsin College and Presidency College and was among the **first graduates of the University of Calcutta**. Like his father, he went into British government service — he became a deputy magistrate in **1858** and retired in **1891** from the post of deputy magistrate and deputy collector. The government also gave him the title "Rai Bahadur".

It is an interesting paradox that the man whose song later gave voice to the movement against British rule remained an officer of that same rule all his life. Bengali readers call him **"Sahitya Samrat"** (Emperor of Literature).

## The Foundation of the Bengali Novel

Bankim's first novel, *Rajmohan's Wife*, was in English, but his real recognition came through Bengali. **'Durgeshnandini'**, published in **1865**, is considered the first Bengali novel. After it came works such as 'Kapalkundala', 'Vishavriksha' and 'Devi Chaudhurani'. In **1872** he started the literary magazine **'Bangadarshan'**, which gave a new direction to Bengali prose and thought. He is counted among the leading figures of the **Bengal Renaissance**, because he turned Indian writing from the tradition of verse toward the modern prose novel.

## Anandamath (1882)

**'Anandamath'** was published in **1882**. Its story is set against the background of the **terrible Bengal famine of 1770** and the **Sannyasi Rebellion** against the East India Company. Fleeing the famine, **Mahendra** and **Kalyani** are separated and reach the rebel ascetics called the "Santan" (Children), whose leader is **Satyananda**. **Shanti**, the wife of **Jivananda**, fights in male dress as "Navin". The Santans win their first battle against the Company's army.

The novel is not an exact account of history but a fiction built on a historical background. Its assessment is also not uniform. Many scholars see in it a soft attitude toward the British and **prejudice against Muslims**; such criticism has also been made of some of Bankim's essays. On the other hand, many regard it as a work that awakened patriotism. It is right to understand it by keeping both aspects together.

## The Song That Became a Slogan

Bankim wrote **'Vande Mataram'** in the **1870s** in Sanskrit-mixed Bengali and later placed it in 'Anandamath' as the song of the Santans. The song sees the motherland as a mother — "Sujalam suphalam malayajashitalam". Some scholars hold that in its original form it was a hymn to "Mother Bengal", which later became a hymn to all of India.

In **1896**, at the Congress session held in Calcutta, **Rabindranath Tagore** sang it. In the protest against the **1905** Partition of Bengal and in the **Swadeshi movement**, "Vande Mataram" became the most popular slogan. The British government banned its public singing and treated it as a crime, yet people kept singing it again and again. **Aurobindo Ghose** made an English translation of it, and the newspaper named "Bande Mataram" was also inspired by this song.

Bankim could not see all this — he died in Calcutta on **8 April 1894**.

## Controversy and the Status of National Song

In the later stanzas of the song there is mention of goddesses such as Durga. The **Muslim League** and many Muslim leaders objected to this. So in **1937** the Congress decided to adopt only the **first two stanzas** as the national song, in which no goddess is named.

After independence, on **24 January 1950**, in the Constituent Assembly the president **Rajendra Prasad** announced that 'Vande Mataram' would have honour equal to the national anthem **'Jana Gana Mana'**. In this way 'Jana Gana Mana' became the national anthem and 'Vande Mataram' the **national song**.

Aurobindo Ghose had written about Bankim that the earlier Bankim was only a poet and stylist, while the later Bankim became a "seer and nation-builder". The debate about Bankim continues even today, but there is no doubt that one song of his gave Indian nationalism its most famous slogan.
