## The Chalukyas' 'Laboratory'

In the time of the **Chalukyas of Badami** (6th–8th century) the Malaprabha river region of Karnataka became a great laboratory of Indian temple architecture. Early experiments took place at **Aihole**, rock-cut cave temples were made at **Badami**, and at **Pattadakal** this art reached its mature form. Pattadakal is about 23 kilometres from Badami and about 10 kilometres from Aihole.

According to tradition Pattadakal was the place of the **coronation** of the Chalukya kings, and its name too is linked with 'pattabhisheka'.

## Nagara and Dravida in One Courtyard

Indian temple architecture has two great styles:
- **Nagara (North Indian)** — a curved, tall **shikhara** above the sanctum.
- **Dravida (South Indian)** — a stepped **vimana** above the sanctum, diminishing storey by storey.

The special feature of Pattadakal is that both styles are found here together. Of the ten main temples here, nine are Hindu temples dedicated to Shiva and one is a Jain temple. Of the Hindu temples, four are of the Dravida style, four of the Nagara style, and in the **Papanatha** temple a mixture of both is seen. For this reason UNESCO has called it a 'harmonious blend' of the architectural forms of North and South India.

## The Virupaksha Temple: A Queen's Victory Memorial

The largest and most famous temple is the **Virupaksha**, built in about **740–745 CE**. It was built by **Lokamahadevi**, queen of King **Vikramaditya II**. In inscriptions its name is found as 'Lokeshvara'. This temple is linked to the memory of Vikramaditya II's successful campaigns against the **Pallavas**, in which the Chalukya army reached the Pallava capital **Kanchipuram**.

Scholars believe that the design of the Virupaksha was influenced by the **Kailasanatha Temple** of Kanchipuram — that is, in spite of the war, artists and ideas moved back and forth between the two kingdoms. The temple has a tall Dravida vimana, a pillared hall, a **Nandi mandapa** in front, and scenes from the Ramayana, the Mahabharata and Shiva stories carved on the walls. It is still a place of worship today.

Close to it is a temple built by Queen **Trailokyamahadevi** — Lokamahadevi's sister — known as the **Mallikarjuna** temple, and dating from about the same time.

## Other Main Temples

- **Sangameshvara** — the oldest Hindu temple here, from the time of King **Vijayaditya** (about 697–733).
- **Papanatha** — of the middle of the 8th century, a mixture of Nagara and Dravida elements.
- **Galaganatha** and **Kashivishvanatha** — Nagara-style temples; scholars differ on their dates.
- **Jain Narayana temple** — probably of the 9th century, linked with the Rashtrakuta period.

Excavation beneath the Sangameshvara temple has found the remains of another, older brick structure, which shows that this place had religious importance even before the Chalukyas' large stone temples. Note that the exact dates of these temples are often estimated differently by the Archaeological Survey of India and scholars such as George Michell, so most of the dates given above should be taken as approximate.

## Significance

Pattadakal shows that by the 8th century the different regional traditions of temple building in India were in dialogue with one another. After the Chalukyas the influence of the style here can be seen as far as such constructions of the Rashtrakutas as the **Kailasa Temple at Ellora**. In **1987** UNESCO declared the Pattadakal group of monuments a **World Heritage Site**, and today it is under the protection of the Archaeological Survey of India.
