## Background of 1942: The Failure of the Cripps Mission and the Second World War

By 1942 the Second World War had taken a terrible turn. Japan had taken Southeast Asia (Malaya, Singapore, Burma) and Japanese forces had reached the eastern borders of India.

To win Indian support, the British government sent Sir Stafford Cripps to India, but the 'Cripps Mission' failed to hand over real power to Indians. Gandhi felt that India could be defended only if the British left India immediately.

## 8 August 1942: The Historic Call of 'Do or Die'

The All India Congress Committee passed the 'Quit India Resolution' at the **Gowalia Tank ground** (now August Kranti Maidan) in Mumbai. In his speech Gandhi said:
> *"I give you a mantra — 'Do or Die'. We shall either free India or die in the attempt."*

In the early hours of 9 August the British government carried out 'Operation Zero Hour' and arrested all the top Congress leaders, including Gandhi, Nehru, Patel and Azad.

## A Spontaneous Mass Revolution and Parallel Governments

Without its leaders, this movement became the largest spontaneous mass uprising in history:
- The young **Aruna Asaf Ali** hoisted the tricolour at Gowalia Tank ground amid the arrests.
- **Jayaprakash Narayan** and his companions scaled the wall of Hazaribagh jail and formed underground freedom squads.
- In many areas British rule collapsed completely and **parallel governments (Prati Sarkar)** were set up:
  - at **Ballia** in Uttar Pradesh, under the leadership of Chittu Pandey,
  - the 'Jatiya Sarkar' at **Tamluk** in West Bengal,
  - Nana Patil's 'Prati Sarkar' at **Satara** in Maharashtra.

Railway tracks were torn up, telegraph wires were cut, and the tricolour was hoisted on government police stations and post offices. The government unleashed a cycle of brutal repression and gunned down thousands of people.

## Netaji and the Military Campaign of the Indian National Army (INA)

At the same time another decisive front was opening outside the country. **Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose**, who in 1941 had escaped in disguise from British house arrest in Calcutta, reached Germany and then, by submarine, Japan and Singapore.

- Taking over the command of the Indian National Army from Rash Behari Bose, Netaji announced on 21 October 1943 the **'Arzi Hukumat-e-Azad Hind'** (Provisional Government of Free India).
- With the slogans *"Give me blood, and I will give you freedom"* and *"Delhi Chalo"*, the brave soldiers of the INA crossed the forests of Burma and fought up to the borders of Kohima and Imphal.
- The women's **'Rani Jhansi Regiment'**, led by Captain **Lakshmi Sahgal**, showed the world that Indian heroines were second to none even on the battlefront.

## Impact: The Final Door to Freedom

Although by 1945 the INA had to retreat militarily and the Quit India Movement was suppressed, the trials of the INA officers (Sahgal, Dhillon, Shahnawaz) at the Red Fort and the 1946 Royal Indian Navy mutiny made it clear to the British that they could no longer rule India even for a day on the strength of the Indian army and police.
