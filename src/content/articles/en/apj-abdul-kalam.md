## The Boy from Rameswaram

**Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam** was born on **15 October 1931** in the pilgrim town of **Rameswaram** in Tamil Nadu, into a Tamil Muslim family. The family was of modest means; his father owned a boat in which he took pilgrims from Rameswaram to Dhanushkodi. As a child, Kalam also delivered newspapers to help add to the family's income.

In 1954 he graduated in physics from **St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli**, and then studied **aeronautical engineering** at the **Madras Institute of Technology**. He wanted to become a fighter pilot, but ranked ninth in the selection, when only eight places were vacant.

## ISRO and SLV-3

In 1960 he became a scientist at the Aeronautical Development Establishment of the **DRDO** (Defence Research and Development Organisation), where he worked on the design of a small hovercraft. In 1969 he moved to **ISRO** (Indian Space Research Organisation) and became project director of India's first **Satellite Launch Vehicle, SLV-3**.

SLV-3 was a four-stage, solid-fuel rocket weighing about 17 tonnes and standing 22 metres tall. Its first experimental flight in **August 1979** was only partially successful. Kalam took responsibility for the failure himself and got back to work with his team. On **18 July 1980**, from **Sriharikota**, SLV-3 placed the **Rohini (RS-1)** satellite in low Earth orbit. According to ISRO, this success made India the **sixth member** of the exclusive group of countries able to launch a satellite with their own vehicle.

## 'Missile Man'

In the 1980s the government gave him major responsibility in the defence sector. He became chief executive of the **Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP)**, begun in 1983. Several missiles were developed under this programme, the main ones being:

- **Prithvi** — a tactical surface-to-surface missile
- **Agni** — India's first intermediate-range ballistic missile, first flown in **1989**, which also used SLV-3 technology

Because of this work he came to be called the **"Missile Man of India"**. It is also worth noting that some of these projects ran well over their cost and time limits, which drew criticism.

From **July 1992** to **December 1999** he was the Prime Minister's **Scientific Adviser** and head of DRDO. During this period his organisational and technical role in the **Pokhran-II** nuclear tests of **May 1998** was important. He received the Padma Bhushan in **1981**, the Padma Vibhushan in **1990**, and in **1997** the country's highest civilian honour, the **Bharat Ratna**.

## The 'People's President' (2002–2007)

In 2002 the NDA government of Atal Bihari Vajpayee nominated him for the presidency, and many opposition parties also supported him. In the count on **18 July 2002** he received **9,22,884** electoral votes, while his rival, the freedom fighter **Lakshmi Sahgal**, received 1,07,366 votes. On **25 July 2002** he was sworn in as India's **11th President**.

As President he became known for his simple nature and for speaking directly with students. He repeatedly promoted an action plan to make India a "knowledge superpower" and a developed nation by 2020. Because of this closeness to ordinary people he was widely called the **"People's President"**. His term ended in **2007**.

## A Teacher to the Last Day

After leaving the presidency he continued to teach at **IIM Shillong**, Anna University and several institutions, and became the first Chancellor of the **Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology** in Thiruvananthapuram. On **27 July 2015**, while delivering a lecture at **IIM Shillong**, he collapsed and died of a heart attack. On **30 July 2015** he was buried with full state honours at Rameswaram, where his memorial, built by DRDO, now stands.

His books such as 'Wings of Fire', 'Ignited Minds' and 'India 2020' still inspire students. Kalam's life as a scientist, administrator and teacher is regarded as an example of how, even from a humble background, one can reach the highest office in the country through dedication and hard work.
