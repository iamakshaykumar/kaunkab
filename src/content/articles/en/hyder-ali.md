## An Ordinary Beginning

Hyder Ali was born in about **1720** (the sources give dates from 1717 to 1722) at **Budikote** in Kolar district of today's Karnataka. His father **Fateh Muhammad** was a military officer in the service of the Wodeyar kings of Mysore. Hyder began in the Mysore army as an ordinary soldier. He could not read or write, but was extraordinary in horsemanship, military skill, memory and accounts.

## Rise to Power in Mysore

At that time the Wodeyar kings of Mysore were rulers in name only and real power lay in the hands of dalavais (ministers) such as **Nanjaraja**. Hyder made a name in the siege of Devanahalli around 1749, and in **1755** he was made faujdar of **Dindigul**. There, with the help of French experts, he raised artillery and trained infantry.

Amid court intrigues and the turmoil of the Maratha invasion, Hyder gradually pushed aside his rivals and by **1761** became the real ruler ('Sarvadhikari') of Mysore. The Wodeyar king remained on the throne, but Hyder ran the government.

## Expansion of the Kingdom

Hyder made Mysore a major power of South India. In **1763** he conquered the rich kingdom of **Bednur** (Ikkeri) and then, with a campaign on the **Malabar** coast, took control as far as Calicut (Kozhikode) in **1766**. But in the north Maratha power proved too heavy for him — in the campaigns of Peshwa **Madhavrao I** in the 1760s and early 1770s, Hyder had to admit defeat several times, give up territory and pay heavy sums.

## The First Anglo-Mysore War (1767–1769)

In 1767 an alliance of the East India Company, the Nizam of Hyderabad and the Marathas opened a front against Mysore. Through diplomacy Hyder won the Nizam over for a time, though in 1768 the Nizam split off again. The war was full of ups and downs; finally, in early 1769, Hyder marched rapidly with his cavalry and reached the vicinity of **Madras**. The alarmed Company negotiated, and the **Treaty of Madras** was made on **29 March 1769**.

In this treaty both sides returned conquered territories (Mysore kept Karur) and agreed to help each other in defence in case of an attack by a third party. But in **1770–71**, when the Marathas invaded Mysore, the British did not help. This breach of promise created deep distrust of the Company in Hyder's mind.

## The Second Anglo-Mysore War and Death

In 1778 war broke out between the British and the French, and in 1779 the British seized **Mahe**, a French port in Malabar, which Hyder considered to be under his protection. In **1780** Hyder attacked the Carnatic (Arcot) region with a huge army. On **10 September 1780** at **Pollilur**, Hyder and his son **Tipu** surrounded the army of **Colonel William Baillie** and inflicted a crushing defeat — counted among the worst defeats of the British in India.

But the next year **General Eyre Coote** decisively defeated Hyder at **Porto Novo** on **1 July 1781**, and the war dragged on. Meanwhile, from a cancer-like boil on his back, Hyder died on **6 December 1782** (7 December in some sources) in his camp near Chittoor. His son **Tipu Sultan** took the throne and continued the war, which ended with the Treaty of Mangalore of 1784. Hyder was buried in the **Gumbaz** at Srirangapatna.

## Assessment

Hyder Ali was among those Indian rulers of the eighteenth century who first understood that the British could not be faced without modern weapons, a trained army and a strong treasury. His rule involved heavy tax collection, and local people in areas such as Malabar and Coorg still remember his campaigns for their harshness — historians hold differing views on this. Even so, his journey from soldier to state-builder and his firm resistance to the British make him an important figure in Indian history.
