## Background: From a Chandela Princess to Regent of the Gond Kingdom

**Durgavati** was born in 1524 CE, on the sacred day of Durgashtami, in the house of the mighty Chandela king Kirti Singh (Kirtivarman) of Mahoba. From childhood she was skilled in the use of weapons, archery and horse riding.

She was married to **Dalpat Shah**, the son of Sangram Shah, king of Garha-Mandla (Gondwana). This marriage created a historic political friendship between Rajput and Gond cultures. Only a few years after the marriage, Dalpat Shah died an untimely death. Placing her minor son **Vir Narayan** on the throne, the queen took the reins of the kingdom into her own hands.

## Skilled Administration and a Prosperous Gondwana

Rani Durgavati ruled Garha-Mandla for about 16 years. In her time the kingdom was a very prosperous realm spread over 70,000 inhabited villages and 52 garhs (forts):
- Abul Fazl wrote in the *Akbarnama* that the farmers of Garha paid their revenue in gold coins and elephants.
- The queen made Jabalpur a cultural centre and had a chain of lakes, ponds and stepwells built across the region for water management.
- When Baz Bahadur, the sultan of Malwa, marched against Gondwana, the queen defeated him so badly that he could never again look in that direction.

## The Mughal Invasion of 1564 and Betrayal

Seeing the immense wealth of Gondwana and the splendour of its elephants, the eyes of **Khwaja Abdul Majid (Asaf Khan)**, the Mughal governor of Kara-Manikpur, filled with greed. Without any provocation he marched on Gondwana with a large artillery force and cavalry.

With her feudal lords, the queen came out of Singorgarh Fort and took position in the dense forests and narrow valleys of **Narai Nala** near Jabalpur, so that the Mughal artillery would be rendered useless.

## 24 June 1564: The Immortal Sacrifice on the Battlefield

On the first day of the battle, riding her beloved elephant **Sarman**, the queen struck the Mughal army so fiercely that the Mughal force fled and the queen pursued them.

The next day the Mughals brought heavy cannon to the front:
- The queen's brave son, Prince Narayan, was wounded while fighting, and the queen sent him to a safe place.
- The queen herself kept slaying the enemy like the war goddess Ranachandi. Suddenly one arrow struck her right eye and another her neck.
- She pulled the arrow out, but she understood that there was now a danger of losing consciousness and being taken prisoner.
- Her mahout requested her to retreat, but the queen said: *"It is far better to give up one's life with honour on the battlefield than to be dishonoured alive at the hands of the Mughals."*
- She drew her dagger and plunged it into her own chest, making the supreme sacrifice for her motherland.

## Historical Significance

The self-sacrifice of Rani Durgavati became an immortal symbol of self-respect, courage and the power of women. Every year on **24 June** the whole country observes it as her 'Balidan Diwas' (Day of Sacrifice), and the university of Jabalpur is known by her name as 'Rani Durgavati University'.
