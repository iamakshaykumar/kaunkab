## The beginning at Bordowa

**Srimanta Sankardev** was born, according to the generally accepted date, in **1449** at **Bordowa** (Alipukhuri) in today's Nagaon district, in a **Siromoni Bhuyan** family. His parents died in his childhood and his grandmother raised him. He received his education in Sanskrit and the scriptures at the school of **Mahendra Kandali**. In Assam he is reverently called 'Mahapurusha'.

## Pilgrimage and a new vision

Around **1481** Sankardev set out on a long pilgrimage, which lasted about twelve years. During this time he visited places such as Puri, Vrindavan and Badrikashram and became acquainted with the bhakti streams running in other parts of the country. According to tradition, his first **borgeet** was composed at Badrikashram. His second long pilgrimage took place around 1550.

## Ek Sarana Nama Dharma

The path of devotion that Sankardev preached is called **Ek Sarana Nama Dharma**. Its essence is to take refuge in only one God, **Krishna**, and to hear and chant his name. It gave no importance to elaborate ritual, sacrifice and discrimination on the basis of caste. The **Bhagavata Purana** became the main text of this tradition.

Two major institutions of this faith are still spread across Assam:
- **Namghar**: a community prayer hall built in village after village, which became, along with name-chanting, the place for village meetings and social decisions.
- **Sattra**: centres of devotion, education and art, many of them on the river island of **Majuli** in the Brahmaputra.

## Literature, music and theatre

Sankardev was not merely a preacher of religion but also a versatile artist. His major contributions:
- **Kirtan Ghosha**: a collection of verses composed for group singing, read in almost every namghar of Assam.
- **Borgeet**: devotional songs based on ragas.
- **Ankiya Nat**: one-act plays, whose performance is called **Bhaona**. They use song, dance, dialogue and masks.
- **Sattriya dance**: a dance developed in the sattras, which the Sangeet Natak Akademi included among the classical dances of India on **15 November 2000**.

He wrote many of his works in **Brajavali**, a literary language made from a blend of Assamese and Braj. In his early production **Cihna Yatra**, painted scenery panels were used.

## Madhavdev and the Koch kingdom

After a theological debate, **Madhavdev**, of the Shakta tradition, became his disciple and later his successor. Sankardev's growing influence made some traditionalists hostile, and a complaint against him was made at the court of the Ahom king **Suhungmung**. After his son-in-law Hari was put to death in the 1540s, Sankardev left the Ahom kingdom and went west to the **Koch kingdom**.

The Koch king **Naranarayan** at first ordered his arrest, but at the urging of the king's brother and commander **Chilarai** he got the chance to present his case in court. After this he received freedom to preach. In this period, under his supervision, the famous **Vrindavani Vastra** with scenes of the Krishna-lila was woven.

## Death and legacy

In **1568**, Sankardev died at **Bhelamdonga** in today's Cooch Behar (West Bengal). Madhavdev and later acharyas spread his tradition across Assam. The namghar promoted democratic-style collective life in villages, and his works gave the Assamese language, music and theatre a lasting foundation. That is why he is regarded as the maker of Assamese society.
