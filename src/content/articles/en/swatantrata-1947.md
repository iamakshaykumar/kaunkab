## Background

The struggle for freedom that began in 1857 became a vast mass movement in the 20th century. The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920), Civil Disobedience and the Dandi March (1930), and the **Quit India Movement** (1942) shook the foundations of British rule. Subhas Chandra Bose's **Indian National Army** and the naval mutiny of 1946 also increased the pressure on the British.

After the Second World War (1939–45) Britain had become economically weak, and it became difficult for it to keep ruling India.

## The Road to Partition

In the 1940s the Muslim League intensified its demand for a separate country, **Pakistan**, and communal tension in the country kept growing. The last Viceroy, **Lord Mountbatten**, announced the partition plan on 3 June 1947. In July 1947 the British Parliament passed the **Indian Independence Act**.

## Freedom at Midnight

At midnight of 14-15 August 1947, in the Constituent Assembly, **Jawaharlal Nehru** gave his famous speech **"Tryst with Destiny"**. On 15 August India became independent. Nehru became the first Prime Minister, and **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** the first Deputy Prime Minister and Home Minister.

**Mahatma Gandhi** did not take part in the celebration. He was working in Kolkata to stop communal violence.

## The Tragedy of Partition

Along with independence the country was divided into two parts — India and Pakistan. A boundary line was drawn through Punjab and Bengal. One of the largest migrations in history took place — millions of families had to leave their homes, and in the terrible riots a large number of people were killed. The pain of it is still in the memories of many families today.

## The Road Ahead

After independence, under the leadership of Sardar Patel, more than 500 **princely states merged with India**. On 26 January 1950 the **Constitution** of India came into force and India became a republic. 15 August is today celebrated across the country as **Independence Day**.
