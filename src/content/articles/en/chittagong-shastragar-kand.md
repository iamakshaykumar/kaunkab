## Background: Masterda and His Band

From the early twentieth century revolutionary organisations such as the Anushilan Samiti and Jugantar had been active in Bengal. **Surya Sen**, a school teacher of Chittagong (today in Bangladesh) — whom his students and comrades called **'Masterda'** — entered politics during the Non-Cooperation Movement and gradually took the path of armed revolution.

Surya Sen built a disciplined band of young people in Chittagong. His plan was that one bold action would paralyse British administration in the district for a time and show that British power was not invincible. The band called itself the **'Indian Republican Army, Chittagong Branch'**.

## The Night of 18 April 1930

On the night of **18 April 1930** about 65 revolutionaries, divided into several groups, acted simultaneously:

- A group led by **Ganesh Ghosh** captured the police armoury.
- A second group led by **Lokenath Bal** raided the armoury of the Auxiliary Force.
- Other groups cut the telephone and telegraph lines and disrupted rail links.

The revolutionaries got weapons but could not find ammunition — this proved the greatest weakness of the plan. After this the revolutionaries hoisted the national flag and Surya Sen proclaimed a provisional revolutionary government. Then the band left the city and went towards the nearby hills.

## The Battle of Jalalabad Hill

On **22 April 1930** government troops surrounded the revolutionaries camped on **Jalalabad Hill**. In the face-to-face fight twelve revolutionaries were killed, many of them teenagers. Different claims are found about the number of casualties on the British side. The surviving revolutionaries scattered into small groups and, hiding in villages, kept up guerrilla actions.

## Pritilata Waddedar and the Later Struggle

For the next few years the clashes between police and revolutionaries in the Chittagong region continued. Villagers repeatedly took risks to shelter the revolutionaries.

On **24 September 1932** Surya Sen's comrade **Pritilata Waddedar** led an attack on the **European Club** at Pahartali. After being wounded, she swallowed cyanide to avoid arrest. **Kalpana Dutta** was also a leading woman member of this movement, and was later sentenced to transportation for life.

In the armoury case verdict delivered on 1 March 1932, twelve accused, including Ganesh Ghosh, Lokenath Bal and Ananta Singh, were sentenced to transportation for life (Kala Pani).

## The Hanging of Masterda

On **16 February 1933**, on information from an informer, Surya Sen was captured in Gairala village. After the trial he was hanged in Chittagong Jail on **12 January 1934** along with his comrade **Tarakeswar Dastidar**. According to popular accounts he was treated inhumanly before the hanging, but no official record of this is available.

## Significance

The Chittagong uprising did not reach its goal militarily, but it showed that even a small, disciplined band could shake the administration of an empire for a time. The participation of young people, especially young women, in it gave a new direction to Bengal's revolutionary movement. Today Surya Sen and Pritilata are remembered with respect in both India and Bangladesh.
