## A New Dynasty After Civil War

In the eighth century, in the time of Lalitaditya Muktapida, Kashmir's **Karkota dynasty** was at its peak, but by the ninth century the dynasty had become weak. For about forty years there was a tussle for power and civil war. In this turmoil a man named **Utpala** and his brothers gained influence.

Utpala's grandson **Avantivarman** took the throne in **855 CE** and founded the **Utpala dynasty**. According to Kalhana he had been raised and guided by the minister **Shura**. Avantivarman curbed the unruliness of the rural landlords — the **Damaras** — and restored peace in the kingdom.

## Suyya: Kashmir's Engineer

The greatest hallmark of Avantivarman's reign is his minister **Suyya**, who in today's words can be called a **water engineer**. The Kashmir Valley suffered repeated floods, and because water stood on it, much land remained marshy. According to the Rajatarangini, Suyya:
- had silt removed from the bed of the **Vitasta (Jhelum)** river and the rocks that blocked its flow cleared away;
- had **embankments** built along the river;
- in some places had the confluences of rivers and the direction of the current changed, so that marshy land became fit for farming;
- fixed the **distribution of water** for each village according to its needs.

Kalhana writes that after these works so much grain was produced that the price of rice fell sharply. **Suyyapura**, founded in Suyya's name, is today the town of **Sopore** in Baramulla district.

## The Story of the Coins

The Rajatarangini also has an interesting **story**: to get labourers to enter the water for cleaning the river, coins from the treasury were thrown into the water. People kept removing silt to find the coins, and the river went on becoming clean. Rather than taking this as literal history, it is better to understand it as a **folk tale** that shows the fame of Suyya's ingenuity.

## Avantipura and Its Temples

Avantivarman founded the city of **Avantipura** on the bank of the Vitasta — today's **Awantipora** (Pulwama district), between Srinagar and Anantnag. Here he built two large temples:
- **Avantiswami** — dedicated to Vishnu
- **Avantishvara** — dedicated to Shiva

Both temples were built in the Kashmiri architectural style — high plinths, enclosure walls of huge stones, pillared galleries and triangular pediments. Today they are ruins in the care of the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), but their carvings still show the craftsmanship of that period.

## A Court of Scholars

Avantivarman's time is also remembered for literature. **Anandavardhana**, who established the 'dhvani' theory of poetry in the 'Dhvanyaloka', belonged to this period. The name of the poet **Ratnakara** (author of the 'Haravijaya') is also linked with this court.

## End and Legacy

Avantivarman died in **883 CE**. After him there was again a succession dispute in the Utpala family, after which his son **Shankaravarman** (c. 885–902) became king. Avantivarman's reign is a rare example of how, in medieval India, the prosperity of a kingdom was credited more to **water management and farming** than to wars.
