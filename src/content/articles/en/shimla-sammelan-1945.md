## Background: the end of the war and a deadlock

By mid-1945 the **Second World War** had ended in Europe, although the fight against Japan was still going on. In India, since the **Quit India Movement** (1942), almost all the major Congress leaders were in jail and a political deadlock continued. On the other side, the **Muslim League**, under the leadership of **Muhammad Ali Jinnah**, was firm on the demand for "Pakistan" and claimed to be the sole representative of Indian Muslims.

Britain knew that after the war it would be difficult to rule India in the old way. Viceroy **Lord Wavell** went to London, obtained the government's permission for talks and returned, and in **June 1945** the members of the Congress Working Committee were released.

## The Wavell Plan (14 June 1945)

The main points of the **Wavell Plan**, announced on **14 June 1945**, were:

- The Viceroy's **Executive Council** would be reconstituted so that, apart from the **Viceroy and the Commander-in-Chief**, **all members would be Indian**.
- In the Council the number of **caste Hindus and Muslims** would be **equal**; Scheduled Castes and other minorities would also get places.
- This Council would function like an **interim government** within the framework of the Act of 1935.
- **Defence** would remain in British hands until power was transferred.
- The work of framing a new constitution would be taken up after the war.

The plan contained neither a date for independence nor a clear promise of a Constituent Assembly. Even so, it was the first time that a proposal was made to hand over the central executive government almost entirely to Indians.

## The Simla Conference (25 June – 14 July 1945)

To obtain agreement on this plan, Wavell called a conference on **25 June 1945** at the **Viceregal Lodge** in Shimla. Leading figures of the Congress, the Muslim League, the Sikhs, the Scheduled Castes and the provinces took part. The Congress was represented by its president, **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad**. **Gandhiji** was not a formal delegate, but he was present in Shimla, and both the Viceroy and the Congress Working Committee kept consulting him.

## Why the conference broke down

The main point of dispute was: **who would nominate the Muslim members of the Council?**

- **Jinnah** insisted that **all the Muslim members of the Council be nominated only by the Muslim League**, because the League alone was the sole representative of Muslims. He feared that, joined with other minorities, the League would be left in a minority in the Council, so he also wanted special safeguards against decisions that went against Muslim interests.
- The **Congress** regarded itself as a national organisation of the whole country, not of any one community, and its president was himself Maulana Azad. So it was not ready to give up the right to include Muslims in its list. Other non-League Muslim leaders also did not agree with the League's claim.

Wavell was not ready to accept this condition of the League, nor did he think it proper to go ahead without the League. Jinnah refused to submit a list of names on behalf of the League, and on **14 July 1945** the conference ended without any agreement. According to sources, Wavell took the responsibility for the failure on himself rather than placing it on any party.

## Consequences and importance

Historians regard the failure of the Simla Conference as an important turning point. Jinnah's "veto" showed that no constitutional solution could move forward without the League's consent, and this strengthened his position as the **spokesman of the Muslims**. Some historians also say that the Viceroy's not going ahead without the League itself gave Jinnah so much power.

In July 1945 a **Labour Party** government was formed in Britain. In August, after Japan's surrender, Wavell announced central and provincial **elections** and a constitution-making body. In the elections of 1945–46 the Congress won overwhelmingly in the general seats and the Muslim League in the Muslim seats. After this the **Cabinet Mission** came in 1946, and new negotiations took the place of the Wavell Plan.
