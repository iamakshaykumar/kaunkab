## From Lachman Dev to Banda Singh

Banda Singh Bahadur was born on **27 October 1670** at **Rajouri** (in present-day Jammu and Kashmir). His name was **Lachman Dev**. According to tradition, the incident of killing a pregnant doe while hunting awakened a spirit of renunciation in him. He became a Bairagi ascetic, was called **Madho Das**, and finally set up his hermitage at **Nanded** on the Godavari in the Deccan.

In **1708** Guru Gobind Singh reached Nanded and met Madho Das. After this meeting Madho Das called himself the Guru's "banda" (servant) and, taking amrit, became **Banda Singh**. The Guru gave him the title "Bahadur", a few companions and the order to go to Punjab and fight injustice. In October of the same year Guru Gobind Singh died at Nanded.

## The Campaign in Punjab

On reaching Punjab, large numbers of Sikhs and poor peasants began to join Banda Singh. After Sonepat and Kaithal, he captured **Samana** in **November 1709**, and then won several towns including Sadhaura.

The biggest target was **Sirhind**, whose faujdar **Wazir Khan** was held responsible for the death of Guru Gobind Singh's younger sons. On **12 May 1710**, the Mughal army was defeated in the Battle of **Chappar Chiri** and Wazir Khan was killed. Within a few days Sirhind came under Sikh control. There was much bloodshed in these campaigns; contemporary Persian historians have also exaggerated the description of plunder and violence at Sirhind and Samana, so historians read these accounts with care.

## Lohgarh: A New Rule

Banda Singh renamed the fort of Mukhlisgarh in the Shivalik foothills **Lohgarh** and made it his capital (in Yamunanagar district of present-day Haryana). He issued his own **coins** and **seal**, which bore the names of Guru Nanak and Guru Gobind Singh — a declaration that authority was in the name of the Guru, not in the name of any individual.

## Land Reforms for Peasants

Banda Singh is remembered most for breaking the hold of big **zamindars** in many areas and recognising the **peasants** who tilled the land as its owners. This increased his popularity among the lower castes and poor peasants. Historians debate how systematic and widespread this reform was, since his rule lasted a very short time; but almost all accept its social impact.

## Struggle, Capture and Death

The Mughal emperor **Bahadur Shah I** himself marched against Lohgarh at the end of 1710. The fort fell, but Banda Singh escaped into the hills and continued the struggle. In the time of Emperor **Farrukhsiyar**, the Mughal army besieged him in the fortified house at **Gurdas Nangal** (near Gurdaspur). After a siege of about eight months, the Sikhs, exhausted by hunger, were captured in **December 1715**.

Banda Singh and hundreds of companions were brought to Delhi in chains. His companions were killed daily in batches. On **9 June 1716**, Banda Singh and his young son **Ajay Singh** were executed with extreme cruelty.

## Legacy

Banda Singh Bahadur showed for the first time that a Sikh political power could be raised against the Mughals in Punjab. Even after his death this idea did not fade — in the 18th century the Sikh misls emerged and later the kingdom of Maharaja Ranjit Singh was formed. Even today he is remembered as a ruler who favoured the peasants and as a courageous commander.
