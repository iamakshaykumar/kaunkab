## The Road to the Peshwaship

Bajirao was born on **18 August 1700** in the Sinnar area near Nashik. His father **Balaji Vishwanath** was the first influential Peshwa of Chhatrapati Shahu, and had obtained from the Mughal court the right to collect **chauth** and **sardeshmukhi** in the Deccan. From childhood Bajirao accompanied his father on campaigns.

After Balaji Vishwanath's death in 1720, despite the opposition of several old chiefs, Shahu appointed the nineteen- or twenty-year-old Bajirao as Peshwa on **17 April 1720**. This decision proved very far-reaching in Maratha history.

## The Policy of Expansion Northwards

Bajirao believed that, taking advantage of the weakening Mughal empire, the Marathas should advance out of the Deccan towards the north. In the court some favoured first consolidating the position in the south, but Shahu in the end backed Bajirao's policy. His greatest strength was a light, fast-moving cavalry, which covered long distances without heavy artillery and suddenly surrounded the enemy.

## Palkhed: The Decisive Gamble Against the Nizam

The biggest rival in the Deccan was **Nizam-ul-Mulk** of Hyderabad, who was encouraging the Kolhapur party against Shahu. In the campaign of 1727–28 Bajirao avoided a direct clash, kept chasing the Nizam's army, and finally in **February 1728**, at **Palkhed** near Aurangabad, surrounded it in a place where supplies and water were cut off. Without a major battle the Nizam had to yield, and on **6 March 1728** the **Treaty of Mungi-Shevgaon** was concluded, in which Shahu's position as Chhatrapati and the Marathas' tax rights were accepted. Military historians regard Palkhed as an outstanding example of the skill of mobile warfare.

## Bundelkhand, Malwa and Delhi

In **1729** **Chhatrasal**, the king of Bundelkhand, asked Bajirao for help against the Mughal commander Muhammad Khan Bangash. Bajirao forced Bangash to retreat, and Chhatrasal gave him a part of his kingdom as a jagir. According to tradition, Chhatrasal's daughter **Mastani** also married Bajirao; many things about Mastani's life are not known for certain.

In the same decade chiefs such as Malharrao Holkar and Ranoji Shinde strengthened the Maratha hold over **Malwa**. In **March 1737** Bajirao, slipping past the Mughal armies, rapidly reached the outskirts of **Delhi** and defeated a Mughal detachment there. He did not occupy the capital, but this raid shook the court of the emperor **Muhammad Shah**.

After this the Nizam came with a large army on behalf of the Mughals. In **December 1737**, near **Bhopal**, Bajirao surrounded him, and in the **Treaty of Doraha** of **7 January 1738** the Nizam promised to get Malwa for the Marathas.

## Last Years and Death

In **1739** Bajirao's brother **Chimaji Appa** won the fort of **Vasai** from the Portuguese. In the same year Nadir Shah's invasion exposed the weakness of North India. In his personal life there was tension over Mastani with the family and the Brahmin society of Pune. On **28 April 1740**, during a campaign at **Raverkhedi** on the bank of the Narmada, he died of illness. At that time he was only 39 years old.

## Assessment

In twenty years Bajirao turned Maratha power from a regional state into a decisive force in North Indian politics, and the foundation of the later Maratha confederacy — the Holkars, Shindes and others — was laid in his time. It is popularly said that he never lost a battle; in his major battles this is largely true, but in some campaigns the Marathas also suffered setbacks, so it should not be taken literally. His campaigns also involved heavy tax collection and plunder, the burden of which fell on local peasants — this side too is part of his history.
