## From Khandra Village to the Javelin

**Neeraj Chopra** was born on **24 December 1997** into a farming family in **Khandra** village in Panipat district, Haryana. As a child he was sent to the stadium in Panipat to lose weight, where he watched other athletes throwing the javelin and tried it himself. His talent soon became clear, and he began training at the Tau Devi Lal Stadium in Panchkula.

At the **2016** World Junior (Under-20) Championships in Poland he set a junior world record with a throw of **86.48 metres**. The same year he joined the **Indian Army** as a Junior Commissioned Officer (Naib Subedar). In **2018** he won gold at the Gold Coast Commonwealth Games (86.47 m) and the Jakarta Asian Games (88.06 m). After that, an elbow injury and surgery kept him out of action for almost all of 2019, and then the Covid pandemic postponed the Tokyo Olympics by a year.

## The Historic Final in Tokyo

In the qualifying round of the Tokyo Olympics (called "Tokyo 2020" but held in 2021), Neeraj threw **86.65 metres** with his very first attempt to top his group. In the final on the evening of **7 August 2021** he threw **87.03 metres** in his first attempt, and in his second the javelin reached **87.58 metres**. No other athlete could match that distance.

Germany's **Johannes Vetter**, regarded beforehand as the strong favourite for the title, failed to make the top eight of the final. **Jakub Vadlejch** of the Czech Republic (86.67 m) took silver and his compatriot **Vitezslav Vesely** (85.44 m) took bronze. Pakistan's **Arshad Nadeem** finished fifth.

This was **India's first medal in Olympic athletics** — independent India had never before won a track and field medal. It was also the second individual Olympic gold by an Indian, after **Abhinav Bindra** (2008, shooting). At the time Neeraj's coach was Germany's **Klaus Bartonietz**; before that, former javelin world record holder **Uwe Hohn** had also trained him.

## Welcome at Home

After the gold Neeraj became famous across the country as the "Golden Boy". He was honoured with the **Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna** (2021) and the **Padma Shri** (2022). To mark the victory, the Athletics Federation of India began celebrating **7 August** as Javelin Day. After his success, interest in the javelin grew rapidly among many young people in the country.

## Further Achievements

After Tokyo Neeraj kept winning major titles:

- **2022** — silver at the World Championships in Eugene, USA, and on **8 September 2022** victory in the **Diamond League Final** in Zurich with 88.44 metres.
- **2023** — gold at the **World Championships** in **Budapest**, Hungary (88.17 m); this was India's first gold at the World Athletics Championships. The same year he also won gold at the Hangzhou Asian Games with 88.88 metres.
- **2024** — on **8 August 2024** at the **Paris Olympics** he won **silver** with a throw of 89.45 metres. The gold went to Pakistan's Arshad Nadeem, who set an Olympic record of 92.97 metres. With this Neeraj joined the select group of Indian athletes who have won both gold and silver at the Olympics.
- **2025** — on **16 May 2025** at the **Doha Diamond League** he threw **90.23 metres**, crossing the 90-metre mark for the first time and setting a new national record. In this period his coach was three-time Olympic champion and world record holder **Jan Zelezny**. However, at the 2025 World Championships held in Tokyo he finished eighth.

## Significance

Neeraj Chopra's Tokyo gold is regarded as a defining moment in Indian sporting history. For decades India's Olympic identity was tied mainly to hockey, wrestling, shooting and boxing, while Indian athletes kept missing out on medals in athletics. Neeraj not only filled that gap but, by staying among the world's top javelin throwers for years, showed that Indian athletes can compete consistently at world level. His success gave young athletes from small villages and towns the confidence to dream big. Today far more athletes than before take part in javelin competitions in India, and several Indian javelin throwers have begun reaching the finals of international competitions.
