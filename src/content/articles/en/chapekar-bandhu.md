## Plague and Pune

In 1896 **bubonic plague** broke out in Bombay and soon reached Pune. To check it, the government took extraordinary powers under the Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897, and in Pune formed a **Special Plague Committee** headed by **Walter Charles Rand**. From the beginning of 1897 the committee, with the help of soldiers, began actions such as house-to-house searches, forcibly sending patients and suspects to hospitals or camps, burning belongings, and public examination of people.

From a medical point of view some of these steps were understandable, but the harsh and humiliating manner in which they were carried out — entering homes and places of worship wearing shoes, public examination of women — spread great anger in Pune. Complaints were ignored.

## The Chapekar Brothers

The three sons of Hari Chapekar, a kirtankar (devotional preacher) of **Chinchwad** near Pune — **Damodar**, **Balkrishna** and **Vasudeo** — were staunch opponents of British rule. They had formed an association of young men that practised exercise and the use of arms. They regarded the Plague Committee's actions as an attack on their religion and honour, and resolved to kill Rand.

## The Night of 22 June 1897

On **22 June 1897** there was a celebration of **Queen Victoria's Diamond Jubilee** (60 years of her reign). At night, as Rand and his military escort **Lieutenant Charles Egerton Ayerst** were returning from the function at Government House in Ganeshkhind, **Damodar** and **Balkrishna** fired at them. Ayerst died on the spot. Rand, seriously wounded, died on **3 July 1897**.

## Arrest, Revenge and Hangings

For some time the attackers could not be traced. Then the **Dravid brothers**, tempted by a reward, informed the police and Damodar was caught. He was hanged at Yerawada Jail on **18 April 1898**. Balkrishna remained at large until January 1899, and was then caught.

In February 1899 Vasudeo Chapekar, together with his companions **Mahadev Vinayak Ranade** and **Khando Sathe**, killed the Dravid brothers and also tried to shoot at a police officer. After this **Vasudeo** was hanged on 8 May, **Ranade** on 10 May and **Balkrishna** on 12 May 1899. Sathe was a minor, so he was given ten years' rigorous imprisonment.

## Tilak and the Sedition Trial

**Bal Gangadhar Tilak** was an outspoken critic of the Plague Committee's methods, and sharp articles on this had appeared in his paper 'Kesari'. After the Rand assassination the government prosecuted Tilak for sedition and he was imprisoned for 18 months. No solid proof of a direct link between Tilak and the Chapekars came out in court; this allegation has been a subject of debate ever since.

## Historical Significance

The Rand assassination is counted among the first planned political killings of a British officer in India. It showed how the insensitivity of the colonial administration could give rise to a violent reaction. The Chapekar brothers are remembered in Maharashtra as the early martyrs of the revolutionary movement, and many later revolutionary organisations drew inspiration from them.
