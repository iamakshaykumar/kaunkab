## The Partition of Bengal and the Revolutionary Surge

The partition of Bengal in 1905 created deep anger among the youth of Bengal. Along with the Swadeshi movement, preparations for armed resistance also began in secret organisations such as the **Anushilan Samiti**. At Maniktala in Calcutta, Barindra Kumar Ghose and his companions were experimenting with making bombs; **Hemchandra Kanungo** had given training in bomb-making.

**Khudiram Bose** was born on **3 December 1889** in the village of Habibpur in Midnapore district. He lost his parents in childhood and was brought up by his elder sister. In his teens he joined the revolutionary group of Midnapore and became active in the Swadeshi movement.

## The Target: Kingsford

**Douglas Kingsford** had been Chief Presidency Magistrate of Calcutta. Because of the harsh sentences he gave against Swadeshi activists and nationalist newspapers, he had become a symbol of repression in the eyes of the revolutionaries. In early 1908 he was promoted and sent to **Muzaffarpur** in Bihar as District Judge. The revolutionaries planned to attack him there, and the task was given to Khudiram and **Prafulla Chaki**.

## The Night of 30 April 1908

For some days the two young men kept watch on Kingsford's routine. On the evening of **30 April 1908**, at about half past eight, a bomb was thrown at a carriage coming out of the European Club, taking it to be Kingsford's carriage. But Kingsford was not in it — it carried the **wife and daughter** of the barrister Pringle Kennedy. The daughter died a short while later and the mother on 2 May.

It is not certain who actually threw the bomb; at the trial the defence argued that Prafulla must have thrown it.

## Arrest and Trial

After the attack the two fled in different directions. The next morning Khudiram was caught at **Waini** station. When the police surrounded Prafulla Chaki at **Mokama Ghat**, he shot himself to avoid arrest.

Khudiram was tried at Muzaffarpur. On **13 June 1908** he was sentenced to death, and the Calcutta High Court rejected his appeal in July. Meanwhile, the investigation of this incident led to the discovery of the Maniktala bomb centre and the **Alipore Bomb Case** began, in which Aurobindo Ghose was also an accused.

## 11 August 1908: The Hanging

On the morning of **11 August 1908**, Khudiram Bose was hanged in Muzaffarpur jail. He was only **18 years old** at the time. He is counted among the youngest revolutionary martyrs of India.

## Reaction and Legacy

Nationalist leaders were divided in their opinion of the incident. Many regarded the deaths of innocent women as regrettable, and the Moderates condemned the violence. On the other hand, **Tilak** wrote in 'Kesari' that the government's policy of repression itself was pushing the youth onto this path — after this he was tried for sedition and sent to Mandalay.

In Bengal, Khudiram soon became a folk hero. Folk songs were composed about him, Kazi Nazrul Islam wrote a poem, and in 1990 the Indian postal service issued a postage stamp on him. A metro station in Kolkata is also named after him.
