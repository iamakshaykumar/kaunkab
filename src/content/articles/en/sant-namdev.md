## A Devotee from a Tailor Family

According to tradition, **Namdev**'s lifetime is taken to be **1270 to 1350** CE, although the details of his life are hazy and some scholars place him a little earlier. He is linked with the village of **Narsi Bamani** in Hingoli district of Maharashtra and especially with **Pandharpur**. He belonged to the **Shimpi** (tailor) community, which was counted as Shudra in the varna system of that time. His wife is said to have been named Rajai.

From childhood he was an exclusive devotee of **Vitthal** (Vithoba) of Pandharpur. Later saint legends contain many miracles of his childhood, such as the idol of Vitthal accepting the food offering from his hand. These stories are part of devotion; many of them were written centuries after him and do not even agree with one another.

## Companion of Dnyaneshwar

Namdev was a contemporary of the saint **Dnyaneshwar**. According to the Warkari tradition, he made a long pilgrimage to the holy places of north India with Dnyaneshwar and his siblings. Tradition also says that at the urging of Dnyaneshwar's circle he took **Visoba Khechar** as his guru, from whom he learned that God is not only in the idol in a temple but everywhere.

**Janabai**, who lived in his household, also went on to become a famous saint-poet. In this way a community of devotees of different castes, women and men, formed around Namdev, which is the hallmark of the Warkari tradition.

## Abhangs and Kirtan

Namdev composed **abhangs** in Marathi and popularised **kirtan**, collective devotion through singing and dancing. About 2,500 abhangs are in circulation in his name, but scholars estimate that only 600–700 of them may be authentic.

In his devotion God is both the embodied Vitthal and the formless Truth. He criticises caste distinctions and showy ritualism. A famous story concerns the **Aundha Nagnath** temple: it is said that when the priests moved him from the front, he went to the back and began singing kirtan, and the temple turned to face him. This is a folk tale, but it gives a glimpse of the struggle of devotees from the castes then called "low".

## Punjab and the Guru Granth Sahib

Namdev also composed verses in Hindi (an old language mixing Hindi and Punjabi), through which his utterances reached north India. According to tradition, he spent more than twenty years of his life in the village of **Ghuman** in Gurdaspur district of Punjab, where there is a gurdwara in his memory today.

When the fifth Sikh Guru, **Guru Arjan Dev**, compiled the Adi Granth in 1604, he included **61 hymns** of Namdev (in 18 ragas). Sikh tradition respectfully calls him **Bhagat Namdev**. His influence is also believed on later saints such as Kabir, Ravidas and Pipa.

## Death and Legacy

According to the Warkari tradition, Namdev left his body at Pandharpur around 1350, and the first step at the main gate of the Vitthal temple is called "**Namdev Payri**"; it is believed he wished to stay there so that the feet of every devotee would touch him. Some traditions link Ghuman with him as well.

Today Namdev is remembered as a saint who joined two worlds, Marathi and Punjabi, Hindu and Sikh. In 1970 the Government of India issued a postage stamp on his 700th birth anniversary.
