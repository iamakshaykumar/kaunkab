## Four Siblings and a Hard Childhood

According to tradition, **Dnyaneshwar** (Dnyandev or Mauli) was born in **1275 CE** at **Apegaon**, near Paithan on the Godavari. His father was **Vitthalpant** and his mother **Rukminibai**. His elder brother **Nivruttinath**, younger brother **Sopandev** and sister **Muktabai**: all four later became famous as saints.

Most of what we know of his life comes from later saint legends, in which history and miracle stories are blended together. According to tradition, Vitthalpant, after taking sannyasa, returned to household life at his guru's command, so society boycotted the family. The children were even denied rites such as the sacred thread ceremony. According to the stories, after their parents died the orphaned children went to the scholars of Paithan to ask for a certificate of purification.

## Initiation in the Nath Tradition

Tradition says that Nivruttinath was initiated by the Nath yogi **Gahininath**, and that he in turn made Dnyaneshwar his disciple. This is why Dnyaneshwar's thought shows traces of three streams: Nath yoga, Advaita Vedanta and devotion to Vitthal.

## The Dnyaneshwari: The Gita in the People's Language

Dnyaneshwar's most famous work is the **Dnyaneshwari** (Bhavartha-Deepika), a Marathi commentary on the Bhagavad Gita in ovi metre. The end of the text itself states that it was completed in **Shaka 1212, that is 1290 CE**, when the Yadava king Ramachandra was ruling. According to tradition, Dnyaneshwar delivered it as a discourse at **Nevasa**, and a disciple named **Sachchidanand** wrote it down.

In that period the language of philosophy and scripture was mainly Sanskrit. Dnyaneshwar explained the deep ideas of the Gita in Marathi with everyday examples and beautiful similes, so that they would reach ordinary people. The Dnyaneshwari and his other work, the **Amritanubhav**, are counted among the oldest available literary works in Marathi. The prayer at the end of the Dnyaneshwari, the **Pasayadan**, is still widely sung in Maharashtra.

## Abhangs, the Haripath and Changdev

The abhangs of the **Haripath** and several devotional verses are also attributed to Dnyaneshwar, and Warkaris sing them daily. The **Changdev Pasashti** is a letter of 65 ovis that he is believed to have written to the yogi Changdev.

A famous **legend** connected to this says that Changdev came to meet Dnyaneshwar riding a tiger, and the very wall on which Dnyaneshwar and his siblings were sitting moved forward to welcome him. Another story tells of Dnyaneshwar making a buffalo recite the Vedas. These stories are part of the devotional tradition, not historical evidence.

## Samadhi at Alandi

According to tradition, in **1296 CE**, at about the age of 21, Dnyaneshwar took **sanjeevan samadhi** at **Alandi** on the bank of the Indrayani river near Pune, that is, he entered samadhi alive, in meditation. Alandi is today one of the biggest pilgrimage centres of Maharashtra. Tradition says that his siblings too left their bodies soon after.

## Foundation of the Warkari Tradition

Dnyaneshwar is regarded as the foundation pillar of the **Warkari** tradition of devotion to **Vitthal** of Pandharpur. His contemporary **Namdev** and later saints such as **Eknath** and **Tukaram** carried this stream forward. In the sixteenth century Eknath did the work of preparing a corrected text of the Dnyaneshwari. In the Warkari tradition it is said that Dnyandev laid the foundation of this temple of devotion and Tukaram became its crowning finial. Even today, walking with his palanquin in the Ashadhi Wari is, for lakhs of people, a festival of faith and togetherness.
