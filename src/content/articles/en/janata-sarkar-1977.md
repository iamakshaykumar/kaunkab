## From the Emergency to the Election

During the **Emergency** imposed on **25 June 1975**, thousands of opposition leaders were in jail, newspapers were censored and civil rights were suspended. On **18 January 1977** Prime Minister **Indira Gandhi** suddenly announced Lok Sabha elections and released many leaders. Her supporters believed the Congress would win easily.

The opposition leaders who came out of jail decided to unite, inspired by **Jayaprakash Narayan** (JP). On **23 January 1977** parties such as the **Congress (O)**, the **Bharatiya Jana Sangh**, the **Bharatiya Lok Dal** and the **Socialist Party** came together to form the **Janata Party**. Because time was short, its candidates contested on the **Bharatiya Lok Dal** symbol, the **"farmer with a plough"**. In February **Jagjivan Ram**, a senior member of Indira Gandhi's cabinet, left the Congress, formed the **Congress for Democracy** and joined hands with the Janata Party. This greatly strengthened the opposition.

## The Historic Mandate of 1977

Voting took place from **16 to 20 March 1977**. The opposition turned the election into a question of **"democracy versus dictatorship"**. Forced **sterilisation**, arrests and censorship during the Emergency were the main causes of public anger.

The results were stunning. The Janata Party and its allies won about **298** of the **542** seats, while the Congress was reduced to **154** seats. The Congress was almost wiped out in north India — in **Uttar Pradesh** and **Bihar** it did not win a single seat. In **Rae Bareli** Indira Gandhi herself lost to **Raj Narain**, and her son **Sanjay Gandhi** lost from **Amethi**. On the other hand, the Congress did well in the southern states.

On **21 March 1977** the Emergency was formally lifted. Within the Janata Party the names of **Morarji Desai**, **Charan Singh** and **Jagjivan Ram** were in contention for the leadership; on the advice of JP and Acharya Kripalani, Desai was chosen. On **24 March 1977** the **81-year-old Morarji Desai** was sworn in as **independent India's first non-Congress Prime Minister**.

## Main Work of the Government

In Desai's cabinet **Charan Singh** became Home Minister, **Jagjivan Ram** Defence Minister, **Atal Bihari Vajpayee** External Affairs Minister, **Lal Krishna Advani** Minister of Information and Broadcasting, and **George Fernandes** Minister of Industry. The government's main steps were:

- **Inquiry into the Emergency:** The **Shah Commission** was set up to investigate the excesses of the Emergency.
- **44th Constitutional Amendment (1978):** Many changes made during the Emergency were reversed. A national emergency could now be declared not on grounds of "internal disturbance" but only in case of **"armed rebellion"**, and only on the written advice of the cabinet. The **right to property** was removed from the list of fundamental rights and made a legal right.
- **Freedom of the press:** Censorship was lifted and the freedom of newspapers was restored.
- **Economic policy:** Rules for foreign companies were tightened, after which companies such as **Coca-Cola** and **IBM** left India. In **January 1978** the large notes of 1,000, 5,000 and 10,000 rupees were demonetised.
- **Foreign policy:** Efforts were made to improve relations with Pakistan and China; Vajpayee visited China in **1979**. The Desai government refused to sign the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty.

## Internal Strife and Fall

The Janata Party was a combination of parties with different ideologies, and from the start there was a tussle between its big leaders — Desai, Charan Singh and Jagjivan Ram. One major dispute was over **"dual membership"**: people such as the socialist leader **Madhu Limaye** argued that members of the former Jana Sangh could not remain linked to the **Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh** while also being in the Janata Party, while the leaders who had come from the Jana Sangh rejected this.

In 1978 Charan Singh was dropped from the cabinet, and in January 1979 he returned as Deputy Prime Minister. In July 1979 many MPs, including **Raj Narain** and supporters of Charan Singh, broke away from the party and the government was reduced to a minority. Amid a no-confidence motion, Morarji Desai resigned on **15 July 1979**.

On **28 July 1979** **Charan Singh** became Prime Minister with outside support from Indira Gandhi's Congress, but the Congress withdrew its support even before he could face the Lok Sabha. The Lok Sabha was dissolved, and in the **January 1980** election Indira Gandhi returned to power with a huge majority; the Janata Party was reduced to just 31 seats.

## Legacy

The Janata government lasted less than two and a half years, but its significance is very great. It proved for the first time that in India voters could change power at the Centre peacefully through the ballot. The **44th Amendment** provided constitutional safeguards so that a situation like the Emergency could not easily arise again. Leaders and factions that emerged from the Janata Party shaped later Indian politics — in 1980 leaders of the former Jana Sangh formed the **Bharatiya Janata Party**, and parties such as the **Janata Dal** later grew out of the socialist factions. The 1977 Janata government was the first big laboratory of the era of coalition governments that followed.
