## The Sannyasi Who Settled in the Hills

**Alluri Sitarama Raju** was born on 4 July, but sources differ on whether the year was 1897 or 1898; as his birthplace, too, three names come up — Pandrangi (near Visakhapatnam), Bhimavaram and Mogallu. He lost his father in childhood. After studying at Kakinada and Visakhapatnam, at about the age of 18 he chose the path of sannyasa and made long pilgrimages.

Around 1921 he began living among the tribal people in the Eastern Ghat hills of Godavari and Visakhapatnam districts — the **Rampa** and **Gudem** areas. Because of his knowledge of herbs and his simple life, the Koya and other hill communities came to regard him as a miracle-working saint.

## The Tribal People's Grievances

British rule had increased government control over the forests through the **Madras Forest Act (1882)**. The tribals' traditional **'podu'** (shifting or jhum) cultivation, wood cutting and gathering of forest produce were banned. **Begar** (unpaid labour) was also being extracted for building roads in the hills. The old chiefs of the area, called **muttadars**, were angry at the loss of their authority, and some of them also joined the rebellion. Raju linked these grievances with opposition to British rule.

## Attacks on the Police Stations (August 1922)

Between **22 and 24 August 1922**, about 500 of Raju's companions attacked the police stations at **Chintapalli**, **Krishnadevipeta** and **Rajavommangi** and carried off guns and ammunition. The Koya brothers named **Gam Gantam Dora** and **Gam Mallu Dora** became his chief associates. On 23 September 1922, in an ambush in the Dammanapalli ghat, two British officers were killed. Later, attacks were also made on police stations such as Annavaram, Addateegala, Narsipatnam and Rampachodavaram.

## Guerrilla Fighting and Repression

The rebels fought in guerrilla style, relying on dense forest and their knowledge of hill paths — attacking suddenly and then vanishing into the forest. The government sent detachments of the **Malabar Special Police** and the **Assam Rifles**. A reward of 10,000 rupees was announced on Raju and 1,000 rupees each on the two Dora brothers. Villages were dealt with harshly on suspicion of sheltering the rebels. In April 1924 **T. G. Rutherford** was specially given command of the operation; in his time there are recorded allegations of pressure and torture on villagers.

## 7 May 1924

On **7 May 1924** Raju was captured in the forests of Chintapalli and shot near **Koyyuru** without any trial. Accounts of the circumstances of his arrest differ — according to some accounts he surrendered himself to save the villagers from torture, while other accounts describe it as a capture in a cordon. In June 1924 Gantam Dora was also killed and Mallu Dora was imprisoned; the same Mallu Dora was elected a member of the Lok Sabha in 1952, after independence. Raju's samadhi is at Krishnadevipeta.

## Significance

The Rampa rebellion took place at the very time the Non-Cooperation Movement was under way in the country, but its path was not non-violence but armed resistance. Historians regard it as an example of the meeting of the tribals' struggle for forest rights and the national movement. In the folk memory of Andhra Pradesh, Raju is remembered as **'Manyam Veerudu'** (Hero of the Forest). A postage stamp was issued on him in 1986, in 2022 the **Alluri Sitarama Raju district** was created, and many memorials and institutions are named after him.
