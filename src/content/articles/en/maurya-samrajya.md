## Background

In the 4th century BCE, Magadha (today's Bihar) was the most powerful kingdom in northern India, ruled by the **Nanda dynasty**. Around 322-321 BCE, **Chandragupta Maurya**, with the help of his teacher and adviser **Chanakya** (Kautilya), removed the Nanda king and founded the Maurya dynasty.

## Expansion of the Empire

After a conflict with the Greek general **Seleucus** in the north-west, Chandragupta made a treaty with him, and some regions up to present-day Afghanistan came under the Maurya Empire. Seleucus sent his ambassador **Megasthenes** to Pataliputra, who described the Maurya court and city in his book **Indica**.

After Chandragupta, his son **Bindusara** and then his grandson **Ashoka** became kings. Under Ashoka the empire reached its greatest size — from the north-west to a large part of the south.

## System of Government

- The capital **Pataliputra** (today's Patna) was a huge and well-protected city.
- The empire was divided into provinces, some of whose main centres were Taxila, Ujjain and Tosali.
- Attention was paid to tax collection, roads, the army and a system of spies.
- The **Arthashastra**, a text on politics and governance, is traditionally linked with Kautilya (Chanakya), although scholars debate the period of its composition.

## Decline

After Ashoka's death (about 232 BCE) the empire began to weaken. Around 185 BCE the last Maurya king, **Brihadratha**, was removed by his commander **Pushyamitra Shunga**, who founded the Shunga dynasty.
