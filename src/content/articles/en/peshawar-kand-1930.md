## Background: Civil Disobedience on the Frontier

In **March–April 1930**, with Gandhi's **Dandi March**, the **Civil Disobedience Movement** spread across the country. In the North-West Frontier Province, the movement was led by **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** and his non-violent organisation, the **Khudai Khidmatgar** (the 'Red Shirts'). The British government regarded the Frontier Province as strategically very sensitive, so any mass movement there was harshly suppressed.

On **23 April 1930** the government arrested Ghaffar Khan and several other leaders. As soon as the news spread, large numbers of people began to gather in the famous **Qissa Khwani Bazaar** of old **Peshawar**. 'Qissa Khwani' means 'the bazaar of storytellers' — in the old days, traders and travellers would stop here to hear and tell stories.

## The Firing

To disperse the crowd, troops and **armoured cars** were sent into the bazaar. In the chaos stones were thrown, and an army motorcycle dispatch rider was killed. The armoured cars drove into the crowd, crushing several people. After this, officers ordered **machine-gun** fire on the unarmed crowd. According to accounts, the firing went on intermittently for several hours. People kept falling, but the Khudai Khidmatgar did not respond with violence.

There is deep disagreement over the number of dead. **Official reports** gave about **20** deaths, while **local and nationalist sources** speak of about **400** people killed and many more wounded. The incident caused outrage across the country, and the Khudai Khidmatgar movement spread even faster in the Frontier Province.

## Chandra Singh Garhwali's Refusal

Units of the **2/18 Royal Garhwal Rifles** were also deployed in Peshawar that day. When these Garhwali soldiers were ordered to fire on the unarmed protesters, two platoons led by **Havildar Chandra Singh** **refused to fire**. Their argument was that they would not shoot their unarmed fellow countrymen. This was an extraordinary event in the history of discipline in the British Indian Army, because disobeying a military order meant severe punishment.

These soldiers were disarmed and taken into custody. In **June 1930** they were **court-martialled** at **Abbottabad**. According to the government's reply in the British Parliament in 1934, **17 soldiers** were convicted in this case. **Havildar Chandra Singh** was sentenced to **transportation for life**; Havildar Narain Singh got 15 years, and two naiks 10 years each of transportation, while the remaining 13 soldiers received rigorous imprisonment of 3 to 8 years. Chandra Singh's property was also confiscated. (Some accounts also say a death sentence was first passed and later commuted.)

## The Life of Chandra Singh Garhwali

Chandra Singh was born on **25 December 1891** in a village in **Garhwal** (today Uttarakhand). His family name was **Bhandari**. He joined the army in **1914** and fought on the fronts of France and Mesopotamia (Baghdad) during the **First World War**. After the Peshawar incident people began to call him respectfully **'Veer Chandra Singh Garhwali'**.

His sentence was gradually reduced, and he was released from jail in **September 1941**. But soon he was arrested again for taking part in the **Quit India Movement**, and was released in **1945**. After independence he joined the **Communist Party**, returned to Garhwal and continued his social and political work, and contested an election in 1957, in which he was not successful. He died on **1 October 1979**.

## Legacy

The Qissa Khwani Bazaar massacre showed how brutally a non-violent movement could be answered, and at the same time brought out the patience of the Khudai Khidmatgar. In Pakistan the martyrs of this incident are still remembered on 23 April.

The refusal of Chandra Singh Garhwali and his comrades became a symbol that Indian soldiers serving in the British army were not untouched by national feeling. A Hindu Garhwali soldier's refusal to fire on Muslim Pathan protesters is also seen as an example of **communal harmony**. In **1994** India Post issued a postage stamp on him. In Uttarakhand, many roads and institutions, and the **'Veer Chandra Singh Garhwali Tourism Self-Employment Scheme'**, are named after him.
