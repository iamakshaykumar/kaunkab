## The First Case and Early Steps

In December 2019, reports began to come from the Chinese city of Wuhan of a disease spread by a new coronavirus, **SARS-CoV-2**, which was later named **COVID-19**. On **30 January 2020** India's first case was found at **Thrissur** in **Kerala** — in a medical student who had returned from Wuhan. Cases remained few in February, but by March 2020 the infection had spread to several states. On 11 March the World Health Organization declared it a global **pandemic**.

The government began screening arrivals from abroad, suspended visas and launched missions to bring back Indians stranded abroad. On **22 March 2020**, at the call of Prime Minister **Narendra Modi**, a one-day **"Janata Curfew"** was observed across the country.

## The Nationwide Lockdown

On the evening of **24 March 2020** the Prime Minister announced that from the next day a **21-day lockdown** would be in force across the whole country. Trains, air travel, factories, offices and markets shut down almost completely. The lockdown was extended in several phases until **31 May 2020**, after which relaxations were given gradually through the **"Unlock"** process.

The most painful aspect of the lockdown was the crisis of **migrant workers**. Having lost work and income, lakhs of workers set out from the big cities towards their villages on foot, on bicycles and in trucks; many lost their lives on the way. From May 2020 **"Shramik Special"** trains were run for them. The government began giving free grain to poor families under the **Pradhan Mantri Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana** and announced an economic package of about **20 lakh crore rupees** under the name **"Atmanirbhar Bharat"**. Even so, India's economy contracted in 2020–21 — for the first time in decades.

According to the government, the lockdown gave the health system time to prepare — testing laboratories, COVID hospitals and the production of India-made **PPE kits** grew rapidly. Critics said that a lockdown imposed with only four hours' notice created a serious crisis for the poor and for migrants. The peak of the first wave came in September 2020, when more than 90,000 cases a day were recorded; after this cases kept falling.

## The Second Wave (2021)

As cases fell in early 2021, it seemed the danger had passed, but in **March–April 2021** a **second wave** rose rapidly. Behind it was mainly the new, more infectious **Delta** variant. Experts and critics also cited election rallies, large events such as the Kumbh Mela and laxity in precautions as causes. On **30 April 2021** more than **four lakh** new cases were recorded in a single day.

In this wave the health system came under unprecedented pressure. There was a severe shortage of hospital beds, **medical oxygen**, medicines and ambulances. In many hospitals there were reports of patients dying when oxygen ran out. Long queues formed at cremation grounds and graveyards. **"Oxygen Express"** trains were run to deliver oxygen, the Air Force carried oxygen tankers and many countries sent help. This time, instead of a national lockdown, states imposed their own restrictions. After May the wave gradually subsided. In December 2021–January 2022 a third wave came with the **Omicron** variant, in which cases rose sharply but the death rate was much lower than in the second wave.

## One of the World's Big Vaccination Drives

On **16 January 2021** India began its vaccination drive. In the first phase health workers and frontline workers were vaccinated, then the elderly and people with serious illnesses, and later all adults. The main vaccines were **Covishield** — the Oxford–AstraZeneca vaccine made by the **Serum Institute** of Pune — and **Covaxin**, developed by **Bharat Biotech** of Hyderabad together with the **Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR)**. The **CoWIN** digital platform was built for registration and certificates.

At first there was a shortage of vaccines and a dispute over the procurement policy for states; from June 2021 the Centre began buying vaccines for adults itself and giving them to the states free of cost. The country crossed the mark of **100 crore** doses on **21 October 2021** and **200 crore** doses on **17 July 2022**. Later, adolescents were also vaccinated and a precaution (booster) dose was introduced.

## The Cost and the Lessons

According to government figures, more than four and a half crore cases of infection were recorded in India and about **5.3 lakh** people died. The **World Health Organization** estimated the pandemic-related "excess deaths" in India during 2020–21 at about **47 lakh**; the Government of India objected to the methodology of this estimate and rejected it. Estimates by other independent studies were also far higher than the official figures, so the real death toll is still a matter of debate.

The pandemic exposed the weaknesses of India's public health system, but it also brought to the fore its vaccine-manufacturing capacity, its digital systems and the dedication of health workers. Schools staying closed for a long time had a deep impact on children's education, while online education, digital payments and working from home grew rapidly. This period of 2020–22 is counted among the hardest health crises in the history of independent India.
