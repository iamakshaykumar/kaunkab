## The Three Farm Laws

In **September 2020** Parliament passed three bills relating to farming and agricultural trade. The Lok Sabha passed them on 15 and 18 September and the Rajya Sabha on 20 and 22 September by voice vote, and after the President's assent on **28 September 2020** they became law. The three laws were:

- **The Farmers' Produce Trade and Commerce (Promotion and Facilitation) Act** — under it farmers could sell their produce outside the government mandis (APMC) as well, without mandi fees.
- **The Farmers (Empowerment and Protection) Agreement on Price Assurance and Farm Services Act** — it provided a framework for contract farming at a price fixed with the buyer even before sowing.
- **The Essential Commodities (Amendment) Act** — it removed stock limits on items such as cereals, pulses, oilseeds, onions and potatoes in normal circumstances.

## The Arguments of Both Sides

**According to the government**, these laws were meant to free farmers from middlemen, give them the freedom to sell produce directly to large buyers and private companies, and increase investment in agriculture. The government repeatedly said that the **minimum support price (MSP)** and the mandis would continue as before, and that the protest was largely driven by misinformation.

**The protesting farmers' unions argued** that fee-free trade outside the mandis would gradually weaken the government mandis, and that procurement at MSP could then also come to an end. They also objected that disputes between farmers and traders would go to the sub-divisional magistrate (SDM) rather than to the courts, and that small farmers would be weak in front of big companies. Several opposition parties and some state governments also said that agriculture is a state subject and that there had not been enough consultation before the laws were made. Over this very issue the **Shiromani Akali Dal** broke with the central government and the NDA alliance.

## Camped on Delhi's Borders

The protest began in Punjab in August 2020, and after the laws were passed it spread to Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan. At the end of November, under the **"Dilli Chalo"** campaign, thousands of farmers moved towards Delhi in tractor-trolleys. When the police stopped them at the borders on **25–26 November 2020**, the farmers camped right there. Temporary settlements grew up at the **Singhu, Tikri** and **Ghazipur** borders, where community kitchens (langars), libraries and medical camps were run. About forty farmers' organisations together formed a joint platform called the **Samyukt Kisan Morcha**; Bharatiya Kisan Union leader **Rakesh Tikait** became a well-known face of the protest.

The farmers kept up the sit-in through cold, heat and even the second wave of the Covid pandemic. A large number of farmers died during the protest; there is no agreement on the figure — the farmers' unions put it at more than 700, while the government told Parliament that it had no such data.

## Talks, the Court and Confrontation

**Eleven rounds** of talks were held between the government, led by Agriculture Minister **Narendra Singh Tomar**, and the farmers' organisations. The government offered to amend the laws and to put them on hold for a year and a half, but the organisations insisted on the complete withdrawal of all three laws and a legal guarantee of MSP.

On **12 January 2021** the **Supreme Court** stayed the implementation of the three laws and set up a committee of experts. The farmers' organisations refused to appear before the committee; its report was submitted to the court in March 2021.

During the tractor parade held on Republic Day, **26 January 2021**, some groups left the agreed routes. There were clashes with the police at several places, and some protesters reached the **Red Fort** and began hoisting flags there. Hundreds of police personnel were injured. The Samyukt Kisan Morcha distanced itself from this violence, while the government and critics called it a serious blot on the protest. On **3 October 2021** at **Lakhimpur Kheri** in Uttar Pradesh, eight people lost their lives when protesting farmers were run over by a vehicle and in the violence that followed, which made the protest even sharper.

## The Repeal of the Laws

On **19 November 2021**, the day of Guru Nanak Jayanti, Prime Minister **Narendra Modi** announced in an address to the nation that the three laws would be withdrawn. He said the government had been unable to make some farmers understand the benefits of the laws. On **29 November 2021** both houses of Parliament passed the **Farm Laws Repeal Bill, 2021**, and after President **Ram Nath Kovind** gave his assent at the end of November, it was notified on 1 December.

The government then gave assurances in a letter on the farmers' remaining demands — such as setting up a committee on MSP and considering the withdrawal of cases linked to the protest. On **9 December 2021** the Samyukt Kisan Morcha announced that the protest was "suspended", and from **11 December** the farmers began returning home from the borders in victory processions.

## Significance and Legacy

This is counted among the longest and largest farmers' movements of independent India. For its supporters it was an example of the strength of a peaceful mass movement, while economists who supported the laws felt that it halted necessary agricultural reforms. The episode also sharpened the debate on how much consultation should take place with the states and affected groups before major economic reforms. The demand for a **legal guarantee of MSP** remained on the agenda of farmers' organisations even after the protest ended.
