## Early Life

**Rash Behari Bose** was born on **25 May 1886** in the village of **Subaldaha** in the Bardhaman district of Bengal. A large part of his schooling took place in the French settlement of **Chandannagar**, where he came into contact with the revolutionary groups of Bengal. Later he took up a job as head clerk at the Forest Research Institute at **Dehradun**. Under cover of the government job he was secretly spreading a network of revolutionaries across north India and Punjab.

## 1912: The Bomb on the Viceroy

In 1911 the shifting of the capital from Calcutta to Delhi was announced. On **23 December 1912**, as Viceroy **Lord Hardinge** was entering the new capital in a procession, riding an elephant, a bomb was thrown at him in **Chandni Chowk**. Hardinge was wounded but survived, while one of his attendants died.

Rash Behari was regarded as the chief planner of this plot. The name usually given for the bomb thrower is the young **Basanta Kumar Biswas**, although this is not entirely certain. In the later **Delhi–Lahore Conspiracy Case**, Basanta Kumar Biswas, Amir Chand, Awadh Behari and Bhai Balmukund were hanged. Rash Behari was not caught — it is said that he returned to Dehradun and continued in his job as usual.

## 1915: The Plan for a Ghadar Revolution

At the time of the First World War (1914), a large number of members of the **Ghadar Party** returned to India from America and Canada. Rash Behari, together with **Kartar Singh Sarabha** and other Ghadarites, made contact with Indian soldiers in the cantonments of Punjab and north India. **21 February 1915** was fixed as the date for a simultaneous revolt.

But because of an informer the news of the plan reached the government. Despite the change of date, there were widespread arrests and the revolt failed. In the Lahore Conspiracy Case many Ghadarites were hanged. In 1915, disguised under the name 'Priyanath Tagore', Rash Behari slipped out of India and reached Japan.

## Exile in Japan

The British government pressed Japan to hand him over. In this situation the Japanese nationalist leader **Toyama Mitsuru** and his associates hid him, and the **Soma family**, who ran the **Nakamuraya** bakery of Tokyo, gave him shelter in their home. In **1918** he married the Soma family's daughter **Toshiko** (Toshiko died in 1925), and in **1923** he took Japanese citizenship.

While living in Japan he kept raising the question of India's freedom through articles, speeches and organisations. There is debate among historians about his relations with Japanese nationalists — some see it as a strategic compulsion against the British, while others criticise his closeness to Japanese expansionism.

## The Indian Independence League and the Indian National Army

After Japan entered the war in December 1941 and the British forces were defeated in Southeast Asia, thousands of Indian soldiers became prisoners of war of Japan. In **1942** Rash Behari called first the **Tokyo Conference** and then, in June 1942, the **Bangkok Conference**, from which the **Indian Independence League** was formed and he became its president. The Bangkok Conference also invited Subhas Chandra Bose to come to East Asia and take over the leadership.

Under Captain **Mohan Singh**, the first **Indian National Army** was formed from prisoners of war on **1 September 1942**, but because of differences with the Japanese it fell into crisis by December 1942. When Subhas Chandra Bose reached Singapore in July 1943, Rash Behari handed over the leadership of the League and the army to him and himself became an adviser. Most sources give this date as **4 July 1943**; there is also disagreement on whether he gave up this post of his own will or under Japanese pressure.

## Last Days and Legacy

Struggling with illness, he died in Tokyo on **21 January 1945** — two and a half years before India's independence. Rash Behari Bose is among the few revolutionaries whose activity spanned the secret movements of Bengal and Punjab in the 1910s and the Indian National Army of the 1940s, and who linked India's freedom struggle with the larger politics of Asia.
