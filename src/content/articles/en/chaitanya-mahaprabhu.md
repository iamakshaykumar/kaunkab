## Nimai Pandit of Navadvip

**Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** was born in February **1486 CE** at **Navadvip** (Nadia) in Bengal. His original name was **Vishvambhar Mishra**; his father was **Jagannath Mishra** and his mother **Shachidevi**. At home he was affectionately called **Nimai**. At that time Navadvip was a famous centre of Sanskrit learning, especially of Nyaya philosophy.

The young Vishvambhar became a brilliant scholar and began running a school of grammar, so people started calling him **Nimai Pandit**. After the death of his first wife Lakshmipriya he married Vishnupriya.

## The Change at Gaya

In about **1508–09**, Vishvambhar went to **Gaya** to perform his father's shraddha and met the ascetic **Ishvara Puri**, from whom he received initiation into a Krishna mantra. The biographical texts say that after this his nature changed completely — in place of pride in learning came a mood immersed in love of Krishna.

Returning to Navadvip, he began singing Krishna's name with companions all night long. This collective singing of the name is called **sankirtan** — a band of devotees dancing and singing to the beat of the drum (mridanga/khol) and cymbals, which sometimes went out into the streets as well. Among his close companions **Nityananda** was the foremost.

## Sannyasa and Puri

In **1510 CE**, at about the age of 24, he took sannyasa from **Keshava Bharati** at **Katwa** and was named **Sri Krishna Chaitanya**. According to the biographical texts, at his mother's request he chose **Puri** as his residence so that news from Navadvip could keep reaching her.

In Puri the **Jagannath Temple** became the centre of his devotion. According to the biographical texts, the famous Vedantin **Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya**, the learned official **Ramananda Raya** and the Gajapati king of Odisha **Prataparudra** became his devotees. His sankirtan dance before the chariot at the time of the Rath Yatra is among the dearest memories of the Gaudiya tradition.

## Journeys to South India and Vrindavan

With Puri as his base, Chaitanya made a long journey to South India and went to **Vrindavan** around **1515**. For Vrindavan he inspired disciples such as **Rupa** and **Sanatana Goswami** to rediscover the places associated with Krishna's lila and to write devotional scriptures. These same disciples later became famous as the 'Six Goswamis', whose texts and temples made Vrindavan a major centre of Krishna devotion.

## Teachings: The Name, Love and Achintya Bhedabheda

Chaitanya himself wrote very little; the tradition regards only the eight verses of the **Shikshashtaka** as his composition. The main threads of his teachings are these:
- In the Kali age, the simplest path to God is the **sankirtan of the name of Hari**.
- A devotee should be humbler than a blade of grass and more tolerant than a tree — this sentiment appears in the Shikshashtaka.
- The soul and God are at once different and non-different — this philosophy was called **Achintya Bhedabheda** (inconceivable difference and non-difference), which was later explained in detail by **Jiva Goswami**.

In name-sankirtan there was no distinction of caste or learning, so people of every section of society joined in it.

## Death and Legacy

Chaitanya died at Puri in **1534 CE**. The biographical texts give different accounts of his end — some say that he merged into the image of Jagannath; historians view these as traditions of faith.

His tradition is called the **Gaudiya Vaishnava** stream, in which he himself is regarded as the combined incarnation of Radha and Krishna. The **Chaitanya Bhagavata** of Vrindavana Dasa and the **Chaitanya Charitamrita** of Krishnadasa Kaviraja are his main biographies. This tradition has left a deep mark on the music, literature and folk culture of Bengal, and in the twentieth century organisations such as ISKCON, which emerged from this stream, carried kirtan to many countries of the world.
