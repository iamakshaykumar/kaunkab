## A Boy from Chennai

**Dommaraju Gukesh** was born on **29 May 2006** in **Chennai**. His father **Rajinikanth** is an ear, nose and throat surgeon and his mother **Padma** is a microbiologist. Gukesh began playing chess at about the age of seven. Chennai is the same city that produced **Viswanathan Anand**, India's first grandmaster and five-time world champion, and it has a strong chess tradition.

Gukesh progressed very fast. In **January 2019**, at only **12 years 7 months** of age, he became a **grandmaster** — at that time he was among the youngest grandmasters in history. His long-time coach was grandmaster **Vishnu Prasanna**. He also received guidance from the **WestBridge Anand Chess Academy** founded by Anand.

## The Road to the Challenge

In September 2023 Gukesh's world rating overtook that of **Viswanathan Anand**. It was the first time in about 37 years that a player other than Anand became India's top-rated chess player on the official list. This made it clear that a new generation had arrived in Indian chess.

The player who challenges the world champion is chosen through the **Candidates Tournament**. In April 2024 this eight-player tournament was held in **Toronto**, Canada, with experienced top players taking part. Gukesh won the tournament with **9 points** out of 14 and, at the age of **17**, became the youngest challenger in the history of the World Championship.

In September of the same year, at the **Chess Olympiad** in Budapest (Hungary), Gukesh performed brilliantly playing on the first board for India. Both India's men's and women's teams won Olympiad gold for the first time, and Gukesh also won the individual gold on the first board.

## The Match in Singapore

The World Championship match was played from **25 November to 12 December 2024** on **Sentosa Island** in **Singapore**. His opponent was the reigning world champion **Ding Liren** of China, who had won the title in 2023. The match was of 14 classical games, and **7½ points** were needed to win.

The start of the match did not go well for Gukesh — Ding won the **first game**. But Gukesh levelled by winning the **third game**. After that seven games in a row were drawn, in which Gukesh repeatedly kept up the pressure and Ding defended solidly. In the **11th game** Gukesh took the lead, but in the very next, the **12th game**, Ding won to level the score again. The 13th game was also drawn, and the score stood at 6½–6½.

## The Decisive Fourteenth Game

On **12 December 2024**, in the last classical game, Gukesh was playing with the black pieces. For a long time the game seemed to be heading for a draw, and most people believed the decision would come in the fast games of the tiebreak. Gukesh still kept looking for small chances. On move 55, under time pressure, Ding placed his rook on **f2**, opening the way to an exchange of rooks. It was a serious mistake — the endgame that followed was a sure win for Gukesh. A few moves later Ding resigned.

Gukesh won the match **7½–6½**. He broke down in tears right at the board. At just **18 years** of age, he became the **youngest undisputed world champion** in the history of chess and the **18th** world champion. After the match Ding Liren too said he had been shocked by his mistake, but that he had given his best throughout the match.

Besides his team, the role of mental conditioning coach **Paddy Upton**, who had earlier worked with India's 2011 cricket World Cup-winning team, was also discussed in Gukesh's preparation.

## Legacy

Gukesh is the second Indian after **Viswanathan Anand** to become world champion. Anand called the win a proud moment for Indian chess. In January 2025 the Government of India announced that Gukesh would receive the country's highest sporting honour, the **Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna**.

Gukesh's win became a symbol of the rapid rise of chess in India. In this same generation many young players such as **R. Praggnanandhaa** and **Arjun Erigaisi** have reached the world's top ranks. In chess clubs and schools across the country, Gukesh's story became a message to children that even the biggest goal can be achieved with patience, discipline and self-confidence.
