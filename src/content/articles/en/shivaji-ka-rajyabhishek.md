## Background

**Shivaji Maharaj** was born in 1630 in Shivneri fort near Pune. His mother **Jijabai** awakened in him from childhood the dream of swarajya. From his youth Shivaji began to take the forts of the Western Ghats and built his kingdom by struggling against both the Adilshahi of Bijapur and the Mughal empire.

The victory over Afzal Khan (1659), the raid on Surat (1664) and his escape from Aurangzeb's custody at Agra in 1666 made him famous all over India.

## Why the coronation was necessary

In spite of ruling such a large territory, formally Shivaji was still regarded as the son of a jagirdar. To be recognised as an **independent, sovereign king** in the eyes of other states and of his subjects, a proper coronation was necessary.

## The ceremony at Raigad

On **6 June 1674** a grand ceremony was held at Raigad fort. The scholar **Gaga Bhatt** of Kashi performed the coronation by Vedic rites. Shivaji Maharaj took the title **"Chhatrapati"**. On this occasion a new era, the **Rajyabhishek Shaka**, began and new coins were also issued.

## System of government

To run the state Shivaji Maharaj created the **Ashtapradhan**, a council of eight ministers, which included the Peshwa (prime minister). He laid stress on strong forts, an efficient navy and a well-organised revenue system.

## Importance

The coronation at Raigad was the formal declaration of Maratha swarajya. The kingdom went on growing even after Shivaji Maharaj's death in 1680, and in the 18th century Maratha power came to be counted among the greatest powers in India. Every year Coronation Day is still celebrated at Raigad.
