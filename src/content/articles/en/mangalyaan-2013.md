## From Chandrayaan Towards Mars

After the success of **Chandrayaan-1** in 2008, ISRO thought of taking the next big step into deep space — reaching Earth's neighbouring planet **Mars**. Mars missions were considered extremely difficult; by then more than half of all Mars missions in the world had failed, and no country had managed to reach Mars on its first attempt.

A feasibility study for the mission was done in 2010. On **3 August 2012** the government of Prime Minister **Manmohan Singh** approved the project, and it was announced in the Independence Day speech of 15 August 2012. The official name was **Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM)**, but among the people it became popular as **Mangalyaan**.

Time was very short. A favourable opportunity to reach Mars while saving fuel comes about every **26 months**, and the next opportunity was in November 2013. ISRO's team prepared the spacecraft in about **15 months**.

## Launch: 5 November 2013

At about 2:38 in the afternoon of **5 November 2013**, the **PSLV-C25** rocket lifted off with Mangalyaan from the **Satish Dhawan Space Centre** at **Sriharikota**. The spacecraft weighed about **1,337 kilograms**, of which 852 kilograms was fuel.

The PSLV was not powerful enough to send the spacecraft directly towards Mars. So ISRO adopted a clever method: the spacecraft was first placed in an elliptical Earth orbit, and for about a month its engine was fired several times to enlarge the orbit. On **1 December 2013** (Indian time) the engine was fired for the last time to send the spacecraft out of Earth's gravity and onto the path to Mars.

## A 300-Day Journey

For about the next **298 days** Mangalyaan moved towards Mars while travelling around the Sun. During this time its distance from Earth grew so great that messages took several minutes to arrive. So the spacecraft was made largely **autonomous**, so that it could recover by itself if something went wrong. It was tracked from the **ISTRAC** control centre in Bengaluru and the **Indian Deep Space Network** antenna at Byalalu; the American space agency **NASA** also helped with its Deep Space Network.

The biggest worry was whether the spacecraft's main **440-newton engine**, which had been switched off for about 300 days, would start again near Mars. On **22 September 2014** it was fired for four seconds as a test, and it worked.

## 24 September 2014: Into Mars Orbit

On the morning of **24 September 2014** the spacecraft's engines fired for about 24 minutes, to reduce its speed so that Mars's gravity would pull it into orbit. This process took place while the spacecraft was behind Mars and there was no direct contact with it. After a wait of a few minutes, the signal came that Mangalyaan had successfully reached **Mars orbit**.

At the control centre in Bengaluru, along with ISRO chairman **K. Radhakrishnan**, programme director **Mylswamy Annadurai**, project director **S. Arunan** and the whole team, Prime Minister **Narendra Modi** was also present. The important role of many women scientists in this mission, such as **Ritu Karidhal**, **Nandini Harinath** and **Moumita Dutta**, was also widely discussed.

With this success ISRO became the **fourth** space agency to reach Mars orbit, after the Soviet space programme, NASA and the European Space Agency. India became the first country to reach Mars **on its first attempt** and the **first Asian country** to reach Mars.

## Low Cost, Big Achievement

The total cost of Mangalyaan was about **450 crore rupees** (about 74 million US dollars) — less than the budget of many Hollywood films. According to experts, the reasons were indigenous technology, a simple design, a small scientific payload and the reuse of systems developed earlier. The American magazine *Time* counted it among the best inventions of 2014.

The spacecraft carried **five scientific instruments** weighing about 15 kilograms — the **Mars Colour Camera**, the **Methane Sensor for Mars**, the **Lyman Alpha Photometer**, **MENCA** (an analyser of neutral particles in the atmosphere) and the **Thermal Infrared Imaging Spectrometer**. The Mars Colour Camera sent thousands of pictures of the full disc of Mars, its moons and dust storms, from which a **Mars Atlas** was also prepared.

## Legacy

Mangalyaan was built for only **six months**, but it kept orbiting Mars for about **eight years**. In April 2022 contact with it was lost during a long eclipse, and in October 2022 ISRO declared the mission over.

Mangalyaan showed the world that an interplanetary mission can succeed even with limited resources. It awakened a deep interest in science and space among India's young generation and gave confidence for later missions — **Chandrayaan-2**, **Chandrayaan-3** and **Aditya-L1**, which studies the Sun.
