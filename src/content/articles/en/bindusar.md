## Between Father and Son

In the history of the Mauryan dynasty two names shine brightest — the founder **Chandragupta** and the great **Ashoka**. Very little is known about **Bindusara**, who held the empire for about 25 years between them. No inscription of his has been found, and most of what we know comes from Jain, Buddhist and Brahmanical texts written centuries later, or from Greek writers, which often do not agree with one another.

His reign is generally placed at **about 297 to 273 BCE**. The capital remained **Pataliputra**.

## Tales of His Name and Birth

Greek writers called him **Amitrochates** (or Allitrochades), which is believed to be the Greek form of the Sanskrit title **"Amitraghata"** (slayer of enemies).

Traditions about his mother also differ. The Jain text *Parishishtaparvan* (Hemachandra, 12th century) tells a story: to protect Chandragupta from poison, Chanakya used to mix a little poison into his food. One day the pregnant queen **Durdhara** ate that food and collapsed. Chanakya arrived at that very moment and saved the baby. According to the tale, a **drop (bindu)** of the poison had touched the baby's head, and so he was named **Bindusara**. This is a folk tale and cannot be considered historical fact.

## The Empire and Chanakya

Some texts say that Chanakya remained chief minister in Bindusara's time as well, while according to others he withdrew from affairs of state along with Chandragupta. The 16th-century Tibetan historian **Taranatha** writes that Chanakya, a great noble of Bindusara, destroyed the kings of **16 towns** and made Bindusara master of all the land **between the eastern and western seas**.

Some historians regard this as a conquest of the Deccan or South India, while others say it only describes the suppression of rebellions. What is certain is that when Ashoka ascended the throne, the empire extended in the south up to Karnataka, and Ashoka himself conquered only Kalinga — so the expansion in the south must have happened in the time of Chandragupta or Bindusara.

## Relations with the Greek World

Bindusara had good relations with the Greek kingdoms. The Seleucid king **Antiochus I** sent **Deimachus of Plataea** as his ambassador to his court — just as Megasthenes had earlier come to Chandragupta's court. According to the Roman writer **Pliny**, the Egyptian king **Ptolemy II Philadelphus** also sent an envoy named **Dionysius** to India.

The Greek writer Athenaeus (citing Hegesander) tells an interesting episode: Bindusara asked Antiochus for sweet wine, dried figs and a philosopher. Antiochus' reply was that he would send the wine and figs, but the laws of Greece did not allow the sale of a philosopher.

## The Ajivikas and Religion

On Bindusara's religious leanings the sources point in different directions. The Buddhist text *Divyavadana* mentions at his court an **Ajivika** wanderer named **Pingalavatsa**, who, on seeing the princes, predicted that Ashoka would become king. The commentary on the Mahavamsa calls the same person **Janasana**. The Ajivika sect, whose chief teacher was Makkhali Gosala, believed in determinism (that everything is predetermined). From these stories some scholars infer that Bindusara had a close connection with the Ajivikas, but there is no firm proof of this.

## The Struggle for Succession

According to the *Ashokavadana*, when a revolt broke out at **Taxila**, Bindusara sent Prince **Ashoka** to suppress it. In Bindusara's last days a revolt broke out there again; this time the eldest prince **Susima** was sent, but he was not successful. After Bindusara's death in about 273 BCE, there was a struggle among the princes for the throne. The Ashokavadana says that the ministers **Khallataka** and **Radhagupta** helped Ashoka become emperor. Ashoka's formal coronation is believed to have taken place some years later.

Though Bindusara's rule is less discussed, he not only kept intact the empire received from Chandragupta but probably expanded it further and handed it to Ashoka — and he continued India's diplomatic relations with the Greek world.
