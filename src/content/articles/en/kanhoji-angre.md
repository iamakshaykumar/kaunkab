## Early Life

According to tradition, Kanhoji Angre was born around **1669** in the Suvarnadurg area near Ratnagiri; there is some disagreement about his origin and birth year. His father **Tukoji** was connected with the navy in Shivaji's time. It was Shivaji who had laid the foundation of the navy and the sea forts (jaldurg) to defend the Konkan coast.

The young Kanhoji made his name by playing a role in the defence of Suvarnadurg. Around **1698** he was made head of the Maratha navy and received the title **"Sarkhel"** (admiral). He made **Vijaydurg** (earlier called Gheria) his first major base.

## Control of the Coast

Kanhoji's domain stretched from near Mumbai to the southern Konkan. His main bases were:

- **Vijaydurg** — a strong sea fort of the southern Konkan
- **Kolaba** (Alibag) — the main centre in the north, to which the islands of Khanderi and Underi were attached
- Other coastal forts such as **Suvarnadurg**, Sindhudurg and Jaigad

The Maratha fleet mainly consisted of light, nimble vessels such as the "gurab" and "galbat", which could move better than large European ships in shallow water and creeks. Kanhoji demanded that merchant ships sailing in his waters take a pass (dastak); ships that did not comply were captured.

## Settlement with Shahu and the Peshwa

After Chhatrapati Rajaram, a struggle arose in the Maratha state between the factions of **Shahu** and **Tarabai**. At first Kanhoji was on Tarabai's side. In **1713** the Peshwa **Balaji Vishwanath** won him over to Shahu through negotiation; in return his post of Sarkhel and his authority over the Konkan forts were recognised. This gave the Maratha state stable coastal security.

## Clashes with the European Powers

In Kanhoji's time the East India Company (Bombay), the Portuguese (Goa, Vasai) and Dutch traders were active on the western coast, and the **Siddis** of Janjira were also old rivals of the Marathas.

- In 1712 he captured an English ship, which was released after a ransom was taken.
- In 1718 the English attacked Khanderi, and in 1720 Vijaydurg, but failed both times.
- In 1721–22 the English and the Portuguese together mounted a major expedition against Kolaba, which also failed.

British records called him a "pirate", but historians now believe that he was asserting authority over his coastal waters as head of the navy of an organised state.

## Death and Legacy

Kanhoji Angre died on **4 July 1729** at Alibag, where his tomb stands. After him his son **Sekhoji**, then Sambhaji and **Tulaji Angre** took charge of the navy. Amid internal disputes and strained relations with the Peshwa, Vijaydurg was captured in 1756 in a joint action by the English and the Peshwa, and the power of the Angre navy was broken.

Today Kanhoji is counted among the foremost heroes of India's naval tradition. In 1951 the Mumbai base of the Western Naval Command of the Indian Navy was named **INS Angre**. Forts such as Vijaydurg, Suvarnadurg and Khanderi were included in 2025 in the UNESCO World Heritage site "Maratha Military Landscapes".
