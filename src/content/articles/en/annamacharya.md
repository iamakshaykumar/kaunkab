## The Boy from Tallapaka

**Annamacharya** was born in **1408** on the day of **Vaishakha Purnima** in the village of **Tallapaka** in present-day Andhra Pradesh (modern sources give the English date as 9 May or 22 May 1408). The village lies some distance from Tirupati, near Rajampet. In Telugu he is affectionately called **Annamayya**, and his family took the name "Tallapaka" from the village.

Traditional life stories — the most important being a Telugu work by his grandson **Chinnanna** — say that from childhood his mind was set on devotional songs. As a teenager he left home, reached the hill of Tirumala and became a devoted follower of Lord **Venkateswara**. Here he was initiated by a Vaishnava teacher named **Ghana Vishnu**. Later he lived for many years at **Ahobilam**, where he learned the works of the Tamil Alvar saints — the **Nalayira Divya Prabandham** — from **Satakopa Yati**, founder of the Ahobila Matha.

## Sankirtanas to Venkateswara

Annamacharya devoted his whole life to composing songs in praise of Venkateswara. According to tradition, he resolved to compose at least one song every day and wrote about **32,000 sankirtanas** in all. This figure comes from tradition; today only about **13,000–14,000** of these songs are available.

His songs are mainly in **Telugu**, with some in **Sanskrit**. By theme they are divided into two groups:

- **Adhyatma sankirtanas** — on devotion, detachment, ethics and the truths of life;
- **Shringara sankirtanas** — on the love of the Lord and the goddess Alamelumanga, expressing the union of the devotee's soul with God.

Some of his famous songs are **"Adivo Alladivo Sriharivasamu"**, **"Brahmam Okkate"**, **"Bhavamulona Bahyamunandunu"**, **"Muddugare Yashoda"**, **"Nanati Baduku Natakamu"** and the lullaby **"Jo Achyutananda Jo Jo Mukunda"**. He also wrote a treatise on music called **"Sankirtana Lakshanamu"**.

## The Pada Form and Carnatic Music

Annamacharya is considered the earliest known composer of the **"pada"** or **sankirtana** form, which has a **pallavi** (refrain) followed by several **charanams** (verses). This structure later became the foundation of Carnatic music compositions. That is why Telugu literature calls him the **"Pada-kavita Pitamaha"** (grandfather of pada poetry).

The language of his songs is simple and close to everyday speech. In "Brahmam Okkate" he says that the sleep of a king and the sleep of a servant are the same, that the earth is the same for the Brahmin and the outcaste — God is one. In the rigid caste order of the time, this was a clear message of equality.

**Purandaradasa** of the Kannada Haridasa tradition was his younger contemporary. Some manuscripts of the Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams mention a meeting between the two, though scholars treat this as part of tradition.

## Family and Royal Court

Annamacharya's wife **Timmakka** wrote a poem called **"Subhadra Kalyanam"** and is regarded as the **first woman poet of Telugu literature**. His son **Pedda Tirumalacharya** and grandson **Chinnanna** (China Tiruvengalanatha) were also poet-composers; thus the "Tallapaka poets" became the tradition of a whole family.

According to tradition, **Saluva Narasimha**, a ruler in the Vijayanagara region, held him in high regard. A famous story says that when the king wanted songs written in his own praise, Annamacharya refused, saying his voice was for God alone, and for this he even suffered imprisonment for a time. This story has no independent historical confirmation.

He died at Tirumala in **1503**, at about 95 years of age.

## Rediscovery from the Copper Plates

After Annamacharya's death his son Pedda Tirumalacharya had his songs engraved on **copper plates**. These plates were kept in a stone chamber inside the Tirumala temple, later called the **Sankirtana Bhandaram**. For centuries they were forgotten, and most of the songs nearly vanished even from popular memory.

In **1922** the plates came to light again. After this the **Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams (TTD)** began publishing them, and scholars edited them in volumes. The renowned singer **M. Balamuralikrishna** set more than 800 sankirtanas to music, and many leading Carnatic singers made them popular.

## Legacy

Today Annamacharya's sankirtanas are part of daily life at Tirumala and are regularly sung on Carnatic concert stages. The TTD holds events every year on his birth and death anniversaries. In **2004** India Post issued a postage stamp in his honour, and in **1997** the Telugu film **"Annamayya"** was made on his life and became very popular. In 2022 Andhra Pradesh's new **Annamayya district** was also named after him.

Annamacharya is among the saints of the Bhakti movement who brought God closer to ordinary people by singing in the people's language instead of Sanskrit — and at the same time gave Indian classical music a new and lasting structure.
