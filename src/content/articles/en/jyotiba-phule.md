## Early Life

**Jyotirao Govindrao Phule** was born on **11 April 1827**. Most sources take Pune as his birthplace, while the family's roots were linked to Satara district. He belonged to the **Mali** caste, which did farming and flower-garland making — the family name 'Phule' came from this occupation. In the caste system of the time the Mali community was counted among the Shudra varna.

His schooling was interrupted for a time in childhood, but later he completed his English education in 1847 at the **Scottish Mission High School** in Pune. Here he became familiar with books such as Thomas Paine's *Rights of Man*, from which he got the idea of equality and human rights.

## An Insult, a Resolve

According to popular accounts, in **1848** Phule went to the wedding procession of a Brahmin friend, where some people insulted him over his caste and told him to leave the procession. This experience made him resolve to work against caste discrimination.

In the same year he taught his wife **Savitribai Phule**, and together they began a school for girls in Pune. Later he also opened schools for children regarded as untouchable. In 1863 a home for the prevention of infanticide for widows was begun. These efforts for women's education are discussed in detail in a separate article; here the focus is on Jyotirao's ideas.

## Gulamgiri (1873)

**'Gulamgiri'** (Slavery), published in Marathi in **1873**, is Phule's most famous work. In this book, written in dialogue style, he argued that the caste system is not God's decree but a centuries-old arrangement in which Shudras and Ati-Shudras were kept deprived of knowledge and property.

Phule gave new interpretations of mythological stories — for instance he saw King Bali as a just king of the peasants. He dedicated the book to **those who struggled against slavery in America** and compared India's caste slavery with American slavery. This interpretation of his is still a subject of discussion and disagreement, but it gave the lower castes a language to question their condition.

## The Satyashodhak Samaj

On **24 September 1873** Phule founded the **Satyashodhak Samaj** ('Society of Truth-Seekers') in Pune. Its main aims were:

- opposition to caste-based hierarchy and the equality of all human beings;
- conducting marriages and other rites without the mediation of priests;
- spreading education among Shudras, Ati-Shudras and women.

The Samaj conducted marriages without Brahmin priests and carried its message to the villages. Even after Phule, this movement became the base of Maharashtra's non-Brahmin movement.

## Peasants, Education and the Municipality

From 1876 Phule worked for some years as a nominated member of the Pune Municipality. In **1882** he gave evidence before the Education Commission (Hunter Commission), demanding compulsory primary education in villages and encouragement for children of the lower castes.

In **'Shetkaryacha Asud'** (The Cultivator's Whipcord; written around 1881, with sources differing on the year of publication) he described the exploitation of peasants at the hands of moneylenders, priests and colonial officials. His other works include the play **'Tritiya Ratna'** (1855) and **'Sarvajanik Satyadharma'**.

## Last Years and Legacy

In **1888** he was given the title **'Mahatma'**. In the same year, after a paralytic stroke, his health kept declining and he died in Pune on **28 November 1890**.

Phule was among the first thinkers to say that India's progress was linked not only to freedom from British rule but also to freedom from internal social slavery. Dr. Bhimrao Ambedkar counted him among his three gurus. Today, in debates on education, reservation and social justice, Phule's name is invoked as a source of inspiration.
