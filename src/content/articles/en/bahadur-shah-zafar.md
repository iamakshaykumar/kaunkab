## An Emperor in Name

**Bahadur Shah II**, known by his pen name **'Zafar'**, was born in Delhi on **24 October 1775**. He was the son of the emperor **Akbar Shah II** and Lal Bai. On **28 September 1837**, at about 62 years of age, he came to the throne.

By then the Mughal empire had shrunk to a name. Real power was in the hands of the East India Company; the emperor received a pension from the Company, and his authority was confined almost to the **Red Fort** and the city of Delhi. The Company had also hinted that after Zafar his successor might not receive the title of emperor.

## The Poet Emperor

Even though political power was gone, Zafar's court was a major centre of Urdu poetry. His teacher was **Ibrahim Zauq**, and **Mirza Ghalib** was also connected with his court. Zafar himself composed ghazals and was also skilled in calligraphy. His poetry shows a Sufi colouring, the transience of the world and a deep sadness.

## 1857: The Symbol of the Revolt

The sepoys who had rebelled at Meerut on **10 May 1857** reached Delhi the next day and demanded leadership from the aged emperor. Zafar hesitated at first, but on **12 May** he formally met the sepoys and then gave his support to the revolt. For rebels across the country the name of the Mughal emperor became a common symbol — both Hindus and Muslims fought in his name.

But Zafar held little real control. In Delhi there was tussling among the sepoys, courtiers and princes, and a shortage of money and supplies. In September 1857 the British recaptured Delhi.

## Arrest and Trial

Zafar took refuge in **Humayun's Tomb**, where on **20 September 1857** Captain **William Hodson** made him surrender on a promise to spare his life. The next day Hodson shot his sons **Mirza Mughal** and **Mirza Khizr Sultan** and his grandson **Mirza Abu Bakr**.

On **27 January 1858** Zafar's trial began, in the Red Fort itself. He faced four charges, including aiding the rebellion and waging war against the British government. On **9 March 1858** he was found guilty. Instead of the death sentence he was sentenced to exile. Sources give differing figures on the duration of the trial.

## Exile in Rangoon and the End

On the morning of **7 October 1858** Zafar set out from Delhi in bullock carts with his begum **Zinat Mahal** and some members of the family, and reached **Rangoon** in Burma in December 1858. There he lived under strict guard. He died on **7 November 1862** at about 87 years of age. The British buried him secretly so that his grave would not become the centre of any movement.

With Zafar the nearly three-hundred-year-old Mughal dynasty came to an end. Historians remember him as a weak ruler but a sensitive poet and as the most important symbol of the struggle of 1857. In 1975, on the bicentenary of his birth, the Government of India also issued a postage stamp.
