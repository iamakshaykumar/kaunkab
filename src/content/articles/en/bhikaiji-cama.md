## A Parsi Daughter of Bombay

**Bhikaiji Cama** was born in **1861** in a wealthy **Parsi** family (most sources say Bombay, some Navsari). On **3 August 1885** she married the lawyer **Rustomji Cama**, who was a supporter of British rule. The two did not share the same views, and the marriage was not happy. Bhikaiji devoted her time to social service.

In **1896** the **plague** broke out in the Bombay Presidency. Bhikaiji nursed the patients and herself fell ill. In **1902** she was sent to Britain for treatment — and from here a new chapter of her life began.

## A Revolutionary in London and Paris

In **1904** she met **Shyamji Krishna Varma** in London, and for some time she also worked as a private secretary to **Dadabhai Naoroji**. In **1905** she took part in founding the **Indian Home Rule Society** and was also present at the inauguration of **India House** in London. After this she moved to **Paris**, where she became a co-founder of the **Paris Indian Society**.

From Paris she had revolutionary papers such as **'Bande Mataram'** and **'Madan's Talwar'** (named after Madan Lal Dhingra) printed and secretly sent to India. Because she lived in France, the British government could not lay hands on her directly.

## Stuttgart, 22 August 1907

In August 1907 the **International Socialist Congress** was held at **Stuttgart** in Germany, where socialist leaders from all over the world had gathered. On **22 August 1907** Bhikaiji Cama came to the platform, drew the world's attention to the harmful effects of British rule on India, and unfurled a flag, calling it **"the flag of India's independence"**. According to one of her biographers, she asked the assembly to stand and salute this flag. It is often called **the first Indian flag to be unfurled on foreign soil**.

## What Did the Flag Look Like?

Cama's flag was based on the **"Calcutta Flag"** hoisted at Parsi Bagan Square in Calcutta on **7 August 1906**, the design of which is credited to **Sachindra Prasad Bose** and **Hemchandra Kanungo**. Many sources also mention a role for **Savarkar** and Shyamji Krishna Varma in designing Cama's flag.

The flag had three horizontal stripes — generally described as **green, saffron and red**, although sources do not agree on the order of the stripes. The top stripe had **eight lotuses**, believed to symbolise the eight provinces of British India. On the middle stripe was written **"Vande Mataram"** in Devanagari. At the bottom were the **sun** and a **crescent**; according to one interpretation, these symbolised the Hindu and Muslim faiths. This original flag is displayed today at the **Maratha and Kesari Library** in **Pune**.

## The Long Years of Exile

After Stuttgart she also went to the **United States** for a while and returned to England in **1908**, but soon made Paris her base again. Her home in Paris was regarded as a meeting place for Indian revolutionaries travelling to and from Europe, and British intelligence kept constant watch on her.

After the assassination of Curzon Wyllie in London in **1909**, Britain demanded Cama's extradition from France, but France refused. During the First World War she was kept under house arrest at **Vichy** in France from **1915** to **1917**.

When she grew weak with old age and paralysis, she asked for permission to return to India. In **1935** she was allowed to return to Bombay on the condition that she stay away from seditious activities. She died in Bombay on **13 August 1936** at the age of 74. She donated a large part of her property for the education of girls.

The Stuttgart flag was different from today's Tiranga, but it gave the world the message that India was fashioning the symbol of its own freedom — and this message was given by a woman, from a foreign platform.
