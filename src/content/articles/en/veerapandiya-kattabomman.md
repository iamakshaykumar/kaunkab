## Who Were the Palayakkarars

Since the time of Vijayanagara and then the Nayakas of Madurai, southern Tamil Nadu had many small feudal rulers, called **palayakkarars** ("polygars" in English). Each palayakkarar was the master of a **palayam** (territory), where he collected taxes, kept an army and dispensed justice, giving tribute to the ruler above him in return. In the 18th century, when the **East India Company** obtained from the Nawab of the Carnatic (Arcot) the right to collect revenue from this region, conflict between the Company and the polygars began to grow.

## The Ruler of Panchalankurichi

**Veerapandiya Kattabomman** was born on **3 January 1760** at **Panchalankurichi** in today's Thoothukudi district. His family was of Telugu origin. At about the age of thirty he became the palayakkarar of Panchalankurichi after his father.

The Company wanted the polygars to pay it tribute (kist) directly and to accept its supremacy. Kattabomman regarded this as an attack on his rights. Citing drought, he stopped paying the arrears of tax.

## The Ramanathapuram Incident (1798)

In **1798** the dispute over arrears of tax between Kattabomman and **Jackson**, the Collector of Tirunelveli, deepened. A few months later the two met at **Ramanathapuram**, and the meeting turned into a clash. In it **Clarke**, an officer of the Company's army, was killed, and Kattabomman got away from there. An inquiry into the matter was held later, in which Kattabomman was found not guilty. Even so, distrust remained between the Company and him.

## The First Polygar War (1799)

In **1799**, when Kattabomman refused to meet the Collector, the Company sent an army under **Major John Alexander Bannerman**. The fort of Panchalankurichi was attacked. Kattabomman left the fort and for some time continued the fight from the forests.

The Company put pressure on neighbouring rulers to have him captured. With the help of polygars such as **Ettappan** of Ettayapuram, who were allied with the British, and **Vijaya Raghunatha Tondaiman**, the king of Pudukkottai, Kattabomman was captured on **1 October 1799** (some sources give the date of arrest as late September). Many historians regard the trial held before Bannerman as a sham. On **16 October 1799**, at about the age of 39, he was publicly hanged at **Kayathar**. His associate **Subramania Pillai** was also hanged, and the fort of Panchalankurichi was demolished.

## The Second Polygar War (1800–1801)

Even after Kattabomman's death the resistance did not end. In **1800** rebellion flared up again, with his brother **Oomaidurai** as its main leader. Leaders such as the **Marudu brothers** of Sivaganga and **Dheeran Chinnamalai** in the Kongu region also rose against the Company. In May 1801 the Company's army captured Panchalankurichi again. On **16 November 1801** Oomaidurai and the Marudu brothers were hanged. In the same year, after the Carnatic Treaty of **31 July 1801**, the Company took direct control of this whole region, and the zamindari system replaced the polygar system.

## Memory and Debate

In Tamil Nadu, Kattabomman is regarded as a symbol of early resistance against the British. There is a memorial to him at Kayathar, a postage stamp was issued on **16 October 1999** on the 200th anniversary of his martyrdom, and a communication station of the Indian Navy is named **INS Kattabomman**.

Historians also remind us that his image has many sides. Folk tales remember him as a protector of the poor, while some local traditions and the Company's records describe him as a feudal lord who plundered neighbouring areas. His struggle was not a national movement in the modern sense but a struggle to defend his own authority and traditional rights — yet this very struggle later became an inspiring story of opposition to British rule in South India.
