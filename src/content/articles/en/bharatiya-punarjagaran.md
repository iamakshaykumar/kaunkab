## Background: The Darkness of the 19th Century and Modern Consciousness

In the latter half of the 18th century, along with colonial expansion, Indian society was struggling with serious social and intellectual stagnation. Caste discrimination, untouchability, child marriage, female infanticide and the inhuman practice of **sati**, in which widows were burnt alive, had taken deep root in society in the name of religion.

In such a period of transition, **Raja Ram Mohan Roy**, born in 1772 at Radhanagar in Bengal, emerged as a new light. He favoured a synthesis of the best ideas of East and West. He is unanimously called the **'Father of Modern India'** and the **'Pioneer of the Indian Renaissance'**.

## The Historic Struggle Against Sati

The biggest turning point in Ram Mohan Roy's life came when, after the death of his elder brother Jagmohan in 1811, his sister-in-law Alakmanjari was forcibly made to sit on the pyre and burnt as a sati before his eyes. Her screams shook Ram Mohan Roy to the core, and he vowed to end this brutal practice.

- By studying the Vedas and Upanishads in depth, he proved that no ancient Hindu scripture supports the practice of sati; it is an evil custom invented later.
- He himself went to the cremation grounds, persuaded the widows' relatives and kept watch.
- Under the pressure of his tireless public awakening and petitions, the then Governor-General **Lord William Bentinck**, on **4 December 1829**, passed Regulation XVII, declaring sati illegal and a punishable offence equivalent to murder.

## 1828: The Founding of the Brahmo Samaj

On 20 August 1828 he founded the **'Brahmo Samaj'**. Its main principles were:
1. **Monotheism:** Worship of only one all-pervading, formless God.
2. **Opposition to idol worship and sacrifice:** An end to religious ostentation and superstition.
3. **Equality and brotherhood:** Respect for all human beings without distinction of caste, varna or gender.

The Brahmo Samaj later inspired social reformers such as Debendranath Tagore, Keshub Chunder Sen and Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar, who did extensive work for widow remarriage and women's education.

## Modern Education and Freedom of the Press

Ram Mohan Roy considered a scientific outlook and modern education essential for building modern India:
- In 1817, together with David Hare, he helped found the renowned **Hindu College** (later Presidency College) in Kolkata.
- In 1825 he founded the **Vedanta College**, where both Indian philosophy and Western science were taught.
- For the freedom of the Indian press, he filed a petition in the Supreme Court against the British government's censorship laws.

## Historical Significance

In 1830 the Mughal emperor Akbar II sent him to England after giving him the title 'Raja'. He died on 27 September 1833 in Bristol, England. Raja Ram Mohan Roy had dreamt of that modern, scientific and reformist India on whose foundation today's democratic India stands.
