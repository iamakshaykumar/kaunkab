## A Small Book, a Great Identity

The **Tirukkural** (or simply the 'Kural') is the most famous Tamil book of ethics. By tradition it is regarded as the work of the saint-poet **Thiruvalluvar** (Valluvar). It is so respected in Tamil society that it is also called by names such as **'Tamil Marai'** (the Tamil Veda) and **'Ulaga Podhu Marai'** (the common scripture of the whole world).

'Kural' means short. Each verse of this book is a short couplet of two lines, written in a metre called **kural venba**. Each kural has only seven words, yet a whole lesson fits into it.

## Composition and Structure

The Tirukkural has **133 chapters** and each chapter has **10 kurals** — that is, **1330 kurals** in all. The whole book is divided into three parts:

- **Aram** (dharma, virtue) — 38 chapters: subjects such as truth, non-violence, compassion, gratitude, householder life and self-restraint.
- **Porul** (wealth and polity) — 70 chapters: the qualities of a king, ministers, the army, friendship, education and lessons on running a government.
- **Inbam** or **Kamam** (love) — 25 chapters: the feelings of lovers in union and separation.

This structure resembles the dharma-artha-kama of the Sanskrit tradition, but it has no separate part on 'moksha'. A great quality of the book is that it does not speak by naming any one sect or deity, which is why people of different religions have claimed it as their own. Its first kural is famous; its sense is — as 'A' is the first of all letters, so the primal God is the beginning of the world.

## Who Was Thiruvalluvar?

There is **almost no reliable information** about Thiruvalluvar's life. The author's name does not appear anywhere in the book. Stories about his birthplace, caste, his wife Vasuki and the examination of the book in the assembly of scholars at Madurai are later **folk traditions**, which historians do not regard as evidence. His popular portrait too is a modern imagining.

## How Old Is the Kural?

The date of composition of the Tirukkural is **disputed**. Some Tamil scholars held it to be as old as the third century BCE, others of the first century CE. On the basis of studies of language and vocabulary, **Kamil Zvelebil** placed it at about **450–500 CE**, and S. Vaiyapuri Pillai in the sixth century or later. Today many scholars consider a time around **500 CE** the most probable. By this reckoning it is counted in the group of the 'Eighteen Minor Works' (Pathinenkilkanakku) that came after the Sangam-age poems.

The Government of Tamil Nadu takes 31 BCE as the base year for the 'Thiruvalluvar era', but this is a traditional calculation, not historical evidence.

## Commentaries, Printing and Translations

In the medieval period **ten ancient commentators** wrote commentaries on the Kural. The most famous is the commentary of **Parimelazhagar**, believed to date from about the thirteenth century (1272 CE or earlier).

For centuries the book was preserved by being written on palm leaves. Its first known **printed edition appeared in 1812**. In Europe the Italian priest **Costanzo Beschi** made a Latin translation of its first two parts in **1730**. Later came the translations of **F. W. Ellis** (1819), **Karl Graul** (1856) and **G. U. Pope** (1886). Today the Tirukkural has been translated into more than fifty languages of the world, making it one of the most translated Indian texts.

## Significance

The Tirukkural's lessons are not limited to any one time or society — honest earning, just government, testing of friendship and restraint of speech remain as useful today. Through Tolstoy its teaching of non-violence reached Gandhi. The 133-foot statue of Thiruvalluvar standing in the sea at Kanyakumari is a symbol of how high a place this small book holds in Tamil culture.
