## Background: From the Deccan towards North India

After the death of the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb in 1707 CE, the Mughal Empire began to break up rapidly. Released from Mughal captivity, **Chhatrapati Shahu Maharaj** took the throne at Satara and in 1713 CE appointed **Balaji Vishwanath** as his "Peshwa" (prime minister). From here the golden age of the Peshwas began.

Balaji Vishwanath strengthened the economic foundation of the Marathas by obtaining from the Mughal court the legal right to collect **"chauth"** (25% of the total produce) and **"sardeshmukhi"** (10%) in the six subas of the Deccan.

## Baji Rao I: The Undefeated Warrior and a Stormy Expansion

In 1720 CE, at the age of just 20, **Baji Rao I** became Peshwa. He spoke the famous words to Chhatrapati Shahu:
> *"Let us strike at the hollow trunk of this old tree, and the branches will fall by themselves."*

Baji Rao turned Maratha strategy from a defensive posture in the Deccan into an aggressive all-India expansion:
1. **The Battle of Palkhed (1728):** He surrounded the Nizam-ul-Mulk of Hyderabad on all sides and forced him to surrender without heavy bloodshed.
2. **The conquest of Malwa and Gujarat:** Maratha commanders (Malharrao Holkar, Ranoji Scindia) established control over central India.
3. **The raid on Delhi (1737):** Dodging the Mughal armies, Baji Rao reached Delhi with only 500 horsemen, which made the Mughal emperor Muhammad Shah tremble.
4. **The Battle of Bhopal (1737):** By jointly defeating the Nizam and the Mughal army, he obtained a formal charter of authority over the whole of central India.

## Shaniwar Wada and the Maratha Confederacy

The Peshwas' capital **Pune** became the throbbing centre of political power. The huge **Shaniwar Wada** was the symbol of this empire's pride.

For smooth governance of the vast territory the "Maratha Confederacy" was formed:
- The **Scindias (Shinde)** at Gwalior
- The **Holkars** at Indore
- The **Gaekwads** at Baroda
- The **Bhonsles** at Nagpur

## The Third Battle of Panipat (1761) and Revival

In the 1750s the Marathas had become the protectors of the throne of Delhi. When the ruler of Afghanistan, **Ahmad Shah Abdali**, attacked the Punjab, the Maratha army marched north under Sadashivrao Bhau and Vishwasrao.

The **Third Battle of Panipat**, fought on 14 January 1761, was extremely fierce. The Marathas showed extraordinary bravery, but because of a shortage of supplies and a lack of local support, the Marathas were defeated and almost an entire young generation was lost.

Although this was a great blow, under Peshwa Madhavrao I and Mahadji Scindia the Marathas, in the very next decade, regained control of Delhi and re-established their lost power.
