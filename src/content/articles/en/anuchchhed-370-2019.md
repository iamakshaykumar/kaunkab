## What Article 370 Was

In **1947** Maharaja **Hari Singh** of the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir signed the Instrument of Accession to India, which handed over subjects such as defence, foreign affairs and communications to India. Then **Article 370**, added in the Constituent Assembly in **1949**, gave the state a special position. Under it, most provisions of the Indian Constitution would apply to the state only when the President issued an order and the state government consented. Jammu and Kashmir had its own constitution and its own flag.

Through the Presidential Order of **1954** many parts of the Constitution were applied to the state, and the same order added **Article 35A**, under which the state legislature could define "permanent residents" and give them special rights such as buying land, government jobs and scholarships. Over time, through many such orders, the scope of 370 kept shrinking, but the article remained politically very important. The Bharatiya Jana Sangh and later the BJP had long demanded its removal, while the main regional parties of Kashmir linked it to the identity of the state.

## The Decision of 5 August 2019

After the BJP–PDP coalition broke up in June 2018, the state came first under Governor's rule and then President's rule, so the legislative assembly stood dissolved. In the first week of August 2019 additional security forces were sent to the Valley and the Amarnath Yatra was halted.

On **5 August 2019** President **Ram Nath Kovind** issued a constitutional order by which all provisions of the Indian Constitution became applicable to Jammu and Kashmir and the 1954 order became inoperative. The same day Home Minister **Amit Shah** moved a resolution and the **Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Bill** in the Rajya Sabha. The Rajya Sabha passed it on 5 August and the Lok Sabha on **6 August**. By a presidential declaration the next day only one clause of Article 370 was kept in force, which ended its character of granting special status.

## Reorganisation and the New Arrangement

Under the Reorganisation Act, from **31 October 2019** the state was divided into two union territories:

- **Jammu and Kashmir** — a union territory with a legislative assembly.
- **Ladakh** — a union territory without a legislative assembly, comprising the Leh and Kargil districts.

Central laws now began to apply directly, and in 2020 new **domicile** rules were framed. Along with the decision, sweeping restrictions on communication were imposed in the Valley — mobile and internet services were shut down, Section 144 was imposed and many leaders, including former chief ministers **Farooq Abdullah**, **Omar Abdullah** and **Mehbooba Mufti**, were detained. Services were restored in phases over many months; high-speed 4G internet returned across the territory in February 2021.

## Support and Opposition

The government said that Article 370 encouraged separatism and terrorism and was an obstacle to development and investment, and that its removal would allow laws such as those on reservation, the right to education and the right to information to apply to all residents. Several other parties besides the BJP also supported it in Parliament, and many people in Jammu and Leh welcomed it.

Opponents — including the Congress, the National Conference, the PDP and the Left parties — said that taking such a major decision without the consent of the state's elected assembly went against the federal structure, and that converting a state into a union territory was unprecedented. Most leaders of the Kashmir Valley and several groups in Kargil opposed it; Mehbooba Mufti called it "the blackest day in Indian democracy". Human rights organisations criticised the long communication blackout and the detentions. Pakistan downgraded its diplomatic relations with India and halted trade; India called it an internal matter.

## The Supreme Court Verdict

Many petitions against the decision were filed in the Supreme Court. A five-judge Constitution Bench headed by Chief Justice **D. Y. Chandrachud** heard the case for 16 days in 2023 and delivered a unanimous verdict on **11 December 2023**. The Court said that Article 370 was a **temporary provision**, that after accession Jammu and Kashmir did not retain an internal sovereignty separate from India, and that the Presidential Orders were valid. The creation of Ladakh as a separate union territory was also upheld. On the question of making Jammu and Kashmir a union territory, the Court recorded the government's assurance that statehood would be restored, and directed that statehood be restored "at the earliest" and that assembly elections be held by **30 September 2024**.

## The Road Ahead and Significance

In **September–October 2024** assembly elections were held in Jammu and Kashmir after ten years, and after the victory of the National Conference-led alliance, **Omar Abdullah** became the first Chief Minister of the union territory. In November 2024 the new assembly passed a resolution demanding the restoration of special status, which has no binding force. In Ladakh, agitations continued over demands for statehood and for protection under the Sixth Schedule of the Constitution.

The decision of 5 August 2019 is counted among the biggest events in the constitutional history of independent India. Supporters regard it as a step towards the full integration of the country, while critics view it in connection with questions of federalism and local consent. The question of restoring statehood is still under discussion.
