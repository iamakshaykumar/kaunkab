## The Babu Sahib of Jagdishpur

Kunwar Singh was born in **1777** at **Jagdishpur** in the Shahabad (now Bhojpur) region of Bihar. He belonged to a branch of the Ujjainiya Rajput clan and became the master of the Jagdishpur estate after his father's death in 1826. People respectfully called him **"Babu Sahib"**. By the 1850s his estate was sunk in debt and his relations with the Company government's revenue system had turned bitter — historians count this among the causes of his rebellion.

## The Siege of Arrah (July–August 1857)

On **25 July 1857**, three native infantry regiments of the **Danapur** cantonment near Patna rebelled and crossed the Son river to reach **Arrah**. Kunwar Singh, then about eighty years old, took command of them. The rebels seized the treasury and jail of Arrah. A few British officers and Sikh soldiers fortified themselves in a small two-storey building — **"Arrah House"** — and held out; it was besieged for about a week (27 July to 3 August).

The relief party sent from Danapur under Captain Dunbar was badly defeated in an ambush at night. But on **3 August**, **Major Vincent Eyre**, with artillery, defeated Kunwar Singh's force near **Bibiganj** and freed Arrah. In mid-August the British took Jagdishpur and did heavy damage to it.

## The Nine-Month Campaign

Even after losing his home, Kunwar Singh did not give up. With his soldiers he moved west through areas such as **Rewa** and **Banda** and reached **Lucknow** in December 1857, where the rebel leadership of Awadh welcomed him. In **March 1858** he captured **Azamgarh** and kept several British detachments engaged. Speed, sudden attacks and the art of retreat — that is, guerrilla warfare — were his strength.

## Crossing the Ganga and the Final Victory

In April 1858 he was crossing the **Ganga** to return to his estate. According to the popular account, a bullet hit his arm or wrist at this time, and fearing that poison would spread, he cut off his hand himself. Reaching Jagdishpur in a wounded state, on **23 April 1858** he defeated a British detachment that had come there and regained his estate.

But the wound and fatigue took their toll. He died at Jagdishpur on **26 April 1858**.

## Legacy

After his death his brother **Babu Amar Singh** continued the struggle for several months. Kunwar Singh was among the few leaders of 1857 who ran a long military campaign at a very advanced age, and in Bihar he is remembered as **"Veer Kunwar Singh"**. Tales of his bravery are still sung in Bhojpuri folk songs. In 1966 a postage stamp was issued on him, and in 1992 a university was founded in Bihar in his name. The details of some stories, such as the severed hand, differ in different sources, but the basic events of his struggle are confirmed even by contemporary British records.
