## Meenakshi and Sundareshwarar

The **Meenakshi-Sundareshwarar Temple**, standing in the heart of the city of **Madurai** in Tamil Nadu, is dedicated to the goddess **Meenakshi** — a form of Parvati — and her husband **Sundareshwarar** (Shiva). 'Meenakshi' means 'the fish-eyed one'. In local mythology Meenakshi is regarded as a Pandya princess who married Shiva. This is a **legend**, but it is so deeply tied to Madurai's identity that every year the wedding is re-enacted as a grand festival.

## An Ancient Pilgrimage Site

Madurai is a very old city — it is described in Tamil texts of the Sangam period (about the 1st to 4th century CE). The temple is counted among the **Paadal Petra Sthalams**, the Shiva shrines whose glory was sung by the Nayanar saints in the **Tevaram** hymns (composed roughly between the 6th and 11th centuries). Tamil literature suggests that a temple existed here by about the 6th century. But the structure of that time was not like today's vast complex. The oldest surviving gopuram — the eastern one — is said to date from the 13th century, in the time of the Pandya king Maravarman Sundara Pandya.

## Destruction and Rebuilding

In 1310-11 **Malik Kafur**, a general of the Delhi Sultanate, raided South India and reached Madurai. According to available accounts, the temple was badly damaged and its wealth looted during this time, although sources differ on the exact scale of the damage. For a few decades afterwards Madurai was under rule linked to the Sultanate.

In the latter half of the 14th century the **Vijayanagara Empire** took control of Madurai, and worship and repairs at the temple began again. The real revival came in the 16th century, when **Viswanatha Nayak** (ruled about 1529-1564), appointed by Vijayanagara, laid the foundation of the **Nayak dynasty** at Madurai. The major rebuilding of the temple began in his time and was carried forward by later Nayak rulers.

## Expansion in the Nayak Period

The magnificent complex seen today is mainly of the **16th-17th century**:
- The complex spreads over about **14 acres** and has **14 gopurams** (tower gateways). The tallest, the **southern gopuram**, is about **52 metres (170 feet)** high and was built in the latter half of the 16th century.
- The **Ayirakkal Mandapam** (Hall of a Thousand Pillars) has 985 carved pillars; it was built under the supervision of **Ariyanatha Mudaliar**, minister of Viswanatha Nayak.
- The **Potramarai Kulam** (Golden Lotus Tank) is a sacred pool of about 50 × 37 metres, with a gold-plated lotus in the middle.
- **Tirumala Nayak** (ruled 1623-1655) greatly expanded the complex and added structures such as the Vasanta Mandapam.

The gopurams carry thousands of colourful **lime-plaster (stucco) sculptures** of gods, goddesses, mythical figures and animals, which are repainted from time to time. Tall gopurams, long corridors and vast pillared halls — these are the hallmarks of the **Nayak style**.

## Festivals and Significance

The temple's biggest festival is the **Chithirai Thiruvizha**, in which the **Tirukalyanam** (divine wedding) of Meenakshi and Sundareshwarar is celebrated for about fifteen days, drawing lakhs of devotees.

The Meenakshi Temple shows how a pilgrimage site is built, broken and restored again and again over centuries. Its roots lie in the ancient Tamil devotional tradition, while its grand present form is the gift of the Nayak period. It is regarded as the soul of Madurai — even the city's streets are traditionally laid out in concentric circles around the temple.
