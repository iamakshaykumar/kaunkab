## Childhood and a Difficult Youth

Sambhaji was born on **14 May 1657** at the fort of **Purandar**. His mother **Saibai** died when he was about two years old, so he was raised by his grandmother **Jijabai**. After the Treaty of Purandar in **1665**, the boy Sambhaji had to stay with the Mughals as a hostage, and in **1666** he escaped with his father from Aurangzeb's house arrest at Agra.

In his youth, relations between father and son were strained. Shivaji kept him at Panhala, and in **December 1678** Sambhaji left there and joined the Mughal commander **Dilir Khan**. He returned about a year later. The sources do not agree on the reasons for this rift: some later accounts make accusations against his character, but modern historians view these charges with caution and also consider court factionalism a major cause.

## Coronation

After Shivaji's death in **April 1680**, a faction at court tried to place the younger son **Rajaram** on the throne. Sambhaji advanced from Panhala, took control of Raigad, removed the opposing ministers, and was formally crowned at **Raigad** on **16 January 1681**.

## War on Many Fronts

Sambhaji's entire reign was spent in wars:

- **Mughals:** In 1681 Aurangzeb's rebel son **Prince Akbar** took refuge with him. After this Aurangzeb himself came to the Deccan with a huge army. Forts such as **Ramsej** near Nashik withstood a long Mughal siege, and Maratha armies kept raiding Mughal territories.
- **Siddis:** A campaign was fought against the Siddis of Janjira, but the sea fort could not be captured.
- **Portuguese:** Around 1683 he attacked the Bardez and Salcete areas of Goa; in January 1684, when a Mughal army approached, he had to return.
- **Mysore:** There was also conflict with the Wodeyar king of Mysore in the south.

Meanwhile Bijapur fell to the Mughals in **1686** and Golconda in **1687**, which increased the pressure on the Marathas further.

## A Scholar King

Sambhaji was not only a warrior. He wrote a Sanskrit work on politics and the duties of a king, **'Budhbhushanam'**, and works such as 'Nayikabheda', 'Satsatak' and 'Nakhshikh' in Braj Bhasha/Hindustani are also attributed to him. His close adviser **Kavi Kalash** was himself a poet and scholar.

## Capture at Sangameshwar and Martyrdom

In **February 1689**, at **Sangameshwar** in the Konkan, the Mughal commander **Muqarrab Khan** made a sudden attack and captured Sambhaji and Kavi Kalash; it is believed that some of their own people had given away their hiding place. Both were taken to Aurangzeb's camp, humiliated and subjected to harsh torture. On **11 March 1689** he was executed at **Tulapur** on the bank of the Bhima river. Mughal and Maratha accounts differ on the reasons for the execution and on the treatment of the body.

## Legacy

After Sambhaji's death his wife **Yesubai** and son **Shahu** went into Mughal captivity, and Rajaram took command of the struggle, making **Jinji** in the south his base. Aurangzeb hoped that the king's death would break Maratha power, but the opposite happened: the struggle became a people's war. Older historians often judged Sambhaji negatively, but later research has given greater weight to his difficult circumstances, military resolve and scholarship. Today in Maharashtra he is remembered as 'Dharmaveer' and as a defender of Swarajya.
