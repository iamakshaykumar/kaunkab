## Birth and a Childhood of Struggle

Chhatrasal was born on **4 May 1649** (in the present-day Tikamgarh region) to the Bundela Rajput **Champat Rai** and queen **Sarandha**. The Bundelas were connected with the royal house of Orchha. Champat Rai struggled against the Mughals for a long time, and Chhatrasal's parents died while he was still a child. According to tradition, when surrounded by the Mughal army they chose death rather than surrender.

In his youth Chhatrasal also served the Mughals for a short time. According to Bundeli tradition, he wanted to meet **Shivaji** and join his army, but Shivaji advised him to return to his own region and free Bundelkhand. This meeting is very famous in folk tradition, but its details rest on later sources.

## Revolt and the Building of a Kingdom

In **1671**, at the age of about 22, Chhatrasal raised a revolt against the Mughals with only a few horsemen and foot companions. The ravines, forests and hills of Bundelkhand were suited to guerrilla warfare. Gradually local chiefs and peasants joined him, and he began attacking Mughal outposts and collecting taxes.

In the last decades of Aurangzeb's rule, when the Mughal army was caught up in the wars of the Deccan, Chhatrasal brought a large area under his control. He made **Panna** his capital and ruled as 'Maharaja'. Panna is also the main centre of the Pranami sect, and according to tradition the saint Prannath was Chhatrasal's spiritual guide.

## Bangash's Invasion and Baji Rao's Help

In the 1720s **Muhammad Khan Bangash**, the Mughal governor of Allahabad, launched a major campaign against Bundelkhand. In **December 1728** Chhatrasal, then about 79 years old, was defeated and had to fall back towards **Jaitpur**, where he was besieged.

In this crisis Chhatrasal sent a letter to the Maratha Peshwa **Baji Rao I** asking for help. Baji Rao reached Bundelkhand swiftly in **March 1729** and cut off the supplies of Bangash's army. The besieged Mughal army was finally allowed to leave on the condition that it withdraw.

## Gratitude and Legacy

In return for this help Chhatrasal gave about **one-third of his kingdom** to Baji Rao. This gave the Marathas a strong base in north India, which later became the source of Maratha influence in Jhansi, Sagar and the surrounding regions. Baji Rao's wife **Mastani** is believed to have been Chhatrasal's daughter.

Chhatrasal died in **December 1731** (most sources say 20 December, some 14 December). After him the kingdom was divided among his sons. At **Dhubela** near Chhatarpur are his cenotaph and the Maharaja Chhatrasal Museum.

## Literature and Memory

The famous poet of the Riti period **Bhushan**, who also composed verse in praise of Shivaji, was an admirer of Chhatrasal too. Works such as Lal Kavi's 'Chhatraprakash' describe his life, although exaggeration is also found in such eulogistic compositions.

In Bundelkhand Chhatrasal still lives on in folk songs and sayings. His life shows how, in the eighteenth century, as Mughal power weakened, regional powers emerged and how their mutual alliances changed the political map of India.
