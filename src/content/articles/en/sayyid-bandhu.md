## The Sayyids of Barha

After the death of Aurangzeb (1707), the Mughal court became an arena of factional rivalry. In this period two brothers rose: **Sayyid Hasan Ali Khan**, who became famous as **Abdullah Khan** and "Qutb-ul-Mulk", and his younger brother **Sayyid Husain Ali Khan**. They belonged to the **Barha Sayyid** community of the Upper Doab, known for its military valour. Both served under Bahadur Shah I and held high posts in Allahabad and Bihar.

## Farrukhsiyar placed on the throne (1713)

After Bahadur Shah's death in 1712, **Jahandar Shah** became emperor in the war of succession. His nephew **Farrukhsiyar** put forward his claim from Bihar, and the Sayyid brothers backed him. In January 1713 Jahandar Shah was defeated near Agra, and in February Delhi fell to Farrukhsiyar.

As a reward, Abdullah Khan became **wazir** and Husain Ali Khan became **Mir Bakhshi** (chief of the army). Because of these two highest posts, real power came into their hands and Farrukhsiyar was left almost a nominal emperor.

## Clash with the emperor

Farrukhsiyar was unhappy with this situation. Through his favourites he kept trying to weaken the brothers. Husain Ali was made governor of the Deccan and sent away from Delhi, and it is said that the emperor also secretly incited his opponents.

In the Deccan, Husain Ali came to an agreement with the Marathas. In **1719**, through Mughal grants (sanads), the Peshwa **Balaji Vishwanath** received the right to collect **chauth** and **sardeshmukhi** in the six provinces of the Deccan, along with recognition of Shahu's swarajya. In return, a Maratha army came to Delhi with Husain Ali.

## 1719: the year of making and unmaking emperors

In February 1719 Husain Ali reached Delhi with Maratha soldiers. Farrukhsiyar was removed from the throne, blinded and kept in prison, where he was murdered in April 1719.

After this, within a few months the brothers installed three emperors: the sickly **Rafi-ud-Darajat**, then **Shah Jahan II**, and finally, in September 1719, the seventeen-year-old **Muhammad Shah**. It is because of this ability to "make emperors" that history calls them the **"kingmakers"**. In their time the jizya tax was abolished and a policy of reconciliation with Rajput and Maratha chiefs was adopted.

## Downfall (1720–1722)

Muhammad Shah also wanted freedom from Sayyid control. The faction of **Turani** nobles (of Central Asian origin), led by **Nizam-ul-Mulk**, was hostile to the brothers. In 1720 the Nizam rebelled in the Deccan.

While marching to the Deccan against the Nizam, Husain Ali Khan was stabbed to death on **8 October 1720** by **Haidar Beg Dughlat** at the instigation of Turani conspirators. A few weeks later, in November 1720, Abdullah Khan was defeated at the Battle of **Hasanpur**, southwest of Delhi, and taken prisoner. In October 1722 he was poisoned to death in captivity.

## Assessment

For a long time the Sayyid brothers were seen as power-hungry conspirators. Scholars such as the historian Satish Chandra have also read their politics differently: as an attempt to run the empire by taking Hindustani nobles, Rajputs and Marathas along. But their era also shows that the emperor had now become a puppet in the hands of the nobles, which was the biggest sign of the Mughal decline.
