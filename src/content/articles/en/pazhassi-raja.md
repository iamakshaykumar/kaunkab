## The Prince of Kottayam

**Kerala Varma** was born on 3 January 1753 in the western branch of the **Kottayam** royal family of Malabar (today's Kannur district, Kerala). His palace was at a place called Pazhassi, so he became famous as **Pazhassi Raja**. From the 1770s he fought against the occupation of Malabar by Hyder Ali of Mysore and then Tipu Sultan. In this period he gained experience of guerrilla war in the forests of Purlimala and Wayanad.

## The Root of the Clash with the British

After the Treaty of Seringapatam of 1792, Malabar passed into the hands of the East India Company. The Company gave the right to collect Kottayam's revenue not to Pazhassi Raja but to one of his uncles, **Veera Varma**, and fixed a heavy land revenue. Pazhassi Raja saw this as a blow to both his rights and the peasants. In British records these fights are called the **'Cotiote War'**.

## The First Revolt (1793–1797)

From 1793 the Raja stopped revenue collection and began open opposition to the Company. The British sent an army to capture him, but he withdrew into the dense forests of Wayanad. In 1797 his army ambushed the detachment of Major Cameron in the **Periya Pass**; Cameron was killed and the Company suffered heavy losses. After this a settlement was made in 1797, in which the Raja was granted pardon and his property and annual allowance were returned.

## The Second Revolt (1800–1805) and Wayanad

After Tipu's death in 1799 the Company laid claim to **Wayanad**, which Pazhassi Raja regarded as his own. The struggle flared up again. This time the front for the Company was taken by **Colonel Arthur Wellesley** (later the Duke of Wellington). He had roads and outposts built in the forests, but even after several years of effort there was no decisive victory, and in 1803 he left for the battles of the Deccan.

On **11 October 1802**, archers led by the Raja's commander **Edachena Kunkan** and the Kurichya warrior **Thalakkal Chanthu** made a sudden assault on the fort of **Panamaram** and almost wiped out the garrison there; Captain Dickinson was killed. Sources differ on the number of attackers — it is given as between 150 and 175. This event gave new energy to the revolt throughout Wayanad.

## The Final Encounter (30 November 1805)

In 1804 **Thomas Harvey Baber** became sub-collector of Malabar. With the help of informers he found the Raja's whereabouts and set detachments of local soldiers on his trail. On **30 November 1805**, in an encounter on the bank of a stream called **Mavila Thodu** near Pulpally in Wayanad, Pazhassi Raja died.

There is no agreement on how he died. Baber gave the credit to one of his clerks; another British account links it to the detachment of a British captain. Folk tradition says that to avoid being captured the Raja swallowed his diamond ring and gave up his life. It is said that Baber handed over his body with respect for the last rites. Companions such as Thalakkal Chanthu and Edachena Kunkan were also killed in this same period.

## Legacy

Pazhassi Raja's struggle is counted among the earliest and longest resistances to the Company in South India. The basis of his strength was peasants, Nair warriors and tribal communities such as the Kurichya and Kurumba, who had a deep knowledge of the forest paths. In Kerala he is called the **'Kerala Simham'** (Lion of Kerala). His memorial is at Mananthavady (Wayanad).
