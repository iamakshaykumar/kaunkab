## The Beginning

**Babur**, the founder of the Mughal dynasty, defeated the Sultan of Delhi, Ibrahim Lodi, in the First Battle of Panipat in 1526. Babur came from Central Asia and described his life and campaigns in his autobiography, the *Baburnama*. His successor Humayun had to be out of power for some time, but later Mughal rule was re-established.

## Akbar and the System of Government

The empire expanded in the time of **Akbar** (reigned 1556-1605). He formed different kinds of political relations with many Rajput rulers and regional powers. Akbar made Fatehpur Sikri his capital, where the court stayed for some time.

In Mughal rule the rank and military responsibilities of officers were fixed through the **mansabdari system**. By being granted jagirs, they got the means to meet their salary and administrative duties. This system was not static; rulers changed ranks and jagirs from time to time.

## Court, Art and Construction

In the Mughal court Persian became the main language of administration and literature, while there was also contact with the languages and arts of different regions. Painters made scenes of history, war, the court and nature. In works such as the Akbarnama, the image of royal power was presented through these paintings and written accounts.

In Jahangir's time painting and natural subjects received patronage. In Shah Jahan's reign grand structures such as the Red Fort of Delhi and the Taj Mahal were built. These buildings show forms of a blending of Indian, Persian and Central Asian architectural traditions.

## Expansion and Change

In Aurangzeb's reign the empire spread to the south, but long military campaigns put pressure on resources. In the last years of the 17th century and in the 18th century regional powers grew stronger and the grip of the Mughal centre began to weaken. The Marathas, Sikhs, Rajputs and other powers increased their influence in their own regions.

After the revolt of 1857 the British removed the last Mughal emperor, Bahadur Shah Zafar, from his position and exiled him. Mughal power ended, but its legacy is still seen today in administration, language, food, music and architecture.
