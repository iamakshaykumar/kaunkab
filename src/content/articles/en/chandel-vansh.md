## From Feudatory to Independent Kingdom

The Chandela dynasty is held to have begun in the ninth century with **Nannuka** (c. 831–845 CE), the ruler of a small kingdom around **Khajuraho**. The early Chandela rulers were **feudatories** of the powerful **Gurjara-Pratihara** empire of Kannauj.

Their territory is called **Jejakabhukti** in inscriptions. According to an inscription from Mahoba, this name came from **Jayashakti (Jeja)**, an early ruler of the dynasty. This same region later came to be called **Bundelkhand**. The main capitals and strongholds of the Chandelas were **Khajuraho**, **Kalinjar** (Kalanjara) and **Mahoba**.

Chandela inscriptions describe the dynasty as **Chandravanshi** (of the lunar race). According to a later bardic tale (the Mahoba Khand), Chandravarma, the founding ancestor of the dynasty, was born of Hemavati and the Moon god — historians regard this as a **later fancy**.

## Yashovarman and Dhanga: Towards the Peak

In the tenth century the Pratihara empire began to weaken, and the Chandelas benefited from this.

- **Yashovarman** (c. 925–950 CE) — became practically independent. He won the strong fort of **Kalinjar** and built the **Lakshmana Temple** at Khajuraho, dedicated to Vishnu. (He is a different king from the eighth-century Yashovarman of Kannauj.)
- **Dhanga** (c. 950–999 CE; some scholars say until 1002 CE) — his inscriptions make no mention of any Pratihara overlord, so he is regarded as the founder of the **complete independence** of the Chandelas. The **Vishvanatha Temple** of Khajuraho dates from his time. According to Firishta, the king of Kalinjar (who is taken to be Dhanga) sent an army to help the Shahi king Jayapala against the Ghaznavids.

## Vidyadhara and Mahmud of Ghazni

Chandela power was at its height in the time of **Vidyadhara** (c. 1003–1035 CE). The grandest temple of Khajuraho, the **Kandariya Mahadeva Temple**, is associated with him.

In 1018 **Mahmud of Ghazni** plundered Kannauj, and the Pratihara king there fled. According to Ibn al-Athir, a king named 'Bida' (Vidya) then killed the king of Kannauj as punishment for cowardice — the Mahoba inscription also speaks of Vidyadhara defeating the king of Kannauj.

After this Mahmud marched against the Chandela territory twice:

- **1019 CE** — the two armies stood facing each other across a river, but Vidyadhara withdrew at night and Mahmud plundered his camp.
- **1022 CE** — Mahmud besieged first Gwalior and then **Kalinjar**. According to Muslim historians the Chandela king made peace by presenting elephants; some modern historians regard this as an equal 'exchange of gifts'. It is clear that Mahmud did not capture the fort.

## A Period of Ups and Downs

After Vidyadhara, the Kalachuri kings **Gangeyadeva** and **Lakshmikarna** increased pressure on Chandela lands. **Kirtivarman** defeated Lakshmikarna and restored the prestige of the dynasty. According to tradition, Krishna Mishra's philosophical play **'Prabodhachandrodaya'** was staged at his court. Later **Madanavarman** (c. 1128–1165 CE) defeated the Kalachuri king Gayakarna.

## Paramardideva and the Decline

In the time of **Paramardideva** (Parmal, c. 1165–1203 CE), **Prithviraj Chauhan** attacked Mahoba, which is confirmed by inscriptions. The tales of the bravery of **Alha and Udal** in this war live on in folk songs, but many parts of them are historically doubtful.

Around **1202–1203 CE** **Qutb-ud-din Aibak** besieged Kalinjar. Paramardideva surrendered and died during that time. After this the Chandelas remained a small local power. By the end of the thirteenth century their kings did not even use a grand title like 'Maharajadhiraja', and around **1315 CE** the dynasty came to an end. Later, the power of the **Bundelas** rose in this region.

## Significance

The most lasting legacy of the Chandelas is the **temples of Khajuraho**, which are today a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The fort of **Kalinjar** was counted among the strongest forts of north India for centuries. In addition, the Chandela inscriptions, coins and reservoirs — including the lakes of Mahoba — are the foundation of the medieval history of Bundelkhand.
