## Prince Khurram

Shah Jahan was born on **5 January 1592** in Lahore to Prince Salim (later Emperor Jahangir). He was named **Khurram**. Raised at the court of his grandfather Akbar, Khurram proved a capable commander while still young. After successes in the Mewar and Deccan campaigns, Jahangir gave him the title **"Shah Jahan"** in 1617.

In 1612 he married Arjumand Banu Begum, daughter of Asaf Khan, known as **Mumtaz Mahal**. But in Jahangir's last years his conflict with Nur Jahan's faction grew, and from 1622 to 1626 Khurram rebelled against his father. He was defeated and was eventually forgiven.

## The road to the throne

After Jahangir's death in October 1627, with the help of his father-in-law Asaf Khan, Shah Jahan outmanoeuvred his stepbrother Shahryar. He was crowned at Agra on **14 February 1628**. To make his throne secure, Shahryar and other possible claimants were put to death; this harsh Mughal tradition of succession was later repeated among his own sons too.

## Empire and wars

- **Deccan:** In 1630–32 a terrible famine struck the Deccan and Gujarat, in which large numbers of people died; the emperor had free kitchens opened in some places. In 1632–33 Daulatabad fort was captured and by 1636 the Nizam Shahi of Ahmednagar had been completely ended. In 1635–36 Golconda and Bijapur accepted Mughal overlordship.
- **Bundelkhand:** The revolt of Jujhar Singh Bundela of Orchha was suppressed.
- **Central Asia:** In 1646–47 there was a campaign against Balkh and Badakhshan. Balkh was held for a short time but had to be given up after heavy expense.
- **Kandahar:** Kandahar came to the Mughals in 1638, but in 1649 the Safavids of Iran took it back. In spite of three major campaigns between 1649 and 1653, the Mughals could never win it again.

## Splendour and building

Shah Jahan's era was the peak of the pomp of the Mughal court. Soon after ascending the throne he had the jewel-studded **Peacock Throne** (Takht-e-Taus) made, which took several years to complete. In 1631 Mumtaz Mahal died after childbirth at Burhanpur, and in her memory the **Taj Mahal** was built at Agra. In 1639 work began on the new capital **Shahjahanabad** in Delhi and its fort (today's Red Fort). The Moti Masjid in Agra Fort and many buildings in Lahore and Ajmer are also from his time.

This splendour also had a cost. Historians point out that the land revenue demand rose in this period and that the burden of long military campaigns and buildings finally fell on the peasants.

## The war of succession and imprisonment

In September 1657 Shah Jahan fell seriously ill. He regarded his eldest son **Dara Shikoh** as his successor, but the other three sons, Shuja, Aurangzeb and Murad, raised armies for their own claims. In 1658 Aurangzeb won the battles of Dharmat and Samugarh. In June 1658 he laid siege to Agra Fort and cut off the water supply, and Shah Jahan had to surrender.

## The last years in Agra Fort

Shah Jahan spent about the last eight years of his life under house arrest in Agra Fort. His eldest daughter **Jahanara** went on caring for him. A popular tale says that he would gaze at the Taj Mahal across the Yamuna from the Musamman Burj of the fort; this emotional picture is popular, but no solid contemporary evidence confirms it. He died on **22 January 1666** and was buried beside Mumtaz Mahal in the Taj Mahal.
