## Early Life

Guru Tegh Bahadur was born on **1 April 1621** at **Amritsar**, the youngest son of the sixth Guru **Guru Hargobind**. His childhood name was **Tyag Mal**. According to tradition he got the name 'Tegh Bahadur' — 'master of the sword' — because of the bravery he showed in the battle of Kartarpur (1635). After this he spent a long time at **Bakala** in solitary practice and meditation.

## Becoming the Ninth Guru

In 1664 the eighth Guru, **Guru Har Krishan**, died in Delhi at a very young age. His last words are believed to be 'Baba Bakale', meaning the next Guru is at Bakala. Many people claimed to be the Guru. According to the popular story the merchant **Makhan Shah Labana** recognised the true Guru in Tegh Bahadur. In **August 1664** the congregation accepted him as the ninth Guru.

## Anandpur and Long Journeys

The Guru took land from Rani Champa of Bilaspur and founded a new town at the foot of the Shivaliks, which later came to be called **Anandpur Sahib** (in 1665 CE according to tradition). This same town became the centre of the founding of the Khalsa Panth in 1699.

He travelled through Malwa, Bangar, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Bengal and as far as **Assam**, and set up congregations, wells and langars in many places. During this journey, in **1666**, his son **Gobind Rai** (later Guru Gobind Singh) was born at **Patna**.

## Arrest and Martyrdom

According to Sikh tradition, in 1675 some pandits of Kashmir, troubled by pressure to convert, came to Anandpur and asked the Guru for help. The Guru decided to stand up before Aurangzeb on their behalf. He was arrested near **Ropar** and brought to **Sirhind** and then to **Delhi**.

It is said that he was asked to accept Islam or show a miracle, which he refused. Before his eyes his three companions — **Bhai Mati Das** (sawn in two), **Bhai Sati Das** (wrapped in cotton and burnt) and **Bhai Dayala** (in boiling water) — were put to death. On **11 November 1675** Guru Tegh Bahadur was beheaded at **Chandni Chowk**. (In many calendars and official events his martyrdom day is observed on 24 November.)

**Bhai Jaita** (later Bhai Jiwan Singh) carried the Guru's head secretly to Anandpur, while the body was cremated by **Lakhi Shah Vanjara**.

## Views of Historians

Sikh sources regard this event as a sacrifice made for religious freedom, while some Persian and pro-Mughal accounts link the Guru to anti-state activity. Many modern historians believe that both religious and political reasons must have been involved, and that because of the lack of contemporary sources many details rest on later tradition. Even so, it is an undisputed fact that the Guru was publicly put to death on the state's order and that he refused to give up his faith.

## Legacy

In Delhi **Gurdwara Sis Ganj Sahib** stands at the place of martyrdom and **Gurdwara Rakab Ganj Sahib** at the place of cremation. His hymns are in the Guru Granth Sahib, carrying a message of detachment, fearlessness and the transience of the world. His father's sacrifice deeply influenced Guru Gobind Singh, and 24 years later the Khalsa Panth was founded at Anandpur.
