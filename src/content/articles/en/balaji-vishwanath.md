## From the Konkan to Satara

Balaji Vishwanath Bhat was born around **1662** at **Shrivardhan** on the Konkan coast. His family were the hereditary deshmukhs there, in an area that then fell under the Siddis of Janjira. After a quarrel with the Siddis, they left the Konkan and came to the Desh (the Maratha country above the ghats). In the late 17th century he began working in revenue and administration under the Maratha commander **Dhanaji Jadhav**, and held responsibilities in areas such as Pune and Daulatabad. He became known more for accounts and diplomacy than for the sword.

## The Struggle between Shahu and Tarabai

After Aurangzeb's death in 1707, the Mughals released Sambhaji's son **Shahu** from captivity. On returning to Maharashtra, Shahu faced his aunt **Tarabai**, who was ruling in the name of her son Shivaji II. In this civil war Balaji Vishwanath supported Shahu and played a role in bringing many chiefs over to his side. In 1708 he received the title **"Senakarte"**.

On **16 November 1713**, Shahu appointed him **Peshwa** (prime minister). The office of Peshwa already existed among Shivaji's Ashtapradhan, but in the hands of Balaji Vishwanath and his descendants it gradually became the most powerful office in the Maratha state.

## The Agreement with Kanhoji Angre

A major challenge for Shahu was the powerful naval chief of the western coast, **Kanhoji Angre**, who sided with Tarabai. Balaji Vishwanath chose negotiation over war. In the agreement of about 1713–14, Kanhoji accepted Shahu as Chhatrapati and in return kept his post of **Sarkhel** (admiral) and his hold over the Konkan forts. This strengthened Shahu's position and secured the coast.

## The 1719 Delhi Expedition and the Sanads

At that time in Delhi there was a tug-of-war between the Mughal emperor **Farrukhsiyar** and the powerful **Syed Brothers** (Hussain Ali Khan and Abdullah Khan). **Syed Hussain Ali Khan**, the governor of the Deccan, made an agreement with the Marathas in 1718: the Marathas would provide about 15,000 horsemen, and in return they would get the right to collect **chauth** and **sardeshmukhi** from six Mughal provinces of the Deccan, and the right of **"swarajya"** over Shivaji's old territories.

Farrukhsiyar did not accept this agreement. Then the armies of Hussain Ali and Balaji Vishwanath reached Delhi (February 1719). Around **28 February 1719** the Syed Brothers deposed Farrukhsiyar, and in **March 1719** the sanads were issued to the Marathas in the name of the new emperor **Rafi-ud-Darajat**. During this time Shahu's mother **Yesubai**, who had been living in Mughal captivity, was also freed. Some Maratha soldiers were also killed in the disturbances in the streets of Delhi.

## Death and Legacy

After returning from Delhi, Balaji Vishwanath's health began to fail. He died on **12 April 1720** at **Saswad** near Pune. In the same month Shahu made his young son **Baji Rao I** the Peshwa.

Historians see Balaji Vishwanath as an administrator who organised a Maratha state broken by civil war and, by obtaining **legal recognition** from the Mughal court, laid the base for Maratha expansion. The arrangement of dividing the right to collect chauth and sardeshmukhi among Maratha chiefs increased the state's income, but later this same arrangement also became a cause of the semi-independent power of the big chiefs and of the looseness of the **Maratha confederacy**.
