## Background

Even after the defeat at Panipat in 1761 the Marathas recovered quickly, but domestic quarrels over the office of Peshwa kept growing. In 1773 the young Peshwa **Narayanrao** was murdered and suspicion fell on his uncle **Raghunathrao** (Raghoba). Narayanrao's posthumous son **Madhavrao II** was made Peshwa, and the 'Barbhai' council under **Nana Phadnavis** took over the government. Raghunathrao, who was out of power, sought help from the British of Bombay — the conflict began from here.

## The First War (1775–1782)

In the **Treaty of Surat** of March 1775, Raghunathrao promised Salsette and Bassein in return for military help. The Company government at Calcutta disliked this and in 1776 tried to cancel it through the **Treaty of Purandar**, but the Bombay officials kept on fighting. In January 1779 the British army advancing towards Pune was surrounded at **Wadgaon** and on 16 January 1779 had to make a humiliating treaty.

After this General Goddard captured Ahmedabad and Bassein in Gujarat, and in 1780 Captain Popham took the fort of Gwalior. But neither side could win a decisive victory. Finally, on **17 May 1782**, through the mediation of **Mahadji Shinde**, the **Treaty of Salbai** was made: the British abandoned Raghunathrao and recognised Madhavrao II as Peshwa, while Salsette remained with the British. After this there was peace for about twenty years.

## The Treaty of Bassein and the Second War (1803–1805)

After Nana Phadnavis's death in 1800, discord grew among the Maratha chiefs — Shinde, Holkar and Bhonsle. In October 1802 **Yashwantrao Holkar** defeated the armies of the Peshwa **Baji Rao II** and Shinde near Pune. The Peshwa fled to the British for refuge and on **31 December 1802** signed the **Treaty of Bassein**. In it he accepted the 'Subsidiary Alliance' — giving territory in return for keeping the Company's troops and handing over foreign policy to the Company.

Shinde and Bhonsle regarded this as the end of Maratha independence and war broke out. In September 1803, at **Assaye**, Arthur Wellesley, and at **Delhi** and in November at **Laswari**, General Lake, defeated Shinde's armies. In December 1803 Bhonsle made the Treaty of **Deogaon** and Shinde the Treaty of **Surji-Anjangaon**, by which Cuttack, the Delhi-Agra region and several parts of Gujarat went to the Company. Holkar continued guerrilla fighting in 1804–05 and in December 1805 made the **Treaty of Rajghat**.

## The Third War (1817–1818) and the End of the Peshwa's Rule

The Governor-General **Lord Hastings** began a campaign against the Pindaris, which soon turned into a war with the Marathas. By the **Treaty of Poona** of June 1817 still harsher terms were imposed on the Peshwa. The angered Baji Rao II attacked the British Residency at **Khadki** on **5 November 1817**, but was defeated. In November 1817 at Nagpur (Sitabuldi) and on 21 December at **Mahidpur**, the Holkar army was defeated. The battle of **Koregaon** was fought on **1 January 1818**, and in February 1818 at **Ashti** the Peshwa's general Bapu Gokhale was killed.

On **3 June 1818** Baji Rao II surrendered on the terms offered by John Malcolm. He was given a pension and sent to **Bithur** near Kanpur. The office of Peshwa was abolished and his territory was merged into the Bombay Presidency; **Pratap Singh**, a descendant of Shivaji, was given the small principality of Satara. Some historians date the end of the war to the fall of Asirgarh in April 1819.

## Results and Significance

By the end of the three wars the Maratha Confederacy — which had been the greatest power in India after the Mughals — had broken up. Shinde, Holkar and Bhonsle became princely states under the Company. Historians regard the main causes of the Marathas' defeat as the chiefs' mutual discord, the lack of leadership after Nana Phadnavis, and the Company's organised army and financial resources. After 1818, apart from Punjab and Sindh, almost all of India came under the Company's direct or indirect control.
