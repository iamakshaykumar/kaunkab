## Kittur and Rani Chennamma

Kittur was a small but prosperous kingdom in what is now Belagavi district of Karnataka, whose rulers were called "Desai". Chennamma was born in **1778** at **Kakati** near Belagavi. From childhood she learned horse riding, swordsmanship and archery.

She was married to **Mallasarja**, the king of Kittur. In 1816 Mallasarja died, and in 1824 their son also passed away. To give the kingdom an heir, the Rani adopted a boy named **Shivalingappa**.

## The Succession Dispute

The East India Company refused to recognise this adoption and wanted to take Kittur under its control. **St John Thackeray**, the Collector of Dharwad, tried to take over Kittur's treasury and administration. The Rani appealed to the Governor of Bombay, **Mountstuart Elphinstone**, but it was rejected.

It is worth noting that Lord Dalhousie's famous **Doctrine of Lapse** was formally applied about two decades after this (from 1848), but the idea of seizing a kingdom by not recognising an adopted heir was already visible in Kittur.

## October 1824: The First Battle

In October 1824 Thackeray reached Kittur with an army. In the clash outside the fort, Kittur's soldiers inflicted heavy losses on the Company's army. On **23 October 1824** Thackeray was killed. Two British officers — **Walter Elliot** and **Stevenson** — were taken prisoner.

The Rani released the hostages on the condition that the Company would stop the war. But the Company did not keep its promise and sent a large army.

## The Fall of the Fort and the Rani's Imprisonment

A large army led by Lieutenant-Colonel Deacon besieged Kittur fort. After heavy shelling the fort surrendered on **4 December 1824**. Rani Chennamma was taken prisoner and held in the fort of **Bailhongal**, where she died on **21 February 1829**.

## Sangolli Rayanna's Guerrilla Struggle

One of the Rani's commanders, **Sangolli Rayanna** (born 1798), did not accept defeat. He organised the local people and continued guerrilla attacks on the Company's posts and government offices, with the aim of placing Shivalingappa on the throne of Kittur. In the end he was captured (the year of his arrest is given in sources as 1829 or 1830) and was hanged on **26 January 1831** from a banyan tree near **Nandagad** in Belagavi district. In folk tales, betrayal is said to lie behind his arrest.

## Memory and Significance

Rani Chennamma's struggle took place 33 years before 1857, so she is counted among the earliest women rulers to take up arms against the Company. In the folk songs (lavani) of north Karnataka, the stories of her and Rayanna are still sung. In 1977 a postage stamp was issued on her, in 2007 her statue was installed in the Parliament complex, and in 2024, when 200 years of the Kittur struggle were completed, special events were held in Karnataka.
