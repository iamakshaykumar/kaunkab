## Who the Paikas Were

A large part of the army of the kings of Odisha, especially the Gajapati kings of **Khurda**, consisted of **Paikas**. They were peasant-soldiers who received **rent-free land** (jagir) in return for service in time of war. In peacetime they farmed, and when needed they set out to fight with sword, shield and matchlock gun. They had standing in village society and regarded themselves as servants of the king and Lord Jagannath.

## Company Rule and Growing Discontent

In **1803**, during the Second Anglo-Maratha War, the East India Company took over Odisha. When the king of Khurda, **Mukunda Deva II**, resisted, he was removed from the throne and the Company took over the administration of the state. With this, the Paikas' jagirs were taken away and they became ordinary peasants paying rent.

There were other causes of discontent too:

- Heavy rent and auction of lands under the new revenue system, because of which many old zamindar families were dispossessed.
- The Company's monopoly on **salt**, which hit the livelihood of coastal people.
- The demand to pay taxes **in silver** instead of the local cowrie currency, which increased the burden on peasants.
- Corruption of officials and employees who had come from outside.

## Bakshi Jagabandhu

**Jagabandhu Vidyadhar** had been the **Bakshi** (commander) of the army of the king of Khurda. Around **1814** his ancestral estate, **Fort Rorang**, was also taken from him under the Company's revenue rules and he became almost penniless. The anger of the Paikas and his own humiliation made him the leader of the revolt.

## The Revolt of 1817

In **March 1817**, about four hundred **Kandha** tribals from the neighbouring **Ghumsur** region came to Khurda and joined the Paikas. Under Jagabandhu's leadership the rebels burned the police station and government buildings at **Banapur** and looted the treasury, then advanced towards **Khurda**, from where Company officials fled. A detachment sent in early April was pushed back and one of its British officers was killed.

After this the rebels reached **Puri** and for a time took control of the town; the Company's forces had to withdraw towards Cuttack. The rebels spoke of making King Mukunda Deva II the ruler again. But the British sent a large force with cannons and better guns, retook Puri and captured the fleeing king. By **May 1817** the Company's control over the province had returned, although guerrilla fighting in the forests went on for several years.

## Suppression and Legacy

Captured rebels were given sentences of hanging, transportation (Kalapani) or imprisonment. Jagabandhu hid in the forests for years and in **1825**, after negotiations, he surrendered. He was kept under watch in Cuttack, where he died in **1829**. After the revolt the Company also gave some concessions in rent in Khurda.

In Odisha the Paika Rebellion is proudly remembered as the **"Paika Bidroha"**, and many regard it as a major struggle for freedom before 1857. Historians see it as a joint resistance of the local soldier-peasant society, tribals and the old ruling class, rooted in the Company's revenue and salt policies. Historians debate the claim that it should be called the "first war of independence", but not its importance.
