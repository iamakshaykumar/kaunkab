## The Foundation: NPCI and IMPS

The story of digital payments in India begins with the **National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI)**. It was set up in **2008** as a not-for-profit organisation on the initiative of the **Reserve Bank of India** and the **Indian Banks' Association**, to build a shared framework for the country's retail payment system. NPCI launched services such as **RuPay** cards, **IMPS** (Immediate Payment Service, 2010) and the **Aadhaar-enabled Payment System**.

With IMPS money could be sent instantly round the clock, but it needed long details such as the account number and IFSC code. What was needed was a simple arrangement in which anyone could pay a customer of any bank from a mobile phone as easily as sending an SMS.

## The Launch of UPI

This thinking produced the **Unified Payments Interface (UPI)**. On **11 April 2016** in Mumbai, Reserve Bank Governor **Raghuram Rajan** launched its pilot with **21 banks**, and from **August 2016** banks' UPI apps began to be available to the public. The key features of UPI were:

- **Virtual Payment Address (VPA)**: such as *name@bank*, so that money can be received and sent without sharing an account number.
- **One app, many accounts**: the facility to link accounts of different banks in a single app.
- **Instant and round the clock**: account-to-account payments even on holidays.
- **Open framework**: besides banks, private companies could also build UPI apps, which brought competition into the market.

UPI is part of the larger digital framework often called the **'India Stack'**, which includes **Aadhaar** identity, **Jan Dhan** bank accounts and cheap mobile internet. **Nandan Nilekani**, the first chairman of Aadhaar, has been one of the leading advocates of this approach.

## The Surge after Demonetisation

After the **demonetisation** of **8 November 2016**, the severe shortage of cash pushed people towards digital payments. On **30 December 2016** Prime Minister **Narendra Modi** released the UPI-based government app **BHIM** (Bharat Interface for Money) at Talkatora Stadium in New Delhi. After this, apps such as **PhonePe**, **Google Pay** and **Paytm** quickly became popular.

The biggest change came from the **QR codes** displayed in shops. From tea stalls and vegetable carts to grocery shops, people began paying within seconds by scanning a code with their phones. From 2020 the government removed the fee charged to merchants on UPI transactions (MDR), which made it even easier for small traders to adopt it. The need for contactless payment during the **COVID-19** pandemic also boosted its use.

## Growth in Numbers

The growth of UPI is clearly visible in the figures:

- In **October 2019**, more than **1 billion** transactions took place in a single month for the first time.
- In **October 2025** there were about **20.7 billion** transactions, worth about **27.28 lakh crore rupees**.
- In **2026** monthly transactions crossed **23–24 billion**.

Today most of India's digital payments are made through UPI, and it is regarded as the largest real-time payment system in the world. Over time new features were added: linking **RuPay credit cards** to UPI (2022), **UPI Lite** for small payments (2022), and **UPI 123PAY** for feature phones without internet.

## Expansion Abroad and Challenges

Through NPCI's international arm, UPI has now reached several countries. In 2023 it was linked with **Singapore's** **PayNow** system, and Indian tourists can also pay with UPI in countries such as the **United Arab Emirates**, **France**, **Bhutan**, **Nepal**, **Sri Lanka** and **Mauritius**. Several countries have sought India's cooperation in building a similar system.

Experts also point to some challenges. A large share of the market is held by two private apps, PhonePe and Google Pay; that is why NPCI proposed a 30 per cent cap for any single app, whose deadline has been extended repeatedly. Because the service is free, there is also an ongoing debate about how banks and app companies will recover their costs. Rising cases of **online fraud** have underlined the need for cyber security and digital literacy.

## Significance

In less than a decade UPI changed the way money changes hands in India. It connected small shopkeepers and ordinary people to the digital economy without card machines and promoted financial inclusion. This model of private companies building services on government and public infrastructure, called 'digital public infrastructure', is today studied around the world.
