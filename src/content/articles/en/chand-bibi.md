## Background: Daughter and Daughter-in-Law of Two Sultanates

**Chand Bibi** was born in about 1550. She was the daughter of **Hussain Nizam Shah I**, the Nizam Shahi sultan of Ahmednagar. Contemporary and later writers describe her as knowing many languages, fond of music and skilled in horse riding, although some of these details seem to have been added later.

In the 1560s, to strengthen the alliance among the Deccan sultanates, she was married to **Ali Adil Shah I**, the sultan of Bijapur. This same alliance was formed against Vijayanagara at the time of the Battle of Talikota in 1565.

## Regent in Bijapur

Ali Adil Shah died in 1580. His successor, **Ibrahim Adil Shah II**, was still a child. For about the next ten years Chand Bibi acted as regent at the Bijapur court.

This was not easy. At court there was fierce rivalry among the Deccani, Habshi (of African origin) and Afaqi (of Iranian-Turkish origin) nobles. Many ambitious chiefs tried to seize power by becoming regent, and at one point Chand Bibi herself was even imprisoned. Even so, she returned and remained influential.

## The Crisis at Ahmednagar

After about 1590 Chand Bibi returned to her father's home, Ahmednagar. In 1595 Sultan **Ibrahim Nizam Shah** was killed in a war against Bijapur. The nobles of Ahmednagar split into several factions over the succession. Chand Bibi recognised the infant **Bahadur Nizam Shah** as sultan and tried to run the government on his behalf.

In the midst of this internal division, one faction asked the Mughal emperor Akbar for help. For Akbar, who had his eye on the Deccan, this was a good opportunity.

## The First Siege of 1595–96

At the end of 1595 a Mughal army led by Prince **Murad** and the commander **Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khanan** besieged the fort of Ahmednagar. Chand Bibi led the defence of the fort.

- The Mughals dug tunnels under the fort wall and blew them up with gunpowder, and a breach opened in the wall.
- According to traditional accounts, Chand Bibi herself went there and, encouraging the defenders, had the breach filled overnight. Such accounts became more embellished in later writing.
- In the Mughal camp too there were differences between Murad and Khan-i-Khanan, and both sides were struggling with a shortage of supplies.

At the beginning of **1596** a treaty was made: Ahmednagar handed over the province of **Berar** to the Mughals and in return the siege was lifted. This defence gave Chand Bibi extraordinary prestige in the Deccan; in tradition she was also called "Chand Sultana".

## The Second Siege of 1600 and a Tragic End

Even after the treaty, the internal strife of Ahmednagar did not stop. The Mughals again increased the pressure and in 1599–1600 besieged the fort once more, under the leadership of Prince **Daniyal**.

This time the circumstances were unfavourable. It is said that Chand Bibi considered a settlement on honourable terms in order to save the lives of the defenders. A rumour of treachery spread against her, and some of her own soldiers murdered her. Some accounts describe it as suicide — the exact details of her death are disputed. In **August 1600** the fort of Ahmednagar passed into Mughal hands.

## Legacy

Even after Chand Bibi, the Nizam Shahi sultanate did not end completely — leaders such as Malik Ambar kept it alive for decades. But in the memory of the Deccan, Chand Bibi is remembered as a woman who faced with courage both the faction-ridden courts and the army of a vast empire.
