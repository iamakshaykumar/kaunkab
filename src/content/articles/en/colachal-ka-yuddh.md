## Background: The Coast of Spices

In the eighteenth century the Kerala coast was a centre of the trade in pepper and other spices. The **Dutch East India Company** had taken Cochin from the Portuguese in 1663 and, to maintain a monopoly on the spice trade of Malabar, had made treaties with small local states.

In the south, **Marthanda Varma**, king of Travancore, after taking power in 1729 strengthened the state by suppressing internal opponents and began conquering the small neighbouring states. His expansion was a direct challenge to the trading interests of the Dutch and to their allied states.

## Towards War

In 1739 the Dutch governor **Gustaaf Willem van Imhoff**, who came from Sri Lanka, met Marthanda Varma and objected to his expansion (especially the takeover of Kottarakkara), but the king did not back down. After this the Travancore-Dutch war broke out. The Dutch blockaded the Travancore coast and called in soldiers from Sri Lanka.

In 1741 the Dutch army landed at the port of **Colachel**, close to **Padmanabhapuram**, the capital of Travancore, built fortifications and seized the surrounding area. Their aim was to reach the capital.

## The Siege of Colachel

Marthanda Varma was away on a campaign in the north at the time. On receiving the news he returned south with his army and, together with his commander **Ramayyan Dalawa**, besieged the Dutch base at Colachel.

- The Dutch could not get the large reinforcements they expected from Sri Lanka, because of a shortage of troops there owing to the conflict going on in Batavia (Java).
- Some sources say that local fishing communities also helped the Travancore side.

Finally, in August 1741 the Dutch army surrendered. Most sources give the date as **10 August 1741**, but because of Dutch records and the old (Julian) reckoning, 31 July or 7 August are also found in some sources. About two dozen European officers were taken prisoner.

## De Lannoy: From Prisoner to Commander

Among the prisoners was the Dutch officer **Eustachius De Lannoy** (1715–1777). Marthanda Varma took him into his service. De Lannoy equipped the Travancore army with European-style drill, guns and artillery, strengthened **Udayagiri Fort**, and planned a defensive line in the north called the 'Nedumkotta'. He was called 'Valiya Kappitan' (chief commander) and died at Udayagiri in 1777.

## Results and Significance

Even after Colachel, sporadic fighting continued for some years, but in the **Treaty of Mavelikkara** of 1753 the Dutch promised not to obstruct Travancore's expansion. After this Marthanda Varma made Travancore the greatest power of Kerala by annexing states such as Kayamkulam and Ambalappuzha.

Historians regard this war as the beginning of the decline of Dutch power in India. At the same time they point out that it was not a huge battle but a limited military clash and siege, whose real importance lay in its political consequences. The victory pillar standing at Kulachal (Colachel) still recalls this event.
