## A Girl from Near Surat

**Usha Mehta** was born on **25 March 1920** in the village of **Saras**, near Surat in Gujarat. From childhood she was deeply influenced by Gandhiji. In **1928**, at only eight years of age, she shouted **"Simon Go Back"** slogans in a protest against the **Simon Commission**. Later her family moved to Bombay.

In **1939** she graduated in philosophy with a first class from Bombay's **Wilson College** and then began studying law. As soon as the Quit India Movement began in **1942**, she left her studies and threw herself fully into the movement.

## A Voice Amid Censorship

On **9 August 1942** the senior Congress leaders were arrested and strict censorship was imposed on newspapers. News of what was happening in different parts of the country could not reach the people. Then 22-year-old Usha Mehta and some of her companions thought that if the government's radio could speak its mind, the movement too should have a radio of its own.

Around **14 August 1942** they began work on setting up a secret radio station. Her companions included **Vithalbhai Jhaveri**, **Chandrakant Jhaveri** and **Babubhai Thakkar**. **Nanik Motwane** of the Chicago Radio company provided the equipment, and the technician **Nariman Printer** assembled the transmitter. The socialist leader **Ram Manohar Lohia** also joined and read the news in English.

## "From Somewhere in India..."

On the evening of **27 August 1942** the first broadcast went out from the **Sea View building** at Chowpatty. Usha Mehta's voice rang out — "This is the Congress Radio calling on **42.34 metres** from somewhere in India."

The Congress Radio carried news of the movement that newspapers could not print — where strikes had taken place, where arrests had been made, where the police had opened fire. Along with this, leaders' speeches and appeals to students, lawyers and even policemen to support the movement were broadcast. Broadcasts were in **English and Hindustani**; at first once a day, then twice, morning and evening.

## Hide-and-Seek with the Police

The police were searching for the station and their vehicles were chasing the team. To avoid being caught, the team kept changing location — the Sea View building at Chowpatty, Ajit Villa on Laburnum Road, Laxmi Bhavan on Sandhurst Road, Parekh Wadi in Girgaum and the Paradise bungalow near the Mahalakshmi temple. For a while there were broadcasts from Nashik too. To reduce the risk, the recording and broadcasting locations were kept separate.

In the end the technician Nariman Printer, who had been arrested earlier, led the police to the hideout. On **12 November 1942**, while a broadcast was going on from the Paradise bungalow, the police broke down the locked doors and arrested Usha Mehta and her companions.

## Trial and Sentence

Usha Mehta was interrogated at length and kept in solitary confinement. The trial in this case, called the **"Radio Conspiracy Case"**, was held in secret in a special court. She refused to give any information. She was sentenced to rigorous imprisonment — most accounts of Usha Mehta give the sentence as **four years**, while some sources say **five years**. She was held in Pune's **Yerawada Jail**. In return for cooperating, Nariman Printer was granted immunity from prosecution. Usha Mehta was released in **March 1946**.

## Later Life and Legacy

After returning from jail Usha Mehta resumed her studies. She earned a **PhD** from the **University of Bombay** on Gandhiji's political and social thought and taught political science there; she retired in **1980**. She remained associated with the **Gandhi Smarak Nidhi** and the **Gandhi Peace Foundation**. She lived simply all her life, kept wearing khadi and never married.

In **1998** the Government of India honoured her with the **Padma Vibhushan**. She died in Mumbai on **11 August 2000** at the age of 80. The Congress Radio ran for only three months, but it showed that even in an age of censorship the voice of truth could be carried to the people. Usha Mehta herself regarded it as the finest moment of her life. The **2024** Hindi film **"Ae Watan Mere Watan"** brought her story to a new generation.
