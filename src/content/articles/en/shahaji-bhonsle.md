## Early life

Shahaji was born on **15 March 1594** to Maloji Bhonsle. Maloji was a chief of the Nizam Shahi of Ahmednagar and held jagirs around Pune and Supe. Shahaji was married to **Jijabai**, daughter of the influential chief Lakhuji Jadhavrao of Sindkhed. They had two sons: the elder **Sambhaji** and the younger **Shivaji**, who was born in 1630 at Shivneri fort.

## With Malik Ambar

At the beginning of the 17th century the Deccan was divided among three powers: Ahmednagar, Bijapur and the Mughals advancing from the north. The real running of Ahmednagar was in the hands of the prime minister **Malik Ambar**, who held back the Mughals through guerrilla warfare using Maratha cavalry. The young Shahaji developed in this army.

In the **Battle of Bhatvadi in 1624**, Malik Ambar defeated the combined Mughal–Bijapur army. Shortly before the battle Shahaji had gone over to the Mughals, but he returned and fought on Ambar's side. This victory showed the military capacity of the Maratha chiefs to all, but later, after differences with Malik Ambar, Shahaji also served Bijapur for some time.

## The last attempt to save the Nizam Shahi

After Malik Ambar's death in 1626, Ahmednagar weakened rapidly. Shahaji also served the Mughals for a while, but at the start of the 1630s he openly changed sides. After Daulatabad passed into Mughal hands in 1633, he declared a boy of the Nizam Shahi line as sultan and, as his guardian, kept fighting the Mughals in northern Maharashtra. But this effort could not stand against Shah Jahan's large armies. After the Mughal–Bijapur treaty of 1636, Shahaji had to surrender, handing over the forts of **Mahuli** and Junnar, and the Nizam Shahi came to an end.

## Service to Bijapur and Bengaluru

Under the terms of the treaty Shahaji was sent south into the Adilshahi service of Bijapur. He took part in the Karnataka campaigns with the Bijapuri commander Randulla Khan, in which, after the defeat of Kempegowda III, he received the jagir of **Bengaluru** from **1638**. From here he ruled almost like an independent ruler. His court became a centre for scholars and poets.

Meanwhile in Maharashtra his **Pune jagir** was looked after by Jijabai and the boy Shivaji, with administrators such as Dadoji Konddev appointed to assist them. This same jagir later became the base of Shivaji's swarajya.

## Imprisonment, mediator and end

In the 1640s Shivaji began taking the forts of the Pune region on his own strength. To put pressure on Shahaji, Bijapur had him imprisoned in **July 1648**; this was done by another Maratha chief, Baji Ghorpade. He was later pardoned and released. Shahaji publicly kept his distance from Shivaji's rebellion, but in later years he also became a mediator between Shivaji and Bijapur.

His elder son Sambhaji was killed during a siege in Karnataka. On **23 January 1664**, Shahaji died near Hodigere in Karnataka in an accident, falling from his horse while hunting. His samadhi at Hodigere still exists today. Shahaji is often remembered only as "Shivaji's father", but he himself played a major role in making the Maratha chiefs a decisive force in the changing politics of the Deccan.
