## One State, Two Paths

When British rule ended in 1947, hundreds of princely states faced a choice — join India or Pakistan. The ruler of the state of **Junagadh** in Kathiawar (today's Saurashtra, Gujarat) was **Nawab Muhammad Mahabat Khanji III**, while about **80 percent of the subjects were Hindu**. The state's entire land border was surrounded by Indian territory; its contact with Pakistan was only by sea, from **Veraval** to **Karachi**. Pilgrimage sites such as Girnar and Somnath were also in this region.

Before independence, Junagadh's ministers kept giving Viceroy Mountbatten the impression that the state would join India. But from May 1947 Muslim League leaders from Sindh had entered the state's executive council, and the post of Dewan was held by **Shah Nawaz Bhutto**. According to sources, Bhutto met **Jinnah** in July, who advised him to hold out at all costs until 15 August.

## The Announcement of Accession to Pakistan

On **15 August 1947** the Nawab announced the accession of Junagadh to Pakistan. About a month later, on **13 September 1947**, Pakistan accepted this accession. The Government of India opposed it. Prime Minister **Nehru** and Home Minister **Sardar Patel** argued that the future of a state should be decided by the will of its people, not merely the wish of the ruler. Patel also feared that Junagadh going to Pakistan could inflame communal tension in Gujarat.

Legally the matter was tangled. Mountbatten's "geographical contiguity" suggestion was only political advice, not a constitutional condition, and many experts held that the ruler's instrument of accession was technically valid. That is why India put the stress on the will of the people. Meanwhile small areas linked to Junagadh such as **Mangrol** and **Babariawad** announced their accession to India, which added to the dispute.

## The Arzi Hukumat and the Blockade

Discontent spread among the people of Junagadh and Kathiawar against the Nawab's decision. On **25 September 1947**, at a meeting at Madhavbagh in Bombay, an **'Arzi Hukumat'** (provisional government) was declared under the leadership of **Samaldas Gandhi**, nephew of Mahatma Gandhi, which later worked from **Rajkot**. Its volunteers took control of about 160 villages of Junagadh from the end of September to the beginning of November.

A **blockade** by neighbouring states and volunteers brought Junagadh's trade with India to a standstill and the food situation began to worsen. Amid the growing crisis the Nawab went with his family to **Karachi** at the end of October 1947, leaving the administration in the hands of Dewan Bhutto. It is worth noting that India later denied at the United Nations that it had supported the Arzi Hukumat or arranged the blockade, while Pakistan and some historians question this role.

## India Takes Over the Administration

With no help coming from Pakistan, Dewan Bhutto asked India's regional commissioner at Rajkot for administrative assistance. On **9 November 1947** the Government of India took over the administration of Junagadh; Bhutto had left for Pakistan a day earlier. Nehru sent a telegram to Pakistan's Prime Minister **Liaquat Ali Khan** saying this was a temporary arrangement and that the final decision should be by plebiscite. Liaquat Ali Khan called it a violation of Pakistani territory. Some sources also record allegations of violence against Muslims in this period and of their migration to Pakistan.

## The Plebiscite of 20 February 1948

The plebiscite was held in Junagadh on **20 February 1948**. Of about **2,01,457** registered voters in the state of Junagadh, **1,90,870** voted, and of these only **91** voted for Pakistan. Counting the votes of the neighbouring attached estates, more than 2,22,000 votes were cast for India and 130 for Pakistan.

There are differences over this plebiscite too. Pakistan opposed it because it was held without its consent and without United Nations supervision. Some scholars also pointed out that participation by Muslim voters was very low. On the other hand, the Indian side regards it as proof of the clear will of the people of the state.

## What Happened Next

On 1 June 1948 three non-official members, including Samaldas Gandhi, were inducted into the administration. By January 1949 Junagadh merged into the **State of Saurashtra**, which became part of Bombay State in 1956 and of **Gujarat** in 1960. Pakistan raised the matter at the United Nations in January 1948, but it was overshadowed by the Kashmir dispute; Pakistan even today shows a claim on Junagadh in its official maps.

The Junagadh episode is counted, along with **Hyderabad** and **Kashmir**, among the three most discussed cases of the integration of princely states. The irony was that in Junagadh India took the "will of the people" as its basis, while in Kashmir the ruler's instrument of accession was in its favour — which is why the debate over both cases remains linked to this day.
