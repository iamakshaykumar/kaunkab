## From Goindwal to the Guru's Seat

Arjan was born on **15 April 1563** at **Goindwal** in Punjab. He was the youngest son of the fourth Guru **Ram Das** and **Bibi Bhani** (daughter of the third Guru Amar Das). Before the death of Guru Ram Das in **1581** he was chosen as successor and became the **fifth Guru** of the Sikhs.

This succession was not acceptable to everyone. His elder brother **Prithi Chand** claimed to be the Guru's real heir and formed a separate faction, whose followers Sikh sources call "Minas". This tug-of-war continued throughout Guru Arjan's tenure.

## Harmandir Sahib and New Towns

In the middle of the sarovar whose digging Guru Ram Das had begun, Guru Arjan had the **Harmandir Sahib** built. According to a popular tradition its foundation was laid by the Sufi saint of Lahore, **Mian Mir**, but many historians regard this as a later tradition. The floor of the temple was kept lower than the surrounding ground and doors were made on all sides. The golden covering seen today was added much later, in the nineteenth century, in the time of Maharaja Ranjit Singh.

The Guru also founded towns such as **Tarn Taran**, **Kartarpur** near Jalandhar and **Hargobindpur**. He organised the **masand** system begun by Guru Ram Das and inspired Sikhs to give a tenth of their income (**dasvandh**) for common works. From this the expenses of langar, the sarovar and the gurdwaras were met.

## The Compilation of the Adi Granth (1604)

Guru Arjan's most lasting work is the compilation of the **Adi Granth**. He collected the hymns of the first four Gurus, added his own compositions and also included the hymns of saints such as Kabir, Namdev, Ravidas and Sheikh Farid. The whole material was arranged in the order of ragas. The work of writing out the manuscript was done by **Bhai Gurdas**.

The book was completed in **August 1604** and installed in the Harmandir Sahib on **1 September 1604**; **Baba Buddha** became its first granthi. Later, in the time of the tenth Guru Gobind Singh, the hymns of Guru Tegh Bahadur were added to it and it came to be called the **Guru Granth Sahib**. Among Guru Arjan's own compositions **Sukhmani Sahib** ("treasure of peace of mind") is the most famous.

## Jahangir, Khusrau and the Arrest

After Akbar's death in 1605, **Jahangir** became emperor. In 1606 his son **Prince Khusrau** rebelled and fled towards Punjab. The charge was made that on the way Guru Arjan met Khusrau and blessed him. In his autobiography Jahangir recorded his displeasure at the Guru's growing influence and ordered him to be punished.

The Guru was taken to **Lahore**, where he died in Mughal custody on **30 May 1606**. The sources differ on how he died — Sikh tradition describes severe tortures (being made to sit on a heated plate, hot sand being poured on him) and then being taken to the Ravi River. Some accounts also give the personal enmity of the local official **Chandu Shah** as a cause, but historians are not unanimous on this. At the place in Lahore where his body is believed to have merged into the Ravi there is today the **Gurdwara Dera Sahib**.

## Legacy

Sikh tradition regards Guru Arjan as the **"Crown of Martyrs"** and the first martyr Guru. His son and successor **Guru Hargobind** thereafter took up **Miri-Piri** — the two swords of spiritual and worldly authority — and began organising the Sikhs for self-defence. Historians consider this martyrdom a major turning point in Sikh history. At the same time, the Adi Granth he compiled remains the centre of the Sikh faith, in which the hymns of saints of varied backgrounds are honoured together.
