## Prisoner of Gujarat, Commander of Delhi

The early life of **Malik Kafur** is almost unknown. In the Gujarat campaign of 1299 the commander **Nusrat Khan** captured him in the port city of **Khambhat**. He accepted Islam and entered the service of Sultan **Alauddin Khalji**. Later writers call him "Hazar-dinari" — it is said that he was bought for a thousand dinars, but many historians regard this number as figurative.

Through his ability Kafur rose quickly. After success against the Mongols in 1306, Alauddin gave him command of the major campaigns in the south.

## The Aim of the Campaigns

Alauddin's southern policy was different from his northern one. Regions such as Gujarat and Malwa were merged into the Sultanate, but from the kingdoms of the far south he mainly wanted **wealth, elephants and annual tribute**. Defeated kings were generally allowed to remain on the throne, provided they accepted Delhi's overlordship. This plunder helped to pay for Delhi's huge standing army and defence against the Mongols.

## Devagiri (1308) and Warangal (1310)

The Yadava king of Devagiri, **Ramachandra**, had not sent tribute for several years after 1296. In 1307–08 Kafur's army defeated him easily and took him to Delhi. Alauddin honoured him and sent him back as a subordinate ruler. In the later campaigns Devagiri became the southern base of the Delhi army.

At the end of **1309** Kafur advanced towards the Kakatiya kingdom. In **January 1310** his army reached Warangal and, after a siege of about a month, broke through the outer fortifications. King **Prataparudra** made a treaty, handed over a huge treasure and elephants and horses, and promised annual tribute. In June 1310 Kafur returned to Delhi with this wealth.

## Dwarasamudra and Madurai (1311)

At the end of 1310 Kafur set out once more to the south. On **25 February 1311** he besieged the Hoysala capital **Dwarasamudra** (today's Halebidu, Karnataka). King **Veera Ballala III** made a settlement instead of fighting, handed over a large sum and agreed to pay tribute.

After this Kafur entered the Pandya kingdom. There, after the death of King Kulasekhara, a war of succession was going on between his sons **Sundara Pandya** and **Vira Pandya**, so resistance was scattered. The Pandya kings kept avoiding direct battle, so this campaign was mainly limited to raids on temple towns and treasuries. In **April 1311** Kafur's army entered **Madurai**. The sources also mention the plundering and damaging of several temples during these raids.

Kafur returned to Delhi on **18 October 1311**. Khusrau and Barani describe thousands of elephants and horses and immense gold as plunder; these numbers may be court exaggeration, but it is clear that Delhi had reached so far south for the first time.

## End and Impact

From 1313 to 1315 Kafur was governor of Devagiri. When Alauddin fell ill he was called to Delhi and became the most powerful person as **Naib** (deputy ruler). After the Sultan's death in January 1316 he ruled by placing the young prince **Shihabuddin Umar** on the throne, but about a month later, in February 1316, the Sultan's old bodyguards murdered him.

The historian Barani is very hostile to Kafur, so his personal accusations are read with caution. Kafur's campaigns weakened the old dynasties of the south. In the next two decades, in the time of **Muhammad bin Tughluq**, Delhi tried to conquer these regions directly, and from that same turmoil new powers such as **Vijayanagara** and the **Bahmani** Sultanate later emerged.
