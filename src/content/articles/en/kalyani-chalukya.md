## The Chalukyas in Place of the Rashtrakutas

The power of the older Chalukyas of Badami (of whom Pulakeshin II was the most famous) had been taken by the Rashtrakutas in the eighth century. About two hundred years later, in **973 CE**, **Tailapa II**, a feudatory of the Rashtrakutas — when the Paramara attack on Manyakheta had weakened them — displaced the Rashtrakuta king **Karka II** and re-established Chalukya power. This dynasty, which described itself as descended from the older Chalukyas, is called by historians the **Western Chalukyas** or the **Chalukyas of Kalyani**.

At first the capital was **Manyakheta**. In the time of **Someshvara I** (reigned c. 1042–1068) the capital was moved to **Kalyani** — today's **Basavakalyan**, in Bidar district of Karnataka.

## The Long Struggle with the Cholas

The dynasty's greatest rival was the **Chola empire** of the south. Wars between the two went on for more than a century, especially for control of the rich **Vengi** region on the Andhra coast. These wars exhausted both empires. According to tradition, in **1068** Someshvara I, suffering from an incurable disease, ended his life by drowning himself in the **Tungabhadra** river.

## Vikramaditya VI: The Time of the Peak

**Vikramaditya VI**, son of Someshvara I, with the support of the feudatories removed his elder brother Someshvara II and became king in **1076**. From that year he began the **Chalukya-Vikrama Era**. During his rule of about **50 years (1076–1126)** the kingdom extended from the Narmada in the north to around the Kaveri in the south.

He made peace for a time by marrying a Chola princess (a daughter of Virarajendra Chola), but later fought the Cholas again and around 1118 took Vengi. Powerful feudatories such as the Hoysala king **Vishnuvardhana** were also forced to accept subordination by his generals.

## Bilhana and Vijnaneshvara

The court of Vikramaditya VI was a major centre of learning.
- **Bilhana** — this Sanskrit poet, who came from Kashmir, wrote the **Vikramankadevacharita**, an epic based on the king's life. It is a work of praise, so historians read its statements by checking them against inscriptions.
- **Vijnaneshvara** — he wrote the commentary called **Mitakshara** on the **Yajnavalkya Smriti**. Its rules on inheritance and joint-family property were accepted for centuries over a large part of India; in Bengal the **Dayabhaga** tradition prevailed in its place. The influence of the 'Mitakshara' is seen under British rule and in the Hindu laws of independent India as well. In praise of his king, Vijnaneshvara wrote that a king like Vikramarka had neither been seen nor heard of.

Kannada literature also flourished — the poet **Ranna** wrote the *Gadayuddha* in 982. Later **Someshvara III** composed the encyclopaedia-like work *Manasollasa* in 1129.

## Temple Architecture

In the time of the Kalyani Chalukyas a distinctive style of temples developed, regarded as the ancestor of the later Hoysala temples — pillars with fine carving, stepped towers and ornate doorways. The Kashi Vishweshwara Temple at **Lakkundi** and the **Mahadeva Temple** at **Itagi** are famous examples.

## Decline

After Vikramaditya VI the feudatories grew more independent. In **1149** Tailapa III was defeated by the Kakatiyas, and around **1157** the **Kalachuri** feudatory **Bijjala** seized Kalyani — this is the period when **Basavanna**'s Virashaiva movement emerged at Bijjala's court. The last Chalukya king, **Someshvara IV**, recovered Kalyani for a time, but around **1189** the kingdom was divided among the **Hoysala**, the **Yadavas of Devagiri (Seuna)** and the **Kakatiya** powers.
