## From Erode to the Congress

**Erode Venkatappa Ramasamy** was born on **17 September 1879** at **Erode** in the Madras Presidency, in a well-off, Kannada-speaking Balija trading family. Later people began to call him with respect **"Periyar"** — that is, "the elder" or "the respected one".

In **1919** he joined the **Congress** and was active in the work of non-cooperation, khadi and the anti-liquor campaign. In **1924** he took part in the **Vaikom Satyagraha** in Kerala, was arrested, and later also criticised the settlement reached through Gandhi's intervention. But his discontent within the Congress kept growing.

## Break with the Congress (1925)

Two events are considered special. The first was the complaint that at the **Cheranmahadevi Gurukulam**, run with Congress money, Brahmin and non-Brahmin students were served food separately. Periyar publicly opposed this; the gurukulam's head **V. V. S. Aiyar** said that except for two Brahmin boys everyone ate together. The second was the proposal of **communal representation** (reservation) for non-Brahmins in government jobs and education, which he kept raising repeatedly. At the **Kanchipuram** conference of **1925** this proposal was not allowed to be moved, and Periyar left the Congress. His conclusion was that the Congress of that time was greatly influenced by Brahmin interests.

## The Self-Respect Movement

From **1925** propagating the movement became Periyar's full-time work. According to some sources the movement was formally started by **S. Ramanathan**, who invited Periyar to lead it; but Periyar remained its face and driving force. To spread the ideas he started the Tamil weekly **'Kudi Arasu'** in **1925** and the English paper **'Revolt'** in **1928**.

The central idea of the movement was that every person has a right to **self-respect**, and no one is high or low by birth. Its main goals were:

- the end of caste discrimination and untouchability, and opposition to the social dominance of the Brahmin priestly class;
- **rationalism** — questioning superstition and ritual;
- **women's rights** — divorce, remarriage, a share in property, contraception, and opposition to the **devadasi system**;
- simple **self-respect marriages** without a priest, in which inter-caste and widow marriages were promoted.

On **17–18 February 1929** the first provincial conference of the movement was held at **Chengalpattu** under the presidency of **W. P. A. Soundarapandian**. Resolutions such as equal inheritance for women, a ban on child marriage and giving up caste surnames were passed; Periyar himself dropped "Naicker" from his name.

## Politics: From the Justice Party to the Dravidar Kazhagam

In **1929–30** Periyar went to Malaya, and from **December 1931** to **November 1932** he toured Europe, including the Soviet Union. In **1937** Rajaji's government made Hindi compulsory in schools, and in **1938** Periyar launched an agitation against it and went to jail. In **1939** he became president of the **Justice Party**, and in **1944** it was renamed the **Dravidar Kazhagam**. In **1949** **C. N. Annadurai** broke away from him and formed the **DMK**. Today the main Dravidian parties of Tamil Nadu have come out of this tradition.

## Assessment: Achievements and Criticisms

Periyar's supporters regard him as a pioneer of social justice, reservation, women's rights and rationalism; his deep mark is on Tamil Nadu's reservation policy and social reforms.

Critics draw attention to several things. His **anti-Brahmin language** was often very sharp and offensive — Prime Minister **Nehru** wrote a stern letter to Chief Minister Kamaraj about some of his speeches in **1957**. In the same year he was sentenced to six months for a call to burn the Constitution. Programmes in the 1950s such as breaking Ganesha idols and burning pictures of Rama hurt the feelings of many religious people. Some scholars also ask whether the movement's benefits went more to the intermediate castes than to Dalits. Periyar himself used to say that his opposition was not to individuals but to the Brahminical system.

He died at **Vellore** on **24 December 1973** at the age of 94. There is still agreement and disagreement over his ideas, but his role in the social history of South India cannot be ignored.
