## Who the Mongols Were

In the early thirteenth century, under **Genghis Khan**, the tribes of Mongolia built the largest land empire in history. Their armies of swift mounted archers trampled kingdoms from China to Iran. After Genghis the empire split into several khanates; those nearest to India were the **Chagatai Khanate** of Central Asia and the **Ilkhanate** of Iran. Most of the attacks on India were made by the Chagatais and chiefs linked to them.

## 1221: On the Banks of the Indus

The Mongols destroyed the **Khwarazm** empire of Central Asia. Its prince **Jalaluddin Mangburni** fled as far as the Indus River, where in **1221**, in the **Battle of the Indus**, Genghis Khan defeated him. According to tradition, Jalaluddin escaped by leaping into the river with his horse.

Jalaluddin asked the Sultan of Delhi, **Iltutmish**, for refuge or an alliance. Iltutmish politely refused, so that the Mongols' anger would not turn towards Delhi. Genghis's main army did not come deep into India, though a detachment of his raided around Multan.

## Constant Pressure on the Frontier

After this the Punjab and Sindh became a zone of conflict between the Mongols and Delhi. In **December 1241** the Mongols besieged and plundered **Lahore** and carried out a great massacre. In the 1250s the Mongol commander **Sali** attacked Multan and Lahore. Kashmir too was for some time under the Mongols.

**Balban** (Sultan 1266–1287) adopted a policy of **defence** instead of big campaigns. He had the frontier forts repaired, stationed troops in them, and posted his eldest son **Prince Muhammad** on the Multan-Lahore front. Afraid of leaving the capital unprotected, he himself rarely went on distant campaigns. In **1285** Prince Muhammad was killed fighting the Mongols — a deep blow for Balban.

In **1292** Jalaluddin Khalji defeated the Mongols led by **Abdullah** who had entered the Punjab; many Mongols, including **Ulghu**, the leader of his advance party, were taken prisoner and later settled near Delhi.

## Alauddin's Time: The Threat Reaches Delhi

Under **Alauddin Khalji** (1296–1316) the Mongol attacks were most serious:
- **6 February 1298** — At Jaran-Manjur (near Jalandhar) the Sultanate's army defeated the Mongols who had entered the Punjab.
- **1298–99** — The Mongols captured the fort of **Siwistan** in Sindh, which **Zafar Khan** recovered.
- **At the end of 1299** — **Qutlugh Khwaja** came with a huge army as far as **Kili**, near Delhi. The Mongols withdrew, but in this battle Zafar Khan was killed.
- **1303** — Under **Targhi** the Mongols besieged Delhi and plundered the surrounding areas. Alauddin held firm and defended from his camp at **Siri**; after about two months the Mongols went back.
- **December 1305** — Near **Amroha** the army of **Malik Nayak** defeated the Mongols badly.
- **1306** — Near the **Ravi** river, the Sultanate's army led by **Malik Kafur** decisively defeated the Mongols led by Kopek.

## The Cost of Defence and the Result

After the shock of 1303 Alauddin made Siri a fortified city, had the old frontier forts repaired, and raised a large **standing army**. According to the contemporary historian **Ziauddin Barani**, it was to maintain this army on low pay that Alauddin carried out the famous **market reforms** fixing the prices of grain and goods. On the frontier, commanders such as **Ghazi Malik** (later Sultan Ghiyasuddin Tughluq) also fought many battles against the Mongols.

After the death of the Chagatai khan **Duwa** in 1307, the Mongol khanates became tangled in internal quarrels and major attacks on India almost stopped (a big attack by Tarmashirin in 1327 was an exception). Historians believe that the Mongol threat made the Delhi Sultanate more centralised and military-dominated, and in this period scholars, craftsmen and soldiers who fled from Central Asia came and settled in Delhi, which also enriched the culture of the Sultanate.
