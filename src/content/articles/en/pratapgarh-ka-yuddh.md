## Background: Bijapur versus Shivaji

In the 1650s **Shivaji** had taken one fort after another around Pune and in the Konkan. In 1656 he conquered the territory of the More chiefs of Javali and there, on a hill surrounded by dense forest, built the **Pratapgarh** fort. All these areas were formally under the **Adilshahi sultanate of Bijapur**.

To check Shivaji's growing power, Sultan **Ali Adil Shah II** (and the queen mother Badi Sahiba) sent the experienced commander **Afzal Khan** with about ten thousand horsemen and a large army. He set out from Bijapur in the summer of 1659.

## Afzal Khan's Campaign

According to Maratha sources, during the campaign the temples of Tuljapur and Pandharpur were damaged in order to provoke Shivaji into coming out into the open field. Sources differ on this point. Instead of fighting in the open, Shivaji chose to stay in the inaccessible hilly and forested terrain of Javali, where the advantage of a large army and artillery would be reduced.

After talks between the envoys of both sides, it was decided that Shivaji and Afzal Khan would meet in a tent below Pratapgarh, each with only a few bodyguards, while the rest of the soldiers would stay far away.

## 10 November 1659: The Meeting

Neither side trusted the other. Shivaji wore an iron armour under his clothes and an iron cap on his head, and kept a **waghnakh** (a weapon like a tiger's claws) and a dagger with him.

- According to Maratha tradition and most later accounts, while embracing, Afzal Khan gripped Shivaji's neck and struck with a dagger, which the armour stopped.
- In reply Shivaji wounded Afzal Khan with the waghnakh and the dagger, and within a short time he was killed.
- Some Adilshahi (Persian) accounts, on the contrary, accuse Shivaji of treachery. Who struck first is still a matter of debate among historians.

In folk tradition the name of Shivaji's bodyguard **Jiva Mahala** is very famous; it is said that he saved Shivaji's life by blocking the blow of Afzal Khan's companion Sayyid Banda.

## The Fight in the Forest of Javali

As soon as Afzal Khan was killed, the Maratha detachments already posted attacked the Adilshahi army spread out in the forest on receiving the signal. In the narrow paths and dense forest the large army scattered. The Marathas got a large number of horses, elephants, camels, cannon and treasure. Sources differ considerably on the number of casualties.

## Result and Significance

After the victory Shivaji advanced rapidly to the south. On **28 November 1659** the Panhala fort came into Maratha hands, and in December the Bijapuri army led by Rustam-i-Zaman was defeated near Kolhapur. The cavalry of **Netaji Palkar** made raids far and wide.

Pratapgarh showed that, with the help of hilly terrain, forts and guerrilla tactics, even a small army can face a large sultanate. This event spread Shivaji's fame throughout the Deccan and strengthened the foundation of swarajya. After the battle Shivaji arranged for Afzal Khan to be buried with honour, and honoured the companions who had shown bravery on this occasion — such as Kanhoji Jedhe.
