## The Chieftain Who Escaped from Panipat

Mahadji Shinde was born in **1730** (many sources say at **Ujjain**). He was the youngest son of **Ranoji Shinde**, a famous Maratha chieftain of Malwa. The Shinde house (spelled "Scindia" in English sources) had become a main pillar of Maratha expansion in the north in the time of Peshwa Bajirao I.

On **14 January 1761**, in the **Third Battle of Panipat**, the Marathas suffered a heavy defeat at the hands of Ahmad Shah Abdali. Many members of the Shinde family were killed. Mahadji was badly wounded but escaped alive. Maratha power in North India had been shattered, and the work of raising it again was later done mainly by Mahadji.

## Return to Delhi (1771–72)

After succession disputes, in **1768** Mahadji became head of the Shinde house. In the time of Peshwa **Madhavrao I** the Maratha armies advanced north again. In **1771** the Marathas took control of Delhi, and Mahadji brought the Mughal emperor **Shah Alam II**, who was living under British protection at Allahabad, to Delhi; the emperor reached the capital in **early 1772**. Thus, only eleven years after Panipat, Maratha influence returned to the Delhi court.

## Clash with the British and Salbai (1782)

In the **First Anglo-Maratha War** (1775–1782) Mahadji's role was decisive. In **January 1779**, near **Wadgaon**, the Maratha army surrounded the British detachment that had come from Bombay and forced it into a humiliating agreement. Later the British captured the fort of Gwalior in 1780, and the war reached no decision.

Finally, on **17 May 1782**, the **Treaty of Salbai** was made through Mahadji's mediation. The British gave up Raghunathrao's support and recognised Madhavrao II as Peshwa. After this there was peace between the Marathas and the Company for about twenty years, and Mahadji got the opportunity to increase his strength in the north.

## Protector of the Mughal Court

In **1784** Shah Alam made the Peshwa **Wakil-i-Mutlaq** (supreme representative of the empire), and Mahadji, as his **deputy (naib)**, became the real administrator of Delhi. But this road was not easy. In **1787** at **Lalsot** he had to retreat before the armies of Jaipur and Jodhpur.

In **1788** the Rohilla chieftain **Ghulam Qadir** seized Delhi, blinded the aged emperor Shah Alam and inflicted terrible cruelty on the royal family. In **October 1788** Mahadji took Delhi back, seated Shah Alam on the throne again, and captured Ghulam Qadir and had him put to death.

## The New Army and the Final Years

With the help of the French commander **Benoit de Boigne** Mahadji raised infantry battalions and artillery trained in the European style. With this army he defeated the Rajput armies in the battles of **Patan** and **Merta** in **1790**, and defeated Holkar's army at **Lakheri** in **1793**.

In his last years he returned to Pune, where his rivalry with **Nana Phadnavis** in the court was much discussed. He died on **12 February 1794** at **Wanawadi** near Pune (13 February in some sources). **Daulatrao Shinde** became his successor.

## Assessment

Historians regard Mahadji as the most successful leader to revive Maratha power in North India after Panipat. His modern army made the Shinde house the most powerful in the Maratha confederacy. But critics also say that this army was controlled by foreign officers, and after him, in the time of Daulatrao, the same army could not stand against the British in 1803. Later the Shinde house became the rulers of the princely state of Gwalior.
