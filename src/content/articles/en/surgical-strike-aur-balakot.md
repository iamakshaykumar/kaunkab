## The Uri Attack

On the morning of **18 September 2016**, four armed militants attacked the Indian Army's brigade headquarters at **Uri**, near the Line of Control in Jammu and Kashmir. Fire in the soldiers' tents and gunfire killed **19 Indian soldiers**; all four attackers were also killed. It was one of the deadliest attacks on the army in Kashmir in two decades. India held the Pakistan-based organisation **Jaish-e-Mohammed** responsible and said the attackers had come from across the border. Pakistan denied any role.

## The 2016 Surgical Strike

On **29 September 2016** the army's Director General of Military Operations (DGMO), **Lieutenant General Ranbir Singh**, announced at a press conference that the previous night the Indian Army had carried out **"surgical strikes"** on militant **"launch pads"** across the Line of Control. According to reports, units of the **Para (Special Forces)** took part; they crossed the Line of Control after midnight, went a few kilometres in and returned.

According to the Indian government, several hideouts were destroyed and a large number of militants were killed in the operation; in the Indian media the number was put at 35 to 40. Pakistan denied any "surgical strike" and said there had only been firing on the border, in which two of its soldiers were killed. Some independent analysts questioned the casualty claims and said that limited cross-border operations of this kind had taken place before too; but this was the first time the Indian government publicly announced such an operation. For months afterwards, firing from both sides remained high along the Line of Control.

## The Pulwama Attack

On **14 February 2019**, at about a quarter past three in the afternoon, a suicide attack was carried out on a convoy of the **Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF)** near **Lethpora** in **Pulwama** district on the Jammu–Srinagar highway. This convoy of 78 vehicles carried more than 2,500 personnel. A vehicle packed with explosives was rammed into a bus carrying personnel, killing **40 personnel**. The attacker, **Adil Ahmad Dar**, was a 22-year-old youth from Pulwama district itself. **Jaish-e-Mohammed** claimed responsibility for the attack.

India blamed Pakistan, withdrew its **"Most Favoured Nation" (MFN)** status and raised customs duty on Pakistani goods to **200%**. Pakistan condemned the attack but denied any role of its own.

## The Balakot Air Strike

At dawn on **26 February 2019**, Indian Air Force **Mirage 2000** aircraft struck near **Balakot** in Pakistan's **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa** province. Foreign Secretary **Vijay Gokhale** stated that, on the basis of intelligence, a large Jaish-e-Mohammed training camp had been targeted where more suicide attacks were being prepared. He called it a **"non-military pre-emptive action"** and said that **"a very large number"** of militants had been killed; various unconfirmed figures appeared in the Indian media.

Pakistan said the Indian aircraft had hastily dropped their bombs on an empty wooded area, causing no damage apart from some trees. Analyses of satellite images by some international institutions and news agencies did not show clear damage to the targeted buildings, while the Indian government stood by its claim. Thus the results of the strike remain a subject of dispute even today.

## The Aerial Clash and De-escalation

The next day, **27 February 2019**, Pakistan Air Force aircraft struck near Indian military positions close to the Line of Control in Jammu and Kashmir. In the aerial clash with Indian aircraft, an Indian **MiG-21 Bison** went down and its pilot, **Wing Commander Abhinandan Varthaman**, landed in Pakistani territory and was captured. India claimed that before going down Abhinandan had shot down a Pakistani **F-16**; Pakistan denied this, and some American reports also cast doubt on the claim. The same day an Indian helicopter crashed at Budgam because of an error by India's own air defence system, killing six air warriors and one civilian.

As tension rose between two nuclear-armed countries, the United States, China and several other countries appealed for restraint. Pakistan's Prime Minister **Imran Khan** announced in parliament that Abhinandan would be released as a "gesture of peace", and on **1 March 2019** he returned to India through the Wagah border. He was later awarded the **Vir Chakra**.

## Significance

These events are seen as a shift in India's security policy. According to the government and its supporters, India sent the message that cross-border terrorist attacks would be answered militarily, even if this meant crossing the Line of Control or the international border. Critics and many analysts raised questions about the lack of independent confirmation of casualty claims, intelligence failures and the use of these events in electoral politics. Pakistan, too, described its own response on both occasions as successful. Scholars hold that Balakot highlighted both the possibilities and the dangers of limited military action under nuclear deterrence in South Asia.
