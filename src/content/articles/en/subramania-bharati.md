## The Boy Poet of Ettayapuram

**Subramania Bharati** was born on **11 December 1882** at **Ettayapuram** in what is now Thoothukudi district of Tamil Nadu. As a child his name was **Subramanian**. He began composing poetry at a very young age. At about the age of **11**, the scholars of the Ettayapuram court, seeing his poetic gift, gave him the title **"Bharati"** — one blessed by Saraswati, the goddess of learning. This title later became his name.

In **1897** he married **Chellamma**. After his father's death he lived for some years in **Varanasi**. There he learned Sanskrit, Hindi and English, studied Indian spirituality and became familiar with the political ferment of the country. This stay in Kashi widened his thinking beyond the Tamil region to the whole of India.

## Journalist and Preacher of Swadeshi

On returning to Tamil Nadu, Bharati was a teacher in Madurai for a short while and then turned to journalism. In **1904** he became assistant editor of the Madras Tamil daily **'Swadesamitran'**. When the swadeshi movement gathered pace after the Partition of Bengal, he joined the weekly **'India'** and through it sharply criticised British rule. He is believed to have been an early user of political cartoons in Tamil journalism. He was also associated with a journal named **'Chakravarthini'**.

Around 1905 he met **Sister Nivedita**, the disciple of Swami Vivekananda, whom he regarded as his **guru** all his life. It is said that after meeting Nivedita his views on women's freedom and equality grew firmer. In politics Bharati was a supporter of the Congress **Extremists** and of **Bal Gangadhar Tilak**. **V. O. Chidambaram Pillai** of Tuticorin was a close associate; Bharati also appeared in court in the sedition case brought against Chidambaram Pillai in 1908.

## Exile in Pondicherry

In **1908** the government began a harsh crackdown on swadeshi leaders. Action was taken against the paper 'India' and an arrest warrant was issued against Bharati. To escape arrest he went to **French-ruled Pondicherry** (today's Puducherry), where the British police had no direct authority. He lived there for about **ten years**.

This period was one of financial hardship, but also his most productive as a writer. In Pondicherry he restarted publication of 'India', which was banned in British India. It was here that he came into contact with **Aurobindo Ghose** and other revolutionaries, who had also settled in Pondicherry in the same way. Many of his famous works were written in this period:

- **'Panchali Sabatham'** — a poem based on the humiliation and vow of Draupadi in the Mahabharata, in which Draupadi is also read as a symbol of Mother India;
- **'Kannan Pattu'** — songs that see Krishna in many forms such as friend, mother, lover and servant;
- **'Kuyil Pattu'** — a philosophical poem based on the story of a koel (cuckoo).

He also translated the **Bhagavad Gita** and Patanjali's **Yoga Sutra** into Tamil.

## A New India in Poetry

Bharati is regarded as the pioneer of **modern Tamil poetry**. Before him Tamil poetry was usually written in difficult, ornate language that ordinary people could not easily understand. Bharati adopted **simple language close to everyday speech** and rhythms like those of folk songs, so that his poetry could be sung in fields, streets and meetings.

The main themes of his poems were:

- **Patriotism** — he translated 'Vande Mataram' into Tamil and wrote songs on the unity of India, in which the whole country from the Himalayas to Kanyakumari was seen as one;
- **Women's freedom** — he opposed child marriage and dreamed of an educated, fearless "new woman" (puthumai penn);
- **Opposition to caste** — he was a staunch opponent of caste discrimination and untouchability and spoke of the equality of all human beings;
- **Fearlessness** — his song "Achamillai, Achamillai" (No fear, no fear) is still regarded as a symbol of courage.

## Final Years and Legacy

In **1918** Bharati returned to British India but was arrested near **Cuddalore**. He was released after the intervention of **Annie Besant** and **C. P. Ramaswamy Aiyar**. After this he lived in **Triplicane** in Madras and again worked for 'Swadesamitran'. Poverty and illness kept following him.

In 1921 the elephant of the **Parthasarathy temple** in Triplicane, which he used to feed regularly, knocked him down. He did recover from the injury, but his health had become very weak. On **11 September 1921** he died at the age of just **38**.

In his lifetime Bharati did not receive much fame, but after his death his poems became part of the consciousness of Tamil society and he came to be called **"Mahakavi Bharathiyar"**. In **1949** his works were **nationalised** so that they would be accessible to all. There are memorial houses for him in Ettayapuram and Puducherry, his residence in Chennai is called 'Bharathiyar Illam', a postage stamp was issued on him in **1960**, and **Bharathiar University** in Coimbatore is named after him. His message of patriotism, equality and fearlessness makes him the national poet not only of Tamil but of the whole of India.
