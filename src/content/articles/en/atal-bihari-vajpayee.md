## From Gwalior to Politics

**Atal Bihari Vajpayee** was born on **25 December 1924** in **Gwalior** in central India. His father, **Krishna Bihari Vajpayee**, was a teacher who also wrote poetry, so the home had a literary atmosphere. Atal studied at **Victoria College** in Gwalior (today's Laxmibai College) and then took an M.A. in political science from **DAV College, Kanpur**.

As a teenager he joined the **Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh**. In his early years he worked as a journalist for publications such as 'Rashtradharma', 'Panchjanya' and 'Veer Arjun'. When **Syama Prasad Mookerjee** founded the **Bharatiya Jana Sangh** in **1951**, Vajpayee was among its founding members and became a close associate of Mookerjee.

## A Powerful Voice in Parliament

In **1957** Vajpayee entered the Lok Sabha for the first time from the **Balrampur** seat in Uttar Pradesh. His speeches in Parliament drew everyone's attention; it is said that Prime Minister **Jawaharlal Nehru**, introducing him to a foreign guest, remarked that this young man would one day become the country's Prime Minister. He was president of the Jana Sangh from **1968 to 1972**. In his long political life he was elected **ten times to the Lok Sabha** and **twice to the Rajya Sabha**.

During the **Emergency** (1975–77) he was kept in jail. After the Jana Sangh merged into the Janata Party in 1977, he became **External Affairs Minister** in the **Morarji Desai** government. During this time, in **1977**, he gave a speech in **Hindi** at the **UN General Assembly**, which is still remembered. After the Janata Party broke up, the **Bharatiya Janata Party** was formed on **6 April 1980**, and Vajpayee became its **first president**.

## Three Times Prime Minister

In the 1996 Lok Sabha election the BJP emerged as the largest party, and Vajpayee was sworn in as Prime Minister on **16 May 1996**. But as he could not muster a majority, he resigned before the confidence vote; this government lasted only **13 days**.

In **March 1998** he became Prime Minister again as leader of the **National Democratic Alliance (NDA)**. In **May 1998** nuclear tests were carried out at **Pokhran**, and India declared itself a nuclear-weapon state. In **April 1999** the government lost a confidence vote in the Lok Sabha by **one vote**. Meanwhile the **Kargil War** took place, and in the October 1999 election the NDA won a clear majority. Vajpayee became Prime Minister for the third time and remained in office until **May 2004**. He is regarded as the first non-Congress Prime Minister whose government ran almost its full term.

His tenure saw the launch of the **Golden Quadrilateral** highway project linking the four metropolitan cities, the **Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana** to connect villages by road, and the **Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan**. Economic steps such as telecom reforms and the privatisation of some public sector enterprises were also taken. In the same period, Parliament was attacked by terrorists in **December 2001**, and communal riots took place in Gujarat in **2002**, over which his government also faced criticism.

## The Lahore Bus Journey and Efforts for Peace

After the nuclear tests, Vajpayee took a bold step to ease India-Pakistan tension. On **20 February 1999** he rode the inaugural run of the new **Delhi–Lahore bus service**, crossed the **Wagah border** and reached Pakistan, where Prime Minister **Nawaz Sharif** welcomed him. Vajpayee also visited the **Minar-e-Pakistan** and wrote in its visitors' book his wish for Pakistan's stability and prosperity. On **21 February 1999** the two countries signed the **Lahore Declaration**, which promised to settle disputes through dialogue and to take measures to avoid nuclear accidents.

Only a few months later this initiative suffered a severe blow from the intrusion in Kargil, but Vajpayee did not close the path of dialogue completely. In **July 2001** he held a summit at **Agra** with Pakistan's President **Pervez Musharraf**, which ended without any joint declaration. His phrase on the Kashmir question, **"Insaniyat, Jamhuriyat aur Kashmiriyat"** (humanity, democracy and Kashmiri identity), is still often quoted.

## Vajpayee the Poet

Alongside politics, Vajpayee was also a sensitive **poet**. His collection **'Meri Ikyavan Kavitayen'** (My Fifty-One Poems) became very popular. Lines such as **'Geet naya gaata hoon'** (I sing a new song) and **'Haar nahin manoonga, raar nahin thanoonga'** (I will not accept defeat, nor pick a quarrel) reflect his resilience. The pauses, humour and poetic language of his speeches in Parliament and at public meetings make him count among the most effective orators of his time.

## Honours and Legacy

Vajpayee received the **Padma Vibhushan** in **1992** and the Outstanding Parliamentarian award in **1994**. On **27 March 2015** President **Pranab Mukherjee** went to his residence to confer the **Bharat Ratna** on him. Since 2014 his birthday, **25 December**, has been observed as **Good Governance Day**. After a long illness he died at AIIMS, New Delhi, on **16 August 2018**, and his memorial is called **'Sadaiv Atal'** (Forever Atal). As a leader respected even in opposition parties, who ran coalition politics successfully and sought peace with a neighbouring country, Vajpayee is remembered as a distinctive figure of Indian democracy.
