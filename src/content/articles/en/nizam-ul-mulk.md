## A Seasoned Noble of the Mughal Court

**Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan** was born on **11 August 1671** in Agra. His father **Ghazi-ud-din Khan Firuz Jung** and his grandfather were both leading generals of Aurangzeb, and the family had come from Central Asia. In his youth Qamar-ud-din took part in Aurangzeb's Deccan campaigns and received the title 'Chin Qilich Khan'.

In **1713** Emperor **Farrukhsiyar** made him governor of the Deccan with the title **Nizam-ul-Mulk**. But the powerful **Sayyid Brothers** in Delhi soon removed him from there.

## Clash with the Sayyid Brothers

In 1720 Nizam-ul-Mulk advanced from Malwa towards the Deccan and defeated the supporters of the Sayyid Brothers in the battles of **Ratanpur** and **Balapur**. In the same year the Sayyid Brothers fell, and Emperor **Muhammad Shah** made him the **wazir** of the empire in 1722.

But the court at Delhi was sunk in luxury and factionalism. The Nizam's proposals for reform did not get support, and courtiers kept poisoning the emperor's ear against him. At the end of 1723 he left the wazirate and turned back towards the Deccan.

## The Battle of Shakar Kheda (1724)

To stop the Nizam, the emperor set the acting governor of the Deccan, **Mubariz Khan**, against him. On **11 October 1724** the two armies clashed in the field of **Shakar Kheda** in Berar. Mubariz Khan was killed and Nizam-ul-Mulk became the undisputed master of the Deccan. Muhammad Shah was left with no option but to recognise him as governor of the Deccan and later to confer on him the title **Asaf Jah**. (The date of the conferral of the Asaf Jah title differs among sources.)

Historians regard this day as the beginning of the **Asaf Jahi** dynasty. Even so, the Nizam never declared himself an independent emperor — coins and the khutba continued to be in the name of the Mughal emperor. His main centre remained **Aurangabad**; Hyderabad became the capital later, in the time of his son Nizam Ali Khan.

## Struggle with the Marathas

The Nizam's biggest rival in the Deccan was the Maratha state. The Nizam tried to exploit the Marathas' internal split — between Shahu and Sambhaji II of Kolhapur — but in **1728**, in the campaign of **Palkhed**, the swift cavalry of Peshwa **Baji Rao I** surrounded him. The Nizam had to accept Shahu as Chhatrapati and recognise the Maratha rights of chauth and sardeshmukhi in the Deccan. In 1737–38 Baji Rao again surrounded the Nizam near **Bhopal**, and in the treaty of January 1738 he had to accept Maratha authority over Malwa.

At the time of **Nadir Shah's** invasion in 1739 the Nizam was in Delhi. In the Battle of Karnal his army did not take an active part, and later his talks with Nadir Shah to stop the massacre of Delhi are mentioned.

## Death and Legacy

On **1 June 1748**, at **Burhanpur**, Nizam-ul-Mulk died at the age of about 76 and was buried at **Khuldabad**. Immediately after his death a war of succession broke out between his son **Nasir Jung** and his grandson **Muzaffar Jung**, in which the British and French companies also jumped in — this became a major cause of the later **Carnatic Wars**.

The state founded by Nizam-ul-Mulk lasted about two hundred and twenty-five years. In 1798 his descendants made the Subsidiary Alliance with the British, and in 1948, after **Operation Polo**, Hyderabad merged into India.
