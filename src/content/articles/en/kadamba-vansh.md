## The Dynasty of Banavasi

In the middle of the fourth century CE, the **Pallavas of Kanchi** dominated the politics of South India. At this time a new dynasty emerged in western Karnataka — the **Kadambas**. Its capital was **Banavasi** (in today's Uttara Kannada district, on the banks of the Varada river). The Kadambas ruled over large parts of northern Karnataka and the Konkan from about **345 to 540 CE**.

The Kadambas are regarded as the first local dynasty of Karnataka to adopt the **Kannada** language at the administrative level. That is why they hold a special place in the history of the Kannada language and of Karnataka.

## Mayurasharma: From Brahmin to King

The story of **Mayurasharma**, the founder of the Kadamba dynasty, is found in the fifth-century **Talagunda pillar inscription**. According to it, Mayurasharma was born in a Vedic Brahmin family of Talagunda. With his teacher and grandfather **Virasharma** he went to the Pallava capital **Kanchipuram** to study the Vedas. There he was insulted in a quarrel with a Pallava horseman (or guard).

According to the inscription, enraged by this insult, Mayurasharma gave up the life of a Brahmin and **took up the sword**. He built up his strength in the forests around Srisailam and attacked the border regions of the Pallavas. Finally, around **345 CE**, he established his own independent kingdom. This story comes from a prashasti that his descendants had written about a hundred years later — so some parts of it are considered embellished.

## Kakusthavarman: The Peak of Glory

The Kadamba kingdom reached its peak under **Kakusthavarman**. The Talagunda inscription calls him "the ornament of the family". The Kadambas formed marriage alliances with the great dynasties of the time — the **Guptas** of the north and the **Vakatakas** of the Deccan — and one of Kakusthavarman's daughters was married into the **Ganga** dynasty. These ties show that the Kadambas had now become a respected dynasty.

## Two Famous Inscriptions

The **Talagunda pillar inscription** is carved on a stone pillar in front of the **Pranaveshwara Temple** in Talagunda village in Shivamogga district. It is in **Sanskrit** verse and was composed during the time of King **Shantivarman** (c. 455–460 CE) by the court poet **Kubja**. It describes the origin of the Kadamba dynasty, the story of Mayurasharma and a tank built by Kakusthavarman. Talagunda is counted among the oldest known **agraharas** (settlements granted to Brahmins) in Karnataka.

The **Halmidi inscription** was found in Halmidi village in Hassan district. It is generally regarded as the **oldest known inscription in the Kannada language**. It mentions the grant of two villages in the time of King **Kakustha**. There is considerable disagreement about its date — different scholars place it at about 450 to 500 CE or even later, and some also question whether its "Kakustha" is a Kadamba king.

## Decline and Legacy

Around **540 CE** the **Chalukyas of Badami**, who had been subordinate to the Kadambas, defeated them and took power in Karnataka, and the Kadambas became feudatories of the Chalukyas. Even so, branches of the Kadambas survived for a long time — the **Kadambas of Goa** and the **Kadambas of Hangal** continued to rule as small kingdoms from about the 10th to the 14th century.

The **Madhukeshwara Temple** at Banavasi was built in the time of the later Kadambas and constructions in the Chalukya style were added to it. The Government of Karnataka holds the **Kadambotsava** every year in memory of the Kadambas. As the first dynasty of Kannada, the Kadambas remain part of Karnataka's identity even today.
