## Background: Amber and the Mughal Alliance

Man Singh was born on **21 December 1550** in the Kachhwaha royal family of Amber. He was the son of **Raja Bhagwant Das** and grandson of **Raja Bharmal**. In 1562 Bharmal had allied with the Mughals by marrying his daughter to Akbar. Through this alliance the Kachhwahas received high posts at the Mughal court, and Akbar gained the trusted support of the Rajputs.

The young Man Singh was in Mughal service from childhood and soon emerged as a capable military officer.

## Haldighati and Mewar

In 1576 Akbar sent Man Singh to negotiate with **Maharana Pratap** of Mewar. When no agreement was reached, the Mughal army under Man Singh faced the Maharana at **Haldighati**. The Mughal side gained the upper hand in the battle, but Maharana Pratap escaped safely and the struggle in Mewar went on for years. In Rajasthani tradition the story of Chetak climbing onto Man Singh's elephant is very famous.

## Campaigns from the North-West to the East

Man Singh worked at both ends of the Mughal empire:

- **Kabul:** In Akbar's Kabul campaign of 1581 he crossed the Indus with the advance force and later served as governor of Kabul. In the 1580s he also suppressed revolts of Afghan tribes.
- **Bihar and Odisha:** As governor of Bihar he brought local rajas under control and then led a campaign in Odisha against the Afghan chiefs.
- **Bengal:** From 1594 he was governor of Bengal and made **Akbarnagar** (today's Rajmahal) the capital. He struggled for a long time with revolts of Bengal's zamindars and the Afghans.

After his father's death in 1589 he became king of Amber. In **1605** he was given a mansab of **7,000**.

## Builder and Devotee

Man Singh was a devotee of Krishna and gave donations for temples at Amber, Vrindavan and Varanasi.

- **Govind Dev Temple, Vrindavan:** This huge temple of red sandstone is believed to have been completed in 1590. An inscription on the temple names Man Singh as its builder. Later the upper parts of the temple were destroyed, but its huge hall still stands today.
- **Amber Fort:** The major construction of this hilltop palace-fort began around 1592 in Man Singh's time and his successors extended it. In 2013 it was included in UNESCO's "Hill Forts of Rajasthan" group.

## Jahangir's Time and the Last Years

In Akbar's last days a faction at court wanted to make Salim's (Jahangir's) son **Khusrau** the successor in place of Salim. Man Singh was Khusrau's maternal uncle and was considered linked with this faction. Even so, after taking the throne Jahangir kept him in service and in 1611 sent him to the Deccan against **Malik Ambar**.

He died on **6 July 1614** at **Elichpur** in the Deccan. His son **Bhau Singh** became king of Amber.

## Legacy and Assessment

Man Singh is among the greatest symbols of Mughal-Rajput cooperation. Mughal historians see him as a great pillar of the empire, while in the tradition of Mewar his image has been critical because of Haldighati. The Amber Fort and temples he had built are still fine examples of the blending of Rajput and Mughal architecture.
