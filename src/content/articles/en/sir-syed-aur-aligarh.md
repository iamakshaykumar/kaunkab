## From Delhi to Service under the Company

Syed Ahmad Khan was born on **17 October 1817** in **Delhi**. His family was connected with the Mughal court. In his youth he took a job in the judicial service of the East India Company and served in various places as a munsif and then a sadr amin (judicial officer). During this period he also wrote on history and religion; in **1847** his book on the monuments of Delhi, **Asar-us-Sanadid**, was published.

## 1857 and 'Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind'

At the time of the Revolt of 1857 he was posted at **Bijnor**, and he is said to have played a role in saving the lives of some English families there. After the revolt the British government held Muslims mainly responsible and viewed them with suspicion. In such an atmosphere, in **1859** Sir Syed wrote **Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind** (The Causes of the Indian Revolt). In it he said plainly that a major cause of the revolt was the government's own policies, and that the biggest mistake was that no Indian had been given a place in the council that made the laws.

## The Scientific Society (1864)

Sir Syed believed that the backwardness of Indian Muslims was due to their distance from modern science and English education. On **9 January 1864** he founded the **Scientific Society** at **Ghazipur** (some sources say 1863), whose aim was to translate books of Western science and history into Urdu. In April 1864 it moved to **Aligarh**. From **1866** the society brought out the **Aligarh Institute Gazette**, which was printed in both English and Urdu. From 1870 his magazine **Tehzeeb-ul-Akhlaq** spread ideas of social and religious reform.

## From the MAO College to Aligarh Muslim University

During his visit to England in **1869–70**, Sir Syed saw the universities there, and on returning he set about fulfilling his dream of a residential educational institution. After a long campaign to raise funds, the **Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental (MAO) School** began at Aligarh on **24 May 1875**. In **January 1877** the Viceroy **Lord Lytton** laid the foundation stone of the college, and it became the **MAO College**. Islamic studies were taught here along with English and modern subjects, and some Hindu students studied here from the very beginning.

Sir Syed died on **27 March 1898** at Aligarh. Through later efforts, the college became **Aligarh Muslim University** (AMU) in **1920**.

## Reformer, Thinker and Controversy

Sir Syed stressed a **rational interpretation of the Quran** and said there should be no conflict between religion and science. For this reason many orthodox ulema strongly opposed him, and fatwas were even issued against him. In 1886 he began the **Muhammadan Educational Conference**, which became a forum for discussing Muslim education across the country.

His views on politics were more controversial. In the 1880s he had described Hindus and Muslims as "the two eyes of a bride", but he advised Muslims to stay away from the **Indian National Congress** formed in **1885**, and in his speeches of 1888 he called the two communities separate "qaums" (nations). Some historians regard this as the background to the later "two-nation" idea, while others say that his main concern was the educational weakness of Muslims and avoiding a clash with the British government.

## Legacy

The Aligarh movement created a modern educated class among Indian Muslims, which later played a major role in journalism, literature, administration and politics. The poet **Altaf Hussain Hali** wrote his biography, **Hayat-e-Javed**. Even today "Sir Syed Day" is celebrated every year on 17 October at Aligarh.
