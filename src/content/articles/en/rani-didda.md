## The Princess of Lohara

**Didda** is believed to have been born around 924 CE. She was the daughter of King **Simharaja** of **Lohara** (a hill kingdom in the present-day Poonch region), and through her mother she is said to have been connected to the powerful **Hindu Shahi** dynasty of Kabul and Punjab. She was married to King **Kshemagupta** of Kashmir (reign about 950–958 CE). This marriage joined the royal houses of Lohara and Kashmir — a link that later proved decisive in the history of Kashmir.

Kshemagupta's rule is considered weak and pleasure-loving. According to Kalhana, the queen's influence at court was so great that people called the king by her name.

## As Regent (958–980)

After Kshemagupta died in 958 CE, his young son **Abhimanyu II** became king and Didda ruled as his **regent**. Seeing power in the hands of a woman, many ministers and feudal lords rose in revolt. Kalhana says that Didda sometimes divided her opponents among themselves with bribery and diplomacy, and sometimes crushed rebellions with harsh punishment — there are mentions of the rebels being put to death along with their families.

Abhimanyu died in 972 CE. After that, three of his sons — **Nandigupta**, **Tribhuvanagupta** and **Bhimagupta** — became kings in name one after another, and each time the real power remained in Didda's hands. The first two grandsons died in quick succession; Kalhana holds Didda responsible for these deaths, but the sources differ in the details of these events.

## Independent Ruler (980–1003)

Around **980 CE** Didda removed her last grandson, Bhimagupta, from the throne as well. According to Kalhana, he was tortured and put to death. After this, Didda gave up the cover of a minor's name and ruled Kashmir as its ruler in her own right.

Her most trusted man in this period was **Tunga**, an ordinary shepherd of the Poonch region, who became Didda's lover and then her **prime minister**. The nobility and the **Damaras** (landholding feudal lords) resented him, but Tunga put down many rebellions for Didda. According to Kalhana, Didda also built many monasteries and temples during her reign.

## Succession and the Lohara Dynasty

Instead of choosing a successor from her husband's line, Didda adopted her brother's son, her nephew **Samgramaraja**, and made him heir to the throne. After Didda's death in 1003 CE, Samgramaraja became king and the rule of the **Lohara dynasty** began in Kashmir, which lasted until the twelfth century. The period of Mahmud of Ghazni's invasions came during the time of this dynasty.

## Kalhana's View and Today's View

The main source of our information about Didda is the sixth book of **Kalhana's Rajatarangini**. Kalhana describes her as scheming, cruel and immoral, but also admits that amid repeated rebellions she kept power in her hands for about forty-five years. The scholar **M. A. Stein**, who translated the Rajatarangini, praised her political understanding and skill in governing.

Modern historians point out that Kalhana wrote about a hundred and fifty years after her death and that his account may be influenced by the male-dominated outlook of that time. Even so, the available evidence makes it clear that Didda was among the few women of medieval India who ruled in their own name.
