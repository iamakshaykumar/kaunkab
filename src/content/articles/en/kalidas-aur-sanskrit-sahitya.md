## Who Was Kalidasa?

Kalidasa is regarded as the most famous poet and dramatist of Sanskrit. Even so, very little is known for certain about his life. He wrote almost nothing about himself in his works, so his period and birthplace still rest on conjecture.

Most scholars place him at **about the 4th–5th century CE** and link him with the time of the Gupta emperor **Chandragupta II** (c. 380–415 CE), who took the title "Vikramaditya". But this too is only one view, and some scholars regard him as earlier or later than this. His deep attachment to **Ujjayini (Ujjain)** is clear in the Meghaduta, so many scholars link him to the Malwa region. Claims for Bengal, Kashmir and Vidarbha are also found.

The oldest inscription to name Kalidasa is the **Aihole inscription (634 CE)** of Karnataka, from the time of the Chalukya king Pulakeshin II. This establishes that he had become famous by the seventh century. Some lines of a Mandasor inscription of about 473 CE seem similar to the Meghaduta, although the poet's name does not appear in it.

## Major Works

Seven works are generally regarded as Kalidasa's:

- **Plays:** *Abhijnanashakuntalam* — the story of King Dushyanta and Shakuntala; *Malavikagnimitram* — the love story of the Shunga king Agnimitra and Malavika; *Vikramorvashiyam* — the story of King Pururavas and the apsara Urvashi.
- **Epics:** *Raghuvamsha* — a description of the kings of the Raghu line, including the ancestors and descendants of Rama; *Kumarasambhava* — the story of the marriage of Shiva and Parvati and the birth of Kumara (Kartikeya).
- **Short and lyric poems:** *Meghaduta* — a Yaksha, separated from his wife by a curse, makes a cloud his messenger to send a message; *Ritusamhara* — a description of the six seasons.

## The Abhijnanashakuntalam

This is regarded as Kalidasa's finest work. The root of the story is in the Mahabharata, but Kalidasa gave it a new dramatic form by adding the **curse of the sage Durvasa** and the **ring**. Because of the curse Dushyanta forgets Shakuntala, and on seeing the ring found by a fisherman he remembers everything. This portrayal of nature, love and separation is regarded as the peak of Sanskrit dramatic art.

## The Meghaduta and Depiction of Nature

In the Meghaduta the cloud's path runs from Ramagiri to Alaka. Along the way there are beautiful descriptions of the Vindhya, the Narmada, Ujjayini and the Himalaya. The poem is in the *Mandakranta* metre. Kalidasa's **similes** became so famous that in the Sanskrit tradition the saying "Upama Kalidasasya" (in similes Kalidasa is supreme) arose.

## Legends

Many folk tales are current about Kalidasa: that he was at first illiterate and became learned through the grace of the goddess Kali, or that he was among the "Navaratnas" (nine jewels) of King Vikramaditya's court. These stories are much later and have no historical proof.

## Legacy

In the late eighteenth century the Shakuntalam reached Europe through **William Jones**'s English translation, and poets such as **Goethe** praised it. In India too later poets such as Banabhatta regarded Kalidasa as an ideal. Even today his works are taught in schools, and a "Kalidasa Samaroh" is held every year in Ujjain.
