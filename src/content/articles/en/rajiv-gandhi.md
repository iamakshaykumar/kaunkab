## Early Life: From Pilot to Politics

**Rajiv Gandhi** was born on **20 August 1944** in Bombay (Mumbai). He was the elder son of **Indira Gandhi** and **Feroze Gandhi**, and a grandson of **Jawaharlal Nehru**. He studied at the Doon School and then at the University of **Cambridge** in England, but did not complete his degree. At Cambridge he met **Sonia Maino** of Italy, whom he married in **1968**.

After returning to India he trained as a pilot and became a commercial pilot with **Indian Airlines**. He stayed away from politics; at that time his younger brother **Sanjay Gandhi** was seen as Indira Gandhi's political heir. After Sanjay died in a plane crash on **23 June 1980**, Rajiv reluctantly entered politics and reached the Lok Sabha by winning the by-election from **Amethi** in **1981**.

## The Youngest Prime Minister (1984)

After Indira Gandhi's assassination on **31 October 1984**, Rajiv Gandhi took the oath as Prime Minister that same evening. At the age of **40** he became the country's youngest Prime Minister. In the general election of **December 1984** the Congress won an unprecedented majority, taking more than 400 seats. He came to be seen as a clean young leader with modern ideas, whom the media called 'Mr Clean'.

## Computer and Telecom Initiatives

Rajiv Gandhi's dream was for India to enter the twenty-first century on the strength of technology. He made it easier to import and use computers, which many people opposed at the time, saying it would reduce jobs. The computerisation of services such as railway reservations moved forward in this period.

In telecommunications, **C-DOT (Centre for Development of Telematics)** was set up in **August 1984**, led by **Sam Pitroda**. C-DOT developed indigenous digital telephone exchanges suited to Indian conditions. After this, yellow **PCO (public call office) booths** began to spread across villages and towns, making long-distance calls easier for ordinary people. In **1986** **MTNL** was created for telephone service in the big metropolitan cities and **VSNL** for international communication. **'Technology Missions'** were also launched in areas such as drinking water, immunisation, literacy and telecommunications.

## Major Laws and Decisions

- **Anti-defection law (1985)** — The **52nd Constitutional Amendment** added the Tenth Schedule, to stop elected representatives from switching parties.
- **National Policy on Education (1986)** — Under it, **Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalayas** were opened for talented rural children.
- **The right to vote at 18** — The **61st Constitutional Amendment** lowered the voting age from 21 to **18 years**, effective from **28 March 1989**. Crores of young people became voters for the first time.
- **Accords** — The **Punjab Accord** with Akali leader **Harchand Singh Longowal** on **24 July 1985**, the **Assam Accord** on **15 August 1985** and the **Mizo Accord** in **1986**, which ended two decades of insurgency in Mizoram.
- **Panchayati Raj** — The **64th Amendment Bill (1989)**, which would have given panchayats constitutional status, could not pass the Rajya Sabha, but the same idea later became the basis of the 73rd and 74th Amendments of **1992**.

In foreign policy, the **India-Sri Lanka Accord** of **1987**, **Operation Cactus** of **1988**, which foiled a coup attempt in the Maldives, and his **visit to China** in December **1988** were notable.

## Controversies and Criticism

His tenure also saw several controversies. The government's role during the **1984 anti-Sikh riots**, and a statement of his, drew sharp criticism. In **1986**, in the **Shah Bano** case, the **Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act** was brought in, limiting the effect of the Supreme Court's verdict; critics accused the government of appeasement, while supporters called it respect for the community's sentiments. In **1987** the **Bofors controversy** arose, with allegations of kickbacks in the purchase of guns from a Swedish company, which dealt a heavy blow to his 'Mr Clean' image; later, in **2004**, the Delhi High Court quashed the charges against Rajiv Gandhi. The Congress lost the **1989** election and **V. P. Singh** became Prime Minister; Rajiv served as Leader of the Opposition.

## Sriperumbudur (21 May 1991) and Legacy

During the campaign for the **1991** Lok Sabha election, on the night of **21 May 1991**, at a rally in **Sriperumbudur** in Tamil Nadu, a suicide bomber of the **LTTE**, who came to be known as **Dhanu**, came close on the pretext of garlanding him and set off an explosion. Rajiv Gandhi and several others were killed. The attack is believed to have been linked to his decision to send the Indian Peace Keeping Force to Sri Lanka. His memorial in Delhi is **Vir Bhumi**.

In **1991** he was posthumously awarded the **Bharat Ratna**. Rajiv Gandhi is remembered for laying the foundation of India's computer and telecom revolution, giving young people the vote and advancing the idea of Panchayati Raj, while the controversies of his tenure also remain part of the debates of Indian politics.
