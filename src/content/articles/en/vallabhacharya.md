## Birth and Education

According to tradition, **Vallabhacharya** was born in 1479 CE (Vikram Samvat 1535) at a forest place called Champaranya, which is today identified with Champaran in Chhattisgarh. Some calculations give the year of birth as 1478 and some as 1473; another tradition links his birthplace with Mathura. His family was a Telugu Brahmin one — his father was **Lakshmana Bhatta** and his mother **Illammagaru**.

In childhood he was sent to **Varanasi** to study, where he made a deep study of the Vedas, Upanishads and Puranas. Around 1490 his father died.

## Debates and Travels

According to the sect's accounts, the young Vallabha won a debate against scholars of Advaita Vedanta at the court of **Vijayanagara** and received the title 'Acharya'. But historians point out that this event is linked with the time of Krishnadevaraya, who became king in 1509 — so its date and details belong to tradition and are not certain.

He made pilgrimages across India. The places where he recited the Bhagavata are called the **Chaurasi Baithak** (eighty-four seats), though the list of these comes from a later text, the 'Chaurasi Baithak Charitra'.

## Gokul, Govardhan and Shrinathji

According to the Vallabha tradition, in 1494 at **Gokul** Shri Krishna appeared before him and gave him the **Brahmasambandha** mantra — that is, the devotee's resolve to surrender everything of his own to Krishna. With this the tradition of initiation in Pushtimarg began. He began the service of the **Shrinathji** form that had appeared on Mount **Govardhan**, and in 1499 construction of a temple there began. Much later, in the 17th century, this image was taken to **Nathdwara** in Rajasthan.

## Shuddhadvaita and Pushtimarg

Vallabhacharya's philosophy is called **Shuddhadvaita** (pure non-dualism). In Shankaracharya's Advaita the world was regarded as maya (illusion); Vallabha said that Brahman (Shri Krishna) is untouched by maya, "pure", and that this world too is his real form — not a delusion. The soul is a part of God.

The path of devotion based on this is **Pushtimarg**. 'Pushti' means the grace of God. On this path renunciation is not necessary; living as a householder, one's spiritual practice is **seva** (service) — offerings of food, dressing and adornment, and kirtan — done with loving feeling towards Krishna's childhood and youthful pastimes.

For this reason Pushtimarg temples are called 'havelis' (mansions) more than 'temples' — as if the Lord were a beloved child of a household. Through the day there are **darshans** at different times, from Mangala, Shringar and Rajbhog to Shayan, and with each darshan the offerings, clothing and kirtan change according to the season and occasion. On this path initiation was given without distinction of caste or of man and woman, which the Varta literature stresses repeatedly.

His major works are the **Anubhashya** on the Brahma Sutras, the **Subodhini** commentary on the Bhagavata Purana, the **Tattvarthadipa Nibandha**, and the **Shodasha Grantha**, a collection of sixteen short works.

## Family, Death and Legacy

He married Mahalakshmi and had two sons — **Gopinath** and **Vitthalnath**. According to the sect's tradition, he took sannyasa at the end of his life and in 1531 CE entered the Ganga at Varanasi in a water-samadhi; some calculations give this year as 1530.

His tradition was given an organised form by Vitthalnath. He formed the **Ashtachhap**, a group of eight poet-singers, among whom **Surdas** became the most famous. The Brajbhasha verses of these poets enriched the Krishna-devotion poetry of Hindi literature. Even today followers of Pushtimarg are numerous in Gujarat, Rajasthan and Braj, and arts such as haveli music (haveli sangeet) and Pichwai painting are linked to this tradition of service.
