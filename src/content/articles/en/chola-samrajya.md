## Rise and Expansion

The history of the Chola dynasty spans many centuries. The revival of medieval Chola power is linked to **Vijayalaya** in about the ninth century, who took control of Thanjavur. The fertile land of the Kaveri delta supported agriculture and the state's income.

Chola power reached its peak in the time of **Rajaraja I** (reigned about 985–1014) and his son **Rajendra I** (about 1014–1044). The influence of their rule spread over a large part of South India. The Chola rulers also launched campaigns towards Sri Lanka and the sea trade routes. The effect of these campaigns lasted for different periods in different places.

## Governance and Local Institutions

Under the king there was a system of provinces and local officials. In villages and towns, assemblies and other local institutions played a role in matters such as land, irrigation and taxes. Temples were given land and gifts; besides worship, they also became centres of economic and social life. Inscriptions give details of these arrangements and gifts.

## Temples and Maritime Contacts

The **Brihadisvara Temple** of Thanjavur was built in the time of Rajaraja I and is a famous example of Chola architecture. Later Rajendra I established a new temple and capital at Gangaikonda Cholapuram. Chola-period bronze sculptures, stone carvings and Tamil inscriptions give information about the art and society of that time.

Chola merchants and ships had contacts with the Bay of Bengal and the ports of South-East Asia. These contacts included trade, diplomacy and military expeditions.

## Decline and Legacy

In the twelfth and thirteenth centuries the Pandyas and other regional powers grew strong. The influence of the Chola state shrank, and by the end of the thirteenth century independent Chola power had ended. Their temples, copper plates and stone inscriptions still help in understanding the political, economic and cultural history of South India.
