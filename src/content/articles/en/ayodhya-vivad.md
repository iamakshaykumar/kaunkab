## Roots of the Dispute

The **Babri Masjid** in **Ayodhya**, Uttar Pradesh, was built in 1528–29; according to the mosque's inscriptions it was built by **Mir Baqi**, a commander of the Mughal emperor **Babur**. Many Hindus believe that this spot is the **birthplace of Lord Rama** and that the mosque was built by destroying an older temple; historians have differed on this claim.

In the 19th century there were clashes between the two communities here, and the British administration separated the inner and outer parts of the compound with a railing. In 1885 a suit seeking permission to build a temple on the outer platform was dismissed. In **December 1949** idols of Rama were placed inside the mosque; the administration declared the compound disputed and locked it, and both sides filed civil suits.

## From 1986 to 1992

On **1 February 1986** the district judge of Faizabad ordered the locks to be opened and Hindus to be allowed to worship. After this the Ram temple movement led by the **Vishva Hindu Parishad** gathered pace, and the Bharatiya Janata Party supported it. On **25 September 1990** BJP leader **L. K. Advani** began a **rath yatra** from Somnath, which was stopped on **23 October 1990** at Samastipur in Bihar, where he was arrested. In October–November 1990 several people were killed in police firing on kar sevaks in Ayodhya.

On **6 December 1992**, during a large gathering in Ayodhya, a crowd of kar sevaks **demolished the Babri Masjid** within a few hours. This happened even though the Uttar Pradesh government had assured the Supreme Court that the structure would be protected. The same day Chief Minister **Kalyan Singh** resigned and the central government of **P. V. Narasimha Rao** imposed President's Rule in the state. Communal riots followed in many parts of the country, including Mumbai, in which about **2,000** people are estimated to have died.

## Inquiry and the Legal Battle

On **16 December 1992** a commission was set up under Justice **M. S. Liberhan** to inquire into the demolition; it submitted its report about 17 years later, in **June 2009**. In 1993 the central government passed a law acquiring about 67 acres of land around the disputed site. In 2003, on the court's orders, the **Archaeological Survey of India** (ASI) excavated the site.

On **30 September 2010** the Lucknow bench of the **Allahabad High Court** ruled that the disputed land be divided into three equal parts — among Ram Lalla Virajman, the Nirmohi Akhara and the Sunni Central Waqf Board. All the parties challenged this in the Supreme Court, and it was stayed in 2011. In 2019, after an attempt at mediation did not succeed, the court held continuous hearings from August to October.

## The Supreme Court Verdict (2019)

On **9 November 2019** a five-judge bench headed by Chief Justice **Ranjan Gogoi** delivered a **unanimous** verdict. The main points were:

- The disputed **2.77 acres** were given to **Ram Lalla Virajman**; the central government was asked to form a **trust** within three months to arrange for the construction of a temple.
- Using **Article 142** of the Constitution, the court ordered that **5 acres** of alternative land at a prominent place in Ayodhya be given to the **Sunni Waqf Board** for a mosque.
- The court said that placing the idols in 1949 and demolishing the mosque in 1992 were **serious violations of the rule of law**.
- According to the ASI report there were remains of a non-Islamic structure beneath the mosque, but the court said the report did not establish that a temple had been demolished to build the mosque.
- The Nirmohi Akhara's claim was dismissed, but it was to be given appropriate representation in the trust.

Review petitions were dismissed in December 2019. Supporters described the verdict as a peaceful end to a long dispute, while some Muslim organisations and legal experts disagreed with parts of its reasoning. Peace was maintained across the country after the verdict.

## The Trust, the Temple and the Mosque

On **5 February 2020** the government announced the **Shri Ram Janmabhoomi Teerth Kshetra** trust, and 5 acres of land were allotted for the mosque in the village of **Dhannipur**. On **5 August 2020** Prime Minister **Narendra Modi** performed the bhoomi pujan (groundbreaking ceremony) of the temple. On **30 September 2020** a special CBI court in Lucknow acquitted all **32** accused in the criminal case over the demolition, including Advani, Murli Manohar Joshi, Uma Bharti and Kalyan Singh, for lack of evidence.

On **22 January 2024** the **consecration** (pran-pratishtha) of a new idol of Ram Lalla took place in the sanctum of the new Ram temple, with the Prime Minister as the chief host. Several opposition parties stayed away from the ceremony and some religious leaders objected to consecration in an unfinished temple, while large numbers of devotees in India and abroad celebrated it as a festival.

## Significance

The Ayodhya dispute deeply influenced debates on Indian politics, religion and law for nearly four decades. The 1992 demolition and the riots that followed are counted among the most tragic events in independent India. At the same time, the 2019 verdict shows that even such a complex and emotional dispute was ultimately settled through the constitutional court, even though differences over its reasoning remain.
