## Background: The Boy Sultan

The Gujarat Sultanate was founded in the early fifteenth century, and Ahmad Shah I founded **Ahmedabad** in 1411. His grandson **Fath Khan** was born at Ahmedabad in 1445. In 1458–59 the nobles of the court removed Sultan Daud from the throne within a few days and made Fath Khan, then about 13 years old, Sultan under the name **Mahmud Shah**. (The date of his accession is given as both 1458 and 1459 in the sources.) The young Sultan soon suppressed the scheming nobles and took power into his own hands.

## The Mystery of the Name "Begada"

There is no agreement on the explanation of the nickname "Begada". According to one view it is linked to the conquest of two forts ("do garh") — **Junagadh** and **Pavagadh**. According to another it came from his long moustache, curled upward like the horns of a bull. Contemporary accounts describe him as tall and broad, with a beard reaching his waist.

## Conquest of Forts

- **Junagadh (Girnar):** After about two years of pressure, in 1472–73 the Chudasama king **Ra Mandlik III** surrendered the fort of Girnar and accepted Islam. The Sultan founded the city of **Mustafabad** here.
- **Dwarka:** According to the sources, pirates of Bet Dwarka had plundered the family and goods of a maulana. After this, in 1473 the Sultan marched against Dwarka. In this campaign the temple there was also damaged.
- **Pavagadh and Champaner:** After a siege of about twenty months (1483–84), the hill fort of Pavagadh fell on **21 November 1484**. **Rawal Jaisingh** of Champaner and his minister Dungarshi refused to change their religion and were killed.

## Champaner: The New Capital

Mahmud renamed Champaner **Muhammadabad** and made it his capital. Mosques, palaces, stepwells, reservoirs and a city wall were built here. The **Jami Masjid** is the most famous among them, showing a beautiful blend of Hindu-Jain craft traditions and Islamic architecture.

After Humayun's invasion in 1535 the capital shifted back to Ahmedabad, and Champaner gradually fell into ruin. For this reason the city survived almost unchanged. In **2004** the **Champaner-Pavagadh Archaeological Park** was added to the UNESCO World Heritage list. The **Kalika Mata temple** on the Pavagadh hill is still a major pilgrimage site.

## Clash with the Portuguese at Sea

Gujarat's ports such as Khambhat, Surat and Diu were major centres of Indian Ocean trade. After Vasco da Gama's arrival in 1498 the **Portuguese** wanted to seize this trade. To stop this, Gujarat joined hands with the **Mamluk** Sultan of Egypt and the Zamorin of Calicut.

- In **1508**, in the Battle of **Chaul**, the Gujarat commander **Malik Ayaz** and the Mamluk fleet defeated the Portuguese; in it Lourenço de Almeida, son of the Portuguese viceroy, was killed.
- The viceroy **Francisco de Almeida**, who came to take revenge, decisively defeated the combined fleet near **Diu** on **3 February 1509**.

After the defeat at Diu, the Portuguese navy dominated the Indian Ocean for about the next century.

## Death and Legacy

Mahmud Begada died at Ahmedabad on **23 November 1511**. He was buried at **Sarkhej Roza**, and his son Khalil Khan became Sultan as **Muzaffar Shah II**.

During his reign of about 52 years the Gujarat Sultanate reached its peak — revenue grew, roads became safe and there was a great deal of building. At the same time, many Hindu kingdoms and temples were also damaged in his campaigns. He is therefore judged as a capable but harsh conqueror.
