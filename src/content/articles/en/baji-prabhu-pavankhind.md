## The Crisis After Pratapgad

After the death of Afzal Khan near Pratapgad in **November 1659**, Shivaji advanced rapidly and also took **Panhala**, the strong fort near Kolhapur. This was a big blow to the Adil Shahi sultanate of Bijapur. The sultan **Ali Adil Shah II** sent **Siddi Jauhar** with a large army to take revenge.

In the spring of **1660** (from **2 March**, according to one source) Siddi Jauhar besieged Panhala while Shivaji himself was inside the fort. The siege lasted for months. From outside, **Netaji Palkar** kept trying to loosen the siege by night raids, but did not succeed. The rains arrived, supplies began to run short and the danger of Shivaji being trapped kept growing.

## The Plan to Escape from Panhala

Shivaji opened a line of negotiation with Jauhar, which relaxed the besieging army somewhat. Then, on a rainy night — in Marathi tradition taken to be the Ashadha Purnima night of **12–13 July 1660** — Shivaji slipped quietly out of the fort with chosen soldiers. His goal was the fort of **Vishalgad**, lying roughly to the west.

According to tradition, a barber named **Shiva Kashid**, who resembled Shivaji in face, sat in a palanquin and misled the enemy. When he was caught the deception was exposed, and Shiva Kashid was killed. Marathi popular memory recalls his role with great respect, though the detailed account comes mainly from later narratives.

## The Last Stand at Ghodkhind

As soon as the deception was discovered, the Bijapuri army led by **Siddi Masud** and **Fazl Khan** (the son of Afzal Khan) began the pursuit. By the time they approached Vishalgad, the enemy had come very close. Then, at a narrow, wooded pass called **Ghodkhind**, **Baji Prabhu Deshpande** took on the responsibility of staying behind and blocking the way so that Shivaji could get ahead.

It was agreed that, on reaching Vishalgad, Shivaji would signal his safe arrival by the **sound of cannon**. Baji Prabhu, his brother **Fulaji Prabhu** and the soldiers of the Bandal force used the narrow pass to repel repeated attacks for several hours. In popular accounts Baji Prabhu is said to have had about **300** soldiers with him, while some sources give a far larger number; the sources do not agree on the figures for the armies and casualties on both sides.

According to the story, despite deep wounds Baji Prabhu kept fighting until the sound of cannon was heard from Vishalgad — only then did he give up his life. His brother Fulaji was also killed in this battle. It is believed that this pass, sanctified by Baji Prabhu's sacrifice, came to be named **"Pavan Khind"** (the sacred pass).

## Disagreement over Dates

The sources differ on the date of this event. Marathi tradition and most Indian sources place the battle on **13 July 1660**, while some English sources, based on older historians such as Jadunath Sarkar, place it in later months. Militarily, the Bijapur side crossed the pass, but its real aim — capturing Shivaji — was not fulfilled. Later, around **22 September 1660**, Panhala fort was handed over to Bijapur; Shivaji won it again in **1673**.

## Memory

Baji Prabhu belonged to the **Chandraseniya Kayastha Prabhu (CKP)** community and was earlier in the service of the Bandal deshmukhs of Rohida. Today there is a statue of him at **Panhala fort**, and Pavan Khind is a memorial site, where many people reach on foot every July. Sri Aurobindo wrote an English poem on him in 1909. The story of Baji Prabhu is a reminder that the foundation of Shivaji's swarajya was laid not by one leader alone, but by the sacrifice of many such companions.
