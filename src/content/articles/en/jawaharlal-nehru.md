## From Allahabad to Cambridge

**Jawaharlal Nehru** was born on **14 November 1889** in **Allahabad**. His father **Motilal Nehru** was a famous lawyer and later a major Congress leader. After studying at home with English tutors, he went to **Harrow** School in England and then to **Trinity College**, Cambridge, from where he took a degree in natural sciences in 1910. Qualifying as a barrister at the Inner Temple, he returned to India in August 1912. In 1916 he married **Kamala Kaul**; their only child, **Indira**, was born in 1917.

## In the Movement with Gandhi

Nehru did not take to the legal profession. After meeting Gandhiji in 1916 he became involved in Home Rule and then in peasant movements, and in 1920 he went among the peasants of Pratapgarh and saw the poverty of villages at close quarters. He went to jail for the first time in December 1921 during the **Non-Cooperation Movement**. Between 1921 and 1945 he was arrested eight times and spent **more than nine years** in jail in all.

The anti-imperialist conference in Brussels in 1927 and a visit to the Soviet Union deepened the influence of socialism on his thinking. At the **Lahore session** of 1929, as Congress President, he had the goal of **'Purna Swaraj'** (complete independence) declared, and on 31 December 1929 he hoisted the tricolour on the bank of the Ravi. He became president again in 1936 and 1937. In 1936 his wife Kamala died in Europe.

## Books Written in Jail

Nehru did much of his major writing in jail. His letters to his daughter Indira became **'Glimpses of World History'**, and in 1936 his **'Autobiography'** was published. After the Quit India Movement, during imprisonment in **Ahmednagar Fort**, between April and September 1944 he wrote **'The Discovery of India'**, which was published in 1946. In it is a search for India's diversity and its shared cultural heritage, from the Indus civilisation to the modern period.

## The First Prime Minister

After the interim government of September 1946, on **15 August 1947** Nehru became the first Prime Minister of independent India and remained in that post until his death on **27 May 1964**. In the difficult period of riots after Partition, the refugee crisis and the integration of the princely states, he ran the government together with Patel. In the general elections of 1951–52, 1957 and 1962 the Congress won under his leadership.

He stressed parliamentary democracy, secularism and a mixed economy. In 1950 the **Planning Commission** was formed and Five-Year Plans began. He called dams such as Bhakra and steel plants the 'temples of modern India'. The **IITs** (the first at Kharagpur in 1951), **AIIMS** (1956), the Atomic Energy Commission and many laboratories were created in his time. In 1955–56 the Hindu law codes gave women new rights of inheritance and divorce.

## Foreign Policy: Panchsheel and Non-Alignment

In the Cold War period Nehru adopted a policy of not joining either bloc. In 1954 the **Panchsheel** agreement was made with China, in 1955 he was a leading voice at the Bandung Conference, and in the formal beginning of the **Non-Aligned Movement** at the 1961 Belgrade Conference he played an important role together with Nasser of Egypt and Tito of Yugoslavia. In the same year Goa was liberated from Portuguese rule.

## Criticism and Legacy

Many of Nehru's policies are still debated today. The defeat in the **1962 India–China War** is linked to his China policy and lack of military preparedness, and it dealt a deep blow to his last years. He is also criticised for taking the Kashmir issue to the United Nations in 1948, for the dismissal of Kerala's elected government in 1959, and for an economic policy with heavy government control. Even so, historians hold that Nehru's contribution was decisive in rooting democratic institutions, regular elections and scientific thinking in the infant republic.
