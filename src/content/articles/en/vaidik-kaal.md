## Background

After the end of the urban life of the Indus Valley, from about 1500 BCE a new rural and pastoral society arose in north-western India, which is called the **Vedic period**. The main source of information about this period is the 'Vedas', which is why it was named the Vedic Age.

Historians divide this period into two main parts:
1. **The Rigvedic or Early Vedic Period (about 1500 – 1000 BCE):** Life was centred mainly in the Sapta Sindhu region (Punjab and its surroundings) and society was organised in tribal form.
2. **The Later Vedic Period (about 1000 – 600 BCE):** The people moved towards the Ganga-Yamuna Doab and the east. In this period agriculture became the main occupation and the social structure became more complex.

## Social and Political Structure

In the Early Vedic period society was divided into tribes, called 'jana'. The chief of the tribe was called 'rajan', and he was checked by institutions such as the 'sabha' and 'samiti'. Women too had the right to take part in religious rituals and education.

In the Later Vedic period, with the coming of iron tools (such as iron ploughshares and axes), forests were cleared and settled farming began. Along with this:
- Tribal identity began to turn into territorial identity — the 'jana' now became the **'janapada'** (where people had set foot and settled).
- The varna system began to be based on birth and the boundaries of the four varnas (Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra) began to harden.

## The 16 Mahajanapadas and the Rise of Magadha

By the sixth century BCE, 16 large and powerful states had emerged in northern India, called the **'16 Mahajanapadas'** in the Buddhist text *Anguttara Nikaya* and the Jain text *Bhagavati Sutra*.

Some of these states were monarchies (such as Magadha, Kosala, Vatsa, Avanti), while states such as Vajji and Malla were **republics (gana-sanghas)**, where decisions were taken by a group of rulers through deliberation.

Because of geographical advantages (iron mines, the fertile Ganges plain, availability of elephants) and diplomatic rulers such as Bimbisara and Ajatashatru, **Magadha** emerged as the most powerful Mahajanapada, which later prepared the base for the vast Mauryan Empire.

## Historical Significance

The Vedic period laid the foundation of Indian philosophy (Upanishads, philosophical systems), the Sanskrit language and the social order. At the end of this period, intellectual debate against rituals gave birth to new ideas such as Buddhism and Jainism.
