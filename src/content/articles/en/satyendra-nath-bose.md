## A Brilliant Student from Calcutta

**Satyendra Nath Bose** was born on **1 January 1894** in **Calcutta**. From childhood he was exceptional at mathematics. He took his B.Sc. from **Presidency College** in 1913 and in **1915** his M.Sc. in mixed mathematics, scoring record marks at Calcutta University. Among his classmates was **Meghnad Saha**, who went on to become a great astrophysicist.

From 1916 to 1921 Bose taught at Calcutta's new science college (Rajabazar Science College). At that time books and papers on modern physics were hard to obtain in India; Bose and Saha learned German and French to read the new research from Europe. Together they also prepared an English translation of **Einstein's** papers on the theory of relativity.

## Dhaka and a New Idea

In **1921** Bose became a reader in the physics department of the newly founded **University of Dhaka**. It was here that he did the work that made him world-famous.

Physics then faced a puzzle. In 1900 **Max Planck** had given the law for radiation emitted by hot bodies, but its derivation mixed in arguments from the older (classical) physics. Bose treated light as a collection of particles called **photons** and counted them in a completely new way. He assumed that identical photons **cannot be told apart** from one another — that is, swapping two photons does not create a new state. With this simple-seeming idea he derived **Planck's law** without relying on classical physics.

## The Paper Sent to Einstein

A British journal had declined to publish Bose's paper. So in **1924** he sent it directly to **Albert Einstein**, with a letter asking him to have it published in a German journal if he thought it worthwhile. Einstein immediately saw the importance of the idea. He himself made a **German translation** and had it published, with his own note, in the famous journal **'Zeitschrift für Physik'**.

Einstein did not stop there. He extended Bose's counting method beyond light to **atoms**. This led to the prediction that at very low temperatures such atoms could gather in a single lowest-energy state and form a new state of matter. This was called the **Bose-Einstein condensate**. The whole method of calculation is today called **Bose-Einstein statistics**.

## Bosons — A Whole Class of Particles

It was later found that all particles in nature fall into two broad classes. Those particles that obey Bose-Einstein statistics were named **"bosons"** in Bose's honour by the British physicist **Paul Dirac**. The photon, and the famous **Higgs boson** discovered in 2012, belong to this class. Particles of the other class — such as the electron — are called **"fermions"**.

The prediction of the Bose-Einstein condensate could not be confirmed experimentally for about seventy years, because it required temperatures extremely close to absolute zero. In **1995** scientists in the United States first created this state in a laboratory, and the **2001** Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded for this work.

## European Journey and Later Life

After the paper was published, Bose spent about two years (1924–26) in Europe. In Paris he worked in **Marie Curie's** laboratory and with **Louis de Broglie**, and in Berlin he met and worked with **Einstein**. Returning to Dhaka, he became head of the physics department and remained there until 1945. He then returned to **Calcutta University** and taught until 1956. After that he became Vice-Chancellor of **Visva-Bharati University** in Santiniketan.

Bose was a strong supporter of teaching science in the mother tongue and worked to popularise science in Bengali. He received the **Padma Vibhushan** in **1954**, became a Fellow of the **Royal Society** of London in **1958**, and in **1959** the Government of India appointed him a **National Professor**. He was also a nominated member of the Rajya Sabha from 1952 to 1960. He died in Calcutta on **4 February 1974**.

## Legacy

Bose was nominated for the Nobel Prize several times, but never received it. Even so, his name is written into the foundations of modern physics — every time a scientist says the word "boson", they remember Bose. In his memory the **S. N. Bose National Centre for Basic Sciences** was established in Kolkata in **1986**. Bose, who sat in a small room in Dhaka and gave a new direction to the greatest scientific debate of his time, is still regarded as an inspiration for Indian science.
