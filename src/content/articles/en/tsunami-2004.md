## A Great Earthquake Under the Sea

On the morning of **26 December 2004**, at about **6:29 Indian time**, a great earthquake struck under the sea near the north-western coast of the island of **Sumatra** in Indonesia. Its magnitude was estimated at between **9.1 and 9.3**, making it one of the most powerful earthquakes ever recorded. Here the Indian plate slides beneath the Burma plate. A rupture about 1,300 kilometres long suddenly slipped and the sea floor was thrust upward.

The **tsunami** waves it raised spread across the whole Indian Ocean at the speed of a jet aircraft. Within a few hours they had reached Indonesia, Thailand, Sri Lanka, India, the Maldives and even distant Somalia in East Africa. About **2.3 lakh** people died in 14 countries. At that time there was no tsunami warning system in the Indian Ocean, so people living on the coasts had no idea of the danger coming towards them.

## The First Blow on the Andaman and Nicobar Islands

The Indian territory closest to the epicentre was the **Andaman and Nicobar Islands**. The waves reached here shortly after the earthquake. In the southern Nicobar Islands the waves were up to about 15 metres high. The Indian Air Force base at **Car Nicobar** was badly destroyed, and more than 100 air warriors and their family members there were swept away by the waves. On small islands such as Chowra, a large part of the population was wiped out.

The earthquake caused several islands to subside permanently. India's southernmost point, **Indira Point**, sank by a few metres and its lighthouse became surrounded by water. Official figures recorded more than about 1,300 dead on these islands, but thousands of people were declared missing and were never found. Notably, several indigenous tribes such as the Jarawa and the Onge moved to higher ground and remained relatively safe; their traditional knowledge of the sea's behaviour is believed to have helped them.

## Devastation on the Coast of Tamil Nadu

About two to two and a half hours later, at around 9 in the morning, the waves struck the eastern coast of mainland India. **Tamil Nadu** was the worst-hit state, with about **8,000** deaths. **Nagapattinam** district alone accounted for about **6,000** deaths. At the pilgrimage centre of Velankanni, many devotees gathered after Christmas were caught by the waves. Many people also died at **Cuddalore**, **Kanyakumari** and on **Chennai's** Marina beach.

The waves also reached **Puducherry** and **Andhra Pradesh**, and, curving around the peninsula, even **Kerala** on the west coast, where coastal villages in Kollam district suffered heavy damage. Fishing communities suffered the most: their homes, boats and nets were all washed away. Altogether, according to official figures, about **10,700** people died in India and about **5,600** went missing, most of them in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. These numbers vary somewhat in figures from different dates.

## Relief Operations

Prime Minister **Dr. Manmohan Singh's** government said that India could face this disaster with its own resources and did not accept immediate relief assistance from other countries. The **Indian Navy**, Air Force, Army and **Coast Guard** carried out rescue and relief work on a large scale. The Navy ran **Operation Sea Waves** for the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and **Operation Madad** for the Tamil Nadu and Andhra coast.

India also helped its neighbours: ships, helicopters, doctors and relief supplies were sent to Sri Lanka under **Operation Rainbow**, to the Maldives under **Operation Castor** and to Indonesia under **Operation Gambhir**. The Government of India gave one lakh rupees each to the families of the dead, and permanent housing was later built in the coastal areas.

## The Warning Centre and the Legacy

The biggest lesson of the 2004 tsunami was that thousands of lives could have been saved if warning had come in time. India then set up the **Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre** at the Hyderabad-based **Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services (INCOIS)** under the Ministry of Earth Sciences, which was inaugurated on **15 October 2007**. Using sensors on the sea floor, tide gauges and a network of seismometers, the centre can issue a warning within a few minutes of an earthquake. Today it is also one of the main centres providing tsunami information to countries across the Indian Ocean region.

Following this experience, the **Disaster Management Act 2005** was enacted in India and disaster-preparedness programmes were started for coastal communities. Tsunami memorials have been built at many places in Tamil Nadu and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, where the dead are remembered every year on 26 December.
