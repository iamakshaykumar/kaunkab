## Thimmappa, the Warrior of Bankapura

**Kanakadasa** was born in the village of **Bada** near **Bankapura**, in today's **Haveri** district of Karnataka. Traditionally his lifetime is taken as **1509 to 1609**, though some sources give his birth as 1508 and his death as 1606. His original name was **Thimmappa Nayaka**. He was the son of **Beerappa** and **Bachchamma** of the **Kuruba** (shepherd) community.

Thimmappa was educated in logic, grammar and Mimamsa, and served as a warrior and chief at the **fort of Bankapura**. According to tradition, after being badly wounded in a battle, his mind turned away from worldly life and he turned to devotion to Vishnu (Hari). A folk story also says that he found **gold** buried in the ground ('kanaka' in Kannada), which he distributed among the people — and that this is how he came to be called 'Kanaka Nayaka' and then **Kanakadasa**.

## Disciple of Vyasatirtha, in the Haridasa Tradition

At that time the **Haridasa** movement was spreading in the Vijayanagara Empire. The Haridasas were devotees who, instead of Sanskrit, sang songs in **Kannada**, the language of ordinary people, to spread **Madhvacharya's** **Dvaita** philosophy and devotion to Vishnu. Kanakadasa became a disciple of **Vyasatirtha**, the great guru of this tradition. Vyasatirtha's other famous disciple was **Purandaradasa**, so Kanakadasa and Purandaradasa are regarded as contemporaries and 'fellow disciples'.

Several stories in the tradition say that Vyasatirtha's other disciples resented so much honour being given to a person of 'low' caste, and that each time the guru, through some incident, showed the superiority of Kanakadasa's sincere devotion. Whether or not these stories are historically proven, they show that the Haridasa tradition carried the message of placing devotion above caste.

## Works — From Kirtanas to Long Poems

In his compositions Kanakadasa used the signature **'Kagineleya Adikeshava'** — after Adikeshava, the deity of **Kaginele**. About **240** of his kirtanas, ugabhogas and suladis are still sung in **Carnatic music** concerts and bhajan groups. His main longer works are:

- **Mohanatarangini** — a long poem based on the story of Krishna, written in the 'sangatya' metre.
- **Nalacharitre** — the story of King Nala and Damayanti, which became very popular in Kannada.
- **Haribhaktisara** — a collection of short verses on devotion and ethics, which generations of children learned by heart.
- **Ramadhanya Charitre** — a unique allegorical poem in which **ragi** and **rice** argue before Rama about which of them is superior. Rice is the symbol of the rich and powerful class, while ragi is the food of the toiling poor. Rama shuts both away for some time; afterwards the rice rots while the ragi survives. Scholars see it as a sharp comment on social inequality.
- **Nrisimhastava** — a hymn in praise of Lord Narasimha.

His kirtanas directly question pride in caste and lineage. One of his famous songs asks: when all are children of the same God, why all this noise about 'lineage, lineage'?

## The 'Kanakana Kindi' of Udupi

The most famous story connected with Kanakadasa is that of the **Udupi Sri Krishna Matha**. According to tradition, when Kanakadasa reached Udupi for darshan of Krishna, the priests, regarding him as being of low caste, did not let him enter the temple. Kanakadasa stood behind the temple and began singing devotional songs. The story says that just then a crack appeared in the back wall, and the **east-facing image of Krishna turned to face west**, so that Kanakadasa could have darshan. **Vadiraja Tirtha**, the head of the matha, instead of sealing the crack, gave it the form of a window.

This window is today called the **'Kanakana Kindi'** (Kanaka's window), and devotees peer through it for darshan of Krishna. It is also often associated with the nine-holed **'Navagraha Kindi'** directly in front of the image. Many historians regard this story as a folk tradition that developed in later centuries, but it made Kanakadasa a symbol of devotion against caste discrimination.

## Legacy

Kanakadasa is counted among the greatest poets of Kannada devotional literature. The Government of India issued a **postage stamp** in his honour in **1990**. In **2008** the Government of Karnataka declared **Kanakadasa Jayanti** a state festival, and it is a public holiday in the state. The **Kanaka Guru Peetha** at Kaginele is named after him, and a memorial has been built at his birthplace, Bada. In the 1960 Kannada film **'Bhakta Kanakadasa'** Dr. Rajkumar played his role, and in 1988 Girish Karnad made the documentary **'Kanaka-Purandara'** on Kanakadasa and Purandaradasa. Four hundred years later, his songs still remind us that at the door of devotion no caste is high or low.
