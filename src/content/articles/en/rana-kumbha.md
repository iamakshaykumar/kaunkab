## Background: A Throne Received in Crisis

**Kumbhakarna**, whom history knows as **Rana Kumbha**, was born around 1417. His father **Rana Mokal** was murdered in 1433 by some courtiers in a conspiracy. In this turmoil the young Kumbha took the throne of Mewar.

In the early years he had to deal with his father's killers and calm the factionalism of the court. After that he turned to strengthening the borders of Mewar.

## Conflict with Malwa and Gujarat

In the 15th century Mewar had two powerful neighbours — the sultanates of Malwa and Gujarat. Some of Mokal's murderers had taken shelter with the sultan of Malwa, **Mahmud Khalji**. When he refused to hand them over, conflict broke out.

- Around 1437, near **Sarangpur**, Kumbha's army defeated the army of Malwa. According to Rajasthani tradition the sultan was taken prisoner and released after some time; but Persian sources describe this event differently, so its details are disputed.
- Later, Malwa and Gujarat attacked Mewar several times, sometimes jointly. Kumbha succeeded in saving his kingdom, although there were victories and defeats on both sides in the border regions.

It is worth noting that the sultan of Malwa also had a monument built at Mandu in memory of his victory — that is, both sides claimed their own victory.

## Vijay Stambh: A Tale of Victory Written in Stone

In memory of his successes against Malwa and Gujarat, Kumbha had the **Vijay Stambh** built at Chittorgarh. This nine-storey tower is about 37 metres high, and one can climb to the top by stairs inside.

- Its walls are filled with sculptures of Hindu gods and goddesses, dancers and musical instruments, so it is also called an "encyclopaedia of Indian sculpture".
- There is disagreement about the date of its completion — some sources say 1448, while others place it between 1458 and 1468.
- The poet Atri and his son Mahesh composed the famous **Kirtistambha Prashasti** of Kumbha's time, which is an important source for the history of Mewar.

## Kumbhalgarh and the Network of Forts

According to tradition, of the 84 forts that defended Mewar, 32 were built or repaired by Kumbha. The most famous among them is **Kumbhalgarh**, set in the Aravalli hills. The Archaeological Survey of India places its construction between about 1443 and 1458. Its chief architect was **Mandan**, who also wrote works on Vastushastra such as "Rajavallabha".

Kumbhalgarh later became a refuge for the royal family of Mewar; according to tradition Maharana Pratap was also born here. The famous **Ranakpur Jain Temple** was also built during Kumbha's reign.

## A Scholar King

Rana Kumbha was not only a warrior. He was deeply learned in music and Sanskrit literature. Works on music such as **"Sangitaraja"** and the commentary **"Rasikapriya"** on Jayadeva's Gita Govinda are linked to his name. Many poets, craftsmen and scholars found shelter at his court.

## A Tragic End and Legacy

In 1468 he was murdered at Kumbhalgarh by his own son **Udai Singh (Uda)**. Because of this act Uda was called a "murderer" in the tradition of Mewar, and he could not stay long on the throne.

The forts built by Rana Kumbha protected Mewar for generations to come. The struggles of successors such as Rana Sanga and Maharana Pratap rested on this strong foundation.
