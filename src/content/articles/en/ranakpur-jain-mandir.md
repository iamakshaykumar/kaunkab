## A Temple in an Aravalli Valley

In the **Pali district** of Rajasthan, some distance from Udaipur, among the green hills of the Aravallis, stands the Jain temple of **Ranakpur**. It belongs to the Shvetambara Jain tradition and is dedicated to the first Tirthankara, **Rishabhanatha (Adinatha)**. It is also called **Chaturmukha Dharan Vihar**, because in the main sanctum stands a white marble **four-faced** image of Adinatha — with faces towards all four directions — and the temple can be entered from all four directions.

## Dharna Shah and Rana Kumbha

The builder of the temple was **Dharna Shah** (Dhanna Shah), a Porwal Jain merchant from Ghanerao who was connected with the court of Mewar. He received the patronage of the ruler of Mewar, **Rana Kumbha** (reign 1433–1468). According to tradition the Rana gave the land for it, and the name of the town, 'Ranakpur', is also believed to be linked to Rana Kumbha. Kumbha was himself a great patron of art and architecture — the Vijay Stambh of Chittor and the fort of Kumbhalgarh also belong to his time.

Tradition says that Dharna Shah saw a divine aerial vehicle called **Nalinigulma** in a dream, and the temple was built on his vision of it. This is a **religious tale**, but it is often told to explain the grand, multi-storeyed form of the temple.

## The Time of Construction — What Is Certain and What Is Not

The start of construction is usually placed in **1437 CE** (Vikram Samvat 1494), although some accounts give other dates. On one pillar of the temple there is an inscription of **1439 CE**, which names Dharna Shah as the builder and **Depak** (Depa) as the architect. Sources also differ on how long the work took — some say up to 1458, while the popular claim is as much as fifty years. It is therefore right to treat the exact period as **disputed**.

## Architecture: A Forest of Pillars

The Ranakpur temple is an excellent example of the **Maru-Gurjara** style of architecture, a distinctive style of the Jain and Hindu temples of western India.
- The whole temple is built of light-coloured **marble** and spreads over about **48,000 square feet**.
- It is said to have about **1,444** carved pillars, **29 halls (mandapas)** and **80 domes**.
- There are five spires including the main one, and all around are rows of small devakulikas (subsidiary shrines).
- Here there is a famous image of **Parshvanatha**, carved from a single piece of marble, with a canopy of 1,008 serpent hoods above it.

The pillars and domes are arranged so that light enters from different angles throughout the day and the colour of the marble seems to change.

## Importance

Ranakpur, together with the **Dilwara temples** of Mount Abu, is regarded as the peak of Jain temple art in western India. It shows how prosperous the merchant community was in 15th-century Mewar and how royal power gave patronage to the building works of different religions. Even today it is a major pilgrimage centre of the Jain community, and tourists from all over the world come to see its carving.
