## Background: Sri Lanka's Ethnic Conflict

In Sri Lanka, tension between the majority **Sinhala** and minority **Tamil** communities grew after independence (1948). Policies on language, education and jobs deepened a sense of discrimination among Tamils. In the 1970s a demand arose for a separate Tamil state, 'Eelam', in the north and east, and several armed groups were formed, of which the **Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE)**, led by **Velupillai Prabhakaran**, became the most powerful.

After the anti-Tamil riots in Colombo and elsewhere in **July 1983** ('Black July'), civil war broke out. Thousands of Tamil refugees reached India, especially Tamil Nadu. There was deep sympathy on this issue in Tamil Nadu, and India was also worried about the growing role of outside powers in its neighbourhood.

## Operation Poomalai and the Accord

In **1987** the Sri Lankan army launched a major offensive in the Jaffna peninsula, which created a shortage of food and medicine for civilians there. When an Indian attempt to send relief by boat was blocked, on **4 June 1987** five **An-32** aircraft of the Indian Air Force dropped about 25 tonnes of relief supplies over Jaffna. This was called **Operation Poomalai**. Sri Lanka called it a violation of its sovereignty, but after it the talks gathered pace.

On **29 July 1987** in Colombo, Prime Minister **Rajiv Gandhi** and President **J. R. Jayewardene** signed the **India-Sri Lanka Accord**. Its main provisions were:

- Devolution of power to the provinces while preserving Sri Lanka's unity and integrity; for this, the **13th Amendment** was made to Sri Lanka's constitution and **Provincial Councils** were created.
- A temporary merger of the **Northern and Eastern Provinces**, on which a referendum was later to be held in the Eastern Province.
- Official language status for Tamil as well.
- Surrender of arms by Tamil militant groups and a ceasefire; an Indian peacekeeping force would be sent to oversee this.

## From Peacekeeping to War

From **30 July 1987** soldiers of the **Indian Peace Keeping Force (IPKF)** began landing at **Palaly** airfield in Jaffna. At first the LTTE did hand over some weapons, but it was not satisfied with the Accord, because it had not been directly included in the negotiations and wanted dominance in the proposed interim administration. In early October 1987, violence flared after the suicides of some LTTE fighters who had been taken into custody.

In **October 1987** the IPKF launched **Operation Pawan** to take control of Jaffna. Fighting guerrillas in a densely populated city was extremely difficult; troops landed by helicopter on the Jaffna University grounds suffered heavy losses. After several weeks of fighting, Jaffna town came under IPKF control, but the LTTE went into the jungles and continued a guerrilla war. The size of the force grew to about 80,000 to 100,000. On **25 November 1987**, for exceptional gallantry in an operation, **Major Ramaswamy Parameswaran** was posthumously awarded the **Param Vir Chakra**.

In this war, allegations of civilian deaths and human rights violations were made against both the LTTE and the IPKF, and they are still debated today. In **1988** Sri Lanka also held elections to the North-Eastern Provincial Council.

## Withdrawal (1989–1990)

In Sri Lanka, too, Sinhala nationalist groups opposed the presence of the Indian army. **Ranasinghe Premadasa**, who became President in **1989**, demanded the IPKF's withdrawal and at the same time also opened talks with the LTTE. In India, the government of **V. P. Singh** came to power in **December 1989** and decided to bring the force back. The last units returned to India on **24 March 1990**. According to a 1999 statement by the then Defence Minister, **1,165** Indian soldiers were killed in this campaign and about **3,000** wounded.

## Legacy and Significance

The IPKF campaign is counted among the hardest experiences of India's foreign and military policy. According to its supporters, it gave Sri Lanka a constitutional framework for provincial autonomy — the 13th Amendment — which is still part of politics there. Critics say that the army was pushed into a long guerrilla war without a clear goal or enough intelligence. Its most tragic consequence came on **21 May 1991**, when an LTTE suicide bomber assassinated Rajiv Gandhi at Sriperumbudur. The LTTE was finally defeated in **2009** in an offensive by the Sri Lankan army. The **IPKF memorial** near Colombo recalls the Indian soldiers who were martyred in this campaign.
