## Birth and a Difficult Beginning

Hargobind was born on **19 June 1595** in the village of **Guru ki Wadali** near Amritsar to the fifth Guru **Guru Arjan Dev** and Mata Ganga (some sources give a different year). His education and training took place under the care of senior Sikhs like **Baba Buddha** and **Bhai Gurdas**.

In 1606, on the order of the Mughal emperor **Jahangir**, Guru Arjan Dev was put to death at Lahore. Amid this blow, at only about eleven years of age, Hargobind became the **sixth Guru** of the Sikhs.

## Miri and Piri

At the time of taking the Guru's seat Hargobind began a new tradition. He wore two swords:
- **Piri** — symbol of spiritual authority,
- **Miri** — symbol of worldly (political-military) authority.

The message was clear — the Sikh community would be ready, along with devotion, to defend itself and to fight for justice. The Guru asked Sikhs to bring horses and weapons as offerings, raised a small army (the **Akal Sena**), and encouraged hunting, horse riding and practice of arms. The Guru's court now began to look regal.

## The Founding of the Akal Takht

In 1606 Guru Hargobind had a high platform built at Amritsar directly in front of the **Harmandir Sahib**, which was called the **Akal Bunga** and later came to be called the **Akal Takht** — "the throne of the Timeless One". Baba Buddha and Bhai Gurdas helped in its construction.

Sitting here the Guru listened to the petitions of Sikhs in worldly matters and gave judgements, while the Harmandir Sahib remained the centre of devotion and kirtan. Even today the Akal Takht is the foremost of the five takhts of the Sikhs and the centre of the community's major decisions.

## Imprisonment at Gwalior

The growing military preparation did not escape the notice of the Mughal rulers. Around 1609 Jahangir placed Guru Hargobind under detention in **Gwalior Fort**. How long this imprisonment lasted is disputed among historians — estimates range from a few years to twelve years.

The famous tradition about the release is that the Guru refused to come out alone and got **52 imprisoned kings** freed along with him. For this reason he is called **'Bandi Chhor'** (liberator of prisoners). This story is deeply rooted in Sikh tradition, though it is not confirmed by contemporary Persian sources.

## The Battles in Shah Jahan's Time

After Jahangir's death (1627), relations worsened under **Shah Jahan**. Guru Hargobind's army fought several skirmishes and battles with Mughal detachments and local faujdars, of which **Amritsar (1634)** and **Kartarpur (1635)** are the main ones. These were not big wars, but they proved that the Sikh community could now take up arms to defend itself.

After this the Guru moved to **Kiratpur** in the Shivalik hills and spent the last years of his life there in peace. Stories of his friendship with the Sufi saint **Mian Mir** are also famous in this period of his growing popularity.

## Succession and Legacy

Guru Hargobind died at Kiratpur Sahib on **28 February 1644** (3 March in some sources). He chose his grandson **Har Rai** as the seventh Guru and passed over **Dhir Mal**, who was favoured by the Mughals. His youngest son Tegh Bahadur later became the ninth Guru.

The idea of the **'saint-soldier'** that Guru Hargobind introduced in Sikhism was fully developed later in Guru Gobind Singh's **Khalsa**.
