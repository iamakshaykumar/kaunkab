## Background: The Rathores of Mandore

In the fifteenth century the **Rathore** dynasty's influence was growing over the Marwar region of western Rajasthan. Their capital was **Mandore**. **Rao Ranmal** of this dynasty also had great influence at the court of Mewar, because his sister was the mother of Rana Mokal, and after Mokal's murder Ranmal had become a powerful guardian in the early years of the young Rana Kumbha.

**Jodha** was born on 28 March 1416 at Mandore as Ranmal's son.

## The Murder of His Father and Exile

The chiefs of Mewar began to resent Ranmal's growing influence. Around 1438 the Sisodia party murdered Ranmal at Chittor. Jodha escaped with a few companions. **Rawat Chunda** of Mewar pursued him, and Mandore too passed under Mewar's control.

For the next several years Jodha spent his time wandering in the desert regions. Slowly he reorganised the Rathore chiefs and allies. Tradition also mentions the blessing of the folk goddess **Karni Mata** during this struggle.

## The Return of Mandore and the Settlement with Mewar

In **1453** Jodha made a sudden attack and took Mandore again. After this a treaty was made between Marwar and Mewar. The borders of the two kingdoms were fixed, and to strengthen the relationship Jodha's daughter was married to Rana Kumbha's son **Raimal**. This settlement opened the way for the expansion of the Rathores to the west.

## 1459: The Foundation of Mehrangarh

The old fort of Mandore was considered weak from the point of view of security. So Jodha chose a high, steep rocky hill about 9 kilometres to the south. On **12 May 1459** the foundation of a new fort was laid here, which later came to be called **Mehrangarh**. The city that grew up below it was called **Jodhpur** after Rao Jodha, and the capital moved here from Mandore.

Many **legends** are linked to the founding of the fort:
- It is said that the ascetic **Cheeriyanath**, who lived on the hill, cursed the place when he was removed.
- According to tradition the foundation stone was laid by Karni Mata.
- Another tale says that a man named **Rajaram Meghwal** was buried alive in the foundation. There is no historical confirmation of these tales, and they should be seen only as folk tradition.

## The Expansion of the Rathore Kingdom

In the time of Rao Jodha, Marwar became a major regional power. Several of his sons established their own seats in different areas. The most famous among them is **Rao Bika**, who laid the foundation of the state of **Bikaner** in the Jangladesh region in the north; **Bida** founded Bidasar. In this way branches of the Rathores spread over a large part of Rajasthan.

Rao Jodha died on **6 April 1489** (some sources say 1488). He was succeeded by **Satal**.

## Legacy

Mehrangarh is counted today among the most impressive forts of India. In the following centuries the Rathore rulers added palaces, gates and temples to it — such as the **Jai Pol** and **Fateh Pol**, built in memory of victories. Today the fort has a large museum, which holds howdahs, palanquins, weapons and paintings. The more than five-hundred-year-old city of Jodhpur is also called the 'Blue City' because of its blue-coloured houses — and it all began with Rao Jodha's far-sightedness.
