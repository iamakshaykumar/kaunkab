## Background: The Increased Revenue

Bardoli was a taluka of Surat district in the then Bombay Province. From the 1920s, Gandhian constructive workers had been working in the villages through ashrams, especially among the tribal 'Kaliparaj' community. In 1922 Gandhi had planned a no-tax movement from here, which was postponed after the Chauri Chaura incident.

After the revision of land revenue in 1926–27, the Bombay government raised Bardoli's revenue by about **22 per cent**; on some villages the burden was much more than this. (The initial recommendation was about 30 per cent, so different figures are found in the sources.) The farmers said that this increase was unjust amid a slump and weak crops. Their petitions were rejected.

## Patel's Leadership

In January 1928 the farmers called **Vallabhbhai Patel** to lead them. Patel warned the farmers in advance that the struggle would be long and that their land and property might even be seized. On 4 February 1928 he arrived to preside over the farmers' conference, and on **12 February 1928** the farmers resolved that they would not pay the increased revenue until the old rate was accepted or an impartial inquiry was set up.

The whole taluka was divided into several camps, in which hundreds of volunteers worked. A bulletin, meetings and songs became the weapons of the movement. Those who sided with the government were also socially boycotted. **Women** took part in large numbers — women such as Mithubehn Petit, Bhaktiba and Patel's daughter Maniben were active in this struggle.

## Repression and Support

The government took harsh steps — lands were seized and auctioned, even utensils, carts and buffaloes were attached, arrests were made, and Pathan constables were brought in from outside. Even so, the farmers remained non-violent and united; to save their cattle from attachment, many families would lock themselves in their houses along with their animals.

The movement received support from across the country. Some Indian members of the Bombay Legislative Council resigned in sympathy. Gandhi also came to Bardoli. On **12 June 1928**, 'Bardoli Day' was observed across the country.

## The Settlement and Inquiry

Amid growing pressure, in July 1928 the Governor of Bombay called Patel for talks. In early **August 1928** an agreement was reached: an inquiry committee of a judicial officer (Broomfield) and a revenue officer (Maxwell) was set up, the satyagrahi prisoners were released and the seized lands were returned. The inquiry committee held the increase to be wrong and reduced it to about **6 per cent** (Bipan Chandra gives 6.03%; a slightly different figure is found in some sources).

## The Title 'Sardar' and Its Significance

During the movement, or after its success, the women of Bardoli addressed Vallabhbhai as **'Sardar'** — that is, leader or chief — and this name later became his identity. Accounts in the sources differ about the exact occasion on which this title was first given.

The Bardoli Satyagraha showed that farmers organised in the Gandhian way can make even a powerful government bend. It established Patel as a national leader and gave both confidence and experience for the Civil Disobedience Movement that began two years later.
