## A Laboratory on the Malaprabha

**Aihole** is a small village on the banks of the **Malaprabha river** in Bagalkot district of Karnataka. It is about 35 kilometres from Badami and about 10 kilometres from Pattadakal. Within and around the village, over about 5 square kilometres, are scattered **more than 120** temples and cave temples — mostly Hindu, some Jain and one Buddhist.

Most of the surviving monuments here date from the **seventh to the tenth century**, that is, from the time of the Chalukyas of Badami and after them. Scholars view Aihole, Badami and Pattadakal together: here many types of temple — flat-roofed mandapas, North Indian Nagara towers, South Indian Dravida vimanas — were tried out side by side for the first time. For this reason Aihole is often called the **'cradle of Indian temple architecture'**.

## Major Temples

- **Durga Temple:** The most famous temple of Aihole. Its rear end is semicircular (apsidal), recalling the old Buddhist chaitya halls, and it has a pillared verandah all around. It was earlier thought to be of the fifth century, but is now placed between the late sixth and early eighth century.
- **Lad Khan Temple:** A flat-roofed Shiva temple, named after a commander of the Bijapur Sultanate who stayed here for a while. Scholars have also disagreed about its date.
- **Ravana Phadi Cave:** A rock-cut temple of the sixth century, one of the oldest caves at Aihole.
- **Huchchimalli Temple:** An inscription of 708 CE shows that it was in use at that time.
- **Meguti Jain Temple:** A temple built on the hill above the village, where the famous Aihole inscription is found.

## Ravikirti's Inscription (634–635 CE)

On the outer wall of the Meguti temple is a poetic inscription of 19 lines in **Sanskrit**. It was composed by the Jain poet **Ravikirti** in praise of his patron, the Chalukya king of Badami, **Pulakeshin II**. The inscription gives its date in two ways — 3735 years after the Bharata war, and **Shaka year 556** — which equals **634–635 CE**.

It gives the genealogy of the Chalukya dynasty and describes Pulakeshin's victories: over the Kadambas, Gangas, Pallavas and other kings, and most famously the halting of the northern emperor **Harshavardhana** near the Narmada. The conflict with Harsha is also corroborated to some extent by the account of the Chinese traveller Xuanzang, although the exact year of the war is disputed.

## How to Read the Inscription

This inscription is a **prashasti** — court poetry meant to glorify the king. Historians therefore do not take every statement in it literally; some claims do not agree with other sources. Even so, it is the most important source for the political history of seventh-century South India. It is also invaluable for the history of literature, because it names **Kalidasa** and **Bharavi**, which fixes the upper time limit for these poets.

## A City of Merchants

In later centuries Aihole (called 'Ayyavole' in inscriptions) became the centre of a famous merchant guild known as the **'Five Hundred of Ayyavole'**. In the eleventh and twelfth centuries this guild traded not only across South India but overseas, and under its patronage new temples continued to be built here.

## Aihole Today

Today most of Aihole's monuments are under the protection of the Archaeological Survey of India. Nearby **Pattadakal** has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1987. Walking around Aihole, one can see several stages of temple building in a single place — this is what makes it special for students of Indian art.
