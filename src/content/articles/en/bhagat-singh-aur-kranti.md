## Background: Revolutionary Consciousness after the Non-Cooperation Movement

When Gandhi suddenly withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement after the Chauri Chaura incident in 1922, deep discontent spread among the youth of the country. Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan and Chandrashekhar Azad formed the Hindustan Republican Association (HRA), which carried out the historic Kakori train action in 1925.

**Bhagat Singh**, born in Lyallpur (Banga) in Punjab, had visited Jallianwala Bagh at the age of just 12 and, keeping the blood-soaked soil of the martyrs in a bottle, took an oath of revolution. In 1926 he founded the **'Naujawan Bharat Sabha'** to organise the youth.

## September 1928: Feroz Shah Kotla and the Formation of the HSRA

In September 1928 a secret meeting of young revolutionaries was held in the ruins of the historic Feroz Shah Kotla in Delhi.
- On Bhagat Singh's proposal the name of the organisation was changed to the **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)**.
- Its aim was not merely to drive out British rule, but to build an exploitation-free socialist India where farmers, workers and the downtrodden would rule.
- Chandrashekhar Azad became its Commander-in-Chief.

## Avenging the Martyrdom of Lala Lajpat Rai

On 30 October 1928, at a demonstration against the Simon Commission in Lahore, the British Superintendent of Police Scott ordered a brutal lathi charge, in which **Lala Lajpat Rai**, the "Lion of Punjab", was seriously injured and died a few days later.

To avenge this insult to the nation, on 17 December 1928 Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev and Chandrashekhar Azad shot the Assistant Superintendent of Police **Saunders** in front of the Lahore police headquarters.

## 8 April 1929: A Blast to Make the Deaf Hear

When the British government tried to bring two repressive laws — the 'Public Safety Bill' and the 'Trade Disputes Bill' — to curb workers and strikes, the HSRA planned an unprecedented protest:

- On 8 April 1929, **Bhagat Singh** and **Batukeshwar Dutt** threw two low-intensity smoke-producing bombs on the empty benches in the Central Legislative Assembly of Delhi (today's Parliament House).
- Their aim was not to take anyone's life but, in their words, *"to make the deaf hear"*.
- They threw leaflets into the air and did not run away, but voluntarily courted arrest while shouting **'Inquilab Zindabad'**, so that the court could be made a platform for spreading the ideas of revolution.

## The Historic Hunger Strike and the Immortal Martyrdom of 23 March 1931

In Lahore Central Jail, Indian political prisoners were treated like animals. In protest Bhagat Singh and his comrades began a hunger strike:
- The young revolutionary **Jatin Das** gave up his life on 13 September 1929 after a hunger strike of 63 days.
- Through a biased special tribunal, the British court sentenced Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru to death.

At 7:33 in the evening of **23 March 1931**, 11 hours before the fixed time (the morning of 24 March), in defiance of the jail rules, the three immortal sons were secretly hanged. They went to the gallows laughing, and their heroism became immortal in the history of India.
