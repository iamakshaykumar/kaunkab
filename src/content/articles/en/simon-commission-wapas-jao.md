## Background: A Commission That Came Early

The Montagu-Chelmsford reforms of 1919 had provided that a commission would be set up ten years later to review them. But the Conservative government of Britain announced the **Indian Statutory Commission** almost two years early, in November 1927. Its chairman was **Sir John Simon**, so it came to be called the **Simon Commission**. Among its seven members was **Clement Attlee**, who later became Prime Minister of Britain.

The biggest objection was that this commission, which was to decide India's future, had **not a single Indian member**. Indians took it as a direct insult.

## The Decision to Boycott

In December 1927 the **Madras session** of the Congress passed a resolution to boycott the commission "at every step and in every form". The Jinnah faction of the Muslim League, the Hindu Mahasabha and Liberal leaders also supported the boycott. On the other hand, some groups and leaders — such as **Dr. Bhimrao Ambedkar** — presented their views before the commission, because they wanted to put on record the demands of the depressed classes.

## 3 February 1928: "Simon Go Back"

On **3 February 1928** the commission reached **Bombay**. On the same day a **strike** was held across the country. Wherever the commission went — Calcutta, Madras, Lahore, Lucknow, Patna — it was greeted with black flags and the slogan **"Simon Go Back"**. Young people and students took an active part, and through this movement leaders such as Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose became more popular among the youth.

## The Lathis of Lahore and Lucknow

The government suppressed the demonstrations by force in many places. On **30 October 1928** in Lahore, the police rained lathis on **Lala Lajpat Rai**, who was leading a demonstration. He died on **17 November 1928**. Bhagat Singh and his comrades took revenge for this, the details of which are in a separate article on Bhagat Singh.

At the end of November 1928, processions in **Lucknow** were also lathi-charged, in which **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Govind Ballabh Pant** were injured. Questions were also raised in the British Parliament on the Lucknow incident on 3 December 1928.

## The Nehru Report and the Commission's Report

The Secretary of State for India, **Lord Birkenhead**, had taunted that Indians could never frame a constitution together. In reply, the All Parties Conference formed a committee under the chairmanship of **Motilal Nehru**, whose **Nehru Report** (August 1928) demanded dominion status and fundamental rights for India.

The commission's own report was published in 1930 (the month is May in some sources and June in others). It recommended ending dyarchy in the provinces and establishing representative government, but made no mention of dominion status.

## Significance

The boycott of the Simon Commission breathed new life into the national movement, which had cooled after the Non-Cooperation Movement. In the midst of this upsurge the goal of Purna Swaraj was declared at the Lahore session of 1929 and the Civil Disobedience Movement began in 1930. The commission's report, together with the Round Table Conferences, later became one of the bases of the **Government of India Act 1935**.
