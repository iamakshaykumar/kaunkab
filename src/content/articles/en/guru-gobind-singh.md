## Childhood in Patna

Guru Gobind Singh was born on **22 December 1666** in Patna (some sources give a different date or year). His childhood name was **Gobind Das** or Gobind Rai. His father **Guru Tegh Bahadur** was then travelling in eastern India, so the early years were spent in Patna with his mother **Gujri**. At about the age of five he came to **Anandpur** (Chakk Nanaki) in Punjab, where he was taught Persian, Sanskrit, Gurmukhi and the art of arms.

## The Nine-Year-Old Guru

In **1675**, on the order of the Mughal emperor **Aurangzeb**, Guru Tegh Bahadur was put to death in Delhi. Nine-year-old Gobind Rai became the **tenth Guru** of the Sikhs. In the next decade he developed Anandpur as a court and military centre — he asked Sikhs for arms and horses as offerings and inspired the congregation to practise arms, as his grandfather Guru Hargobind had done.

## Paonta and the Hill Rajas

The rulers of the Shivalik states around Anandpur — especially Bhim Chand of Kahlur (Bilaspur) — were uneasy about the Guru's growing power. In 1685, at the invitation of the Nahan state, the Guru moved to **Paonta** on the bank of the Yamuna. Here he paid much attention to literary composition and many poets stayed at his court. In 1688, in the battle of **Bhangani**, he drove back Fateh Shah of Garhwal and the allied hill rajas; its description is found in his autobiographical work **Bachittar Natak**. After this he returned to Anandpur, and in 1691 in the battle of **Nadaun** he joined the hill rajas in facing the Mughal commander Alif Khan.

The founding of the **Khalsa** at Anandpur on Baisakhi of 1699 was the decisive turning point of this long struggle (see: The Founding of the Khalsa Panth).

## The Siege of Anandpur and the Martyrdom of the Family

After the Khalsa, clashes with the hill rajas and the Mughal faujdars became sharper. In 1704 there was a long siege of Anandpur. On a promise of safe passage the Guru left Anandpur in **December 1704**, but on the way he was attacked and the family got separated.

- In the battle of **Chamkaur** the elder sons **Ajit Singh** and **Jujhar Singh** were martyred fighting with a handful of companions.
- The younger sons **Zorawar Singh** and **Fateh Singh** were captured along with their grandmother Mata Gujri and were killed at Sirhind on the order of Wazir Khan around 26–27 December 1704; Mata Gujri also died there.

In 1705, in the battle of **Khidrana** (today Muktsar), the forty Sikhs whom tradition calls the 'Chali Mukte' gave their lives.

## Damdama Sahib, the Zafarnama and the Dasam Granth

After this the Guru stayed around 1705–1706 at **Talwandi Sabo** in Malwa, which is today called **Damdama Sahib**. According to Sikh tradition, it was here, with the help of Bhai Mani Singh, that the final recension of the Adi Granth was prepared, to which the hymns of Guru Tegh Bahadur were added. In this period he wrote the **Zafarnama** to Aurangzeb in Persian, in which the emperor is rebuked for breaking his promises.

Among the Guru's works, **Jaap Sahib**, **Chandi di Var** and **Bachittar Natak** are prominent. The collection of these and other works is called the **Dasam Granth**. Whether all the works of the Dasam Granth are by the Guru himself or also by his court poets is still a matter of dispute among scholars.

## The Deccan and Nanded

In the war of succession after Aurangzeb's death in 1707 the Guru sided with Prince Muazzam (**Bahadur Shah**). After meeting Bahadur Shah he went with his camp to the Deccan and reached **Nanded** on the bank of the Godavari. Here he met the Bairagi Madho Das, who was later sent to Punjab under the name **Banda Singh Bahadur**.

At Nanded two Pathans — Jamshed Khan and Wasil Beg — attacked the Guru with a dagger. It is commonly believed that they had been sent by Wazir Khan, though some historians doubt this. Before he could recover from the wound he died on **7 October 1708**. According to Sikh tradition, in his last moments he ended the line of personal Gurus and declared the **Guru Granth Sahib** itself to be the eternal Guru.
