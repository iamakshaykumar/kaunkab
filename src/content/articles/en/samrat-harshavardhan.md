## Background: The Fragmentation of Post-Gupta India

After the fall of the Gupta Empire in the mid-6th century and the invasions of the Huns, northern India was divided into many small kingdoms. Among them, Thanesar in Haryana (the Pushyabhuti dynasty) and Kannauj in Uttar Pradesh (the Maukhari dynasty) were prominent.

At just 16 years of age, Harshavardhana (606 CE) ascended the throne of Thanesar. He faced a grave crisis: Shashanka, the ruler of Malwa and Bengal (Gauda), had murdered his brother-in-law Grahavarman, king of Kannauj, and had treacherously killed Harsha's elder brother Rajyavardhana. Harsha boldly freed Kannauj, rescued his sister Rajyashri, and, uniting the two kingdoms, made **Kannauj (Mahodaya Nagar)** the capital of his vast empire.

## Military Conquests and the Battle on the Narmada

Harsha extended his power to Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Bengal and Odisha. He came to be called 'Sakalottarapathanatha' (lord of all northern India).

When he crossed the Vindhyas and launched a campaign towards south India, on the bank of the Narmada river he faced the mighty Chalukya king of Badami, **Pulakeshin II**. According to the Aihole inscription, Pulakeshin stopped Harsha's army, after which the Narmada became the permanent boundary between the two empires.

## Patronage of Learning, Art and Nalanda

Harsha's court included scholars such as the great Sanskrit prose writer **Banabhatta** (author of the Harshacharita and Kadambari), Mayura Bhatta and Matanga Divakara.

Harsha generously patronised the world-famous **Nalanda University**. He had donated the revenue of more than 100 villages for the upkeep of Nalanda. Under the guidance of its head, Shilabhadra, Xuanzang studied Buddhist philosophy here for years.

## Xuanzang's Account and the Assembly at Prayag

The Chinese pilgrim **Xuanzang**, who stayed in India from 630 CE to 645 CE, praised Harsha's administration, the life of the people and the system of justice at length. He wrote that the emperor worked day and night for the welfare of his subjects.

A huge assembly of all religions was convened at Kannauj, and at the assembly held every fifth year at the Sangam of Prayag, the emperor gave away everything he had.

## Historical Significance

Emperor Harshavardhana was among the last great emperors of ancient India who brought a vast region under central rule. After him, the 'Tripartite Struggle' (Palas, Pratiharas and Rashtrakutas) for control of northern India began, which ushered in the medieval period.
