## Who was Bakhtiyar Khalji

**Ikhtiyar-ud-din Muhammad bin Bakhtiyar Khalji** (also written Khaljī) belonged to the Khalj tribe of the Garmsir region in present-day southern Afghanistan. According to Minhaj-us-Siraj, he first failed to find employment in Delhi; then, serving officers in Badaun and Awadh, he received small land grants (iqta) across the Ganga in eastern Uttar Pradesh. From here he began to raid eastward.

This was the period when, after the Second Battle of Tarain in 1192, **Muhammad of Ghor** and his commander **Qutb-ud-din Aibak** were extending Ghurid power in north India. Bakhtiyar was an ambitious, almost independent commander within this system.

## The Raids on Bihar and Odantapuri

After the end of Pala power, Bihar was at that time fragmented. The date of Bakhtiyar's raids is not certain — traditionally it is given as **c. 1193**, but scholars also offer various other dates between 1197 and 1206.

The clearest account is in Minhaj's *Tabaqat-i-Nasiri* (c. 1260). He writes that Bakhtiyar's troop attacked a "fort", where most of the shaven-headed inhabitants were killed. Afterwards many books were found there, but no one was left alive to read them; then it became clear that it was a school, which the locals called a "vihar". Most historians identify it with the **Odantapuri Mahavihara** in present-day Bihar Sharif.

## Nalanda and Vikramashila: What the Sources Say

It is often said that Bakhtiyar burned down **Nalanda**. But Minhaj, the contemporary Persian source, does not name Nalanda — his description fits Odantapuri better. The 17th-century Tibetan historian **Taranatha** mentions the destruction of Odantapuri and **Vikramashila** by "Turushka" armies, but he was writing about four centuries after the event.

The most valuable testimony is that of the Tibetan monk **Dharmasvamin**, who came to Bihar around **1234–36**. He found Nalanda badly damaged and nearly deserted, yet the teacher **Rahula Shribhadra**, over ninety years old, was still teaching about seventy monks. He also wrote that Odantapuri had by then become a military cantonment. Archaeologists have also found a layer of burning at Nalanda, which is dated to around 1200.

The balanced view today, therefore, is that the Turkish raids of the early 13th century dealt a fatal blow to the Buddhist viharas of Bihar, but the end of Nalanda was not the event of a single day. Historians such as Satish Chandra hold these raids directly responsible, while some scholars such as D. N. Jha question the evidence for Bakhtiyar's attack on Nalanda. The decline of royal patronage and the shrinking of Buddhism in India were also causes of this decline.

## The Sudden Raid on Nadia and Lakhnauti

After Bihar, Bakhtiyar turned toward the **Sena** kingdom of Bengal. According to Minhaj's famous story, he advanced so fast that when he reached the capital **Nadia** (Navadvipa) he had only eighteen horsemen with him, whom people took for horse traders. The aged king **Lakshmana Sena** left the palace and went to eastern Bengal, where the Sena dynasty continued for a few more decades. This event is dated to about **1204**, although some historians place it in 1202–03.

Bakhtiyar made **Lakhnauti** (Gauda), rather than Nadia, the centre of his power. He built mosques, madrasas and khanqahs, and had coins struck in the name of his master Muhammad of Ghor. Turkish rule in Bengal began from here, and later it grew into the Bengal Sultanate.

## The Tibet Expedition and the End

In **1206** Bakhtiyar launched a major expedition across the Himalayas toward Tibet. Guerrilla attacks by local forces in the mountains, difficult routes and shortage of supplies nearly destroyed his army; according to Minhaj, only a handful of soldiers managed to return to Devkot (near present-day South Dinajpur, West Bengal).

The sick and despairing Bakhtiyar was murdered in the same year, **1206**, by his own chief, **Ali Mardan Khalji**. History remembers him as the founder of Turkish rule in Bengal — and also as the era in which the long tradition of India's great Buddhist mahaviharas was broken.
