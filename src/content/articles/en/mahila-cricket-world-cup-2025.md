## A Long Wait

The Indian women's cricket team first took part in the Women's World Cup in 1978. For decades after that the team gave many good performances, but the title eluded it. In **2005**, under the captaincy of **Mithali Raj**, the team lost the final to Australia, and in **2017** it lost the final at Lord's to England by just 9 runs. That 2017 tournament greatly increased the popularity of women's cricket in India and prepared a new generation of players.

## The 2025 World Cup

The **ICC Women's Cricket World Cup 2025** was held in **India** from **30 September to 2 November 2025**, while Pakistan's matches were played in Colombo, **Sri Lanka**. Eight teams took part in a round-robin format. India's captain was **Harmanpreet Kaur** and the head coach was the former domestic stalwart **Amol Muzumdar**.

India began well by beating Sri Lanka and Pakistan, but then lost three matches in a row to South Africa, Australia and England. To stay in the race for the semi-finals a win against New Zealand was essential, and in that match India recorded a big win with the help of centuries from **Smriti Mandhana** and **Pratika Rawal**. The last league match against Bangladesh could not be completed because of rain, and in that same match Pratika Rawal suffered a serious ankle injury while fielding. **Shafali Verma** was called into the team in her place.

## A Historic Win over Australia

On **30 October 2025**, in the second semi-final in Navi Mumbai, India faced seven-time champions **Australia**, who had been unbeaten in 15 consecutive World Cup matches. Australia scored **338**. In reply India lost early wickets quickly, but **Jemimah Rodrigues** (unbeaten **127**) and captain **Harmanpreet Kaur** (89) turned the match with a partnership of 167 runs. India won by 5 wickets with 9 balls to spare. It was the successful chase of the highest target in the history of women's One Day International cricket. After the match an emotional Jemimah expressed her gratitude for the support she had received during a difficult period.

## The Final — Navi Mumbai, 2 November

The final was played on **2 November 2025** at the **D. Y. Patil Stadium** against **South Africa**, who had reached a Women's World Cup final for the first time. The match started late because of rain. South Africa's captain **Laura Wolvaardt** won the toss and chose to bowl first.

India made **298 for 7** in 50 overs. **Shafali Verma** scored **87** off 78 balls, **Deepti Sharma** contributed **58** and Smriti Mandhana 45. In reply Laura Wolvaardt scored a brilliant **101** — she became the first captain to score a century in a Women's World Cup final. But wickets kept falling at the other end. Shafali also took two important wickets with the ball, and **Deepti Sharma** turned the match India's way by taking **5 wickets for 39 runs**. South Africa were all out for **246** in 45.3 overs.

By winning by **52 runs**, India won the Women's One Day World Cup **for the first time**. **Shafali Verma** was chosen 'Player of the Match', and **Deepti Sharma**, with 215 runs and 22 wickets, 'Player of the Tournament'.

## Significance and Legacy

This win is regarded as the greatest moment in the history of Indian women's cricket. Thousands of spectators in the stadium around midnight and tens of millions of people on TV and the internet watched the match. After the win, the team lifted the trophy together with former captains and players such as **Mithali Raj**, **Jhulan Goswami** and Anjum Chopra, honouring the contribution of earlier generations. The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) announced a reward of 510 million rupees for the team and support staff, and several state governments also honoured their players. On **5 November** Prime Minister Narendra Modi met the team in New Delhi.

Many experts compared this win with Kapil Dev's team winning the World Cup in **1983**, which changed the course of men's cricket in India. Coming after steps such as equal match fees (2022) and the Women's Premier League (2023), this win is expected to encourage more girls across the country to take up cricket as a serious career.
