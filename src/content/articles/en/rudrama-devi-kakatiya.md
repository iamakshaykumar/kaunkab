## Background of the Kakatiya Dynasty

The **Kakatiyas** were at first feudatories of the Chalukyas of Kalyani. In the twelfth century they became independent and began to rule over large parts of present-day Telangana and Andhra Pradesh. Their capital was **Orugallu**, which is today called **Warangal**.

During the long reign of King **Ganapatideva** (reign about 1199–1262 CE) the kingdom expanded greatly. He promoted tanks and irrigation, and for sea trade he had an inscription set up at the port of **Motupalli** assuring merchants of protection. The famous **Ramappa Temple** (construction begun in 1213 CE) was also built in his time by his general Recherla Rudra, and it became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021.

## A Daughter Becomes Heir

Ganapatideva had no son. So he chose his daughter **Rudrama Devi** as his successor and made her a partner in the rule during his own lifetime. From about 1262–63 CE Rudrama Devi ruled independently.

In that age it was unusual for a woman to sit on the throne. In inscriptions she is also addressed by a male royal name such as "Rudradeva Maharaja". She was married to the Eastern Chalukya prince **Virabhadra**.

## Challenges and Wars

Rudrama Devi's rule was not easy:
- Some feudal lords hesitated to accept a woman ruler and rose in revolt.
- The **Yadava** king Mahadeva of Devagiri attacked the Kakatiya kingdom, but had to retreat.
- In the south, the Kayastha chief **Ambadeva** rebelled in the 1270s–80s and took over the southern part of the kingdom.

With the help of loyal generals and feudal lords, Rudrama Devi kept a large part of the kingdom safe. She gave responsibilities as nayakas to warriors who came from different castes and communities, which gave new people a chance to come forward in the army and administration. The large tanks built in the Kakatiya period — such as the Ramappa and Pakhal lakes — were the basis of irrigation for farming, and their upkeep was a priority of the state. According to tradition, the work of strengthening Warangal Fort also advanced in her time.

## Marco Polo's Testimony

The Italian traveller **Marco Polo** passed along the eastern coast of India in the last years of the thirteenth century. He described the kingdom of "Mutfili" (Motupalli), where a queen ruled. He praised the queen's justice and her popularity among her subjects, and also mentioned the diamond mines there and the fine cotton cloth. Some details of his account — such as the length of the reign — are not entirely accurate, yet it is an important testimony by a foreigner.

## Death and Legacy

An inscription of November 1289 found at **Chandupatla** (Nalgonda region) shows that Rudrama Devi and her general Mallikarjuna had died a short time before. Many historians believe that she died in the struggle against Ambadeva, but the inscription itself does not give the cause.

After her, her grandson **Prataparudra** became king. In 1323 CE the army of the Delhi Sultanate took Warangal and the Kakatiya kingdom came to an end. Rudrama Devi is remembered today in the memory of the Telugu region as a courageous and capable ruler.
