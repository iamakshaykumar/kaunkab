## Dhanpat Rai of Lamhi

Premchand was born on **31 July 1880** in the village of **Lamhi** near Banaras (Varanasi). His real name was **Dhanpat Rai Srivastava**. His father **Ajaib Lal** was a clerk in the post office. His mother died when he was a child, and when he was fifteen or sixteen his father also passed away. The responsibility for the household fell on his shoulders early, so alongside his studies he earned a living by giving tuition.

His early education was in **Urdu and Persian** at a madrasa. As a teenager he was passionate about tales and stories, especially epics such as 'Tilism-e-Hoshruba'. After matriculation he became a teacher in a government school and later rose to the post of **Deputy Inspector of Schools** in the education department. His second wife, **Shivrani Devi**, was herself a writer, and later wrote her memories of him under the title 'Premchand Ghar Mein' (Premchand at Home).

## From Nawab Rai to Premchand

Dhanpat Rai began writing in **Urdu**, at first under the pen name **'Nawab Rai'**. In **1907** his story collection **'Soz-e-Watan'** (The Lament of the Nation) was published, containing stories of patriotism. British officials considered it seditious, had its copies seized and burned, and the author was warned. Then, on the advice of **Daya Narain Nigam**, editor of the Urdu magazine **'Zamana'** published from Kanpur, he adopted the new name **'Premchand'**. Out of respect, people began to call him **'Munshi Premchand'**.

Gradually he also began writing in **Hindi** and became a favourite of readers in both languages. His language is often called **'Hindustani'** — simple and colloquial, with words from both Hindi and Urdu blended naturally.

## Gandhi's Influence and Literary Life

In **1921**, during the **Non-Cooperation Movement**, Gandhi called on people to leave government jobs. Despite family difficulties, Premchand **resigned** from his government post and devoted himself entirely to writing and publishing. In **1923** he set up the **Saraswati Press** in Banaras. In **1930** he launched the literary magazine **'Hans'** and also edited the weekly **'Jagaran'**. Both magazines ran at a loss, but he kept them going as platforms for ideas. In between he also edited magazines such as 'Madhuri' and 'Maryada'.

In **1934**, because of financial hardship, he went to **Bombay** to write for films. The film he wrote, **'Mazdoor'**, was about the exploitation of workers. But he did not like the commercial atmosphere of the film industry, and in **1935** he returned to Banaras.

## Major Works

Premchand wrote **more than three hundred stories**, about **fourteen novels**, and many essays, plays and translations. His stories were later collected in eight volumes under the title **'Mansarovar'**.

- **Novels** — 'Sevasadan', 'Premashram', 'Rangbhoomi', 'Nirmala', 'Gaban', 'Karmabhoomi' and **'Godaan' (1936)**. The story of **Hori**, the peasant of 'Godaan', with his small wish to own a cow and his life sinking into debt, is called the epic of the Indian peasant. His last novel, **'Mangalsutra'**, remained unfinished.
- **Stories** — **'Idgah'** (Hamid and his grandmother's tongs), **'Kafan'**, **'Poos ki Raat'**, **'Panch Parmeshwar'**, **'Bade Ghar ki Beti'**, **'Namak ka Daroga'**, **'Shatranj ke Khiladi'** and 'Boodhi Kaki', among others.

The main themes of his writing were the **exploitation of peasants** and landlordism, moneylenders' debt, **dowry** and mismatched marriages, the condition of widows, untouchability, and the national movement. He took literature beyond entertainment and made it a mirror of society. For this reason he is also called **'Upanyas Samrat'** (Emperor of the Novel) in Hindi.

## Final Years and Legacy

In **1936** Premchand presided over the first session of the **Progressive Writers' Association** held in **Lucknow**. In his famous speech he said that literature should not be mere entertainment but a criticism of the realities of life and a torch that leads society forward. That same year his health worsened through illness, and on **8 October 1936** he died in Banaras at the age of **56**.

Films were made on many of Premchand's works — **Satyajit Ray** made 'Shatranj Ke Khilari' (1977) and 'Sadgati' (1981), and Hindi films were also made on 'Godaan' and 'Gaban'. His stories are still part of school textbooks and have been translated into many languages. His ancestral house in Lamhi is preserved as a memorial, where events are held every year on his birth anniversary, **31 July**.

Premchand took Hindi-Urdu fiction out of the world of kings, queens and magic and brought it to the village square, the fields and threshing floors, and the homes of ordinary people. That is his greatest contribution.
