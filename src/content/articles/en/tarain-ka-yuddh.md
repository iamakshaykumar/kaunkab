## Background: Pressure from the North-West

In the latter half of the 12th century, Mu'izz al-Din Muhammad bin Sam (Muhammad of Ghor), who had risen from the Ghor province of Afghanistan, began to expand towards India. After taking control of Multan, Sindh and Punjab (Lahore), his borders began to collide with the kingdom of the mighty Chahamana (Chauhan) ruler of Delhi and Ajmer, **Prithviraj III (Prithviraj Chauhan)**.

After Ghor's capture of the border fort of Bhatinda (Tabarhind), a clash between the two armies became inevitable.

## The First Battle of Tarain (1191 CE)

In 1191 CE the two armies met on the field of Tarain near Karnal (Haryana).

- The traditional courage of the Rajput army and the powerful charge of the elephants broke both wings of Ghor's army.
- Govind Rai, the governor of Delhi, struck Ghor with his lance and wounded him seriously.
- Panic broke out in Ghor's army and he escaped with his life.
- Prithviraj did not pursue the fleeing army, which later proved to be a grave strategic mistake.

## The Second Battle of Tarain (1192 CE)

To avenge the defeat, Ghor made intense military preparations for a year. In 1192 CE he returned to Tarain with 120,000 well-trained horsemen and archers.

- **Strategy:** Ghor divided his army into four divisions. His horse archers would shower arrows on the Rajput lines and then withdraw. This trick (a feigned retreat) broke up the Rajput battle formation.
- Because of the sudden attack in the morning and its rapid speed, the Rajput army was forced onto the defensive.
- After heavy fighting, Prithviraj was defeated and captured.

## Historical Consequences

The Second Battle of Tarain is regarded as a decisive turning point (watershed moment) in the history of India:
1. The centuries-old political supremacy of the Rajputs in northern India came to an end.
2. Ghor's general Qutb al-Din Aibak took control of Delhi, Meerut and Aligarh, which led to the establishment of the **Delhi Sultanate** in 1206 CE.
3. New administrative, architectural (Indo-Islamic style) and military ideas entered.
