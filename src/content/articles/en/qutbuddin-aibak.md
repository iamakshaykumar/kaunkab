## From the Market of Nishapur to Ghor's Court

**Qutb-ud-din Aibak** was born around **1150** in a Turkic family in Turkestan. He was sold as a slave in childhood. The qazi of **Nishapur** (in present-day Iran), Fakhr-ud-din Abdul Aziz Kufi, bought him, and there he learned archery and horse riding along with his studies. After the qazi's death he was sold again and finally reached **Muhammad of Ghor** at Ghazni.

Through his loyalty and ability he rose quickly and became **Amir-i-Akhur** — the head of the royal stables. This post carried responsibility for horses, fodder and equipment, which was very important for the Ghurid army, which depended on cavalry.

## Ghor's Commander in India

After the Second Battle of Tarain in **1192**, Muhammad of Ghor entrusted Aibak with the territories he had won in India. In the following years he led several campaigns:
- **Delhi:** In 1192 he kept the Tomar ruler here as a subordinate; in 1193 he removed him and began direct rule.
- **Ajmer (1194):** He suppressed the revolt of Hariraja, brother of Prithviraj.
- **Koil (Aligarh) and Chandawar (1194):** At Chandawar he led the vanguard of Ghor's army against Jaichand.
- **Gujarat (1197):** He defeated the Chalukyas near Abu and plundered Anhilwara, but the territory was not conquered permanently.
- **Kalinjar (1202):** A campaign against the Chandela king Paramardideva, after which Kalinjar, Mahoba and Khajuraho came under Ghurid control.

## 1206: Taking the Reins of Power

Muhammad of Ghor was assassinated on **15 March 1206**. He had no surviving son. Among his slave commanders in India, Aibak was the most powerful. In **June 1206** he informally took power at **Lahore**. Later Ghiyath-ud-din Mahmud, a successor of the Ghurid house, accepted him as ruler of India, and according to some accounts he also received a letter of manumission — though its date and authenticity are uncertain.

Aibak's rule was not stable. **Taj al-Din Yildiz** of Ghazni (who was also his father-in-law) and **Nasir-ud-din Qabacha** of Multan-Uch (his son-in-law) also claimed the Ghurid inheritance. Around 1208 Aibak removed Yildiz and occupied **Ghazni** for a short time, but then withdrew and returned to Lahore. He spent most of his four years of rule at Lahore in these struggles. Because of Aibak and the later sultans of slave origin, this dynasty is called the 'Slave dynasty' or 'Mamluk dynasty'.

## Architecture: The Qutb Minar and the Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra

From Aibak's time Islamic architecture began in north India:
- In Delhi the **Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque** was built. According to the mosque's own inscription, materials from 27 temples were used in it.
- Around **1199** construction of the **Qutb Minar** began; in Aibak's time only the first storey was built, and Iltutmish had the rest completed.
- The **Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra** mosque at **Ajmer** (c. 1192–1199) was also built in his time.

## Death and Assessment

In **November 1210**, while playing **chaugan** (a polo-like game played on horseback) at Lahore, Aibak fell from his horse; the raised part of the saddle pierced his ribs and he died. He was buried at Lahore. After him **Aram Shah** sat on the throne, but soon Aibak's son-in-law **Iltutmish** took power in 1211.

The historian Minhaj-i-Siraj, who wrote some decades later, describes Aibak as generous, brave and just, and says that because he distributed lakhs in charity he was called **'Lakh-Bakhsh'**. These praises come from court historians, so they should be read with caution. Aibak began turning the Ghurid territories in India into an independent state, but it was his successor Iltutmish who gave it the form of a firm sultanate.
