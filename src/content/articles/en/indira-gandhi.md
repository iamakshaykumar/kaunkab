## Childhood in the Nehru Family

**Indira Priyadarshini** was born on **19 November 1917** in **Allahabad** (today Prayagraj). She was the only child of **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Kamala Nehru**. Her childhood passed in the atmosphere of the freedom movement, when the elders of the family were often in jail. She studied for a time at **Santiniketan** (Visva-Bharati) and in 1937 joined Somerville College, **Oxford**, but could not complete her studies because of illness.

In **March 1942** she married **Feroze Gandhi**, and in September that year she was arrested for taking part in the **Quit India Movement**. They had two sons — **Rajiv** and **Sanjay**. After independence she lived with her father, Prime Minister Nehru, as his hostess and assistant, and in 1959 she also became Congress President.

## Rise to the Prime Ministership

After Nehru's death in 1964 she became **Minister of Information and Broadcasting** in the government of **Lal Bahadur Shastri**. After Shastri's sudden death in Tashkent in January 1966, she defeated **Morarji Desai** in the Congress parliamentary party and on **24 January 1966** became India's **first woman Prime Minister**.

At first many senior leaders thought her weak, but she soon strengthened her hold. In **1969**, differences over the presidential election and policies led to a split in the Congress. In the same year, on **19 July 1969**, her government carried out the **nationalisation of 14 major commercial banks**, which rapidly spread bank branches to villages and small towns. She also took steps towards abolishing the **privy purses** (private allowances) of the princes.

## 'Garibi Hatao' and 1971

In the 1971 general election her slogan **"Garibi Hatao"** (Remove Poverty) became very popular and she won a big victory. That same year, because of the crisis in East Pakistan, about ten million refugees came to India. In August 1971 India signed a **Treaty of Friendship** with the Soviet Union, and after India's decisive victory in the December 1971 war, the Pakistani army surrendered in Dhaka on **16 December** and **Bangladesh** was born. This was followed by the **Simla Agreement** in **1972**. She received the **Bharat Ratna** in **1971**.

During her tenure the **Green Revolution** advanced, India's first nuclear test took place at **Pokhran** on **18 May 1974**, and in **1975**, after a referendum, **Sikkim** became a state of India.

## The Emergency (1975–1977)

On **12 June 1975** the **Allahabad High Court**, on a petition by **Raj Narain**, set aside her 1971 Lok Sabha election on grounds of electoral malpractice. The country was already facing inflation and a movement led by Jayaprakash Narayan. On **25 June 1975** President **Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed**, on the advice of the government, declared an **Emergency** on the ground of "internal disturbance".

During this period of about 21 months, civil liberties were suspended, the press was censored, and more than a hundred thousand opposition leaders, journalists and activists were jailed. Programmes such as forced sterilisation were also sharply criticised. Supporters argued that it brought discipline and control over inflation, but most historians regard it as the most difficult period of Indian democracy. In the **1977** election the Congress was badly defeated, and Indira Gandhi herself lost to Raj Narain from **Rae Bareli**. The Emergency was lifted on **21 March 1977**.

## Return and Final Years

Amid the infighting of the Janata Party government, she returned with a huge majority in the **1980** election and became Prime Minister again on **14 January 1980**. In June that year her younger son **Sanjay Gandhi** died in an air crash. In 1983 she also became chair of the **Non-Aligned Movement**.

Amid rising militancy in Punjab, in **June 1984** she ordered the army to carry out **Operation Blue Star** to remove militants from the Golden Temple complex in Amritsar. The action caused heavy loss of life and deep anger among the Sikh community.

On **31 October 1984**, at her Prime Minister's residence in New Delhi, two of her own bodyguards, **Beant Singh** and **Satwant Singh**, shot and killed her; it was described as revenge for Operation Blue Star. The same day **Rajiv Gandhi** became Prime Minister. After her assassination, **anti-Sikh riots** broke out in Delhi and other parts of the country, in which thousands of innocent people were killed. On **3 November** she was cremated near Raj Ghat; that spot is today called **Shakti Sthal**.

## Legacy

Indira Gandhi's assessment remains divided even today. Supporters regard her as a resolute leader who strengthened India through the 1971 victory, bank nationalisation, the Green Revolution and the country's strategic power. Critics see the centralisation of power, the weakening of democratic institutions and the Emergency as her greatest mistakes. Both sides agree that her imprint on the politics of modern India is very deep.
