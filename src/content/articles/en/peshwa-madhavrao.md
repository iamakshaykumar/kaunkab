## The Vacuum after Panipat

On **14 January 1761**, in the Third Battle of Panipat, the Maratha army was badly defeated by Ahmad Shah Abdali. Sadashivrao Bhau and the Peshwa's eldest son Vishwasrao were killed. The Peshwa **Balaji Bajirao** (Nanasaheb), broken by this news, died in June of the same year. The Maratha state faced a crisis of leadership.

His second son **Madhavrao** had been born in February 1745 at Savanur. In 1761, at the age of sixteen, the Chhatrapati of Satara gave him the post of Peshwa. At first his uncle **Raghunathrao** (Raghoba) ran the government as regent.

## The Struggle with His Uncle

Raghunathrao was an experienced commander, but wanted power for himself. As the young Peshwa began to take independent decisions, the clash between them grew. In 1762, in the battle of **Alegaon**, Madhavrao had to accept defeat by his uncle and for a time surrendered. But amid the external dangers, Madhavrao gradually built up his control over the administration and the army. When Raghunathrao rebelled again in 1768, he was captured and kept under house arrest in Pune.

## The Nizam and Hyder Ali

After Panipat, **Nizam Ali Khan** of Hyderabad took advantage of Maratha weakness, advanced as far as Pune and plundered parts of the city. In August 1763, in the battle of **Rakshasbhuvan** on the bank of the Godavari, the Marathas defeated the Nizam's army — this is considered Madhavrao's first great victory. In 1766 a settlement was made between the two sides.

In the south **Hyder Ali** of Mysore was rising rapidly and pressing on the Maratha territories between the Krishna and the Tungabhadra. Madhavrao led several campaigns against him in 1764–65, 1767 and 1770–72. Forts such as Dharwad were retaken, and after many battles Hyder Ali had to make a treaty and give up territory and pay a war indemnity. Many of these campaigns were led by Madhavrao himself.

## The Return to the North

Ten years after Panipat the Marathas returned to the politics of North India. The commanders sent by Madhavrao — Visaji Krishna Binivale, Ramchandra Ganesh and **Mahadji Shinde** — carried out campaigns against the Jats and the Rohillas and in 1771 established control over Delhi. The Mughal emperor **Shah Alam II**, who had been living at Allahabad under British protection, returned to Delhi under Maratha protection and entered the capital in January 1772.

## Administration

Madhavrao is remembered not only as a commander but also as a skilled administrator. He made efforts to bring discipline into the revenue system, to control corrupt officials and to free the treasury from debt. In 1767 he rejected the British proposal that sought a military presence near Vasai and Salsette. **Ramshastri Prabhune**, a scholar famous for his sense of justice, was the judge of Pune in his time.

## Untimely Death and Impact

By the age of twenty-seven Madhavrao had become seriously ill with tuberculosis. On **18 November 1772** he died near the Chintamani Ganapati temple at **Theur** near Pune. After him his younger brother **Narayanrao** became Peshwa, and was murdered in 1773. After this the struggle between Raghunathrao and the 'Barbhai' faction led by Nana Phadnavis gave the British an opportunity to interfere in Maratha politics, and the First Anglo-Maratha War broke out.

Historians believe that his short rule proved decisive for Maratha power after Panipat, and that his untimely death left that revival incomplete.
