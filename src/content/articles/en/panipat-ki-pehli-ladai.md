## Background

At the beginning of the 16th century Delhi was ruled by the Lodi sultan **Ibrahim Lodi**. Many of his nobles were angry with him. Meanwhile **Babur**, the ruler of Farghana in Central Asia, had lost his kingdom and was looking toward India from Kabul. He was a descendant of both Timur and Genghis Khan.

The invitation of some discontented nobles, such as Daulat Khan Lodi, the governor of Punjab, also gave Babur the opportunity to come to India.

## The Battle

On **21 April 1526** the two armies faced each other on the field of Panipat in Haryana. Ibrahim Lodi's army was far larger in number and had elephants too. But Babur had two great weapons:

- **Artillery and guns** — such effective use of gunpowder was new in the open battles of North India. Babur's gunners were Ustad Ali Quli and Mustafa.
- **The Tulughma battle strategy** — dividing the army into detachments and surrounding the enemy from both sides. Cannon were placed behind a row of carts in front.

At the sound of the cannon the elephants of the Lodi army panicked and confusion spread in the army. Within a few hours the battle was decided. **Ibrahim Lodi was killed in the battle** — he is one of the few sultans of Delhi who died on the battlefield.

## Result

- The Delhi Sultanate came to an end.
- Babur took Delhi and Agra and founded the **Mughal Empire**.
- The next year, in 1527, Babur also defeated **Rana Sanga** of Mewar in the Battle of Khanwa.

## Significance

The First Battle of Panipat showed that gunpowder and a new battle strategy could change the nature of war. From this victory began the empire that ruled a large part of India in the times of Akbar, Shah Jahan and Aurangzeb. Decisive battles were later fought on the field of Panipat in 1556 and 1761 as well.
