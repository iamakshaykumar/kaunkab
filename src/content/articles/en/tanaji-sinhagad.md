## Why Kondhana Mattered

The fort built on a high peak of the Sahyadri about **28 kilometres** south-west of Pune was earlier called **Kondhana**. Standing at a height of about **1,317 metres**, it overlooked the whole Pune region, so whoever held Kondhana had a firm grip on Pune.

In **1665**, under the **Treaty of Purandar**, Shivaji Maharaj had to hand over 23 of his forts to the Mughals, and Kondhana was one of them. After returning from Agra in 1666, Shivaji gradually gathered his strength again and in early **1670** began a campaign to recover the lost forts. Kondhana became the first major target of this campaign.

## Who Was Tanaji Malusare

**Tanaji Malusare** was an old and trusted companion of Shivaji, holding the rank of **subedar**. According to Wikipedia he was born in the village of **Godavali** in the Mahabaleshwar area of Satara district, though solid documents about his birthplace and family are scarce. He had taken part in campaigns such as Torna, Javali and Pratapgad. Some historians, such as David Hardiman, link him to the **Koli** community; different claims about his caste are found.

## The Night of 4 February 1670

On the Mughal side, the fort was defended by the Rajput chieftain **Udaybhan Rathod**. Attacking through the front gates was almost impossible, so Tanaji, with his Mavala soldiers, chose to climb the sheer cliff of the fort **in the darkness of night**. The soldiers reached the top with the help of **rope ladders**, and then a fierce face-to-face fight began inside the fort.

Folklore mentions a **ghorpad** (monitor lizard) named "Yashwanti" — it is said that a rope was tied around its waist and it was made to climb the cliff. This story is very popular, but it is not supported by contemporary documents and should be seen only as tradition. Sources also give different figures for the number of soldiers.

During the fight Tanaji and Udaybhan came face to face. According to most accounts both died in this fight, though stories differ on exactly who killed Udaybhan — Tanaji or his maternal uncle **Shelar Mama**. After Tanaji fell, the Maratha soldiers began to lose heart, but his brother **Suryaji Malusare** took command and the fort was finally won.

## "Gad Aala, Pan Sinh Gela"

According to tradition, when Shivaji received the news of the victory and Tanaji's death together, he said — **"Gad aala, pan Sinh gela"**, that is, "The fort has come, but the lion is gone." It is believed that this is how Kondhana got the name **Sinhagad** ("Lion's Fort"). The episode is deeply rooted in Marathi popular memory, but the sentence has come down through tradition rather than being recorded in any contemporary written document.

## Later History and Memory

After the victory at Sinhagad, during 1670 Shivaji also recovered several other forts lost under the Treaty of Purandar. The fort remained with the Marathas for a long time. Later Chhatrapati **Rajaram** took shelter here and died at Sinhagad on **3 March 1700**.

Today Sinhagad has Tanaji's **samadhi** and his bronze bust, where thousands of people come every year. A Marathi novel by Hari Narayan Apte, many powadas, films and serials have been made on his story, of which the 2020 Hindi film "Tanhaji" has been the most talked about. Films and folk songs show many things in dramatic form; it is important for a student of history to separate firm facts — the date, the fort, Tanaji's heroic death — from stories added later.
