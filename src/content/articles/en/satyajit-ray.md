## An Artistic Family and Early Life

**Satyajit Ray** was born in **Calcutta** on **2 May 1921**. His family was well known in Bengal's cultural world. His grandfather **Upendrakishore Ray Chowdhury** was a writer, illustrator and publisher who started the children's magazine **'Sandesh'**. His father **Sukumar Ray** was a famous Bengali humorous poet and children's writer, but he died when Satyajit was only two. His mother Suprabha Ray brought him up in difficult circumstances.

Satyajit studied economics at Presidency College in Calcutta. Then, at his mother's urging, he went to study art at Rabindranath Tagore's **Santiniketan** (Visva-Bharati), where artists such as **Nandalal Bose** and **Benode Behari Mukherjee** deeply influenced him. From 1943 he worked as a "visualiser" at the Calcutta advertising agency **D. J. Keymer** and also designed book covers. Among these was the illustration of a children's edition of Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay's novel 'Pather Panchali'.

## 'Pather Panchali' — An Almost Impossible Film

Ray had loved films since childhood, and in 1947 he helped found the Calcutta Film Society. In **1950** he went to **London** for work, where in just a few months he watched a great many films. The Italian director Vittorio De Sica's **'Bicycle Thieves'** made the deepest impression on him, and he decided to become a filmmaker.

He decided to make a film of **'Pather Panchali'** — the story of the boy **Apu**, his sister **Durga** and their family in a poor Bengal village. He had neither an experienced crew nor money. Most of the actors were new, and the cameraman **Subrata Mitra** had never shot a film before. Shooting stopped again and again for lack of money; Ray pawned his insurance policy and even sold his wife's jewellery. In the end the **West Bengal government**, with the approval of Chief Minister **Bidhan Chandra Roy**, provided funds to complete the film. The music was by the sitar player **Ravi Shankar**.

The film was released in Calcutta on **26 August 1955**. At the **1956 Cannes Film Festival** it won the **"Best Human Document"** award. This film, which showed village life in a simple, truthful and poetic way, drew the world's attention to Indian cinema.

## The Apu Trilogy and Later Films

After 'Pather Panchali', Ray carried Apu's story forward in **'Aparajito'** (1956) and **'Apur Sansar'** (1959). Together the three films are called the **"Apu Trilogy"**, counted among the greatest film series in the world.

In a career of nearly four decades Ray made **36 films**, including feature films, documentaries and short films. Prominent among them are:

- **'Jalsaghar'** (1958) — a story of crumbling feudal grandeur and a love of music.
- **'Charulata'** (1964) — based on a Tagore story; it won the Silver Bear for Best Director at the Berlin Film Festival.
- **'Goopy Gyne Bagha Byne'** (1969) — a musical fantasy equally popular with children and adults.
- **'Shatranj Ke Khilari'** (1977) — based on a story by Premchand and set against the British takeover of Awadh, his only feature film in Hindustani.

From 'Teen Kanya' onwards Ray began composing the music for his films himself. He personally looked after everything from the screenplay and camera planning to editing and poster design.

## Writer, Illustrator and Editor

Ray is also a big name in Bengali literature. In **1961** he revived his grandfather's magazine **'Sandesh'**. In it he wrote stories about the clever detective **Feluda** and the scientist **Professor Shonku**, who makes strange inventions; they remain hugely popular with Bengali children and adults today. He also designed several typefaces and illustrated his own books.

## Honours and Legacy

Ray received countless honours in India and abroad. In **1984** he received Indian cinema's highest honour, the **Dadasaheb Phalke Award**, and in **1987** France honoured him with its highest distinction, the **Legion of Honour**. In **1992** the American Film Academy gave him an **honorary Oscar** for lifetime achievement — he was the first Indian to receive it. Being seriously ill, he accepted it from Calcutta by video. The same year he also received the **Bharat Ratna**.

He died in Calcutta on **23 April 1992**. Filmmakers around the world still regard him as a master of realistic and humane cinema. His films show that deep art lies hidden even in the ordinary lives of ordinary people.
