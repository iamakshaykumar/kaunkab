## The Paramara Dynasty and the Rise of Bhoj

In the tenth century, when the Gurjara-Pratiharas weakened, many of their feudatories became independent. Among these were the **Paramaras** of **Malwa**, whose capital was **Dhar** (Dhara Nagari). The Paramara king **Munja** (Vakpati II) was famous for his valour and love of poetry. After Munja his brother **Sindhuraja** became king, and Sindhuraja's son **Bhoj** came to the throne in about **1010 CE**.

Some later folk tales make Bhoj the direct successor of Munja, but on the basis of inscriptions historians consider Sindhuraja to be his predecessor.

## Military Campaigns

Bhoj ruled for about forty-five years and fought many wars to expand his kingdom.

- He led campaigns against **Lata** (south Gujarat) and the **Konkan** region.
- He was in constant conflict with the **Chalukyas** of Kalyani, the **Chaulukyas (Solankis)** of Gujarat, the **Kalachuris** and the **Chandelas**, winning sometimes and losing at others.
- In the last years of his life the Chaulukya king of Gujarat, **Bhima I**, and the Kalachuri king **Lakshmikarna** attacked Malwa together. In this period, around **1055 CE**, Bhoj died. According to the Jain writer Merutunga he died of illness.

## The Scholar King

Bhoj is remembered most for his scholarship and love of learning. Later tradition attributes **84 works** to him. Among those available today, the main ones are:

- **Samarangana Sutradhara** — a work on town planning, building construction, temple sculpture and machines.
- **Sarasvatikanthabharana** — on Sanskrit grammar.
- **Shringara Prakasha** — on poetics and the theory of rasa.
- **Yuktikalpataru** — on politics, governance and the appraisal of objects.

Whether Bhoj wrote all these works himself or his court scholars prepared them in his name has been a matter of discussion among scholars. But it is clear that many poets and scholars found patronage at his court.

## Bhojpur and the Bhojeshwar Temple

The founding of the town of **Bhojpur**, about 28 km from Bhopal, is traditionally linked with Raja Bhoj. The **Bhojeshwar Shiva temple** here still stands unfinished. In its sanctum is a huge Shiva lingam about 2.3 metres high, and with its platform the total height is more than 12 metres. The long ramp built to haul stones to the temple can still be seen today. Why the temple was left unfinished, no one knows for certain.

A large lake had also been created near Bhojpur by dams, which was broken and drained in the fifteenth century in the time of the Malwa sultan Hoshang Shah.

## The Bhojshala of Dhar

The **Bhojshala** of Dhar is traditionally linked with Raja Bhoj's Sanskrit school and Saraswati temple. Several inscriptions connected with Sanskrit grammar and plays have been found here, although some of them date from the time of Paramara kings after Bhoj. The complex is protected by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), and its religious character has long been disputed, with hearings held in the courts.

## Legacy

Bhoj is among the few kings of medieval India who are remembered more as scholars than as warriors. The **Bhoja Prabandha**, written by Ballala in the seventeenth century, is full of stories of his generosity and respect for scholars, although these tales are more folk memory than history. A proverb such as "Where is Raja Bhoj, and where is Gangu Teli" also shows that his name remains alive in the popular mind to this day.
