## Teacher and Journalist

**Bal Gangadhar Tilak** was born on **23 July 1856** at **Ratnagiri** in Maharashtra in a Chitpavan Brahmin family. After graduating in mathematics from Deccan College, Pune, and taking a law degree in 1879, he chose the path of national education instead of government service. He played a part in founding the **New English School** in 1880 along with Vishnushastri Chiplunkar and **Gopal Ganesh Agarkar** and others, then the **Deccan Education Society** in 1884 and **Fergusson College** in 1885.

The first issue of the Marathi weekly **'Kesari'** came out on **4 January 1881**; along with it the English paper **'Mahratta'** was also started. At first Agarkar was the editor of 'Kesari'; later Tilak made these papers the biggest weapon of his politics.

## Awakening the People through Festivals

Tilak understood that nationalism had to be taken out of the educated class and carried to the common people. In the 1890s he promoted turning the **Ganesh puja, which was confined to homes, into a public festival** and had the **Shivaji festival** started. Sources disagree on the exact early dates of these events (1893 or 1894 for the Ganesh festival, 1895 or 1896 for the Shivaji festival). Through speeches, fairs and songs these festivals spread political awareness. Critics say that the use of religious symbols also gave nationalism a particular religious colour.

Tilak was relatively conservative in social matters. He opposed the **Age of Consent Bill** of 1891 — his argument was that a foreign government had no right to legislate on the internal matters of Hindu society, and reform should come from within. On this he had a sharp disagreement with reformers like Gokhale.

## Sedition and Mandalay

In 1897, amid opposition to the harsh plague-control measures in Pune, the officer Rand was murdered. On the basis of articles in 'Kesari', Tilak was tried for sedition and sentenced to **18 months**. It was in this period that he began to be called 'Lokmanya'.

After the Partition of Bengal in 1905, **Lal-Bal-Pal** (Lala Lajpat Rai, Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal) called for swadeshi, boycott and national education. At the **Surat session** of 1907 the Congress split into moderates and extremists. In **June 1908**, after the Muzaffarpur bomb case, Tilak was arrested again for two articles published in 'Kesari', and in **July 1908** he was sentenced to **six years** of transportation. He was sent to **Mandalay** jail in Burma, from where he was released in June 1914.

## Tilak the Scholar

Tilak was also a deep scholar. In 'The Orion' (1893) and **'The Arctic Home in the Vedas'** (1903) he put forward views on the antiquity of the Vedas on astronomical grounds, whose conclusions are still debated among scholars. During his imprisonment at Mandalay he wrote **'Gita Rahasya'** in Marathi, published in 1915 — in it he said that the basic message of the Gita is not renunciation but **karmayoga**.

## Home Rule and the Final Years

After returning from jail, Tilak founded the **Indian Home Rule League** at Belgaum in April 1916 and played a role in bringing the Congress and the Muslim League together in the **Lucknow Pact** of December 1916. His slogan — in Marathi **"Swarajya ha majha janmasiddha hakka aahe ani to mi milavanarach"** (Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it) — reached every home. The words of it are found in several forms, and according to some sources it was first coined by his associate Joseph 'Kaka' Baptista; but it was Tilak who made it immortal.

The English journalist Valentine Chirol, in his book 'Indian Unrest' (1910), described Tilak as the chief instigator of unrest; the epithet 'Father of Indian Unrest' is often linked to this. Tilak died in Bombay on **1 August 1920**. Lakhs of people thronged his funeral procession in Bombay.
