## Background: Concerns over Identity and Population

People had been coming from outside to settle in Assam since the colonial period. They came from different regions for the tea gardens, farming and trade. During Partition and then the **1971** Bangladesh Liberation War, large numbers of people reached Assam from East Pakistan/Bangladesh. Among a large section of Assamese society a worry grew that uncontrolled immigration would endanger their language, culture, land and political representation.

## The Start of the Movement (1979)

In **1979**, after the death of Hiralal Patwari, MP for the **Mangaldoi** Lok Sabha seat, preparations began for a by-election. During the revision of the electoral roll, objections were raised against a large number of names as suspected foreigners. This became the spark for an agitation across the state.

The **All Assam Students Union (AASU)** began the movement with a 12-hour strike on **8 June 1979**. Its main demand was the **detection, disenfranchisement and deportation** of foreigners (the '3Ds'). The **All Assam Gana Sangram Parishad (AAGSP)**, formed by AASU together with several organisations, led it. AASU president **Prafulla Kumar Mahanta** and general secretary **Bhrigu Kumar Phukan** became the main faces of the movement. **Khargeswar Talukdar**, killed in police firing in December 1979, is regarded as the movement's first martyr.

## Satyagraha, Deadlock and Violence

The agitators demanded that no elections be held until the names of foreigners were removed from the electoral rolls. Sit-ins, satyagraha, boycotts of government offices and economic blockades, such as stopping the supply of crude oil and plywood out of Assam, were used. In December 1979 **President's Rule** was imposed on the state. Several rounds of talks took place between the Centre and the agitators, but no agreement could be reached on the cut-off date — the agitators wanted **1951** as the base, while the government insisted on 1971.

In **February 1983** the Centre held assembly elections on the old electoral rolls. The movement boycotted them and there was large-scale violence in the state. In the massacre at **Nellie** and nearby villages on **18 February 1983**, a large number of Muslims of Bengali origin were killed; the official figure was around 2,000, while other estimates are higher. The same year Parliament passed the **Illegal Migrants (Determination by Tribunals) Act, 1983 (IMDT)**, which the agitators opposed because it placed the burden of proving someone a foreigner on the complainant. In **2005** the Supreme Court struck it down.

## The Assam Accord (15 August 1985)

**Rajiv Gandhi**, who became Prime Minister in **1984**, sped up the talks. On **15 August 1985** the **Assam Accord** was signed in New Delhi in the Prime Minister's presence. On behalf of the movement it was signed by Prafulla Kumar Mahanta, Bhrigu Kumar Phukan and **Biraj Kumar Sarma** of the AAGSP, and on behalf of the government by Union Home Secretary **R. D. Pradhan** and Assam's Chief Secretary **P. P. Trivedi**. Its main provisions were:

- People who came to Assam before **1 January 1966** would be treated as citizens.
- People who came between 1 January 1966 and **24 March 1971** would be identified and their names removed from the electoral rolls for ten years, after which they could get citizenship.
- Foreigners who came on or after **25 March 1971** would be detected and expelled.
- **Clause 6** promised constitutional, legislative and administrative safeguards to protect the cultural, social and linguistic identity of the Assamese people.
- Along with fencing the border, there were also commitments to economic development, such as an oil refinery, reopening closed paper mills and educational institutions.

To implement these provisions, **Section 6A** was added to the Citizenship Act in **1985**.

## Legacy and the Debate that Followed

After the Accord, the leaders of the movement formed the **Asom Gana Parishad (AGP)** in **October 1985**, which won the assembly election of December 1985, and **Prafulla Kumar Mahanta** became Chief Minister. The **Swahid Smarak Kshetra** at Boragaon in Guwahati is dedicated to the **860** martyrs of the movement.

Several provisions of the Assam Accord remained under debate for a long time. On its basis the **National Register of Citizens (NRC)** was updated under the Supreme Court's supervision; about **1.9 million** people were left out of the final list of **2019**, to which various sides raised objections. On **17 October 2024** a five-judge bench of the Supreme Court upheld Section 6A as constitutional by a **4:1** majority. The Assam Movement is counted among India's major mass movements that ended in a political agreement reached through negotiation, and it remains a central reference in Assam's politics and debates over identity today.
