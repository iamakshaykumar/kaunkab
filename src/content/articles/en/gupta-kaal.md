## Background

About five hundred years after the fall of the Mauryan Empire, a big empire rose again in north India. Around 320 CE **Chandragupta I** consolidated the foundation of the Gupta dynasty and took the title "Maharajadhiraja".

## Major Rulers

- **Samudragupta** — a great conqueror. His court poet **Harishena** wrote a description of his conquests on the pillar at Prayag (Allahabad). On some coins he is shown playing the veena.
- **Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya)** — in his time the empire extended west to Gujarat. The Chinese Buddhist traveller **Faxian** came to India in his time.
- **Kumaragupta I** — according to tradition, the foundation of the centre of learning at **Nalanda** was laid in his time, which later became a world-famous university.

## Science and Literature

- **Aryabhata** wrote the "Aryabhatiya" in 499 CE, in which he spoke of the Earth's rotation on its axis and of the scientific causes of eclipses.
- This period is also credited with a contribution to the development of the decimal system and the use of zero.
- The great Sanskrit poet **Kalidasa** wrote works such as "Abhijnanashakuntalam" and "Meghaduta". Most scholars place him in this period.

## Art

The paintings in some of the famous caves of Ajanta, the Dashavatara temple at Deogarh and the **Iron Pillar** of Delhi (which has not rusted for centuries) are considered examples of the art and technology of this period.

## Decline

From the end of the fifth century, invasions by the **Huns** from Central Asia began. Because of continuous wars, the feudatories becoming independent and economic pressure, the Gupta Empire broke up by about the middle of the sixth century.

## Significance

Historians today debate the term "Golden Age", because this prosperity was mainly limited to the court and the upper classes. Even so, the achievements of the Gupta period in science, literature and art are a lasting legacy of Indian civilisation.
