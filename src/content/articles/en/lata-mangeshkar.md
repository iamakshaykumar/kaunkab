## Born in Indore, Responsibility at a Young Age

**Lata Mangeshkar** was born on **28 September 1929** in **Indore**. Her father, **Pandit Deenanath Mangeshkar**, was a classical singer and a well-known actor of the Marathi stage. Lata was the eldest of five siblings; her sisters **Meena**, **Asha** and **Usha** and her brother **Hridaynath** also went on to work in music. As a child Lata learnt music from her father and also worked as a child artist in his plays.

In **1942**, when Lata was only 13, her father died. The financial responsibility of the family suddenly fell on her shoulders. Family friend and film-maker **Master Vinayak** got her small roles and chances to sing in Marathi and Hindi films. Around 1945 the family moved to Mumbai, where Lata also studied Hindustani classical music with **Ustad Aman Ali Khan Bhendibazaarwale**.

## From Struggle to the Summit

The beginning was not easy. Heavy voices such as those of Noor Jehan and Shamshad Begum were in fashion then, and some producers found Lata's voice "too thin". The composer **Ghulam Haider** put his faith in her, and the song **"Dil Mera Toda"** from the **1948** film **'Majboor'** became her first big success.

In **1949** the song **"Aayega Aanewala"** from the film **'Mahal'** (music: Khemchand Prakash) became hugely popular. In those days even the singer's name was not printed on the record; the popularity of this song played a big part in winning recognition for playback singers. After this, composers of almost every generation — from **Naushad**, **Shankar-Jaikishan**, **S. D. Burman**, **C. Ramchandra**, **Madan Mohan**, **Laxmikant-Pyarelal** and **R. D. Burman** to **A. R. Rahman** — created immortal songs in her voice. With Laxmikant-Pyarelal alone she sang more than 700 songs. She sang in **more than 36 Indian languages** and a few foreign ones. Thousands of her songs were recorded; estimates of the exact number vary.

## 'Ae Mere Watan Ke Logon' (1963)

The country was in mourning after India's defeat and the deaths of its soldiers in the **1962 India-China War**. The poet **Pradeep** wrote **"Ae Mere Watan Ke Logon"** in memory of the fallen soldiers, and the composer **C. Ramchandra** set it to music. Lata first sang it in **January 1963**, on the occasion of Republic Day, at a fund-raising event for soldiers held at the **National Stadium in New Delhi**. Most sources give the date as **26 January**, while some mention **27 January**.

President **S. Radhakrishnan** and Prime Minister **Jawaharlal Nehru** were present at the event. It is said that Nehru's eyes filled with tears on hearing the song and that he later met Lata and told her the song had made him cry. The event raised money for the Army welfare fund, and the artists associated with the song pledged its royalties to the **War Widows Fund**. The song is still among the patriotic songs sung most often on Republic Day and Independence Day.

## Honours

Lata Mangeshkar received the **Padma Bhushan** in **1969**, the film world's highest honour, the **Dadasaheb Phalke Award**, in **1989** and the **Padma Vibhushan** in **1999**. In **2001** she was honoured with the **Bharat Ratna**. She won three **National Film Awards**, for 'Parichay', 'Kora Kagaz' and 'Lekin...'. After 1969 she withdrew from the competition for Filmfare's best female singer award to give chances to new talent. The French government honoured her with its highest civilian honour, the **Legion of Honour**. She was also a nominated member of the **Rajya Sabha** from **1999 to 2005**.

## Final Farewell and Legacy

In January 2022, after being infected with COVID-19, she was admitted to **Breach Candy Hospital** in Mumbai. After nearly four weeks of treatment she died of **multi-organ failure** on **6 February 2022**, at the age of 92. That same evening she was cremated with **state honours** at **Shivaji Park** in Mumbai, attended by thousands of people including Prime Minister **Narendra Modi**. The central government announced two days of national mourning.

Called the 'Nightingale of India' and the 'Melody Queen', Lata Mangeshkar gave her voice to Indian film music for seven decades. Out of respect, she sang barefoot during recordings. Her voice remained a bridge between generations — the songs beloved of grandmothers are hers, and so are those of the new generation. Very few artists in the Indian subcontinent have had a voice that was part of so many people's lives for so long.
