## From Talaja to Junagadh

Very little is certain about Narsinh Mehta's life; most things come from his own autobiographical poems and the works of later poets. Scholars also do not agree on his dates — generally his birth is placed **c. 1414** and his death around **1481 or 1488**.

According to tradition, he was born in **Talaja** in Saurashtra (today's Bhavnagar district) in a **Nagar Brahmin** family. His parents died when he was five, and he was raised in the house of his elder brother **Bansidhar**. It is said that he could not speak until the age of eight. He was married to **Manekbai**, and they had two children — a daughter, **Kunwarbai**, and a son, **Shamaldas**.

## The Sister-in-Law's Taunt and the Beginning of Devotion

According to the story, Narsinh did not take interest in work, so his sister-in-law taunted him. Hurt, he sat fasting and meditating for seven days near the Shivalinga at **Gopnath** in a nearby forest. It is believed that Shiva was pleased and took him to Vrindavan and showed him the **Rasa Lila of Krishna**. After this, Narsinh made devotion to Krishna his life and settled in a small house in **Junagadh**, where his family lived in poverty.

## Tales of Miracles

Many famous stories are linked with Narsinh's life, which he himself sang in his verses:

- **Kunwarbai's Mameru** — Krishna himself sent the goods to be given as gifts at his daughter's in-laws' house;
- **The Hundi** — Krishna, appearing as "Shamalsha Seth", paid his hundi (bill of exchange);
- **Shamaldas no Vivah** — Krishna's help at his son's wedding;
- **Har Mala** — **Ra Mandlik**, the king of Junagadh, put him to the test, and the Lord placed the garland of the idol round his neck, proving him innocent.

These are stories of the faith of devotees; historians do not regard them as proven events. Yes, the name of Ra Mandlik helps give a rough idea of his time.

## Against Caste Discrimination

Narsinh Mehta did not accept high and low distinctions in devotion. It is said that he went and sang hymns among people of castes regarded as low. Because of this, the Nagar Brahmin community of Junagadh opposed him and he had to face a boycott. Many writers believe that Gandhiji took the word **"Harijan"** (person of Hari) from Narsinh Mehta, although opinions differ on this too.

## Works and 'Vaishnav Jan To'

Narsinh Mehta wrote in the colloquial early **Gujarati** of that time, so that ordinary people could understand. For this reason he is called the **Adi Kavi** of Gujarati. His works include **prabhatiyas** sung in the morning, romantic verses on the Krishna Lila, **Sudama Charit**, **Dan Lila** and autobiographical works. His oldest available manuscript is from about **1612**.

The most famous verse linked to him is **"Vaishnav jan to tene kahiye, je peed parai jane re"** — that is, a true Vaishnava is one who understands the pain of others. This hymn shows that a devotee is known not by caste or sect but by conduct: not being proud after helping others, respecting everyone, staying away from greed and falsehood. It is worth noting that this verse is not found in the oldest (17th-century) collections of his verses, so some scholars question its authorship; but the claim that names the 19th-century poet Dalpatram as its author has been rejected after examining the dates of the manuscripts.

## Gandhiji and Legacy

**Mahatma Gandhi** had this hymn sung daily in the prayers of his ashrams. The Sabarmati Ashram's *Ashram Bhajanavali* begins with the first verse of this very hymn. It was also sung during the **Dandi March of 1930**. Along with the freedom movement, this hymn spread across the whole country.

Today the **Narsinh Mehta Award**, one of Gujarat's highest literary honours, is named after him. It is believed that his last days were spent at **Mangrol**. Even after six centuries, his prabhatiyas are sung early in the morning in the homes of Gujarat.
