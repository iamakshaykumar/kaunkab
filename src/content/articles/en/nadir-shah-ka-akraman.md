## Background: A Weakening Mughal Empire

After Aurangzeb's death in 1707 the Mughal Empire weakened rapidly. In the time of the emperor **Muhammad Shah** (reigned 1719-1748) the court was divided by factionalism and provinces such as Awadh, Hyderabad and Bengal had become almost independent. Defence of the north-western frontier was also lax.

Meanwhile in Iran an Afshar Turkmen chieftain named **Nadir** removed the Safavid dynasty and declared himself Shah in **1736**. He alleged that the Mughals were sheltering Afghan rebels — and this became the pretext for the attack on India.

## From Kabul to Karnal

In 1738 Nadir Shah captured **Ghazni** and **Kabul**, then reached **Lahore** via Peshawar. The court in Delhi was so careless that the army set out very late. The Mughal army was camped near **Karnal**, about 120 kilometres north of Delhi.

## The Battle of Karnal (24 February 1739)

The battle was fought on **24 February 1739**. The Mughal army was very large in numbers, but its commanders were not united. **Saadat Khan**, the governor of Awadh, advanced in haste, and Nadir's disciplined cavalry and musketeers defeated the Mughal contingents one by one. The battle lasted **less than three hours**. The Mir Bakhshi **Khan-i-Dauran** died, mortally wounded, Saadat Khan was taken prisoner, and thousands of Mughal soldiers were killed. (Some older texts give the date as 13 February according to the old European calendar.)

## The Massacre of Delhi

After the defeat Muhammad Shah had to surrender to Nadir. Nadir Shah reached Delhi on **20 March 1739**. On the night of 21 March a rumour spread that Nadir had been killed, and riots broke out in the city in which Iranian soldiers were killed. In response, on **22 March** Nadir ordered a **general massacre**. In a few hours an estimated **20,000 to 30,000** people were killed; the exact number could never be known. According to tradition, Nadir stopped the massacre at the plea of Nizam-ul-Mulk.

## The Plunder: The Peacock Throne and the Koh-i-Noor

For several weeks money was extracted from Delhi's treasuries, nobles and merchants. Nadir took away with him the **Peacock Throne (Takht-i-Taus)** built by Shah Jahan, diamonds such as the **Koh-i-Noor** and the **Darya-i-Noor**, immense gold and silver, elephants, horses and craftsmen. The total plunder is estimated at about 70 crore rupees. The name Koh-i-Noor ('Mountain of Light') was given by Nadir himself — this is a popular story for which there is no firm proof.

On **16 May 1739** Nadir left Delhi. He restored Muhammad Shah as emperor, but annexed all Mughal territories **west of the Indus River** to Iran. On the way back, Sikh bands in Punjab attacked his army and rescued some of the loot and captives.

## Consequences

Nadir Shah's invasion proved that the Mughal Empire was now an empire only in name. With the treasury empty, the army and administration weakened further, regional powers — the Marathas, Awadh, Hyderabad, Bengal — became more independent, and the way was opened for later attacks by Ahmad Shah Abdali. In 1747 Nadir Shah was assassinated by his own officers, after which the Peacock Throne too was broken up and scattered.
