## The Attack in the Baisaran Valley

On the afternoon of **22 April 2025**, armed terrorists opened fire on tourists in the **Baisaran Valley**, near **Pahalgam** in Anantnag district of Jammu and Kashmir. Surrounded by dense deodar forests, this meadow was popular among tourists as "Mini Switzerland" and could be reached only on foot or on horseback. **26 civilians** were killed in the attack — 25 tourists (one of whom was a Nepali citizen) and a local pony operator, **Syed Adil Hussain Shah**, who reportedly tried to stop the attackers. According to survivors and investigators, the attackers targeted several people after asking them their religion.

It was the deadliest attack on civilians in Kashmir since the Pulwama attack of 2019. At first an organisation called **The Resistance Front (TRF)**, which India regards as a front for Lashkar-e-Taiba, claimed responsibility for the attack, but a few days later it withdrew the claim and called it the result of "hacking". Ordinary people and political parties in the Kashmir Valley also strongly condemned the attack.

## Diplomatic Steps

India blamed cross-border terrorism for the attack. Pakistan denied any role and offered a neutral international investigation. On **23 April** India's Cabinet Committee on Security announced several steps — holding the **Indus Waters Treaty** in abeyance, closing the Integrated Check Post at **Attari**, cancelling most visas of Pakistani nationals, and reducing staff strength at both countries' high commissions.

In response Pakistan closed its airspace to Indian aircraft, suspended trade with India and said it could hold bilateral agreements, including the 1972 Simla Agreement, in abeyance. Over the next two weeks there were incidents of firing from both sides on the **Line of Control**.

## Operation Sindoor

From about 1 a.m. on the night of **7 May 2025**, India launched **Operation Sindoor**. According to the Government of India, Indian forces carried out precision strikes on **nine sites** in Pakistan and Pakistan-administered Kashmir, including a Jaish-e-Mohammed centre in **Bahawalpur** and a Lashkar-e-Taiba centre in **Muridke**. India said these were "terrorist infrastructure", that no Pakistani military facility was targeted, and that the action was "measured and non-escalatory". Indian officials later claimed that more than 100 terrorists were killed in these strikes.

Pakistan rejected these claims. According to it, the strikes hit mosques and residential areas, killing many civilians including women and children. Pakistan also claimed that it shot down several Indian fighter aircraft. India later acknowledged the loss of some aircraft, but did not accept the figures given by Pakistan.

## Four Days of Conflict and the Ceasefire

After 7 May there was heavy shelling on the Line of Control, in which civilians were killed in several areas of Jammu and Kashmir, including **Poonch**. In the following days the two countries accused each other of drone and missile attacks. India said it foiled Pakistan's drone and missile attacks and in response struck several Pakistani military sites, including air force bases. On **10 May** Pakistan named its retaliatory action **"Operation Bunyan-um-Marsoos"** and claimed to have damaged many Indian military sites; India called most of these claims false.

A ceasefire took effect from **5 p.m.** (Indian time) on **10 May 2025**. US President **Donald Trump** first announced it on social media and took credit for American mediation. Pakistan praised the role of the United States. India's position was that agreement on the ceasefire was reached through direct talks between the two countries' **Directors General of Military Operations (DGMOs)** and that there was no third-party mediation.

## Outcome and Controversies

Both countries claimed success in this conflict. In India, the government described it as a "new policy" against terrorism — that in future any major terror attack would be treated as an act of war. In Pakistan, after this conflict, Army Chief **Asim Munir** was promoted to the rank of Field Marshal. Independent experts have given differing assessments of many claims of both sides — especially about aircraft losses and the damage done to sites — and many questions remain disputed.

In India, opposition parties supported the government's military action but raised questions about the alleged role of the United States in the ceasefire and about intelligence lapses; a special debate on this was held in Parliament in July 2025. India sent delegations of MPs from various parties to many countries to present its position on terrorism. This episode shows how quickly a terror attack between two nuclear-armed neighbours can turn into a military confrontation, and how important it is to keep channels of dialogue open in times of crisis.
