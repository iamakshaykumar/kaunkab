## The Founding of the Brahmo Sabha (1828)

On **20 August 1828** in Calcutta, **Raja Ram Mohan Roy**, with the help of **Dwarkanath Tagore**, founded the **'Brahmo Sabha'**. Its aim was the worship of a formless, all-pervading God based on the Upanishads. The main ideas of the Sabha were:

- Worship of one God alone; **opposition to idol worship and polytheism**;
- Rejection of the doctrine of avatars, and placing reason and conscience above scripture;
- Opposition to practices such as **caste distinctions**, sati, child marriage and polygamy.

In 1830 the Sabha's trust deed was drawn up and its first house of worship opened. After Ram Mohan Roy's death in Bristol, England, in 1833, the institution grew weak, although Dwarkanath Tagore continued to give financial help.

## Debendranath Tagore and Organisation

Dwarkanath's son **Debendranath Tagore** formed the **Tattvabodhini Sabha** in 1839, which spread Ram Mohan's ideas. In the early 1840s (sources say 1842 or 1843) he joined the Brahmo Samaj and the Tattvabodhini Sabha merged into it. On **21 December 1843**, 21 people took the 'Brahmo Pledge' — from here the Brahmo Samaj became a membership-based religious community. Debendranath gave Brahmo Dharma a systematic form and spread ideas in Bengali through the 'Tattvabodhini Patrika'. Gradually the idea of regarding the Vedas as infallible was also given up.

## Keshub Chunder Sen and the First Split (1866)

In the late 1850s the young **Keshub Chunder Sen** joined the Samaj (sources say 1857 or 1859). Through his eloquence the movement spread beyond Bengal as well. Keshub wanted speed in social reform — inter-caste marriage, widow remarriage, women's education, and the abandoning of the sacred thread by the priests. Debendranath was relatively closer to tradition; Keshub was also accused of Christian influence.

As differences grew, in **November 1866** Keshub formed the **'Brahmo Samaj of India'** (Bharatvarshiya Brahmo Samaj), while Debendranath's original Samaj came to be called the **'Adi Brahmo Samaj'**. Through Keshub's efforts the Special Marriage Act was passed in **1872**, giving legal recognition to Brahmo rites and inter-caste marriage.

## The Second Split (1878) and the Sadharan Brahmo Samaj

Ironically, Keshub, an opponent of child marriage, in 1878 married off his minor daughter **Suniti Devi** to the prince of **Cooch Behar**. His followers considered this a betrayal of principles, and questions were also raised about his autocratic leadership. In **May 1878**, **Ananda Mohan Bose, Sivanath Shastri**, Umesh Chandra Dutta and others broke away and formed the **'Sadharan Brahmo Samaj'**, which functioned on a more democratic structure. Keshub later proclaimed the **'Nava Vidhan'** (New Dispensation), combining elements of various religions (the date is given as between 1880 and 1881).

## Legacy

The number of followers of the Brahmo Samaj was never very large, but its influence was far greater than its numbers. It gave the Bengal Renaissance an intellectual foundation, carried forward the debate on women's education and social reform, and inspired organisations such as the **Prarthana Samaj** (1867) of Bombay. Critics say that it remained mainly confined to the English-educated urban class; even so, its role in laying the foundation of the idea of rational religion and social equality in modern India is undisputed.
