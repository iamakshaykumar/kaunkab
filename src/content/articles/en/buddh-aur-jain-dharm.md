## Background: An Age of Intellectual Revolution

The 6th century BCE (600 BCE) was an age of deep thought and philosophical ferment not only in India but across the whole world. Contemporary with thinkers such as Confucius in China, Zarathustra in Iran and Socrates in Greece, the **Shramana tradition** developed rapidly in India.

By the later Vedic period, sacrifices and rituals had become very expensive and involved animal sacrifice. The birth-based caste system had limited the rights of Shudras and women. On the other hand, the Vaishyas (the merchant class), enriched by the expansion of trade, wanted a simple and ethical religion free from violence and sacrificial pomp. It was against this social and economic background that Buddhism and Jainism arose.

## Gautama Buddha and Buddhism

Prince Siddhartha (Gautama Buddha) of the Shakya republic renounced home on seeing worldly sufferings — old age, disease and death. After years of meditation under a Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya, he attained 'Sambodhi' (enlightenment).

- **The Four Noble Truths:**
  1. There is suffering in the world.
  2. The cause of suffering is craving (desire).
  3. By giving up craving, the end of suffering is possible.
  4. The path to the removal of suffering is the 'Eightfold Path' (the Middle Path).
- **Madhyama Pratipada (the Middle Way):** The Buddha supported neither excessive luxury nor harsh asceticism that tortures the body; he taught a balanced middle path.
- **Founding of the Sangha:** Sanghas were formed for monks and nuns in which there was no distinction of caste, varna or family.

## Vardhamana Mahavira and Jainism

The Jain tradition is very ancient, in which 24 'Tirthankaras' (guides who help one cross the ocean of existence) are recognised. The first Tirthankara was Rishabhadeva and the 23rd was Parshvanatha. The 24th Tirthankara, **Vardhamana Mahavira** (599 – 527 BCE), made this philosophy systematic and popular.

- **The Five Great Vows (Panch Mahavrata):** Ahimsa (not harming living beings), Satya (not lying), Asteya (not stealing), Aparigraha (not accumulating possessions) and Brahmacharya (celibacy).
- **Syadvada and Anekantavada:** Jain philosophy teaches that truth has many aspects and that one should not quarrel with others by treating one viewpoint as complete.

## Historical Impact

Buddhism and Jainism lifted architecture (the stupas of Sanchi and Sarnath, the caves of Ajanta and Ellora), sculpture and literature in India to immense heights. Through the efforts of Emperor Ashoka and Kanishka, Buddhism spread to Central Asia, China, Sri Lanka, Tibet and Southeast Asia and became a global religion.
