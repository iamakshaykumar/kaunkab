## The Purpose of the Journey

**Faxian** (Fa-Hien) was born in the fourth century in what is today Shanxi province of China; most sources give his birth as about 337 CE. He became a Buddhist monk in childhood. At that time Buddhism was spreading in China, but the **Vinaya** texts containing the rules of monastic conduct were incomplete. To bring complete copies of these texts, in 399 CE, at over sixty years of age, he began the journey with a few companions from the capital **Chang'an**.

## From Central Asia to India

Faxian's party went west on foot. They crossed the terrible desert beyond Dunhuang, stayed in Buddhist cities such as **Khotan**, and then crossed the high, snowy passes of the Pamirs and the Hindu Kush. On this difficult route some of his companions turned back and one died of cold.

In the north-west they passed through Uddiyana (Swat), Gandhara, Taxila and the Purushapura (Peshawar) region, then reached the Gangetic plain by way of **Mathura**. He visited the places connected with the life of the Buddha: Kapilavastu, Lumbini, Shravasti, Kushinagar, Vaishali, Rajagriha, **Bodh Gaya** and Varanasi.

## Three Years at Pataliputra

Faxian spent about **three years** at **Pataliputra**, the chief city of the Gupta Empire. Here he learned to read and speak Sanskrit and prepared copies of the Vinaya and other Buddhist texts. His companion **Daojing** stayed on here.

At Pataliputra Faxian saw the ruins of Ashoka's old palace, whose grandeur made people believe that it must have been built not by humans but by spirits. He also wrote that the respected people and householders of the city had set up **hospitals** where the poor, the disabled and the sick received free food and medicine.

## A Picture of the Middle Country

Faxian wrote about the Gangetic plain, which he calls the "Middle Country", that:
- Kings ruled without corporal punishment; criminals were fined according to the offence, and only the right hand of repeated rebels was cut off.
- People generally did not kill living beings, did not drink wine and did not eat onion and garlic.
- The **Chandalas** had to live apart from the rest, and when entering a city or market they had to announce their coming by striking a piece of wood.
- **Cowrie shells** were used in transactions, and only those who tilled the king's land had to give a share of the produce.

Historians believe that as a devout Buddhist monk Faxian's attention was mainly on religious life, and his picture may be somewhat idealised. Even so, his description of discrimination against the Chandalas is important evidence of the social inequality of the time.

## The Return by Sea

His last halt in India was the Bengal port of **Tamralipti**, where he spent about two years copying texts. In about 409 CE he went by ship to **Sri Lanka** and stayed there two years. On the return, a storm drove his ship to an island (probably Java), where he stayed about five months. Finally, after wandering, his ship reached the Shandong coast of China in **412 CE**.

On returning to China Faxian wrote his travel account, famous as the *Foguoji* ("Record of Buddhist Kingdoms"), and, together with the Indian monk Buddhabhadra, translated the texts he had brought. For the history of the Gupta period this account is still an important foreign source.
