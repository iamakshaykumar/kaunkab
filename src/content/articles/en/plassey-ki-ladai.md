## Background: The Lust for Power through Trade

In the middle of the 18th century Bengal was the richest and most resource-rich province of India. A huge trade in cotton cloth, silk and saltpetre (gunpowder) was carried on here. Misusing the Mughal farman, officers of the British East India Company were carrying on private trade without paying tax.

In 1756 the 23-year-old **Siraj-ud-Daula** became the Nawab of Bengal. He gave the British a blunt order:
1. The Company should immediately stop fortifying Calcutta (Fort William).
2. It should honestly pay the trade tax (customs duty) due to the Nawab.

When the Company challenged the Nawab's sovereignty, Siraj-ud-Daula attacked Calcutta and captured Fort William.

## Clive's Conspiracy and the Betrayal

The English officer **Robert Clive** reached Bengal from Madras with an army. Knowing that it was impossible to defeat the Nawab's huge army of 50,000 directly on the battlefield, Clive devised a diplomatic conspiracy.

Clive won over the Nawab's discontented courtiers:
- The Nawab's chief commander **Mir Jafar** was tempted with the promise of being made Nawab.
- **Jagat Seth**, the greatest banker of Bengal, the wealthy merchant **Amichand** and the diwan **Rai Durlabh** were brought into this secret treaty.

## 23 June 1757: The Battlefield of Plassey

About 150 km north of Calcutta, on the bank of the Bhagirathi river, in the mango groves (Lakshmanbagh), the two armies faced each other:

- The Nawab's loyal commanders **Mir Madan** and **Mohan Lal** attacked the British detachment fiercely with artillery and forced them to fall back.
- Suddenly at midday there was a torrential downpour. The British covered their ammunition with tarpaulins, while the Nawab's gunpowder got soaked.
- When, amid the wet gunpowder, Mir Madan was hit by a cannonball and fell as a hero, the Nawab became panic-stricken.
- At this critical moment Mir Jafar advised the Nawab to stop the fighting and withdraw the army. When the Nawab ordered a ceasefire, Clive's army suddenly attacked.
- Mir Jafar's large detachment stood idle throughout the battle. Nawab Siraj-ud-Daula had to flee, and was later murdered.

## Historical Results

The Battle of Plassey was really not a major military struggle but a pre-planned betrayal. The poet Nabinchandra Sen wrote: *"After Plassey a night of endless darkness began for India."*

1. Mir Jafar became a puppet in the Company's hands and the Company's indirect rule over Bengal began.
2. Using the vast resources of Bengal, the British modernised their army and later built their empire over the whole of India.
