## Bhai Lehna

Guru Angad Dev was born on **31 March 1504** in the village of **Matte di Sarai** (today Sarai Naga) in the Muktsar region of Punjab. His original name was **Lehna**. His father Pheru Mal was a trader and his mother's name was Ramo. Around 1520 he married **Khivi**, with whom he had two sons — Dasu and Datu — and daughters.

In his youth Lehna was a devotee of the goddess Durga and led a band of goddess-worshippers at Khadur. According to Sikh tradition, while on a journey to see the goddess he heard the hymns of Guru Nanak and went to Kartarpur to meet him. This meeting changed his life — he stayed there and became a disciple of Guru Nanak.

## Guru Nanak's Successor

At Kartarpur Lehna served Guru Nanak for many years. Sikh sakhis tell of many tests of his obedience, in which the Guru's sons fall behind and Lehna obeys the command without question. These stories are found in the later Janamsakhis and should be read only as tradition.

In **1539** Guru Nanak declared Lehna his successor instead of his sons Sri Chand and Lakhmi Das, and gave him the name **'Angad'** — "his own limb". A few months later, in September 1539, Guru Nanak died. This tradition of choosing a successor on merit is considered very important in Sikh history.

## Khadur Sahib

To avoid opposition from Guru Nanak's sons, Guru Angad left Kartarpur and returned to **Khadur** near the Beas River. This place, today called **Khadur Sahib**, became the centre of the Sikh community during his time as Guru (1539–1552). Here the congregation gathered every day for kirtan and the hymns of Guru Nanak were recited.

## The Gurmukhi Script

Tradition regards Guru Angad Dev as the originator of the **Gurmukhi** script. Scholars say that most letters of this script already existed in scripts like Landa and Takri that were in use in Punjab; so Guru Angad's contribution was less to create a new script than to **standardise** it and adopt it for writing the Gurus' hymns. In this script the shabads of Guru Nanak were preserved, which later laid the foundation for the compilation of the Adi Granth. Today the Punjabi language in India is written mainly in Gurmukhi.

## Langar and the Community

The communal kitchen — **langar** — which had been going on since Guru Nanak's time, was given institutional form by Guru Angad Dev, where people of every caste and class sat in one row and ate. In Sikh tradition the credit for supervising the langar is given especially to his wife **Mata Khivi**, who is also mentioned in a var included in the Guru Granth Sahib.

Guru Angad also stressed physical health and had **wrestling arenas** set up. There is also a story in Sikh sakhis that Humayun, fleeing after defeat by Sher Shah, met him at Khadur around 1540; but this account is found in sources written more than a hundred years after the event, so historians consider it doubtful.

## Succession and Legacy

Like his own Guru, Guru Angad Dev too chose as successor not his sons Dasu and Datu but the elderly and service-minded disciple **Amar Das**. He died at Khadur Sahib on **29 March 1552**. Guru Amar Das later made Goindwal the new centre.

Guru Angad Dev's time was short, but he laid the foundation for turning Guru Nanak's message into an organised community — through a script, a shared kitchen and succession based on merit.
