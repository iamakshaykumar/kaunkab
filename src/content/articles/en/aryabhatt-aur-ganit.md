## Who Was Aryabhata?

**Aryabhata** (also called "Aryabhata I" to distinguish him from another mathematician of the tenth century) was a great mathematician and astronomer of the Gupta period. He himself wrote that when 3600 years of the Kali Yuga had passed, he was 23 years old. From this calculation his birth is placed in **476 CE** and the composition of his work at about **499 CE**.

He mentions **Kusumapura**, which most scholars identify with Pataliputra (present-day Patna). The seventh-century commentator Bhaskara I calls him "Ashmakiya", so there are differing views about his birthplace.

## The Aryabhatiya: A Short Work with a Great Impact

The **Aryabhatiya** is a concise work written in Sanskrit verses, with a total of 121 verses divided into four parts (padas):
- **Gitikapada** — large numbers, units of time and basic planetary data.
- **Ganitapada** — arithmetic, algebra and geometry.
- **Kalakriyapada** — the reckoning of time and the motion of the planets.
- **Golapada** — the celestial sphere, the Earth and eclipses.

Later authors mention another work of his, the "Aryabhata-Siddhanta", but it is not available as an independent text.

## Contributions to Mathematics

- **Place-value system:** Aryabhata wrote that each following place is ten times the previous one. This shows a clear understanding of the decimal place-value system, which implies the idea of an "empty place" (later called zero). Note that the Aryabhatiya does not use a separate symbol for zero; the mathematical rules for treating zero as a number were given later by **Brahmagupta** in 628 CE.
- **Value of pi:** He stated that a circle of diameter 20,000 has a circumference of about 62,832 — that is, pi ≈ 3.1416. He called this the "asanna" (nearest) value.
- **Table of sines:** He gave a table of values of the jya (the sine of today's trigonometry). The journey of the English word "sine" is also traced from the Sanskrit "jya/jiva" through Arabic to Europe.
- **Kuttaka method:** A method of solving equations that have many integer solutions.
- It also contains rules for square roots, cube roots, areas and the sums of series.

## Contributions to Astronomy

- Aryabhata said that the stars appear to move from east to west because in reality the **Earth rotates on its axis**. He explained this with the example of a person sitting in a moving boat, to whom stationary things on the shore appear to move in the opposite direction.
- He stated that the Moon and the planets **shine by sunlight**.
- He gave the shadows of the Earth and the Moon as the cause of **eclipses**, not mythological causes such as Rahu and Ketu.
- The length of the year calculated by him was quite close to the modern value.

## Legacy

Many scholars, including Bhaskara I, wrote commentaries on the Aryabhatiya, and it deeply influenced the astronomical tradition of South India, especially Kerala. In the ninth century its ideas reached the Islamic world through Arabic translations.

Modern India has also honoured him: the country's first satellite, **Aryabhata** (1975), is named after him, and the astronomy research institute at Nainital, the "Aryabhatta Research Institute of Observational Sciences" (ARIES), is also linked to his memory.
