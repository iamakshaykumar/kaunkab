## Memories Made of Stone

In the hilly and plateau regions of South India, strange structures made of huge stones can still be seen in many places — a flat stone resting on upright slabs in one place, a circle of stones set in the ground in another, and a lone tall standing stone elsewhere. Most of these are **graves** or monuments built in memory of the dead. From the Greek words "mega" (big) and "lithos" (stone), they are called **megaliths**, and in Hindi **mahapashan**.

In India, such graves are found most often in the Deccan, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu and Kerala, and they are mainly linked to the **Iron Age**.

## From When to When

The date of the megalithic culture has long been debated. In the 1940s, **Mortimer Wheeler**, on the basis of his excavation at Brahmagiri, thought it was much later — from around the 2nd century BCE. Later discoveries and radiocarbon dates pushed it much further back. At sites such as **Hallur** in Karnataka, the use of iron seems to go back to about **1200–1000 BCE**. Broadly, the megalithic period of South India is taken to run from about 1200 BCE to the early centuries CE, though it began and ended at different times in different regions.

## Types of Graves

Megalithic graves are of many kinds:
- **Dolmen** — a large flat stone resting on upright stone slabs above the ground, like a table
- **Cist** — a box-like chamber made of stone slabs below the ground; one with a round hole in one slab is called a **port-hole cist**
- **Stone circle** (cairn circle) — a heap of stones over the grave with a circle of stones around it
- **Urn burial** — burial in large clay urns
- **Menhir** — a lone tall stone set up as a monument

Along with the body or bones, the graves contained **black-and-red ware** pottery, **iron** weapons and tools, beads and sometimes items of horse gear. These show that people believed in life after death.

## Brahmagiri

**Brahmagiri**, in the **Chitradurga district** of Karnataka, was made famous in 1891 by **B. L. Rice** through his discovery of Ashoka's inscriptions. In 1947 Wheeler excavated here and identified three cultural phases — Neolithic, Megalithic and Early Historic. About **300 graves** were found here, including rectangular stone cists and pits with stone circles. This excavation was the first attempt to place the megaliths of South India in a chronology.

## Adichanallur and Hire Benakal

**Adichanallur**, in the **Thoothukudi district** of Tamil Nadu, is a huge **urn-burial** site. In 1876 the German anthropologist **Jagor** excavated here, and between 1899 and 1905 **Alexander Rea** recovered objects of iron, bronze and gold from it. In excavations after 2004 led by **T. Sathyamurthy**, about 169 urns containing human skeletons were found. Radiocarbon dates from the samples here fall roughly between 1000 and 600 BCE; claims of even older dates are disputed.

On the hill of **Hire Benakal** (local name "Moriyar Gudda") in the **Koppal district** of Karnataka, hundreds of dolmens still stand, believed to date from about 800–200 BCE. The site is on the tentative list of UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

## Significance

The megalithic culture bears witness to the **spread of iron** in South India, the expansion of farming, and growing inequality in society — some graves are very large and rich, others plain. Many scholars regard this period as the background to the later Sangam age and the early kingdoms of the south.
