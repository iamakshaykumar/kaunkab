## Background: Disillusionment After Non-Cooperation

After the Chauri Chaura incident in February 1922, the sudden withdrawal of the Non-Cooperation Movement disappointed many young people. They felt that independence would not come quickly through a non-violent movement alone. These same young people founded the **Hindustan Republican Association (HRA)** in 1924. Among its main founders were **Sachindranath Sanyal** and **Ram Prasad Bismil**. The aim of the organisation was to establish a federal republic in India through armed revolution.

Money was needed for revolutionary activities — buying arms, printing propaganda material and running the organisation. Bismil and his comrades decided that instead of harming ordinary people, they would target the **government treasury** directly.

## 9 August 1925: Near Kakori

On **9 August 1925**, the government's railway cash was being carried in the 8 Down passenger train going from Shahjahanpur to Lucknow. At **Kakori** near Lucknow the revolutionaries stopped the train by pulling the chain.

- Along with Bismil, **Ashfaqulla Khan**, **Rajendra Lahiri**, **Chandrashekhar Azad**, Sachindra Bakshi, Manmathnath Gupta, Mukundi Lal, Keshab Chakravarty and other comrades took part in this action.
- The revolutionaries told the passengers that their target was only government money, not the passengers.
- An iron safe was taken down from the guard's compartment, and breaking it open took considerable time. The revolutionaries left for Lucknow with the money.
- Unfortunately, a passenger, Ahmad Ali, was killed by a shot fired during this time.

## Arrests and Trial

The British government regarded the event as a direct challenge to its authority. Within a few weeks raids took place across North India and dozens of people linked to the HRA were arrested. A long trial was held in Lucknow, called the 'Kakori Conspiracy Case'.

**Chandrashekhar Azad** did not fall into the hands of the police and, remaining in hiding, set about rebuilding the organisation. Ashfaqulla Khan also remained in hiding for some time but was later caught in Delhi.

The verdict came in 1927. **Ram Prasad Bismil**, **Ashfaqulla Khan**, **Rajendra Lahiri** and **Thakur Roshan Singh** were sentenced to death. Many others, including Sachindranath Sanyal and Sachindra Bakshi, were given Kala Pani (transportation for life) or long imprisonment. Appeals and petitions were made across the country to stop the hangings, but the government did not commute the sentences.

## December 1927: Four Martyrdoms

- **Rajendra Lahiri** was hanged on 17 December 1927 in **Gonda** Jail.
- **Ram Prasad Bismil** was hanged on 19 December 1927 in **Gorakhpur** Jail.
- **Ashfaqulla Khan** was hanged on 19 December 1927 in **Faizabad** Jail.
- **Thakur Roshan Singh** was hanged on 19 December 1927 in **Naini** Jail, Allahabad.

Before the hanging, Bismil wrote his autobiography in Gorakhpur Jail, which was smuggled out and later published.

## Historical Significance

The Kakori incident gave the revolutionary movement of North India a new identity. The friendship of Bismil and Ashfaqulla gave the message that there are no walls of religion in the fight for freedom. After this trial, Chandrashekhar Azad, together with Bhagat Singh and other young people, reorganised the organisation in 1928 as the **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)**. In 2021 the Uttar Pradesh government officially changed the name of the event to 'Kakori Train Action'.
