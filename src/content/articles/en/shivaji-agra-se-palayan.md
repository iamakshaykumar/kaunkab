## Background

After his victory over Afzal Khan at Pratapgarh in 1659 and the night raid on the Mughal governor Shaista Khan at the Lal Mahal in Pune in 1663, Shivaji had become a major challenge in the Deccan. In January 1664 he also plundered the Mughals' prosperous port of **Surat**. In response, in 1665 Aurangzeb sent the experienced Rajput commander **Mirza Raja Jai Singh**, together with Dilir Khan, to the Deccan with a large army.

## The siege of Purandar and the treaty

Instead of open battle, Jai Singh adopted a policy of encircling Shivaji's territories from all sides and reducing the forts one by one. After taking the nearby Vajragad in April 1665, the Mughal guns began to thunder on **Purandar**. Murarbaji Deshpande was killed defending the fort, and after heavy losses on the Maratha side, Shivaji himself chose the path of agreement by meeting Jai Singh.

The main terms of the **Treaty of Purandar**, concluded on **11 June 1665**, were:

- Shivaji handed over **23 forts**, including Purandar and Kondhana (Sinhagad), along with the territory attached to them, to the Mughals.
- He kept **12 forts**, provided he remained loyal to the Mughal emperor.
- The boy **Sambhaji** was to receive a Mughal mansab of five thousand.
- When needed, Shivaji would help the Mughals against Bijapur.

## The court at Agra

On Jai Singh's assurance, Shivaji went to Agra with Sambhaji. On **12 May 1666** he was presented at Aurangzeb's court. There he was made to stand in a row of mansabdars whom he considered beneath him; some were even commanders he had defeated in war. Not receiving the expected respect, Shivaji openly expressed his anger in the court itself and walked out. After this he was placed under house arrest in the charge of Jai Singh's son **Ram Singh**. In the court there was even talk of having him killed or sent far away; it is said that such an order was averted through Jai Singh's intervention.

## The escape

After several months of house arrest, in **August 1666** (the date usually given is 17 August, though there is a slight difference among the sources) Shivaji and Sambhaji slipped out of the guard.

The most famous story of how they got out is that of the **baskets of sweets and fruits**: Shivaji pretended to be ill and began to send out big baskets daily for charity, and one day father and son themselves left hidden in those baskets, while his associate Hiroji Farzand lay in his bed. This tale is **popular tradition**. According to the historian Stewart Gordon, no contemporary evidence is found for the basket story, and it is more likely that a way was found by bribing the guards. Aurangzeb suspected Ram Singh, but his complicity could not be proved.

According to tradition, Shivaji returned to Maharashtra in disguise by a long, winding route, while the boy Sambhaji was left for some time with trusted people in Mathura and was later brought back safely. By the end of 1666 Shivaji had reached Rajgad.

## Consequences

After the escape there were a few relatively peaceful years. Aurangzeb recognised Shivaji's title of 'Raja' and restored Sambhaji's mansab and gave him a jagir in Berar, but did not return the forts that had been handed over. Around 1670 relations worsened again, and within a few months Shivaji won back most of the forts lost in the Treaty of Purandar. The Agra episode also showed that Shivaji could not accept being a subordinate mansabdar in the Mughal court; this very road led on to the coronation of 1674.
