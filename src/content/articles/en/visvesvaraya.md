## From Muddenahalli to Engineer

Mokshagundam Visvesvaraya was born on **15 September 1861** in the village of **Muddenahalli** in the then **Kingdom of Mysore** (today in Chikkaballapur district of Karnataka). His father, Srinivasa Sastry, was a Sanskrit scholar who died when Visvesvaraya was still a boy. Amid financial hardship, he continued his studies by giving tuition.

He took his bachelor's degree (University of Madras) from **Central College** in Bengaluru and then studied **civil engineering** at the **College of Science** in Pune (today's College of Engineering, Pune), where he stood first in his class. People also respectfully call him **'Sir M. V.'**.

## Work in the Bombay Presidency: Automatic Gates

In **1885** he became an assistant engineer in the **Bombay Public Works Department**. Water shortage was a major problem in the dry regions of the Deccan. He adopted arrangements such as the **'block system'** for the fair distribution of irrigation water, and also worked on a water-supply scheme for the town of **Sukkur** in Sindh.

His most famous technical innovation was **automatic flood gates** for dams (automatic weir floodgates). These gates opened by themselves as soon as the water in a reservoir rose above a set level, and closed when the level fell. This allowed more water to be stored without raising the height of the dam. The gates were first installed in **1903** on the **Khadakwasla dam** near Pune, and were later used on the Tigra dam at Gwalior and on the Krishna Raja Sagara dam.

## Protecting Hyderabad from Floods

In **1908** a terrible flood of the **Musi river** caused great destruction in Hyderabad, and thousands of people lost their lives. The Nizam's government invited Visvesvaraya for advice. He drew up a **flood-protection** and drainage plan for the city, which proposed building reservoirs on the Musi and its tributary. On the basis of these suggestions, the **Osman Sagar** and **Himayat Sagar** reservoirs were later built, which for a long time protected Hyderabad from floods and supplied drinking water. He also worked on a plan to protect Visakhapatnam port from sea erosion.

## Diwan of Mysore and the Krishna Raja Sagara Dam

In **1909** he became **Chief Engineer** of the Mysore state, and from **1912** to **1918** he was the **Diwan** (chief administrator) of Maharaja **Krishnaraja Wadiyar IV**. During this period his greatest project, the **Krishna Raja Sagara (KRS) dam**, began to take shape. Construction of this dam on the **Kaveri river** in Mandya district began in **1911** and was completed around **1931**. Despite a shortage of funds and objections from the Madras Presidency, Visvesvaraya pushed the project forward. The dam brought irrigation to thousands of acres in Mandya and nearby areas, supplied water to Mysore and Bengaluru, and later the famous **Brindavan Gardens** were laid out beside it.

As Diwan he laid stress on industry and education in the state. His famous slogan was **"Industrialise or Perish"**. During his tenure, institutions such as the **Mysore Soap Factory**, the iron and steel works at **Bhadravati**, the **Bank of Mysore** (later the State Bank of Mysore), the **University of Mysore** and the Government Engineering College in Bengaluru were started or had their foundations laid. This is why he is called **'the maker of modern Mysore'**.

## Honours, Engineers' Day and Legacy

The British government knighted him (KCIE) in **1915**. He was president of the **Indian Science Congress** in **1923**. He wrote books such as **'Reconstructing India'** (1920) and **'Planned Economy for India'**, which argued for the planned economic development of India — an idea later seen in the Five-Year Plans of independent India. In **1955** he was awarded the country's highest civilian honour, the **Bharat Ratna**.

He died in Bengaluru on **14 April 1962** at the age of about 101. In **1968** the Government of India decided to celebrate his birthday, **15 September**, as **Engineers' Day**. Karnataka's **Visvesvaraya Technological University**, Bengaluru's **Visvesvaraya Industrial and Technological Museum** and many other institutions are named after him.

Visvesvaraya was also famous for his honesty, punctuality and discipline. He lived very simply and continued to read, write and work regularly even in old age. He showed that engineering is not merely the work of building structures and dams, but a means of improving people's lives and of building the nation.
