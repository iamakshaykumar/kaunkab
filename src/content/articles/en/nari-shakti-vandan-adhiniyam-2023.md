## The New Parliament Building

The old **Parliament House**, built in the British period, was completed in 1927. The government said that this nearly hundred-year-old circular building was becoming too small for today's needs, that it was difficult to add modern security and technical facilities to it, and that there would not be enough seating when the number of MPs rose after a future delimitation. So it was decided to build a new building under the **Central Vista Redevelopment Project**.

Prime Minister **Narendra Modi** laid its foundation stone on **10 December 2020**. Architect **Bimal Patel** designed it and **Tata Projects** built it. In the triangular four-storey building, the **Lok Sabha chamber** has **888** seats and the **Rajya Sabha chamber** **384**. The decoration of the Lok Sabha chamber is inspired by the national bird, the peacock, and that of the Rajya Sabha chamber by the national flower, the lotus.

On **28 May 2023** the Prime Minister inaugurated the building and installed the **Sengol** (a sceptre) beside the Lok Sabha Speaker's chair. According to the government, the Sengol was a symbol of the transfer of power in 1947. The inauguration was also controversial — **19 opposition parties**, including the Congress, issued a joint statement and boycotted the ceremony. They said that, as the President is part of Parliament, the building should have been inaugurated by President **Droupadi Murmu**. The government called these objections unjustified. On **19 September 2023**, during a special session, the proceedings of Parliament formally began in the new building.

## The Long Wait for Women's Reservation

After the **73rd and 74th Amendments** to the Constitution (1992), women had received one-third of the seats in panchayats and municipal bodies, but women's participation in Parliament and the assemblies remained low. In the 2019 Lok Sabha only about 15 per cent of MPs were women.

The first women's reservation bill in Parliament was introduced in **1996** by the government of Prime Minister **H. D. Deve Gowda**. Several attempts were made after this under the Atal Bihari Vajpayee government as well, but no consensus could be reached. In **2008** the Manmohan Singh government introduced a new bill, which the Rajya Sabha passed on **9 March 2010**; but it could not be taken up in the Lok Sabha and lapsed with the dissolution of the Lok Sabha in **2014**. The main demand of some of the opposing parties was a separate quota within the reservation for women from the Other Backward Classes (OBC).

## The Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, 2023

On **19 September 2023**, as the first bill in the new Parliament building, Law Minister **Arjun Ram Meghwal** introduced the **Constitution (128th Amendment) Bill**, named the **"Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam"**. The Lok Sabha passed it on **20 September** by **454 votes to 2** and the Rajya Sabha on **21 September** by **214–0**. After President Droupadi Murmu's assent on **28 September 2023** it became the **Constitution (106th Amendment) Act, 2023**. Its main provisions are:

- About **one-third of the seats** in the **Lok Sabha, the state legislative assemblies** and the **Delhi assembly** will be reserved for women.
- Of the seats reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes too, one-third will go to women of those groups.
- The reservation will take effect after a **delimitation** based on the **first census** held after this Act, and will last for **15 years**; the reserved seats will rotate after each delimitation.
- The **Rajya Sabha** and the state legislative councils are not covered by it.

## Support, Criticism and the Road Ahead

The government called it a historic step towards women-led development. Almost all parties supported the bill, but many opposition parties objected that because of the condition of a census and delimitation, the reservation would not be implemented for several years. Some parties again raised the demand for a sub-quota for OBC women, and some questioned the exclusion of the Rajya Sabha.

According to reports, the government issued a notification on **16 April 2026** bringing the Act into force, but the reservation still depended on the census and delimitation. To implement the reservation before the 2029 election, the government introduced the **Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill**, which would have increased the number of Lok Sabha seats, but on **17 April 2026** it failed to get the required two-thirds majority in the Lok Sabha (298 votes for, 230 against). Opposition parties said they supported women's reservation but opposed linking it to delimitation.

## Significance

The new Parliament building and the first major constitutional amendment passed in it together made 2023 a symbolic year in the history of Indian democracy. Giving legal form to women's reservation, stuck for nearly three decades, was a major achievement, although its real impact will be seen only when elections are held on the reserved seats. Until then, the law remains a test for political parties to give more tickets to women and increase their participation in leadership.
