## Background: Mumtaz Mahal and Shah Jahan

**Mumtaz Mahal**'s original name was Arjumand Banu Begum. She was the daughter of Asaf Khan, the brother of Nur Jahan. In 1612 she married Prince Khurram, who became the Mughal emperor in 1628 under the name **Shah Jahan**. Mumtaz Mahal often accompanied the emperor even on campaigns.

In 1631, during the Deccan campaign, she died at **Burhanpur** after the birth of her fourteenth child. Shah Jahan, steeped in grief, decided to build a grand mausoleum in her memory.

## Site and Plan

For the mausoleum a plot on the southern bank of the **Yamuna River** at Agra was chosen, which belonged to Raja Jai Singh of Amber. In exchange the emperor gave him other mansions in Agra — this detail is found in court records.

The whole complex was built on a carefully thought-out plan:
- in the south the entrance gate (Darwaza-i-Rauza) and beyond it a bazaar and caravanserais,
- in the middle the **Charbagh** garden divided into four parts, with canals and fountains,
- in the north, on a high platform by the river, the main mausoleum with four minarets,
- on both sides of the mausoleum a mosque of red sandstone and a building matching it (the jawab).

## Construction: Craftsmen and Materials

Construction began in about 1632. The main mausoleum was completed in the 1640s and the whole complex was ready by about 1653; different sources give different years for its completion.

- The name of **Ustad Ahmad Lahori** is often mentioned as the chief architect.
- It is estimated that about 20,000 craftsmen, masons, stone-cutters, calligraphers and labourers worked on it.
- The white marble was brought mainly from **Makrana** in Rajasthan; the red sandstone came from nearby quarries.
- For the inlay work, precious and semi-precious stones were brought from different regions.
- Verses of the Quran were engraved in beautiful calligraphy on the gateway and the mausoleum; the name of the calligrapher **Amanat Khan** is inscribed on the building.

## Features of the Architecture

The Taj Mahal is a confluence of Persian, Central Asian and Indian architectural traditions. Its design developed from earlier Mughal buildings such as Humayun's Tomb, but in balance and proportion it surpassed them.

- A large double dome (separate inner and outer layers),
- minarets at the four corners, which are believed to have been built leaning slightly outward,
- floral carving on marble and **pachchikari** (inlay work),
- precise symmetry left and right throughout the building.

At different times of day and in moonlight the colour of the marble seems to change, which is one reason for its fame.

## Later History and Legacy

In 1658 Shah Jahan's son Aurangzeb took power and Shah Jahan spent his last years in confinement in the Agra Fort. After his death in 1666 he was buried in the Taj Mahal next to the grave of Mumtaz Mahal.

In 1983 the Taj Mahal was declared a **UNESCO World Heritage Site**. Today its conservation is carried out by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI). In view of the problem of yellowing of the marble from air pollution, strict rules on pollution apply in the area around it. Every year millions of Indian and foreign tourists come to see it, and it has become India's most recognised building.
