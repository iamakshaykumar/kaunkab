## A Girl from the Village of Chondi

Ahilyabai was born on **31 May 1725** in a simple Dhangar family in the village of **Chondi** in today's Ahmednagar (Ahilyanagar) district of Maharashtra. According to tradition, the Maratha chief **Malharrao Holkar**, impressed during a journey by the young Ahilya's simplicity and understanding, decided to make her his daughter-in-law. In **1733** she was married to Malharrao's son **Khanderao**. Malharrao was among the chief commanders of Peshwa Baji Rao I and had been granted a large jagir in Malwa, centred on Indore.

## One Blow After Another

In **1754**, Khanderao was killed during the siege of the Jat fort of Kumher. It is said that Ahilyabai wanted to become sati, but her father-in-law Malharrao stopped her and taught her statecraft and military management. Malharrao died in **1766**, and the next year, **1767**, his only son **Maleerao** also died after a reign of only a few months.

After this some people tried to stake a claim to the Holkar state, but Ahilyabai obtained the Peshwa's permission to keep the authority to rule in her own hands. She appointed the experienced chief **Tukojirao Holkar** as commander — the army and campaigns were Tukoji's responsibility, while administration, treasury and policy were run by Ahilyabai herself.

## Ruling from Maheshwar

Ahilyabai made **Maheshwar**, on the Narmada, her residence and seat of government. She held court every day and heard her subjects' complaints herself. In her time the tax system was kept relatively mild and clear, merchants and moneylenders got protection, and agriculture expanded. Weavers were settled at Maheshwar, which made its saris famous. Indore, then a small town, began to grow into a prosperous trading city in her time.

She did not stay entirely away from war either. She sent military expeditions to suppress revolts in some parts of the state and to control bands of robbers. Arrangements were also made with communities such as the Bhils and Gonds so that they could collect a fee in return for protecting the routes.

## Temples, Ghats and Dharamshalas

Ahilyabai is remembered most of all for her construction works. Not only in her own state but at pilgrimage sites across the country, she built or repaired temples, ghats, wells, stepwells, dharamshalas and roads. The most famous of these is the rebuilding of the **Kashi Vishwanath Temple** in **1780** — the basic structure of the present temple is this one. A ghat in Varanasi is also called 'Ahilyabai Ghat' after her. Her works are also mentioned at Somnath, Gaya, Dwarka, Rameswaram and other pilgrimage centres, although the extent of her role differed from site to site. This expenditure came mainly from her personal wealth (khasgi), not from the state treasury.

## Death and Legacy

Ahilyabai died at Maheshwar on **13 August 1795**, and Tukojirao Holkar became her successor. The British officer-historian **John Malcolm** praised her rule highly in his work on Malwa, and **Jawaharlal Nehru** wrote in 'The Discovery of India' that her rule had order and good governance and that her subjects prospered. A postage stamp was issued on her in 1996. In the war-torn eighteenth century, Ahilyabai's rule is remembered as an example of peace, justice and public welfare.
