## The Atmosphere after 1857

After **the revolt of 1857**, the Muslim society of north India suffered a deep shock. The Mughal emperor **Bahadur Shah Zafar** was exiled, many old madrasas and scholarly families of Delhi were ruined, and British officials began to hold Muslims largely responsible for the revolt. At the same time the influence of English education and Christian missionaries was growing.

In this situation Muslim scholars faced the question of how to preserve their religious education and identity. Two major answers emerged. On one side, **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan** stressed modern, English education at Aligarh. On the other, the scholars of Deoband chose to preserve traditional Islamic education in a new, organised form. These scholars were linked to the scholarly tradition of **Shah Waliullah** of Delhi.

## A Beginning in the Chhatta Masjid

On **15 Muharram 1283 AH**, that is **31 May 1866**, an Arabic madrasa began in the **Chhatta Masjid** of **Deoband**, a town in Saharanpur district (some writers also give the date as 30 May 1866 or 1867). Early sources describe **Haji Sayyid Muhammad Abid** as its first proposer, who collected donations from the people of the town. **Maulana Muhammad Qasim Nanautavi** gave it intellectual and organisational direction, and later writers regard him as its principal founder. **Maulana Rashid Ahmad Gangohi**, Maulana Zulfiqar Ali Deobandi and Maulana Fazlur Rahman Usmani are also counted among the founders.

The first teacher was **Mulla Mahmud Deobandi**, who was paid 15 rupees a month. Most accounts name **Mahmud Hasan** as the first student, who later became the institution's most famous teacher. According to a popular story, the first lesson was held under a **pomegranate tree** in the mosque courtyard.

## The Institution's Identity

Deoband's greatest feature was that it ran not on **government grants** but on small **donations** from ordinary people. This kept it independent of the control of the British government and of any single wealthy patron. Teaching was based on the **Dars-i Nizami** curriculum, which included the Quran, Hadith, Islamic law (fiqh, of the Hanafi school), the Arabic language, logic and other subjects.

Old madrasas usually ran in the house of a single scholar or in a mosque, whereas Deoband adopted the structure of an institution — a fixed curriculum, classes, examinations, a library, salaried teachers and hostels. Students came from far and wide. The foundation of a new campus was laid in 1875, and the first separate building was completed in 1879. A department for issuing fatwas (Darul Ifta) was also set up here, which answered people's religious questions.

## Links with the Freedom Movement

Many Deoband scholars were opponents of British rule. During the First World War, **Maulana Mahmud Hasan** (called **Shaikh-ul-Hind**) and **Maulana Ubaidullah Sindhi** planned to gather foreign help against the British, a plan known as the **Silk Letter Movement** (1913–1920). Its secret letters were written on silk cloth. The Punjab police captured these letters. Mahmud Hasan was arrested in the Hijaz (Mecca) and sent to **Malta**, where his disciple **Hussain Ahmad Madani** was also interned with him.

Later, scholars linked to Deoband raised the demand for independence together with the Congress from the platform of the **Jamiat Ulama-e-Hind**. **Maulana Hussain Ahmad Madani** opposed the Muslim League's demand for partition on religious lines and supported a composite nationalism (muttahida qaumiyat). However, not all scholars linked to Deoband were of one opinion; some, such as Maulana Shabbir Ahmad Usmani, supported the demand for Pakistan.

## Legacy

Today Darul Uloom Deoband is counted among the largest and most influential centres of Islamic learning in South Asia; its admirers place it second only to Al-Azhar in Egypt. A whole school of thought is named **"Deobandi"** after it, with madrasas spread across India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Afghanistan and as far as Britain and South Africa.

Some of the institution's fatwas have drawn public debate and criticism from time to time, while in 2008 it made headlines by issuing a fatwa against terrorism. Historically, the founding of Deoband was part of the period of reform and renaissance in Indian society after 1857, in which different communities tried to reorganise their education and culture in new ways under changing circumstances.
