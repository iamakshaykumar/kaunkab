## Background

After the **2004** and **2009** elections, the Congress-led **United Progressive Alliance (UPA)** governed at the Centre, with **Dr Manmohan Singh** as Prime Minister. In the last years of the second term the pace of economic growth slowed, inflation remained high, and debate broke out over allegations of corruption in matters such as the 2G spectrum and coal-block allocations. **Anna Hazare's** Lokpal movement of 2011 also influenced the political climate, and the **Aam Aadmi Party**, which emerged from it, entered a national election for the first time.

In September 2013 the **Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)** declared Gujarat Chief Minister **Narendra Modi** its prime ministerial candidate. The Congress campaign was led by vice-president **Rahul Gandhi**, although the party did not formally declare anyone its prime ministerial candidate.

## The World's Largest Election

Chief Election Commissioner **V. S. Sampath** announced the election schedule on **5 March 2014**. Voting was held in **nine phases** from **7 April to 12 May 2014**. At that time there were about **834 million** registered voters, the largest election in the world until then. **8,251** candidates contested the **543** seats. About **1.4 million** electronic voting machines were used at around **930,000** polling stations.

Overall turnout was **66.4 per cent**, the highest level for any general election up to that time. About **554 million** people cast their votes. Following a 2013 Supreme Court judgment, this was the first Lok Sabha election with a **NOTA** (None of the Above) option on the EVM. New campaign methods such as social media, huge rallies and 3D hologram meetings were also used extensively in this election.

## The Results

Votes were counted on **16 May 2014**. The main results were as follows:

- **BJP** — **282** seats, about **31 per cent** of the vote. For the first time since 1984 a single party won a majority (more than 272) in the Lok Sabha on its own.
- **National Democratic Alliance (NDA)** — **336** seats in total, about 38.5 per cent of the vote.
- **Congress** — only **44** seats and about **19.3 per cent** of the vote, the party's weakest performance ever. The UPA as a whole was limited to 59 seats.
- Among regional parties, the **AIADMK** (J. Jayalalithaa) won **37**, the **Trinamool Congress** (Mamata Banerjee) **34** and the **Biju Janata Dal** (Naveen Patnaik) **20** seats.

The BJP won 71 of the 80 seats in **Uttar Pradesh**. Narendra Modi won from both **Varanasi** and **Vadodara** and later gave up the Vadodara seat. In several states and union territories, including Gujarat, Rajasthan, Delhi and Tamil Nadu, the Congress could not win a single seat.

Among the NDA allies, the **Shiv Sena** won 18 seats and the **Telugu Desam Party** 16, while the **Telangana Rashtra Samithi** won 11 seats. The **Aam Aadmi Party**, contesting a national election for the first time, fielded candidates in hundreds of seats but won only 4 seats in Punjab. About **6 million** voters (1.08%) pressed the NOTA button.

## The New Government

On **26 May 2014**, at Rashtrapati Bhavan, **Narendra Modi** took the oath as India's **14th Prime Minister**. For the first time, the heads of government of all the **SAARC** countries were invited to the ceremony, and Pakistan's Prime Minister **Nawaz Sharif** also attended. No opposition party had 10 per cent of the Lok Sabha seats, so no one received official **Leader of the Opposition** status in the 16th Lok Sabha; the Congress's **Mallikarjun Kharge** remained the leader of the largest opposition party in the House.

## Analysis and Significance

Analysts gave several reasons for this result. According to the BJP and its supporters, it was a mandate for the promise of development, good governance and strong leadership. Many political analysts highlighted discontent with the UPA government, inflation, the corruption allegations, the leanings of young and first-time voters, and the role of a personality-centred campaign. Critics said that winning 282 seats with 31 per cent of the vote showed a feature of the 'first-past-the-post' system, in which there can be a large gap between vote share and seats.

The **2014** election is regarded as a turning point in Indian politics. After an era of coalition governments lasting about **30 years**, a single party won a clear majority, and the high turnout showed the strength of public participation in Indian democracy. In the **2019** election that followed, the BJP increased its seats further, while in **2024** it fell short of a majority and formed the government with its allies.
