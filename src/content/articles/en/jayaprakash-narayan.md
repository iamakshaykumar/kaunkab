## From Sitab Diara to America

**Jayaprakash Narayan**, affectionately called **"JP"** and **"Lok Nayak"** (People's Leader), was born on **11 October 1902** in the village of **Sitab Diara** in Bihar. In 1920 he married **Prabhavati Devi**, who later lived in Gandhiji's ashram and remained a Gandhian all her life. During the Non-Cooperation Movement, JP left his English-medium college.

In 1922 he went to **America** to study. Working on farms and in factories, he studied at the universities of **California (Berkeley), Iowa, Wisconsin** and **Ohio**. During this time he was influenced by **Marxism**. Returning to India in 1929, he joined the Congress.

## A Socialist Warrior of the Freedom Struggle

In **1934**, together with colleagues such as **Acharya Narendra Dev** and **Ram Manohar Lohia**, JP formed the **Congress Socialist Party** within the Congress. During the **Quit India Movement** in **1942**, he and his companions escaped by scaling the wall of **Hazaribagh Jail** and ran the movement from underground. This episode made him a hero to the young.

After independence JP kept his distance from power politics. In 1954 he joined **Vinoba Bhave's** **Bhoodan** and **Sarvodaya** movement. In 1965 he received the **Ramon Magsaysay Award**, and in **1972**, through his efforts, hundreds of dacoits of the **Chambal** surrendered.

## The Beginning of the Bihar Movement

In the early 1970s there was widespread discontent in the country over **inflation, unemployment** and **corruption**. In early 1974, under pressure from the students' **Navnirman Andolan** in Gujarat, the state government had to resign. Inspired by this, the **Bihar Chhatra Sangharsh Samiti** was formed in Bihar in February 1974, with **Lalu Prasad Yadav** as its president.

On **18 March 1974** students surrounded the Assembly in Patna. Violence and police firing followed, in which several people were killed. The students asked JP to lead them. JP accepted the leadership on the condition that the movement would remain **peaceful** and would not belong to any party. The movement's main demands were that the government of Chief Minister **Abdul Ghafoor** should go and that the **Bihar Assembly be dissolved**.

## Gandhi Maidan and 'Total Revolution'

On **5 June 1974**, at a huge rally at Patna's **Gandhi Maidan**, JP declared that the movement was not merely to change one government but for **"Total Revolution"** (Sampoorna Kranti). According to him this revolution had seven aspects — **political, economic, social, cultural, intellectual, educational** and **spiritual**. He stressed that along with changing the system, a change in the way people think and live was also necessary — such as giving up caste practices and dowry.

After this the movement intensified. Hundreds were arrested, schools and colleges stayed closed for months, and legislators were pressed to resign. On **4 November 1974**, during a demonstration in Patna, police action took place in which JP too was injured. JP began bringing non-Congress parties — such as the **Bharatiya Jana Sangh**, the **Congress (O)** and the socialists — onto one platform, and the movement began to take a national form.

The government and critics of the movement said that JP was demanding the removal of elected governments through street pressure, which was not in keeping with the democratic process. Prime Minister **Indira Gandhi** described the movement as one spreading instability. Supporters argued that it was peaceful popular resistance against corruption.

## The Emergency and the Janata Party

On **12 June 1975** the **Allahabad High Court** set aside Indira Gandhi's 1971 election. On **25 June 1975**, at a rally at Delhi's **Ramlila Maidan**, JP recited **Ramdhari Singh 'Dinkar's'** line **"Vacate the throne, for the people are coming"** and demanded the Prime Minister's resignation. That same night the **Emergency** was declared in the country, and many opposition leaders, including JP, were arrested. In jail his kidneys were badly damaged and he had to remain on dialysis for the rest of his life.

When elections were announced in 1977, the opposition parties came together under JP's guidance to form the **Janata Party**. The Janata Party won the March 1977 election, and under **Morarji Desai** the first non-Congress government was formed at the Centre. JP himself took no office.

## Legacy

JP died in Patna on **8 October 1979**. In **1999** he was posthumously awarded the **Bharat Ratna**. Many institutions, including Patna airport, are named after him.

'Total Revolution' could not fully achieve its grand goals, and the Janata Party government also fell in 1979 because of internal differences. Even so, the JP movement showed the power of mass movements in Indian democracy, and many leaders of Bihar and the country entered politics through this movement. JP is still remembered as a leader who stayed away from power while challenging it.
