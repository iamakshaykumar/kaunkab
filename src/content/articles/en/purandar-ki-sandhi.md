## Background: From Shaista Khan to Jai Singh

In the early 1660s, Shivaji's growing power became a worry for the Mughal emperor **Aurangzeb**. Shivaji's night attack in 1663 on the Mughal governor Shaista Khan in Pune, and the sack of Surat in 1664, badly damaged Mughal prestige. Aurangzeb then sent one of his most experienced commanders, **Mirza Raja Jai Singh I** of Amber, to the Deccan. The Afghan general **Dilir Khan** accompanied him.

Jai Singh used diplomacy as well as direct battle. He put pressure on the regions around Shivaji and tried to win over many of his allies and opponents.

## The Siege of Purandar

In **1665** the Mughal army besieged the high **Purandar Fort**, about **50 kilometres** south-east of Pune. In mid-April the nearby fort of **Vajragad** fell to the Mughals, and from there cannon shells were rained on Purandar. **Murarbaji Deshpande**, the fort's commander, fought bravely and was killed defending it. A statue of him stands at the fort today.

Facing continuous bombardment and heavy losses, and seeing the danger of the surrounding lands being laid waste, Shivaji chose the path of negotiation. He went in person to meet Jai Singh in his camp.

## Main Terms of the Treaty

The Treaty of Purandar was concluded in **June 1665** (most sources give the date as **11 June**, some 12 June). Its main terms were:

- Shivaji handed **23 forts** to the Mughals, including **Purandar**, **Kondhana** (later Sinhagad), Lohagad and Rudramal.
- Shivaji kept **12 forts** and the territory around them.
- His son **Sambhaji** was given a mansab of **5,000** in Mughal service.
- Shivaji promised to help the Mughals when needed; his participation in the campaign against Bijapur was also agreed.
- If Shivaji wished to claim lands in the Konkan under Bijapur, he had to pay the Mughals **40 lakh hons** in instalments.

The sources differ on the revenue figures — the income of the territory left with Shivaji is given as one lakh hons in some places and four lakh hons in others.

## After the Treaty

After the treaty the siege was lifted. Jai Singh allowed about **7,000** people trapped inside Purandar, among them around **4,000** Maratha soldiers, to leave safely, and Shivaji handed over the agreed forts to the Mughals. In his letters Shivaji presented himself as one begging forgiveness — this was the diplomatic language of the time, meant to preserve the core of his power somehow. On **15 September 1665** Aurangzeb formally pardoned Shivaji. Shivaji also took part with Jai Singh in the campaign against Bijapur, but that campaign was not successful for the Mughals.

At Jai Singh's urging, Shivaji went to **Agra** in **1666** to meet Aurangzeb. There he faced humiliation at court and then house arrest, from which he made a dramatic escape — an episode that is a separate story. Aurangzeb held Jai Singh's son **Ram Singh** responsible for this event.

## Significance and Assessment

The Treaty of Purandar is often regarded as the hardest settlement of Shivaji's life. Historians view it in two ways — on one side it was a clear victory for Mughal military power and Jai Singh's diplomacy; on the other, Shivaji bought time instead of admitting defeat and saved his army and his main forts. The treaty did not last long. In **1670** Shivaji began taking back the lost forts, the most famous event being the capture of Kondhana by **Tanaji Malusare**. In this way, the defeat at Purandar itself became the background to the new rise of Maratha power.
