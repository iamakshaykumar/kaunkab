## Childhood and the Name 'Azad'

Chandrashekhar was born on **23 July 1906** in the village of **Bhabra** (today in Madhya Pradesh) in the then princely state of Alirajpur. His original name was Chandrashekhar Tiwari; his family's roots are said to be linked to the village of Badarka in Unnao district. Some local claims about his birthplace and date differ, but this is the account generally accepted. As a teenager he was sent to **Banaras** to study Sanskrit.

In 1921, during the Non-Cooperation Movement, the 15-year-old Chandrashekhar was arrested. Before the magistrate he gave his name as **'Azad'** (free), his father's name as 'Swatantrata' (independence) and his home as 'Jail' — this episode is very famous in popular tradition. He was sentenced to flogging, and from then on he came to be called 'Azad'.

## The HRA and Kakori

In 1922, disappointed by the withdrawal of the Non-Cooperation Movement, Azad joined **Ram Prasad Bismil**'s **Hindustan Republican Association (HRA)**. He took part in the **Kakori train action** of 9 August 1925. In the arrests that followed, Bismil, Ashfaqulla, Lahiri and Roshan Singh were hanged, but Azad did not fall into the hands of the police. In disguise, sometimes as a sadhu and sometimes under other names, he kept working to rebuild the organisation.

## Commander of the HSRA

At the meeting held at Feroz Shah Kotla in Delhi on **8–9 September 1928**, the organisation was renamed the **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)**. Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and other young men brought socialist ideas into it, and Azad was regarded as commander-in-chief of its military wing.

- In December 1928, to avenge the death of Lala Lajpat Rai, the Assistant Superintendent of Police **Saunders** was killed in Lahore. In this action Azad helped his comrades get away safely and shot Head Constable Chanan Singh, who was chasing them.
- An attempt to blow up the Viceroy's train in 1929 was also among the HSRA's activities.
- After the arrest of Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru, Azad continued his efforts to free them and to keep the organisation alive.

## 27 February 1931: Alfred Park

On **27 February 1931** Azad was meeting a comrade in **Alfred Park** in Allahabad. On information from an informer, the police under the officer **J. R. H. Nott-Bower** surrounded the park. Taking cover behind a tree, Azad fought back for a long time and gave his comrade the chance to escape.

He had vowed that he would never fall alive into police hands. He fired the last bullet at himself. At that time he was only **24 years old**. Historians and memoirs make differing claims about who informed the police, and this question remains disputed even today.

## Legacy

Only a few weeks after Azad's martyrdom, on 23 March 1931, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were hanged, and the HSRA organisation gradually fell apart. Even so, Azad's discipline, courage and resolve never to be captured alive made him the most beloved hero of the revolutionary movement of north India. Alfred Park is today called **Chandrashekhar Azad Park**, and in 1988 India Post issued a postage stamp on him.
