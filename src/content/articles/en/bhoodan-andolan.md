## Background: The Question of Land

At the time of independence, most of India's population depended on farming, but land was distributed very unequally. Millions of agricultural labourers — among whom Dalits were many — had no land of their own. In the Telangana region of Hyderabad state, an armed peasant struggle led by Communists had been going on since 1946, in which landlords' lands were seized and distributed, and violence was committed by both sides.

**Acharya Vinoba Bhave** (1895–1982) was one of Gandhiji's closest disciples; in 1940 Gandhiji had chosen him as the first satyagrahi of the individual satyagraha. After Gandhiji's death he was carrying forward the idea of 'Sarvodaya' — the uplift of all.

## Pochampally, 18 April 1951

In 1951 Vinoba set out on foot on a peace march through the troubled areas of Telangana. On **18 April 1951**, in the village of **Pochampally** in Nalgonda district, landless Dalit families asked him for about 80 acres of land for farming. When Vinoba put this question before the villagers, the local landowner **Vedire Ramachandra Reddy** offered to give 100 acres of land (the sources differ somewhat in the acreage figures).

Vinoba saw a new path in this: that land might be redistributed neither by violence nor only by law, but by a change of heart and voluntary gift. From here the **Bhoodan** — 'gift of land' — movement began.

## The Padyatra and Expansion

After this Vinoba made journeys on foot across the country for years. Going from village to village, he asked landowners to regard him as a member of their family and give a part of their land — often a sixth — for the landless. He set a large goal of collecting fifty million acres of land for the whole country. The movement received special support in **Bihar** and Uttar Pradesh. Leaders such as **Jayaprakash Narayan** also joined the Sarvodaya movement. Several states made Bhoodan laws and Bhoodan boards to manage the donated land; the rule was that the land received could not be sold and would be used only for farming.

## Gramdan

In 1952 the movement took a new form — **Gramdan**, in which an entire village handed over the ownership of its land collectively to the village. The first Gramdan village is believed to be **Mangroth** in Uttar Pradesh. In later years, especially in Odisha and Bihar, thousands of villages announced Gramdan, although many of them remained on paper only.

## Achievements and Limits

According to estimates, a total of about **4.2–4.4 million acres** of land was announced as donated in Bhoodan, but only about **1.3 million acres** of it could actually be distributed among the landless (the figures differ according to the sources). Much of the donated land was barren, stony or tangled in legal disputes, and many gifts remained limited to announcements. By the 1960s the pace of the movement had slowed.

## Significance

Bhoodan did not reach its great goal, yet it was a unique experiment of independent India — an attempt to solve the land question through nonviolence, moral appeal and public participation. It carried the debate on land reform to every village and got some land for millions of landless families. Vinoba Bhave's padyatra is still regarded as an inspiration for Gandhian social movements.
