## From the Phadnis Family to the Peshwa's Court

Nana Phadnavis's original name was **Balaji Janardan Bhanu**. He was born on **12 February 1742** in **Satara**. His Bhanu family had held the post of **'Phadnis'** — the chief accountant of accounts and revenue — under the Peshwas for generations. This title later became attached to his name.

In **1761**, the young Nana was with the Maratha army at the **Third Battle of Panipat**. In that heavy defeat many Maratha chiefs were killed, but Nana escaped and returned. After that, under Peshwa **Madhavrao I** (1761–1772), he rose in revenue and administration.

## The Murder of Narayanrao and the 'Barbhai'

After the death of Madhavrao I in 1772, his younger brother **Narayanrao** became Peshwa, but on **30 August 1773** he was murdered in the Shaniwar Wada in Pune. His uncle **Raghunathrao** (Raghoba) was believed to have played a part in the plot, although historians have continued to debate the extent of his personal responsibility.

Raghunathrao wanted to become Peshwa, but Narayanrao's widow Gangabai was pregnant. Nana Phadnavis, Sakharam Bapu and some other chiefs together formed a council of regents, which was called the **'Barbhai'** (a body of twelve brothers). The infant **Sawai Madhavrao**, born in April 1774, was declared Peshwa, and the running of the state passed into the hands of this council. Gradually the other members of the council fell behind, and Nana became the most influential man in the Pune court.

## The First Struggle with the British

Raghunathrao, pushed out of power, sought help from the British at Bombay (Treaty of Surat, 1775). This started the **First Anglo-Maratha War** (1775–1782). Nana separately concluded the **Treaty of Purandar** (1776) with the Company government at Calcutta, but the Bombay government did not fully accept it. In 1779 the British army had to admit defeat at **Wadgaon**. Finally, through the mediation of **Mahadji Shinde**, the **Treaty of Salbai** was made on **17 May 1782**, which the Pune court later accepted. After this, there was no major war between the Marathas and the British for about twenty years.

## Administration, Rivalry and Kharda

The basis of Nana's power was his **financial control**, a network of spies and a vast system of correspondence. His rivalry with the growing strength of Mahadji Shinde in North India was also well known, yet the two supported each other when needed. After Mahadji's death in 1794, in **March 1795** at the Battle of **Kharda**, the combined army of the Maratha confederacy defeated the **Nizam** — this is regarded as the last major joint success of the Maratha chiefs.

## Final Years and Legacy

In **October 1795**, the young Sawai Madhavrao died after falling from a height in the palace. Raghunathrao's son **Baji Rao II** became Peshwa, and Nana's relations with him remained strained. Amid court intrigues, **Daulat Rao Shinde** even imprisoned Nana for a few months in 1797–98. After his release he returned to administration, but he died in Pune on **13 March 1800**.

European writers, because of his shrewdness, called him the **"Maratha Machiavelli"**. Critics believe that his politics of holding on to power kept the Peshwa weak and the court full of intrigues. Even so, most historians agree that during his lifetime the Maratha confederacy continued to appear united before the British. Within two years of his death, in 1802, Baji Rao II concluded the **Treaty of Bassein** with the British, which led to the Second Anglo-Maratha War.
