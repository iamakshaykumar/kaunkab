## Background: Babur's Successor

**Humayun** (born 1508) became the Mughal emperor in December 1530 after the death of his father Babur. The empire was new and unstable. In accordance with Timurid tradition his brothers — **Kamran**, **Askari** and **Hindal** — also received territories; Kamran controlled Kabul and Punjab. Distrust among the brothers proved Humayun's greatest weakness.

In the east, the Afghan chief of Bihar **Sher Khan** (later Sher Shah Suri) was rapidly growing powerful and had extended his influence as far as Bengal.

## Chausa (1539) and Kannauj (1540)

Returning from the Bengal campaign, Humayun's army clashed with Sher Khan's army at **Chausa** near Buxar. After a long wait and amid rain, Sher Khan attacked suddenly on **26 June 1539** and the Mughal army scattered. Humayun saved his life by crossing the Ganga with great difficulty. After this victory Sher Khan took the title 'Sher Shah'.

About a year later, on **17 May 1540**, a decisive battle took place near Kannauj (at Bilgram), called the **Battle of Kannauj** or **Bilgram**. Here too the Mughal army was badly defeated. Humayun fled towards Agra and then Lahore; he got no help from his brothers and had to leave India.

## Wandering in Sindh and the Birth of Akbar

For the next few years Humayun kept searching for allies in the desert regions of Sindh and Rajasthan. In 1541 he married **Hamida Banu Begum**. On **15 October 1542**, while he was sheltering with Rana Prasad, the Rajput ruler of **Amarkot** (Umarkot) in Sindh, his son **Akbar** was born.

## Refuge in Iran

Finding no base in India, Humayun moved towards Kandahar, but his brother Askari opposed him there. In haste he had to leave the infant Akbar behind. In 1544 Humayun reached the court of the Safavid ruler of Iran, **Shah Tahmasp**. The Shah welcomed him and gave him an army, but in return set some conditions — according to the sources these included showing respect for the Shia faith and promising to hand Kandahar over to Iran. Historians differ on the exact nature of these conditions.

With Iranian help Humayun conquered Kandahar and Kabul in 1545. The next several years were spent in conflict with his brother Kamran, which ended in 1553 when Kamran was captured.

## The Return: Sirhind and Delhi (1555)

Meanwhile the situation in India had changed. Sher Shah died at Kalinjar in 1545 and, after his son Islam Shah (1553), the Sur empire split among several claimants. Taking advantage of this, Humayun marched towards India at the end of 1554. Under the commander **Bairam Khan**, in 1555 the army of **Sikandar Shah Suri** was defeated first at Machhiwara and then at **Sirhind**, and in **July 1555** Humayun again sat on the throne of Delhi.

## A Sudden End and Legacy

This second reign was very short. In **January 1556**, while descending the stairs of his library **Sher Mandal** in the Purana Qila in Delhi, he fell, and died a few days later. The 13-year-old Akbar became emperor, with Bairam Khan as his guardian.

The years spent in Iran had a deep effect on Mughal culture — the Iranian painters Mir Sayyid Ali and Abd as-Samad joined Humayun's court through contacts of this period, and from them the foundation of Mughal painting was laid. **Humayun's Tomb** in Delhi, built under the supervision of his widow Bega Begum (Haji Begum), is regarded as the forerunner of later Mughal architecture and the Taj Mahal.
