## The City of Temples: Bhubaneswar

**Bhubaneswar**, the capital of Odisha, is called the 'City of Temples'. Hundreds of temples were built here between the 7th and 13th centuries — temples such as Parashurameshvara, Mukteshvara and Rajarani show different stages in the development of the Kalinga style. The highest and most mature form of this development is the **Lingaraja Temple**, dedicated to Shiva as 'Lingaraja', that is, the king of lingas.

In the Puranas this area is called **Ekamra Kshetra**. Tradition says that the deity here was earlier worshipped under a mango tree.

## Who Built It and When

The temple's present form is generally dated to the **last decades of the 11th century**. Credit for it is given to the **Somavamshi** rulers — some accounts name King **Yayati I** (about 1025–1040), while tradition names **Jajati Keshari** as the builder, who is said to have moved the capital from Jajpur to Bhubaneswar. Scholars disagree on the exact builder and year; some parts may be even older.

Later the rulers of the **Eastern Ganga** dynasty — who built the Jagannath temple at Puri — expanded and renovated the Lingaraja complex. Vaishnava elements were also added to the temple in this period.

## The Structure of the Kalinga Style

In the Kalinga style the temple is made of four parts built in a line, decreasing in height one after another:
- **Vimana (rekha deula)** — the sanctum, above which is a curving tall spire. Lingaraja's spire is said to be about **55 metres (180 feet)** high.
- **Jagamohana** — the assembly hall for devotees, with a pyramid-like stepped roof (pidha deula).
- **Natamandapa** — the hall for dance and festivals.
- **Bhogamandapa** — the hall for offerings (bhoga).

The temple is built of reddish-brown sandstone and the outer walls are richly carved with gods and goddesses, dancers, animals and flowers and leaves. Inside the huge laterite enclosure wall there are more than a hundred small shrines.

## Harihara: Shiva and Vishnu Together

A special feature of Lingaraja is that the deity here is also worshipped as **Harihara** — the combined form of Shiva and Vishnu. The temple also has images of Vishnu and Vaishnava doorkeepers. Historians see this as a blending of Shaiva and Vaishnava traditions in Odisha, which deepened further with the rise of Jagannath devotion.

## Festivals and the Temple Today

Lingaraja is still a living place of worship. The main festivals are:
- **Mahashivaratri** (Phalguna) — the biggest festival, which draws lakhs of devotees.
- **Chandana Yatra** — a 22-day festival in which the deity's images are taken around on a boat in the nearby **Bindusagar** tank.
- **Ashokashtami Rath Yatra** — in which the deity is taken on a chariot to the Rameshwar Deula.

Non-Hindus are not allowed inside the temple complex; for them a high platform has been built near the enclosure wall, from which the outer form of the temple can be seen.

## Significance

The Lingaraja Temple is considered the 'ideal specimen' of Kalinga architecture — the **Jagannath temple at Puri** (12th century) and the **Sun Temple at Konark** (13th century), built after it, carried this tradition forward. The temple shows how deeply architecture, religion and royal power were connected in medieval Odisha.
