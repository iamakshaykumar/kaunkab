## A Childhood Full of Struggle

**Rana Sanga**, that is **Maharana Sangram Singh I**, is believed to have been born on 12 April 1482. He was the son of the Sisodia ruler of Mewar, **Rana Raimal**, and the grandson of the famous **Rana Kumbha**. A fierce struggle over succession broke out among Raimal's sons — **Prithviraj**, Jaimal and Sangram Singh. According to tradition, Sanga lost an eye in this quarrel and had to live in hiding, away from Mewar, for some time.

After his brothers died, Sanga returned to Mewar and, on his father's death, took the throne in **1508** (in May 1509 according to some sources).

## Uniting Rajput Power

Sanga began his reign by bringing the neighbouring Rajput states together through diplomacy, marriage alliances and military pressure. Marwar, Amer, Merta, the Tomars of Gwalior and many small states accepted his leadership. A clash with all three — the Lodis of Delhi in the north, the sultanate of Gujarat in the west and the sultanate of Malwa in the east — was certain.

## Khatoli and Dholpur: Clashes with the Lodis

In **1517**, near **Khatoli** in the present-day Kota region, Sanga's army defeated the army of the Delhi sultan **Ibrahim Lodi**. In this battle one of Sanga's arms was cut off. In 1518–19, near **Dholpur**, the Lodi army suffered defeat once again. These victories extended the border of Mewar eastward as far as Bayana.

## Gagron: The Sultan Captured and Then Released

In Malwa, Sultan **Mahmud Khalji II** had gained the throne with the help of the Rajput chief **Medini Rai**, but later the two fell out. Medini Rai asked Sanga for help and entered his service. In the battle fought near **Gagron** in **1519**, Sanga's army decisively defeated the combined army of Malwa and Gujarat. The wounded sultan was taken prisoner.

Sanga kept him in Chittor for about six months, had his wounds treated and then let him return — one of the sultan's sons stayed in Mewar as a guarantee. After this war, areas such as Bhilsa, Raisen, Sarangpur and Chanderi came under Mewar's influence. In 1520 Sanga also marched against Gujarat.

## Eighty Wounds and Khanwa

The marks of constant wars were on Sanga's body. Tradition says that he had more than eighty wounds — he had lost an eye and an arm, and one leg was also crippled by injury. Until about 1521 his power was at its peak, and Babur too, in his memoirs, counted him among the greatest Indian rulers of the time.

After defeating Ibrahim Lodi at Panipat in 1526, Babur and Sanga came face to face. In February 1527 Sanga defeated a detachment of Babur's at **Bayana**, but in the Battle of **Khanwa** in **March 1527**, the Rajput–Afghan alliance was defeated before Babur's cannon and battle formation. The wounded Sanga was carried off the battlefield by his companions.

## Last Days and Legacy

On regaining consciousness, Sanga began preparing for war again, but he died near **Kalpi** on **30 January 1528**. According to many sources, he was poisoned by chiefs who did not want a new war; some sources give the date of death as May 1528. He was succeeded by his son **Ratan Singh II**. His other son **Udai Singh II** later became the founder of Udaipur and the father of **Maharana Pratap**.

In the memory of Rajasthan, Rana Sanga is remembered for his unconquerable courage, as a warrior who did not give up despite his wounds, and as a leader who brought scattered Rajput power under one banner.
