## Birth and Family

Mirabai is believed to have been born around **1498 CE** in the village of **Kudki** in Marwar, in a Rathore Rajput family. Her childhood was spent at **Merta**, which is linked with her grandfather Rao Duda. According to tradition, she found an idol of Krishna in childhood and from then on regarded Krishna as her 'husband'. This story belongs to the devotional tradition; there is no contemporary written evidence for it.

## Marriage in Mewar

Around **1516 CE** Mira was married to **Bhojraj**, the crown prince of Mewar and a son of the famous Rana Sanga. Bhojraj was wounded in a battle around 1518 and died around **1521 CE**. A few years later, after his defeat by Babur at the Battle of Khanwa in 1527, Rana Sanga too died (1528). Mewar was gripped by a political crisis.

The widowed Mira was expected to live by the norms of the royal family, but she kept singing and dancing with holy men in temples. This became a cause of conflict with her in-laws.

## The Cup of Poison and Folk Tales

According to popular tales, Rana Vikramaditya (Bhojraj's brother) and other relatives sent Mira a cup of poison and a snake hidden in a basket of flowers, but by Krishna's grace nothing happened to her. These stories recur in Mira's verses and in the Bhaktamal tradition, but historians regard them as symbolic or added later. Some scholars hold that the conflict was caused not only by social propriety but also by the political tensions of the time.

## Vrindavan and Dwarka

After leaving her in-laws' home, Mira set out on pilgrimage. Tradition places her among Krishna's devotees at **Vrindavan** and then at **Dwarka**. She is believed to have spent her last days in Dwarka and to have died there **around 1546-1547 CE**. Folk tradition says she merged into the idol of Ranchhod Ji (Krishna) — this is a tale of faith, not a historical account.

## Mira's Verses

Mira's verses are mainly in **Rajasthani** and **Braj Bhasha**, with some Gujarati influence as well. They express love, separation and complete surrender. The famous line attributed to her, "Mere to Girdhar Gopal, doosro na koi" ("For me there is only Girdhar Gopal, no one else"), shows this surrender.

The oldest datable mention of Mira is found in **Nabhadas's 'Bhaktamal'** (about 1600 CE). Many verses sung in her name were composed in her style by later devotees, so scholars distinguish between the 'historical Mira' and the 'Mira of folk memory'.

## Significance

Mirabai is among the few women's voices of the Bhakti movement whose works survived for centuries. For a woman of a royal family to leave aside social bonds and choose her own path of faith was extraordinary at that time. For this reason, in the 20th century many people, including Mahatma Gandhi, remembered her as an example of inner strength and non-violent resistance. Even today Mira's bhajans are sung in classical, folk and film music alike.
