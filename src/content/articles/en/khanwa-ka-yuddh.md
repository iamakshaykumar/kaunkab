## Background: After Panipat

In April 1526, after defeating Ibrahim Lodi in the First Battle of Panipat, Babur had taken control of Delhi and Agra. But his position in northern India was not yet secure. At that time the greatest political and military power in northern India was **Rana Sangram Singh** of Mewar, that is, **Rana Sanga**.

Rana Sanga had won many wars against the sultans of Delhi, Gujarat and Malwa, and most of the Rajput states of Rajasthan accepted his leadership. In his autobiography Babur claimed that Sanga had promised cooperation against Ibrahim Lodi but did not keep the promise; Rajput sources describe this differently. Whatever the case, a clash between the two powers was now certain.

## The Alliance and the Skirmish at Bayana

A large alliance formed under Rana Sanga's banner. Along with the Rajput chiefs of Marwar, Amber, Merta, Vagad and other states, it included **Medini Rai** of Chanderi, **Hasan Khan Mewati** of Mewat and **Mahmud Lodi**, brother of Ibrahim Lodi. That is, this conflict was based not on religion but on power, with people of different backgrounds on both sides.

In February 1527, near **Bayana**, Sanga's army defeated a detachment of Babur's. News of the large numbers and valour of the Rajput army spread panic among Babur's soldiers.

## Babur's Preparations

Babur took several steps to raise the morale of his army:
- He vowed to give up wine and had the wine vessels broken.
- He announced the remission of the **tamgha** (a trade tax) on Muslims.
- He gave an emotional speech to the soldiers and united them by giving this war a religious colour.

Militarily, he repeated the formation of Panipat: in front, a line was made by chaining carts together, behind which cannon and matchlockmen were posted under **Ustad Ali Quli** and Mustafa. At both ends, cavalry squads were kept ready for the **tulughma** (a flanking manoeuvre).

## March 1527: The Fight at Khanwa

In March 1527 the two armies faced each other at **Khanwa**, near Fatehpur Sikri, about 60 kilometres west of Agra. Most modern sources give the date as 16 March, while some give 17 March.

The Rajput cavalry repeatedly charged Babur's line and there was fierce fighting. But the cannon and guns firing from behind the wall of carts caused heavy destruction, and the tulughma squads, enveloping from the flank, scattered the alliance's army. Many chiefs such as Hasan Khan Mewati, Ratan Singh of Merta and Udai Singh of Vagad were killed.

During the battle Rana Sanga was seriously wounded and fell unconscious. His companions carried him safely off the battlefield. Some sources mention that Silhadi, the chief of Raisen, changed sides in the middle of the battle, but historians do not agree on this.

## Outcome and Significance

When he regained consciousness Rana Sanga began preparing for war again, but in January 1528 he died near **Kalpi**. According to many sources, some of his own chiefs, who did not want a fresh war, had poisoned him.

The victory at Khanwa is considered even more decisive than Panipat. It broke the biggest alliance of Rajput power of that time and gave Babur the opportunity to stay on in northern India. After the battles of Chanderi in 1528 and Ghaghra in 1529, the foundation of Mughal power was made firm, on which Akbar later built a vast empire.

In military history Khanwa is also important because it showed the decisive role of gunpowder, artillery and disciplined battle formation in northern India. Even so, Rana Sanga's courage and leadership are still remembered with respect in the memory of Rajasthan.
