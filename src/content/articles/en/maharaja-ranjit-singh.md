## Early Life: Heir of the Sukerchakia Misl

At the end of the 18th century, the Punjab was divided into many small Sikh **misls** (independent chiefdoms), which often fought one another. Afghan rulers kept attacking from the north-west.

In such times, on 13 November 1780, Ranjit Singh was born at Gujranwala (in present-day Pakistan) in the house of **Maha Singh**, chief of the Sukerchakia Misl. In childhood he lost the sight of his left eye to smallpox. After his father's death in 1792, at about twelve years of age, he became head of the misl. Despite little formal education, he had extraordinary military and diplomatic talent.

## Taking Lahore and the Title of Maharaja

In 1798–99, after the Afghan ruler **Zaman Shah** went back, Lahore was ruled weakly by three chiefs, with whom the people of the city were unhappy. On 7 July 1799 Ranjit Singh entered Lahore almost without bloodshed and made it his capital.

On 12 April 1801 (on the occasion of Baisakhi) he was proclaimed **Maharaja of the Punjab** at Lahore. Notably, he called his rule "Sarkar-e-Khalsa" and issued coins not in his own name but in the names of Guru Nanak and Guru Gobind Singh. In 1802 he also took **Amritsar**, the holy city of the Sikhs, under his control.

## Expansion of the Empire

Over the next three decades Ranjit Singh added the misls and neighbouring regions to his kingdom one by one:

- **1809** — The **Treaty of Amritsar** was made with the British, in which the Sutlej river was accepted as the boundary between the two. This stopped expansion to the east, but opened the way to advance west and north.
- **1818** — Conquest of Multan.
- **1819** — Kashmir added to the empire after the Battle of Shopian.
- **1834** — Peshawar taken; the commander **Hari Singh Nalwa** played a major role in holding back the Afghans on the north-western frontier.

During this time the famous **Koh-i-Noor diamond** also came to Ranjit Singh from the Afghan ruler Shah Shuja.

## Administration and Army

To modernise his army — the **Khalsa army** — Ranjit Singh appointed French, Italian and other European officers. The infantry and artillery were trained in the European way, so that it came to be counted among the most capable armies of Asia at that time.

His court was known for its diversity. Sikhs, Hindus and Muslims all held high posts; his foreign minister was Fakir Azizuddin, and Dogra chiefs also held important positions. Along with the Harmandir Sahib, he also gave donations to places of worship of other religions.

## Death and Legacy

Maharaja Ranjit Singh died at Lahore on 27 June 1839. After him there was no capable successor; the court became tangled in conspiracies and murders. After two Anglo-Sikh wars (1845–46 and 1848–49), the British annexed the Punjab in 1849.

Even so, Ranjit Singh's legacy is lasting. He is called "Sher-e-Punjab" (the Lion of the Punjab). For about four decades he gave the Punjab unity, stability and strength, and kept the British held back across the Sutlej.
